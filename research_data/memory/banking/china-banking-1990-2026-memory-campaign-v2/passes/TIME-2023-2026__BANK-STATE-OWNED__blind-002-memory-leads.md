## 1. Asset repricing versus deposit beta: the timing problem

- Asset repricing should be decomposed by cohort rather than inferred from a single average loan yield. Legacy mortgages, new mortgages, existing corporate loans, newly originated corporate loans and bond assets can reprice on different schedules.
- Deposit beta likewise has at least two dimensions: posted/new-deposit pricing and the realized average cost of the outstanding deposit stock. A posted cut can have little immediate effect on locked-in term deposits.
- A bank can therefore experience simultaneous falling new-loan prices and only slowly falling average deposit cost, even when both sides are nominally “repricing downward.”
- Deposit termization matters independently from the level of deposit rates. Migration from demand to time deposits can raise average liability cost despite cuts to quoted term-deposit rates.
- FTP is the internal bridge between these effects: branches that appear deposit-rich can still have different economic contribution depending on assigned liquidity/duration value and marginal reinvestment opportunity.
- Average NIM can conceal product-level cross-subsidy. A low-yield strategic-sector loan may still have acceptable economics if expected loss, servicing cost and capital intensity are low and funding is cheap.
- Conversely, large-volume low-yield lending can dilute return on assets even when credit losses remain benign.
- “存款利率下调” and possible period vocabulary around manual interest supplements are useful later search keys, but exact policy events and wording are intentionally unresolved here.

## 2. Mortgage prepayment as an embedded option

- Mortgage prepayment is not only a balance decline; it is an embedded borrower option that changes the effective duration of the mortgage book.
- When borrowers prepay higher-yield legacy mortgages, banks lose above-current-market assets earlier than contractual maturity and must reinvest at lower yields.
- Reducing rates on outstanding mortgages can suppress prepayment incentives and support household cash flow, while mechanically lowering yield on loans that remain outstanding.
- Thus prepayment and repricing can both reduce interest income through different paths: one through faster principal return, the other through lower coupon on retained principal.
- Household mortgage credit risk should be separated from prepayment risk. A borrower may be very low credit risk but still prepay aggressively.
- Housing-price weakness can erode collateral value without producing delinquency if household income and payment willingness remain intact.
- Unfinished-project or delivery uncertainty can affect mortgage willingness and confidence through a different mechanism than post-delivery house-price declines.

## 3. Developer group, project company and project-completion cash-flow priority

- Developer-group solvency, project-company viability, construction completion funding and homebuyer mortgages sit at different legal/cash-flow layers.
- A distressed parent developer can coexist with a project whose presale cash flow and remaining construction economics support completion.
- A project-completion loan can therefore be rational without implying that old developer-group equity or unsecured creditors are rescued.
- Financing coordination or “white-list” vocabulary should be treated as a project-screening/funding-coordination lead, not as proof of blanket credit support.
- Project-level bank analysis should ask where presale proceeds sit, who controls accounts, how construction costs rank, and what collateral or guarantees attach. Exact mechanics need evidence later.
- Completion can preserve mortgage performance, collateral value and social confidence even while the developer's legacy obligations are restructured.
- A newly extended project loan can have lower expected loss than a legacy developer corporate loan despite both being classified broadly as property exposure.
- Counterexample: a project can be completed successfully while bank recovery on unrelated developer-group claims remains poor.
- Counterexample: inclusion in a coordination mechanism does not eliminate sales, cost-overrun or sponsor-execution risk.

## 4. LGFV/local-debt resolution: default risk versus duration and return

- A useful orthogonal frame is to separate liquidity relief from solvency and from bank economics. Refinancing or maturity extension can materially reduce near-term default probability without improving the underlying local fiscal revenue base.
- Lowering the interest burden transfers some economics from creditor yield to borrower debt service. This can be socially/macro stabilizing yet margin-negative for the bank.
- Maturity extension converts some near-term credit-tail risk into longer duration and reinvestment/opportunity-cost risk.
- Loan-to-bond or other instrument conversion can change legal claim, liquidity, accounting classification, market-value sensitivity and RWA treatment; it is not merely a renamed loan.
- Government-bond exposure, recognized local-government debt and corporate LGFV credit should not be collapsed into one category.
- Persistent reliance on land or local fiscal support can survive a refinancing transaction even when the immediate instrument becomes safer.
- Operating LGFVs with utility, transport, toll, lease or other cash-generating assets are important counterexamples to treating every platform as a pure implicit-government claim.
- A bank may rationally accept lower spread in exchange for clearer repayment hierarchy, stronger legal claim or reduced tail loss. The economic tradeoff is not captured by NPL ratio alone.
- Successful “化债” can thus improve near-term asset quality while simultaneously creating a low-return asset-allocation problem for state banks.

## 5. Restructuring, NPL flow and denominator semantics

- The NPL stock should conceptually reconcile opening stock plus new formation minus write-offs, disposals, recoveries/upgrades and other transfers. A flat NPL stock can mask high gross formation and high disposal.
- NPL ratio adds another denominator effect: rapid loan growth can lower the ratio even if absolute NPLs rise.
- Overdue, special-mention, restructured and NPL classifications can move differently because they answer different questions about payment status, risk grade and contractual modification.
- A restructuring that makes payments current does not prove economic cure; equally, restructuring is not automatically concealment if the revised cash flow is genuinely sustainable.
- Cure rate and subsequent re-default are useful future evidence concepts for restructuring-heavy portfolios.
- Write-offs remove recognized assets from the balance sheet but do not erase the historical credit loss; post-write-off recoveries are a separate flow.
- Provision coverage can rise after NPL disposal even without proportionately larger new provisioning because its denominator changes.
- Credit cost is a period flow relative to loans, not the same thing as the stock of loan-loss reserves.
- Reserve-to-loan ratio and provision coverage should remain distinct during any future evidence stitching.

## 6. Capital, RWA and TLAC/loss absorption

- NIM compression matters for capital because lower recurring earnings reduce retained-earnings generation even if headline credit losses are stable.
- Asset growth can dilute capital ratios when RWA expands faster than retained earnings or fresh capital.
- RWA optimization can alter business mix: two loans with similar nominal yield may have different capital economics because collateral, counterparty, maturity or regulatory treatment differs.
- Government and high-quality bond assets may consume less credit-risk capital than private corporate loans but can still create duration and concentration exposure.
- TLAC/loss-absorption instruments are an independent funding/capital-stack lead: greater resolvability can come with a funding-cost tradeoff.
- Exact TLAC requirements, eligible instruments and implementation dates are intentionally left unresolved for later Evidence.
- Capital adequacy ratio, CET1/core capital, absolute capital and total loss-absorbing capacity should not be treated as synonyms.
- Counterexample: a bank can strengthen regulatory loss absorption while reported ROE falls because the denominator/cost of capital rises or margins compress.

## 7. Wealth subsidiaries: role decomposition

- Parent-bank distribution, wealth-subsidiary portfolio management, custody, product accounting and parent-bank balance-sheet exposure are different roles.
- A customer moving money from a parent-bank deposit into an affiliated wealth product can reduce on-balance deposits while preserving the group customer relationship and potentially producing fees.
- This migration changes liquidity/NIM economics even if consolidated customer assets remain stable.
- NAV losses in wealth products can create conduct/reputation pressure without being identical to bank credit losses.
- Any parent support or implicit-guarantee expectation must be established product-by-product; reputation is not a legal balance-sheet identity.
- Wealth AUM growth can coexist with deposit outflow and should therefore be interpreted jointly with customer-asset mix, not as an independent sign of funding strength or weakness.

## 8. Treasury: mark-to-market, carry and reinvestment are separate

- Falling yields can increase the market value of existing longer-duration bonds while depressing the yield available on new purchases.
- A one-time/period valuation gain should not be confused with sustainable interest carry.
- Banking-book and trading-book classifications can make identical market movements appear differently in accounting metrics.
- Reinvestment risk becomes more important when deposits remain abundant but loan demand or loan pricing is weak.
- Extending bond duration to preserve yield can improve current carry but increases sensitivity to a future rate reversal.
- Local-government debt instruments can be low default-risk while still adding duration and geographic/fiscal concentration.
- Treasury gains can temporarily cushion earnings without solving structural NIM compression.

## 9. Policy-priority credit: volume is not economics

- Manufacturing, technology, green and inclusive/micro lending should be evaluated as separate combinations of pricing, expected loss, operating cost and RWA rather than as a single “policy credit” category.
- Large state banks may have advantages in funding and data scale but can face diseconomies when serving very small borrowers through traditional branch processes.
- Digital underwriting can lower acquisition/servicing cost, yet model drift and fraud selection can worsen if historical data do not represent a new macro regime.
- Aggressive volume targets can produce price competition without necessarily producing immediate NPLs; seasoning remains important.
- Low nominal pricing can still be attractive when borrowers are high quality and capital intensity is low.
- Counterexample: higher-yield SME lending can generate worse risk-adjusted return than lower-yield strategic-corporate lending after expected loss and operating cost.

## 10. AI/digital adoption: pilot, productivity and control are different

- Generative-AI or large-model pilots are not equivalent to realized productivity. Benefits depend on workflow integration, data permissions, review loops and error tolerance.
- Customer-service automation, code assistance, document extraction, fraud detection and risk analysis have different model-risk profiles.
- Hallucination or incorrect generation is more tolerable in internal drafting than in binding customer, credit or compliance decisions.
- Data silos can limit the value of AI even for banks with large nominal data volumes; governance and lineage are part of the bottleneck.
- Anti-fraud models can reduce losses while increasing false positives, account restrictions and customer-service burden.
- Stronger remote controls can shift fraud away from easily detected channels rather than eliminate it.
- Third-party models/cloud/tooling create dependency and vendor-risk questions in addition to cybersecurity.
- Branch economics can migrate toward relationship management, advice, complex sales and local service rather than disappear entirely.
- ABC's rural/counties footprint is a counterexample to assuming digital substitution has uniform economics across geography and demographics.

## 11. Four-bank heterogeneity as falsification checks

- ICBC scale: a small spread change or deposit-mix shift can have outsized aggregate earnings effects; scale also broadens diversification.
- CCB housing orientation: mortgage prepayment, outstanding-mortgage repricing and project-completion channels are particularly useful falsification lenses, without assuming exact exposure rankings.
- BOC cross-border/FX: foreign rates, currencies, trade finance and compliance can offset or amplify domestic margin trends in ways not shared equally by peers.
- ABC county/rural footprint: branch density, rural deposits, agricultural policy service and digital substitution can produce liability and operating-cost patterns unlike more urban franchises.
- No bank-specific numerical claim is asserted here; these are heterogeneity prompts for later evidence.

## 13. Counterexamples and failed shortcuts

- Deposit-rate cuts do not imply immediate average funding-cost cuts when the stock is term-heavy.
- Lower mortgage rates can improve household affordability while hurting bank asset yield.
- A completed property project can protect mortgages while old developer-group claims remain impaired.
- LGFV refinancing can lower default probability yet worsen duration and asset-yield economics.
- Flat NPL ratios can coexist with high gross NPL formation and high write-offs; they can also reflect genuine improvement, so flow reconciliation is necessary.
- High provision coverage does not by itself show high current-period provisioning.
- Low-risk bonds do not eliminate market-duration risk.
- Wealth AUM growth can reduce deposits without losing the customer relationship.
- More AI use does not establish lower operating cost or better risk decisions.
- A broad deposit inflow can strengthen liquidity yet worsen marginal reinvestment return.
- Policy-priority lending is not inherently uneconomic; pricing must be read with expected loss, servicing cost and RWA.
