## Orthogonal recall leads

1. [new_category] The predecessor population should include failed, absorbed and renamed urban credit cooperatives, not only the ancestors highlighted by surviving city commercial banks.
2. [new_category] A successor city-level bank may inherit only part of a predecessor network; lineage should not assume one-to-one continuity of licenses, branches, managers or balance sheets.
3. [new_category] Some weak urban credit cooperatives may have been closed, merged into stronger local institutions or otherwise resolved rather than preserved as named predecessors; exact cases require later evidence.
4. [new_category] “Predecessor count” is therefore a poor standalone lineage metric unless it distinguishes legal entities, outlets, cooperatives and balance sheets actually transferred.
5. [new_category] Urban-credit consolidation can create an accounting discontinuity: successor-bank opening balances may combine cleaned assets, transferred liabilities and recognized losses rather than simple summation.
6. [new_category] 清产核资 is a high-value mechanism/search key because municipal reorganization may have required asset verification before or during consolidation.
7. [new_category] Loss-recognition timing is central: a merger can expose losses that existed economically before they were reported in a successor institution.
8. [new_category] A local authority can simultaneously be rescuer, owner/coordinator and source of directed-credit pressure; these roles should be separated in causal analysis.
9. [new_category] Municipal support can stabilize depositor confidence without improving underlying borrower cash flow, so funding stability can outlast asset-quality deterioration.
10. [new_category] Conversely, a solvent local institution can face liquidity stress if depositors doubt implicit support or if local enterprises withdraw funds during tightening.
11. [new_category] Liquidity support from the central bank/local monetary authorities should be analyzed separately from recapitalization; lending liquidity does not repair negative net worth.
12. [new_category] “资不抵债” and “支付困难” can describe different failure modes and should not be collapsed into one generic distress label.
13. [new_category] A local credit cooperative may remain open under administrative support despite weak economic capital, creating a form of forbearance distinct from formal resolution.
14. [new_category] Administrative mergers may socialize losses across stronger and weaker cooperatives; this can protect depositors while diluting incentives for ex ante discipline.
15. [new_category] Deposit protection before formal deposit insurance likely depended on expectations of local/state intervention, but those expectations could differ by city fiscal capacity and political importance.
16. [new_category] Stronger municipal fiscal capacity could make implicit support more credible, while fiscally weaker cities could face tighter constraints; this is a regional heterogeneity hypothesis to test later.
17. [new_category] Old industrial cities may combine concentrated SOE/collective borrowers with weaker new private-commerce growth, producing a different predecessor asset mix from coastal commercial cities.
18. [new_category] Coastal boom cities may instead face faster property, construction, trade and private-enterprise credit expansion; risk can arise from growth volatility rather than legacy SOE burden.
19. [new_category] Port/trade-oriented cities may generate more settlement and working-capital demand, but city-credit institutions' actual FX/trade-finance capability may have been limited relative to specialized/national banks.
20. [new_category] The city-credit franchise can therefore be commercially vibrant while product scope remains narrow; customer demand and institutional capability should not be conflated.
21. [new_category] Local wholesale markets and merchant clusters can create dense deposit-and-credit ecosystems with high information flow but also correlated guarantor networks.
22. [new_category] Mutual guarantees or circular enterprise guarantees can transform many apparently small exposures into one local network-risk cluster.
23. [new_category] A municipal borrower network may include SOEs, collectives, developers/construction firms and suppliers whose cash flows are jointly exposed to local investment cycles.
24. [new_category] Early property exposure should be captured through developer/construction/local-project borrowers and collateral values rather than later mortgage-book concepts.
25. [new_category] Household mortgage credit is unlikely to be the dominant property transmission channel in this slice; enterprise/project-side exposures are the more plausible local-bank route.
26. [new_category] 1993 tightening can reveal hidden rollover dependence: enterprises that serviced debt through continued refinancing may become overdue when credit expansion slows.
27. [new_category] “逾期” can therefore be a liquidity-event signal rather than a full loss estimate; later recovery, restructuring or continued support can change economic outcome.
28. [new_category] “呆滞” and “呆账” categories likely embed administrative/accounting criteria that do not map cleanly to later probability-of-default concepts.
29. [new_category] Problem-loan vintage analysis is likely impossible from headline old categories alone; loan renewal and classification practice need institution-level evidence.
30. [new_category] Write-off capacity may be constrained by thin earnings/capital and approval/accounting rules, so low write-off volume would not imply low losses.
31. [new_category] Municipal consolidation may be partly motivated by supervisory visibility: one consolidated entity is easier to monitor than many fragmented cooperatives.
32. [new_category] Consolidation may also enable centralized treasury/liquidity management, reducing idiosyncratic outlet-level funding mismatches even before major franchise changes.
33. [new_category] Centralized credit approval after consolidation could reduce some insider lending while weakening local soft-information advantages; governance improvement can carry information costs.
34. [new_category] Common IT/accounting systems are a latent consolidation benefit because borrower duplication and related-party exposures become easier to detect once data are unified.
35. [new_category] Data integration itself can generate apparent asset-quality deterioration by revealing connected exposures previously hidden across separate institutions.
36. [new_category] Personnel integration is another risk: managers from predecessor cooperatives may preserve old relationship networks inside the successor institution despite formal governance change.
37. [new_category] A new legal charter does not immediately create a new risk culture; inherited staff incentives and local political ties can persist.
38. [new_category] Local-government board/shareholder influence can become more formal after consolidation rather than disappearing; centralization may change the channel of influence rather than its existence.
39. [new_category] “Modern commercial-bank governance” is therefore not a binary before/after state; ownership, management appointment, credit approval and risk control can transition at different speeds.
40. [new_category] The urban-credit-to-city-bank transition should be analyzed as a bundle of legal, capital, governance, data, treasury and supervisory changes rather than a rename.
41. [new_category] Cooperative versus bank boundary matters for liabilities as well as ownership: deposit-taking permissions, reserve requirements and payment-system access may change with legal form.
42. [new_category] Exact reserve/liquidity rules for urban credit cooperatives should be evidence-verified; modern commercial-bank prudential ratios must not be back-cast.
43. [new_category] Rural credit cooperatives can operate near urban fringes, so geographic location alone is insufficient to classify an institution as BANK-CITY versus BANK-RURAL.
44. [new_category] Institutional lineage and supervisory/legal identity should decide the city/rural boundary, not the later urbanization of a county or district.
45. [new_category] National joint-stock banks may compete for the same merchants/enterprises in reform cities, but national legal scope and ownership/franchise structure keep them outside BANK-CITY.
46. [new_category] Local trust/investment companies or enterprise finance companies may also fund municipal/local firms; they are boundary competitors/adjacent institutions, not city-bank predecessors by default.
47. [new_category] A veteran-analyst trap is to attribute all local urban non-state finance to credit cooperatives; local trust, informal finance and national-bank local branches can share the same borrower ecosystem.
48. [new_category] City-credit institutions may have comparative advantage in borrower access but disadvantage in liquidity depth, capital, technology and product breadth versus large banks.
49. [new_category] This asymmetry helps explain why municipal consolidation can seek both scale and institutional credibility, not merely rescue weak entities.
50. [new_category] Reputation at the city level can become a common asset after consolidation: one municipal bank brand may replace many fragmented cooperative names.
51. [useful_refinement] “Local support” should be decomposed into depositor reassurance, liquidity provision, loss absorption, shareholder recapitalization, borrower coordination and merger sponsorship.
52. [useful_refinement] “Insider lending” should be tested through ownership ties, management ties, municipal assignment and guarantor networks rather than assumed from anecdotes.
53. [useful_refinement] “Soft budget constraint” can operate through repeated refinancing, non-recognition of losses, local guarantees or pressure to maintain employment-sensitive lending.
54. [useful_refinement] “Relationship lending” should be separated into genuine information advantage versus coercive/political relationship lending.
55. [useful_refinement] City-credit deposit franchise should distinguish household savings, merchant operating deposits, local-enterprise deposits and government/administrative balances where applicable.
56. [useful_refinement] Deposit concentration by a few local enterprises can make apparently stable corporate funding vulnerable to one local downturn or policy decision.
57. [useful_refinement] Household deposits may be stickier but require branch/outlet trust and convenient cash/payment access; exact mix is institution-specific.
58. [useful_refinement] Payment-system access and clearing arrangements are likely important operational constraints but require period-specific verification.
59. [useful_refinement] Geographic concentration should be measured not only by city but by industry, guarantor network, fiscal dependence and property collateral correlation.
60. [useful_refinement] A later city-bank “local SME franchise” can contain inherited collective/SOE relationships as well as genuinely new private-enterprise lending; historical continuity should be tested, not presumed.
61. [useful_refinement] Early-1990s tightening may affect regions asynchronously; local economic structure can matter more than national aggregate credit conditions for a city-credit institution.
62. [useful_refinement] Municipal merger timing can follow distress with a lag, so later consolidation dates should not be mistaken for the date underlying problems began.
63. [useful_refinement] New capital injected during reorganization can improve reported solvency while legacy problem assets remain economically unresolved.
64. [useful_refinement] Asset transfers or write-downs during reorganization can create apparent post-merger improvement that is partly perimeter change.
65. [useful_refinement] Staff/branch rationalization can improve costs but may weaken local depositor relationships; consolidation has franchise trade-offs.
66. [useful_refinement] Strong surviving city banks may be selected from municipalities with unusually strong economies/fiscal capacity, creating survivor bias in later peer comparisons.
67. [useful_refinement] The absence of a surviving named city bank does not imply the absence of an urban-credit-cooperative system in the earlier city.
68. [useful_refinement] Modern bank-history narratives may compress many messy predecessor events into one founding story; claim-level evidence should unpack these later.
69. [duplicate] Do not project later city-commercial-bank legal names or modern prudential ratios backward into 1990-1994.
70. [duplicate] Geographic concentration creates both information advantage and correlated local risk.
71. [duplicate] Municipal consolidation can be a governance/resolution/data-integration mechanism, not merely scale expansion.
72. [duplicate] Liquidity support, solvency repair and franchise repair are separate processes.
