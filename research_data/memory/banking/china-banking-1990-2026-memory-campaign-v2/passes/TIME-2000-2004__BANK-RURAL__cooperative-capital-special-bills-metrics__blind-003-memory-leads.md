3. **[R | high] Unified legal person is not the same thing as conversion to a rural commercial bank.** One changes the number/perimeter of legal persons; another changes institutional/legal form and governance. They may occur sequentially or under different reform paths.
4. **[N | high] Reform paths may include multiple destination forms rather than one linear model.** Memory suggests some stronger county institutions could move toward rural commercial-bank or rural cooperative-bank forms while others retained cooperative identity under a unified county legal person.
5. **[N | medium] Early rural commercial banks likely appeared first in stronger county economies.** Treat this only as a cohort lead: strong local economies/capital bases may have enabled earlier commercialization, providing a counterexample to the idea that legal conversion itself created strength.

7. **[R | medium] Local-government management responsibility is not identical to local-government equity ownership.** Administrative responsibility for reform, personnel or risk disposal can coexist with nominal cooperative/shareholder ownership.
8. **[R | medium] Provincial union creation/strengthening is not automatically a parent-bank consolidation.** Industry management, technology, clearing and personnel influence should remain separate from legal ownership and county balance-sheet perimeter.

10. **[R | medium] Legal conversion does not erase predecessor claims.** Historical bad loans, insider exposures, member-share disputes or local guarantees can survive inside the successor and need lineage provenance.

18. **[R | medium] Book net assets and regulatory capital are not interchangeable.** Loss cleanup or support assets can improve accounting equity without necessarily producing the same prudential-capital effect.
