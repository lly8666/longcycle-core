## 1. Scope correction and taxonomy leads

1. **[high][new_category] Four-bank-only framing is materially incomplete.** A large-state-bank history that compares only ICBC/CCB/BOC/ABC misses two later large-bank archetypes: Bank of Communications (BoCom/交通银行) and Postal Savings Bank of China (PSBC/中国邮政储蓄银行).
2. **[high][new_category] Peer-set vocabulary changes through time.** `四大行`, later `五大行`, and later `六大行` are point-in-time labels rather than timeless legal categories. Exact dates and official wording require Evidence-stage verification.
3. **[high][new_category] Legal form and economic/state-control taxonomy diverge.** A bank can be legally a joint-stock commercial bank yet belong economically or administratively to the large state-controlled-bank peer set. BoCom is the clearest counterexample to equating `joint-stock legal form` with the `BANK-JOINT-STOCK` business-model shard.
4. **[high][new_category] PSBC creates the same semantic problem from another direction.** Its later joint-stock/listed form does not erase a postal-savings lineage, China Post relationship, agency-network economics, or large state-owned-bank role.
5. **[medium][useful_refinement] Search-key vocabulary should preserve historical labels.** Leads: `交通银行/交行`, `邮政储蓄/邮储/邮储银行`, `国有控股大型商业银行`, `大型商业银行`, `四大行/五大行/六大行`, `邮银协同`, `代理网点`, `自营网点`.

## 2. Bank of Communications — antecedent, ownership and reform path

7. **[high][new_category] BoCom is an early joint-stock/state-controlled experiment distinct from the Big Four conversion path.** Its governance history is useful precisely because joint-stock corporate form appears earlier than the later large-bank shareholding/listing reforms of ICBC/CCB/BOC/ABC.
8. **[high][new_category] State control and mixed ownership coexist.** Likely relevant actors include central state shareholders plus non-state/market shareholders; the shareholder mix changed through time and should be reconstructed point-in-time rather than projected backward.
9. **[high][new_category] Foreign strategic-investor episode is a separate governance mechanism.** HSBC is the remembered foreign strategic partner. Exact entry date, stake, lock-up/exit mechanics and later changes require verification.
10. **[high][new_category] Capability transfer versus signaling should be separated.** The HSBC relationship may have affected governance, risk, cards/retail, treasury, product or management practices, but the degree of operational transfer versus credibility/signaling is an empirical question.
11. **[medium][new_category] Board representation is not equivalent to control.** A foreign strategic shareholder could influence governance and capability transfer while state control remained decisive; exact rights and representation require source work.
12. **[high][new_category] BoCom's listing path predates or differs from several Big Four sequences.** Hong Kong and mainland listings belong to the reform story, but exact dates/order should be verified rather than reconstructed from memory here.
13. **[medium][useful_refinement] Listing should be decomposed into disclosure, minority-shareholder claims, capital access, valuation discipline and management reputation rather than treated as a single governance switch.
14. **[high][new_category] BoCom is a bridge case between two narratives.** It can resemble national joint-stock banks in corporate form and market discipline while resembling large state banks in state control, systemic role, national network and policy transmission.
15. **[medium][new_category] Shanghai/Yangtze River Delta institutional geography may matter.** Headquarters and regional commercial heritage can affect corporate-client mix and market orientation; exact concentration should be verified.

## 3. BoCom — funding, franchise, subsidiaries and stress-cycle heterogeneity

16. **[high][new_category] Funding/business-model heterogeneity should not be inferred from legal form.** BoCom likely had a broad deposit franchise and national branch network that made it unlike wholesale-funded growth models associated with some national joint-stock peers.
17. **[medium][new_category] Corporate and transport/infrastructure associations need disentangling.** The bank's name can tempt analysts to infer a transport-sector specialization; actual sector concentrations should be verified instead of assumed from branding/history.
18. **[medium][new_category] Retail/card development may be a strategic-investor capability-transfer test.** Product partnerships or imported practices may have mattered, but exact branded programs and causal attribution are uncertain.
19. **[medium][new_category] Wealth, fund, leasing, trust/insurance or other subsidiaries may create a financial-group dimension.** Exact subsidiaries, start dates and ownership structures are Evidence-stage questions; the lead is that BoCom's group perimeter may differ from the four-bank template.
20. **[high][new_category] Cross-border and FX exposure may sit between BOC and domestically focused peers.** BoCom has overseas/cross-border activity, but its sensitivity should be tested rather than automatically treated as BOC-like.
21. **[high][new_category] Stress-cycle comparison should include BoCom explicitly.** Property, LGFV/local-government, interbank/market, SME/private-enterprise and overseas exposures can load differently from ICBC/CCB/BOC/ABC because franchise, geography and group perimeter differ.
22. **[medium][useful_refinement] Capital and asset-quality metrics need BoCom-specific denominators.** NPL ratio, provision coverage, RWA density and capital ratios can look comparable while portfolio mix differs materially.
23. **[medium][new_category] Mixed-ownership expectations can become a counterexample.** A bank may have strategic investors, listed-company governance and mixed shareholders yet still exhibit state-policy objectives and state-control constraints.
24. **[medium][new_category] Strategic partnership can disappoint without formally failing.** Capability transfer may be selective, slow, culturally bounded or subordinate to domestic governance priorities; absence of dramatic failure does not imply full convergence to foreign-bank practices.

## 4. PSBC — postal-savings lineage and institutional conversion

25. **[high][new_category] PSBC's relevant antecedent is postal savings, not a conventional commercial-bank branch network.** Historical deposit mobilization through the postal system is required to explain the modern bank inside the primary horizon.
26. **[high][new_category] China Post is a structural actor, not merely a shareholder label.** Parent/group relationship, postal infrastructure, outlet access, service provision and customer traffic can affect distribution economics and governance simultaneously.
27. **[high][new_category] Self-operated versus agency/postal outlets are economically different units.** `自营网点` and `代理网点` should not be summed as if they created identical control, staffing, economics or customer ownership.
28. **[high][new_category] Agency fees are a core business-model mechanism.** Postal-network access can create extraordinary geographic reach and deposit gathering while generating a recurring expense/transfer-pricing issue between the bank and postal group. Exact fee formula and magnitude require verification.
29. **[high][new_category] Deposit mobilization preceded full commercial-bank asset transformation.** A postal-savings institution can begin with abundant retail deposits yet relatively constrained lending capability, producing a different initial asset mix from a conventional commercial bank.
30. **[high][new_category] Early asset allocation may therefore have leaned toward bonds, interbank placements or other lower-credit-origination channels.** Exact composition and transition timing require source verification.
31. **[high][new_category] Commercialization creates an asset-side transformation risk.** Expanding from savings/deposit conduit toward retail, SME, agriculture and other lending creates underwriting, data, collections and credit-seasoning challenges even when liability funding is strong.
32. **[high][new_category] PSBC's conversion into a commercial-bank/joint-stock/listed institution is a multi-stage process.** Establishment, joint-stock transformation, strategic-investor introduction and domestic/offshore listing should be reconstructed as separate events; exact dates are intentionally not asserted here.
33. **[medium][new_category] Strategic investors in PSBC should be treated as capability/governance leads, not proof of control change.** Exact investor identities, stakes and operational contributions need verification.
34. **[high][new_category] State ownership versus legal joint-stock form again diverges.** PSBC later belongs in the large state-owned-bank comparison even though its corporate form and historical path are unlike a ministry-era specialized bank.

## 5. PSBC — rural/inclusive franchise, network economics and risk seasoning

35. **[high][new_category] Rural reach is not the same mechanism as ABC's rural reach.** ABC inherits a full-service agricultural/rural commercial-bank lineage; PSBC's reach is heavily tied to postal distribution and savings mobilization. Treating them as interchangeable rural banks obscures the mechanism.
36. **[high][new_category] Postal network can create a low-cost/granular funding advantage but not costless funding.** Deposit stickiness, customer mix, agency fees, service obligations and termization all matter to the true economic cost.
37. **[high][new_category] County/rural customer data may be a capability asset.** Postal transactions and broad geographic presence may aid customer acquisition, but credit underwriting requires different information and risk infrastructure than deposit servicing.
38. **[high][new_category] Inclusive/SME/agricultural credit expansion should be analyzed as portfolio seasoning.** Rapid migration into newer credit books can keep reported NPLs benign before cohorts season; overdue, special-mention, restructuring and write-off flows may be more informative than point NPL ratios.
39. **[medium][new_category] Agency network complicates operational-risk accountability.** Service quality, fraud, KYC/AML, conduct, data access and customer complaints may cross the organizational boundary between bank and postal agent.
40. **[medium][new_category] Branch productivity metrics are semantically hazardous.** Revenue or deposits per `outlet` are not comparable across owned bank branches, self-operated PSBC outlets and postal agency points without adjusting for functions and cost allocation.
41. **[high][new_category] Digitalization is complement as well as substitute.** Mobile banking can reduce transaction dependence on physical outlets while the postal network remains valuable for trust, cash services, acquisition and inclusion in lower-density areas.
42. **[medium][new_category] Network moat can become a legacy-cost question.** If customer behavior digitalizes, the optimal economics of maintaining or paying for a very large physical agency network can shift even if reach remains strategically valuable.
43. **[high][new_category] Retail-heavy identity does not imply low risk.** Mortgage, consumer, micro/SME and agricultural credit carry different cycles, and fast asset-side expansion from a deposit-rich base can introduce unfamiliar loss timing.
44. **[medium][useful_refinement] Wealth/deposit migration may be unusually important for PSBC.** A savings-heavy customer base can move among deposits, wealth products, funds or insurance; parent-bank deposits and distributed/AUM products should remain distinct.

## 7. Contemporaneous expectations, dead ends and counterexamples

53. **[high][new_category] BoCom contemporaneous expectation lead:** early shareholding reform plus foreign strategic participation may have been expected to make it a governance/model bridge between state banks and market-oriented joint-stock banks.
54. **[medium][new_category] Counterexpectation:** imported governance/capability does not necessarily displace state objectives, domestic incentive systems or legacy branch culture.
55. **[high][new_category] PSBC contemporaneous expectation lead:** vast postal reach and retail deposits may have been viewed as a durable moat and financial-inclusion platform once commercial lending capability matured.
56. **[high][new_category] Counterexpectation:** deposit abundance does not guarantee high profitability if asset yields are constrained, agency costs are material, or safe assets dominate during transition.
57. **[high][new_category] Counterexample to “rural franchise = ABC”.** PSBC shows rural inclusion can be delivered through postal-agent distribution and savings relationships instead of an agricultural-bank institutional lineage.
58. **[high][new_category] Counterexample to “joint-stock bank = BANK-JOINT-STOCK”.** BoCom shows joint-stock legal form can coexist with state control and large-bank systemic/policy characteristics.
59. **[medium][new_category] Counterexample to “more outlets = more owned operating capacity”.** PSBC's agency network creates reach without identical ownership/control of every outlet.
60. **[medium][new_category] Counterexample to “strategic investor = full capability transplant”.** Both BoCom-style foreign participation and PSBC strategic-investor rounds should be tested for selective transfer, signaling, governance constraints and later dilution/change.
61. **[medium][new_category] Dead-end risk: taxonomy itself can hide actor differences.** Retrospectively calling all six institutions `六大行` can erase decades when BoCom and especially PSBC were institutionally different or not yet in the same peer set.
62. **[medium][new_category] Dead-end risk: modern consolidated metrics can back-project comparability that did not exist.** Historical balance-sheet, branch, deposit and profitability series need vintage-specific denominator and perimeter checks.
