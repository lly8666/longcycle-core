## Orthogonal omissions and semantic traps

### MC-2000-04-O01 — the FX-liquidity chain had multiple institutional steps
- **Novelty:** new_category
- **Importance:** high
- **Confidence:** medium-high
- The mechanism should not be reduced to `PBOC buys FX -> RMB appears`. Commercial banks, foreign-exchange settlement/surrender arrangements and the foreign-exchange administration system mediated how exporter/investor FX became central-bank foreign assets and domestic RMB liquidity.
- Search keys later may include `结售汇`, `外汇指定银行`, `外汇占款`, `国家外汇管理`.
- The accounting boundary between commercial-bank FX settlement, PBOC foreign assets and reserve money needs source-native definitions.

### MC-2000-04-O02 — reserve money should be decomposed from the PBOC balance sheet, not inferred from FX reserves alone
- **Novelty:** new_category
- **Importance:** high
- **Confidence:** high as a semantic requirement
- A later reconstruction should separate currency issuance, financial-institution reserve deposits and other central-bank liabilities, and distinguish foreign assets, claims on banks/government and sterilization liabilities on the asset/liability side.
- `外汇储备增长`, `外汇占款增长`, `基础货币增长` and `银行可贷资金增长` are not interchangeable series.

### MC-2000-04-O03 — sterilization created rollover and interest-cost constraints
- **Novelty:** new_category
- **Importance:** high
- **Confidence:** medium-high
- Repeated issuance of sterilization liabilities can create a stock that must be rolled and remunerated. The cost/yield of central-bank bills therefore matters separately from the quantity absorbed.
- This may have affected bank asset allocation because banks exchanged reserves for central-bank paper rather than simply losing liquidity.
- Exact fiscal/quasi-fiscal treatment is uncertain and needs Evidence.

### MC-2000-04-O04 — OMO depended on a primary-dealer/counterparty architecture
- **Novelty:** new_category
- **Importance:** high
- **Confidence:** medium-high
- A forgotten implementation layer is the set of approved open-market counterparties/primary dealers and how operations were allocated among them.
- Counterparty concentration could make the first-round liquidity effect different from system-wide transmission.
- Exact dealer lists and selection rules are source-detail work.

### MC-2000-04-O05 — interbank bond market and exchange bond market were distinct transmission venues
- **Novelty:** new_category
- **Importance:** high
- **Confidence:** high at structural level
- Commercial-bank bond liquidity in this period was centered on the interbank market rather than being a single unified securities market.
- The segmentation matters for who held government/policy-bank/central-bank securities, repo collateral and how OMO reached bank balance sheets.
- Do not use a generic `bond-market yield` without identifying venue and participant set.

### MC-2000-04-O06 — central-bank-bill yields were both funding prices and managed operating outcomes
- **Novelty:** useful_refinement
- **Importance:** high
- **Confidence:** medium-high
- `央票利率` may contain information about liquidity conditions and policy intent, but issuance quantity, maturity, auction design and policy guidance mean it should not automatically be treated as a freely discovered policy rate.
- A later PIT series should preserve maturity and issuance mechanism before comparing yields.

### MC-2000-04-O07 — required reserves, excess reserves and their remuneration formed a three-part incentive system
- **Novelty:** useful_refinement
- **Importance:** medium-high
- **Confidence:** high as a semantic distinction
- Reserve transmission depends not only on the required-reserve ratio but also on excess reserve holdings and the interest paid on required/excess balances.
- A bank with abundant remunerated excess reserves can react differently from a bank close to its payment/liquidity constraint.
- Exact remuneration changes are deliberately unresolved.

### MC-2000-04-O08 — differentiated reserve requirements likely depended on bank condition, not just credit growth
- **Novelty:** useful_refinement
- **Importance:** high
- **Confidence:** medium
- My recollection is that early differentiated-reserve treatment was linked to institution-specific prudential condition such as capital adequacy/asset quality and possibly excessive expansion, not a simple sectoral reserve ratio.
- This makes it a hybrid monetary-prudential device; exact criteria must be verified before any legal or numerical claim.

### MC-2000-04-O09 — lending-rate liberalization had asymmetric floors, ceilings and institution exceptions
- **Novelty:** useful_refinement
- **Importance:** high
- **Confidence:** medium-high on asymmetry, low on exact multipliers
- The useful historical object is not `rates liberalized in 2004` but the exact evolution of loan-rate lower/upper bounds, deposit ceilings/floors and possible wider treatment for rural/cooperative institutions.
- Later Evidence should preserve the contemporaneous benchmark multiple/band vocabulary rather than map it directly to modern risk-based pricing.

### MC-2000-04-O10 — internal bank credit-authority centralization was a monetary-transmission constraint
- **Novelty:** new_category
- **Importance:** high
- **Confidence:** medium-high
- During large-bank reform/NPL cleanup, head offices appear to have centralized credit approval, branch authorization and risk controls.
- This can explain why identical system liquidity did not create identical regional or borrower credit supply: internal delegation rules became part of transmission.
- This mechanism belongs here even though detailed governance belongs in bank-specific shards.

### MC-2000-04-O11 — PBOC monetary policy and the newly separated banking-supervision function should not be collapsed
- **Novelty:** new_category
- **Importance:** high
- **Confidence:** high at boundary level; exact institutional details deferred
- Around this period banking supervision became institutionally more separated from the central bank. For monetary-credit research, the key lead is to distinguish PBOC liquidity/rate/window-guidance actions from prudential credit restrictions imposed through the banking-supervision channel.
- Exact legal transfer dates and mandates belong to the sealed regulation/resolution source-detail track and are not asserted here.

### MC-2000-04-O12 — bill financing required genuine commercial-paper plumbing
- **Novelty:** useful_refinement
- **Importance:** high
- **Confidence:** medium-high
- Discount financing depended on bank acceptances/commercial bills, endorsement/settlement and a claimed trade background; weak authenticity or circular issuance could turn a transaction-finance instrument into quasi-credit arbitrage.
- `票据融资占比` therefore needs denominator and instrument-definition care before being compared with ordinary RMB loans.

### MC-2000-04-O13 — rural reform support may have been conditional rather than ordinary central-bank lending
- **Novelty:** useful_refinement
- **Importance:** high
- **Confidence:** medium
- Special central-bank paper/re-lending tied to rural-credit reform may have been conditional on reform progress, ownership restructuring or loss-sharing arrangements.
- If so, treating the instrument as routine liquidity injection would erase its reform-contract character.
- Exact instrument form, redemption conditions and balance-sheet treatment require Evidence.

### MC-2000-04-O14 — postal-savings fund flows remained a rural-liquidity counterexample
- **Novelty:** new_category
- **Importance:** medium-high
- **Confidence:** medium
- Postal savings in the early 2000s may still have centralized large household deposits away from rural local credit before reforms allowed more market deployment of those funds.
- This is a counterexample to the assumption that abundant national liquidity automatically translated into abundant local rural lending.
- Exact institutional reform timing is uncertain.

### MC-2000-04-O15 — fiscal/treasury deposits could move banking-system liquidity independently of monetary stance
- **Novelty:** new_category
- **Importance:** high
- **Confidence:** medium-high
- Changes in government deposits, tax collection and fiscal spending can shift funds between commercial banks and central-bank/treasury accounts, changing reserve liquidity even without a discretionary PBOC operation.
- This autonomous factor should be separated from OMO and FX-purchase effects when reconstructing daily/monthly liquidity conditions.

### MC-2000-04-O16 — fixed-investment controls operated through fiscal/administrative channels as well as bank credit
- **Novelty:** useful_refinement
- **Importance:** high
- **Confidence:** high at mechanism level
- The overheating response included non-monetary constraints such as project approval, land/industrial policy and local-government measures. Observed slowing of bank credit or investment cannot automatically be attributed to reserve/rate moves.
- This is a causal-boundary warning, not a cross-shard claim about any specific administrative order.

## Negative-space / counterexample conclusions

- The orthogonal challenge did **not** uncover a missing pre-1990 antecedent or a new top-level monetary-credit regime requiring the horizon to reopen.
- The meaningful additions are implementation and semantic layers: FX settlement actors, PBOC balance-sheet decomposition, sterilization rollover cost, OMO counterparty architecture, market-venue segmentation, reserve remuneration, rate-band asymmetry, bank-internal authorization, supervision/monetary boundary, bill-market authenticity, conditional rural support and autonomous fiscal liquidity.
- Modern labels such as `policy-rate corridor`, `MLF`, `SLF`, `LPR` or `MPA` remain unsafe projections for this slice.
- With the compact broad categories plus these orthogonal implementation/semantic challenges, 2000-2004 is suitable to mark `broad_plus_orthogonal_complete` for now and advance to the next primary time slice. This is not saturation or seal of SYS-MONETARY-CREDIT.
- Exact dates, ratios, actor lists, reserve criteria, issuance/auction terms and policy-document titles remain future Evidence/source-detail work after a valid shard seal.
