## 1. Forgotten municipal predecessor-name challenge

### Lead 1 — 城市信用社 / 城市合作银行 / 城市商业银行 naming sequence
A number of city-bank lineages likely pass through old labels such as urban credit cooperatives, city cooperative banks and later city commercial banks. The exact local sequence differs by institution and must be Evidence-stage verified. This vocabulary matters because an early source may discuss a predecessor under a cooperative-bank label even when a later analyst thinks of the institution as a city commercial bank.

Classification: useful refinement.

### Lead 2 — predecessor bundles can be economic lineages without one-to-one legal continuity
Several local credit cooperatives or predecessor institutions may be consolidated into a later bank. A later legal entity should not automatically be treated as identical to each predecessor for time-series purposes. Legal continuity, accounting continuity, customer-franchise continuity and political-control continuity can diverge.

Classification: useful refinement.

### Lead 3 — old municipal names are mainly search keys, not new actor classes
Forgotten pre-rename municipal bank names could matter for archival retrieval, but they do not by themselves create a new BANK-CITY institution class. Name changes, rebranding and geographic repositioning must be distinguished from true multi-bank merger or resolution-platform formation.

Classification: duplicate / boundary confirmation.

## 2. Absorbed, dead, or restructured institution challenge

### Lead 4 — disappearance can mean absorption rather than failure
A city-bank name can disappear because it was absorbed into a stronger regional/provincial bank, because a legal entity was merged, or because of rebranding. Disappearance must not be coded automatically as insolvency or formal resolution.

Classification: useful refinement.

### Lead 5 — weak-bank rescue can mix recapitalization, shareholder replacement and operational integration
Local-state intervention may combine fresh capital, transfer of shareholder control, disposal or ring-fencing of problem assets, management replacement and eventual merger. The economically important event may span several steps rather than one legal act.

Classification: duplicate / refinement of known resolution tail.

### Lead 6 — 'takeover' is not a universal city-bank failure template
Formal regulatory takeover is only one possible mechanism. Support can occur through shareholder restructuring, local SOE capital, merger into another bank, creation of a provincial platform, or negotiated balance-sheet repair. Exact terminology must be source-specific.

Classification: useful refinement.

### Lead 7 — local-government support has a capacity constraint
The ability of a municipal or provincial government to support a weak bank is not unlimited. Fiscal capacity, local SOE balance sheets, land/property conditions and the number of simultaneous weak institutions all matter. This is a constraint within the existing local-state-support framework, not a new category.

Classification: duplicate / mechanism refinement.

## 3. BANK-CITY versus BANK-RURAL scope-boundary challenge

### Lead 8 — city commercial bank versus rural commercial bank must follow institutional lineage, not geographic service area alone
A bank serving counties, villages or agricultural SMEs is not therefore a rural commercial bank. Conversely, a rural commercial bank operating in a major city is not thereby a city commercial bank. The shard boundary should be based on institutional form and lineage rather than current geography or customer mix.

Classification: useful refinement.

### Lead 9 — provincial consolidation can mix names that look similar across city and rural systems
Recent local-bank consolidation can create region/province-branded institutions in both city-bank and rural-bank reform tracks. Similar naming must not collapse BANK-CITY and BANK-RURAL. Where a transaction mixes institutional forms, the economic bridge can be noted later without changing atomic-shard ownership.

Classification: new category, narrow scope-control clarification.

### Lead 10 — village banks remain outside BANK-CITY despite local ownership links
A city commercial bank may sponsor or hold interests in village banks, but sponsored village banks remain a different institutional category. Parent-level exposure may be a group-perimeter issue; it does not turn the village-bank operating model into BANK-CITY.

Classification: duplicate / scope boundary.

## 4. Old restructuring vocabulary and legal-form semantics

### Lead 11 — '组建', '合并', '吸收合并', '重组', '改制', '增资扩股', '接管', '处置' are not interchangeable
Older and contemporary documents may use these terms with materially different legal and accounting consequences. Search should preserve the vocabulary, while later Evidence work must determine whether a transaction was formation, merger, absorption, recapitalization, takeover or another restructuring form.

Classification: useful refinement.

### Lead 12 — 改制 may indicate legal-form change rather than distress resolution
Conversion from an earlier cooperative or local-finance form into a commercial bank can be described as restructuring/reform without implying failure. Treating all '重组/改制' language as distress would create survivorship and failure-count errors.

Classification: useful refinement.

### Lead 13 — 增资扩股 can be growth capital or rescue capital
Capital raising by expanding share capital can support ordinary growth, regulatory capital needs, ownership change, or distress repair. Context determines whether it belongs to franchise expansion, prudential adaptation or resolution.

Classification: duplicate / semantic refinement.

### Lead 14 — formal resolution and economic rescue are distinct
A bank can be economically rescued without an event that a legal or regulatory taxonomy would call formal resolution. Conversely, a formal legal process may preserve much of the operating franchise. This reinforces existing support-versus-resolution semantics.

Classification: useful refinement.

## 5. Legal entity versus economic lineage

### Lead 15 — institution age is not a single scalar after consolidation
For a province-level consolidated bank, 'age' can refer to the current legal entity, oldest predecessor, operating franchise, technology platform or current ownership regime. Comparative studies should state which concept is used.

Classification: useful refinement.

### Lead 16 — merger can reset denominators without resetting inherited economics
Capital ratios, NPL ratios, branch productivity and cost/income metrics may change mechanically after a merger because the denominator and reporting perimeter change. Yet inherited problem assets, customer relationships and staff/branch structures persist economically.

Classification: duplicate / metric-semantic boundary.

### Lead 17 — branch count after absorption is neither organic expansion nor clean diversification
A larger post-merger branch network may be inherited rather than opened by management. It may increase deposits and geographic reach while also importing duplicated outlets, legacy staff, weak credit books and integration costs.

Classification: duplicate / branch-arithmetic refinement.

## 6. Strong / weak / stable-low-return counterexamples

### Lead 18 — strongest city banks may behave more like mature regional franchises than generic local-government lenders
Banks with durable local deposit franchises, wealthy customer bases, stronger corporate ecosystems or disciplined risk management can remain structurally distinct from weak local-policy-linked peers. This is already represented by the strong-franchise cohort.

Classification: duplicate.

### Lead 19 — weak-bank tails can be governance-driven without a uniquely new asset class
Related-party lending, shareholder extraction, weak internal controls and politically directed concentration can create extreme outcomes even when the nominal asset categories resemble peers. Failure severity therefore does not imply a separate city-bank product ontology.

Classification: duplicate.

### Lead 20 — a middle cohort can survive with low returns rather than converge to either strong or failed states
Some city banks may preserve funding access and local relevance while operating with compressed margins, modest growth and recurring capital needs. This stable-low-return state remains an important counterexample to binary winner/failure narratives.

Classification: duplicate.

## 7. Omitted-actor challenge

### Lead 21 — named large city-bank exemplars do not define the category
Well-known listed or large regional city banks are useful anchors, but the ontology must also accommodate small non-listed municipal banks, merged-away names and banks with limited cross-region ambitions. No recalled omitted actor forces a new category beyond existing strong/weak/middle and consolidation lineages.

Classification: duplicate.

### Lead 22 — Northeast/resource-region weakness is not itself a new bank type
Regional industrial decline, population change, property weakness, fiscal stress and concentrated local SOEs can jointly worsen bank outcomes. These are regional factor exposures acting through the existing local-concentration/franchise/governance model.

Classification: duplicate.

### Lead 23 — coastal manufacturing/private-enterprise exposure can create a different risk mix without new ontology
Private SME/manufacturing-heavy regions may produce more cyclical working-capital, guarantee-chain and export sensitivity than government/real-estate-heavy regions. This is a regional portfolio archetype inside BANK-CITY, not a new institutional class.

Classification: duplicate.

### Lead 24 — major-metropolitan city banks can have unusually strong retail/wealth/technology economics
Large affluent urban markets can support better low-cost deposits, wealth distribution and digital economics, but these remain franchise-quality differences rather than a separate bank type.

Classification: duplicate.

## 8. Failure mechanism falsification

### Lead 25 — wholesale funding freeze remains an amplifier, not the root cause in every failure
A bank dependent on interbank/NCD funding can suffer acute stress when counterparties reprice risk, but the underlying weakness may originate in governance, asset quality or concentration. This distinction is already captured by the funding-plus-governance framework.

Classification: duplicate.

### Lead 26 — local property/LGFV stress can be absorbed, recognized, restructured or delayed
Different banks can show similar economic exposure with different reported NPL timing because of restructuring, collateral, refinancing and recognition practices. This is a metric/timing issue already covered.

Classification: duplicate.

### Lead 27 — digitalization does not erase local franchise economics
Mobile channels can lower service costs and reduce reliance on some physical branches, yet local payroll, government, enterprise and merchant relationships can still anchor deposits. Digital capability modifies but does not replace the city-bank franchise model.

Classification: duplicate.

### Lead 28 — listing does not guarantee governance convergence
Public listing can improve disclosure and market discipline while leaving local-state influence, shareholder concentration, related-party incentives or regional concentration materially intact. This remains an already-recovered expectation failure.

Classification: duplicate.

## 9. Counterfactual checks

### Counterfactual A — If province-wide scale automatically diversified risk
Observed-style reasoning suggests consolidation can import correlated local-government/property exposures from multiple cities, so geographic breadth is not equivalent to risk diversification. Already covered by consolidation-lineage analysis.

### Counterfactual B — If local-state ownership guaranteed support
Support capacity and willingness vary, and support may dilute or replace prior shareholders. Local-state connection reduces neither credit loss nor governance risk mechanically. Already covered.

### Counterfactual C — If all disappeared bank names represented failed entities
Renaming, legal-form conversion and absorption create many disappearing names without implying standalone failure. This is a vocabulary/lineage caution, not a new category.

### Counterfactual D — If rural geography defined rural-bank identity
Institutional lineage defeats this shortcut; city banks can serve rural counties and rural banks can operate in cities. This yields the one narrow new scope-control clarification in this pass.
