## Broad transition picture

My memory of 2000-2004 is a regime in which the PBOC's indirect operating toolkit became materially more usable, but the system was still not a modern price-policy-rate framework. The important transition is from late-1990s deflation/easing into renewed credit acceleration and then an early-2000s tightening response, while WTO-era bank commercialization, large-bank reform, balance-of-payments inflows and administered rate structures all shaped transmission.

### MC-2000-04-001 — foreign-exchange purchases became a major base-money/liquidity source
- **Novelty:** new_category
- **Importance:** high
- **Confidence:** medium-high
- Strong external inflows and the exchange-rate regime appear to have made PBOC foreign-exchange purchases an increasingly important source of domestic base money.
- Period-native/search vocabulary likely includes `外汇占款`, `基础货币`, `外汇储备`, `结售汇`, `冲销`/`对冲`.
- Research trap: do not equate reserve accumulation, PBOC FX assets, commercial-bank FX positions and immediately spendable RMB liquidity without checking period accounting definitions.

### MC-2000-04-002 — sterilization became a central operating problem
- **Novelty:** new_category
- **Importance:** high
- **Confidence:** high at mechanism level
- As FX-linked liquidity expanded, open-market operations increasingly had to absorb rather than merely inject liquidity.
- The key historical question is how much liquidity management was reactive sterilization versus discretionary macro tightening.
- Counterexample: an absorption operation is not automatically evidence that the overall stance was contractionary if autonomous liquidity creation was larger.

### MC-2000-04-003 — PBOC central-bank bills emerged as a sterilization instrument
- **Novelty:** new_category
- **Importance:** high
- **Confidence:** medium-high; exact launch timing deliberately unresolved
- I recall `央行票据` becoming an important early-2000s instrument because the stock of suitable securities for repeated sterilization operations was limited relative to the growing liquidity-management need.
- Search keys: `中央银行票据`, `央票`, `公开市场业务`, `冲销外汇占款`.
- Evidence later must establish exact first issuance, maturities, counterparties and whether different bill forms served different purposes.

### MC-2000-04-004 — open-market operations became a more regular liquidity-management framework
- **Novelty:** useful_refinement
- **Importance:** high
- **Confidence:** high at broad level
- OMO appears to have moved further from experimental market development toward recurring liquidity management through bond/bill transactions and repos/reverse repos.
- Market plumbing matters: eligible counterparties, available collateral/securities, settlement and the depth of the interbank bond market constrained how informative or powerful OMO could be.
- Do not back-project later seven-day-repo-centered policy-framework semantics onto this period.

### MC-2000-04-005 — the interbank bond/repo market became more important for policy transmission
- **Novelty:** new_category
- **Importance:** high
- **Confidence:** medium-high
- The national interbank bond market, policy-bank/treasury securities and repo transactions increasingly supplied the operating infrastructure through which central-bank liquidity changes reached banks.
- Lead questions: which institutions could participate, how segmented were liquidity conditions, and how far did repo/bond yields transmit into loan/deposit pricing under administered-rate constraints?
- Counterexample: a market repo rate can reflect funding segmentation and collateral conditions, not only expected PBOC stance.

### MC-2000-04-006 — `稳健的货币政策` coexisted with materially different cyclical settings
- **Novelty:** new_category
- **Importance:** high
- **Confidence:** medium-high
- Period-native wording likely continued to describe policy as `稳健`, even while the economy moved from lingering deflationary pressure toward credit/investment overheating.
- This vocabulary should not be normalized into a fixed quantitative stance. The same label may cover very different operational combinations over time.
- Later Evidence should recover contemporaneous intent, not infer intent from later outcomes.

### MC-2000-04-007 — early-period deflation exit and later credit acceleration form one regime transition
- **Novelty:** new_category
- **Importance:** high
- **Confidence:** high at directional level
- The beginning of the slice still carried weak-price/deflation concerns from the late 1990s, while by roughly 2002-2003 credit and fixed-investment growth had accelerated strongly.
- Mechanism candidates: stronger external demand/WTO integration, property and infrastructure investment, improved borrower expectations, state-bank reform incentives and abundant liquidity.
- The timing and relative contribution of each mechanism are Evidence tasks.

### MC-2000-04-008 — benchmark-rate easing ended and late-slice rate normalization returned
- **Novelty:** new_category
- **Importance:** high
- **Confidence:** medium; exact moves/dates unresolved
- I recall at least one further easing move early in the slice and a return to benchmark lending/deposit rate tightening by late 2004 after a long interval.
- This should be researched as an administered benchmark-rate system with floors/ceilings/bands, not as a modern single policy-rate reaction function.
- Exact rate levels, dates and corridor/band rules must not be reconstructed from memory.

### MC-2000-04-009 — lending-rate flexibility widened before deposit pricing was liberalized
- **Novelty:** new_category
- **Importance:** high
- **Confidence:** medium-high at broad reform direction
- A key lead is progressive widening/removal of some upper constraints on lending rates while deposit-rate controls remained materially tighter.
- This creates an asymmetric liberalization problem: banks gained more scope to price borrower risk while deposit competition remained administratively bounded.
- Search vocabulary: `贷款利率浮动区间`, `贷款利率上限`, `存款利率上限`, `利率市场化`.

### MC-2000-04-010 — reserve-requirement tightening returned as an overheating-control tool
- **Novelty:** new_category
- **Importance:** high
- **Confidence:** high at direction, low on exact ratios/dates
- After the late-1990s easing phase, required-reserve adjustments appear to have turned upward in 2003-2004 as credit and liquidity accelerated.
- Reserve changes simultaneously affect aggregate liquidity, bank balance-sheet cost and the distribution of usable reserves; they should not be reduced to a single `tightening` scalar.
- Exact reserve ratios and institution-specific applicability are deferred.

### MC-2000-04-011 — differentiated reserve requirements appeared as an early targeted prudential/credit-control hybrid
- **Novelty:** new_category
- **Importance:** high
- **Confidence:** medium; exact introduction criteria unresolved
- I recall `差别存款准备金率` appearing around the later part of this slice, with weaker or faster-expanding institutions potentially facing different reserve treatment.
- This is important because it blurs monetary, supervisory and credit-allocation functions without being identical to later macroprudential frameworks.
- Do not call it later-era MPA or assume the same scoring logic.

### MC-2000-04-012 — window guidance remained important after formal loan quotas had disappeared

- **Novelty:** new_category

### MC-2000-04-017 — state-bank commercialization changed credit transmission incentives
- **Novelty:** new_category
- **Importance:** high
- **Confidence:** high at mechanism level
- Large-bank NPL cleanup, recapitalization preparation and stronger commercial accountability should have changed how banks responded to monetary easing/tightening.
- The transmission story is two-sided: hardening risk discipline can reduce administratively induced bad lending, yet recapitalized/reforming banks can later expand credit more aggressively once balance sheets and incentives improve.
- This is a mechanism lead, not a claim that reform mechanically caused any specific loan-growth number.

### MC-2000-04-018 — broad-money/loan growth and base-money growth diverged under sterilization and changing bank behavior
- **Novelty:** new_category
- **Importance:** high
- **Confidence:** high as semantic warning
- Period analysis should keep separate `基础货币`, reserve money, M1/M2, RMB loans, bill financing and credit aggregates; their growth could diverge because of sterilization, changes in excess reserves, deposit creation and bank risk appetite.
- A monetary target miss or loan surge does not by itself identify which operational instrument failed.
- Later Evidence must preserve contemporaneous statistical definitions.

### MC-2000-04-019 — `超额准备金率` is a transmission-state variable, not merely spare cash
- **Novelty:** new_category
- **Importance:** medium-high
- **Confidence:** medium-high
- Early-2000s banks likely still held meaningful excess reserves, with their remuneration and opportunity cost influencing how strongly reserve injections translated into lending or bond purchases.
- Changes in excess-reserve remuneration may therefore be part of the operating-framework transition.
- Exact remuneration-rate changes and dates remain source-detail work.

### MC-2000-04-020 — credit acceleration had heterogeneous bank-type and borrower effects
- **Novelty:** useful_refinement
- **Importance:** medium-high
- **Confidence:** high at qualitative level
- State-owned banks, joint-stock banks, city banks and rural institutions did not face identical funding, reform or risk constraints; SOEs/infrastructure/property and private/SME borrowers likewise did not receive equal transmission.
- This heterogeneity is a warning against reading one national loan-growth series as a uniform easing impulse.
- Cross-bank-type detail belongs in later atomic shards, but the transmission distinction belongs in this monetary-credit shard.

## Failures, dead ends and counterexamples to preserve

1. **Indirect-tool maturation did not eliminate administrative guidance.** OMO, repos and benchmark-rate reform coexisted with window guidance and sectoral controls.
2. **Sterilization is not synonymous with contraction.** The PBOC could absorb liquidity while the net monetary impulse remained accommodative because FX purchases or bank credit creation were large.
3. **Benchmark-rate changes were not a modern policy-rate corridor.** Administered loan/deposit pricing and credit guidance weakened simple price-channel interpretations.
4. **Reserve increases were not purely aggregate.** Institution differences, reserve remuneration and differential treatment could change distributional effects.
5. **Loan growth is not monetary stance.** Bank recapitalization, risk appetite, borrower demand, bill financing and policy projects can move lending independently of one PBOC instrument.
6. **Market rates were not frictionless pass-through signals.** Interbank segmentation, collateral and participant restrictions could make repo/bond yields diverge from retail loan pricing.
7. **Targeted rural support was not equivalent to general easing.** Central-bank support tied to rural reform should be separated from system-wide liquidity operations.
8. **Modern labels are dangerous.** Do not project later `MLF`, `SLF`, `LPR`, `MPA` or a mature seven-day-repo policy-rate framework backward into 2000-2004.

## Contemporaneous intent/judgment leads

- Early in the period, policymakers likely still worried about weak prices, insufficient domestic demand and incomplete transmission of earlier easing.
- By 2003-2004, the contemporaneous concern appears to have shifted toward excessive credit/fixed-asset investment, sectoral overheating and liquidity generated by external inflows.
- The transition itself is historically valuable: `稳健` policy language may have persisted while the practical mix moved from supporting demand to restraining excess expansion.
- Evidence later should recover the contemporaneous reasoning and conditions rather than replace them with the later-known outcome of the credit/investment cycle.
