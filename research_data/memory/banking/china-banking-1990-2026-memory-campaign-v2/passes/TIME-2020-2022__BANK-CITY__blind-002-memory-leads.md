## Orthogonal question

Does 2020–2022 reveal a second period-wide city-bank structural category centered on pandemic forbearance/recognition timing or property-LGFV-local-fiscal feedback, distinct from the broader local-concentration / funding / capital-adaptation regime?

## Forbearance and borrower-normalization challenge

- Repeated renewal, extension, grace periods, or no-principal-repayment arrangements can postpone classification migration without restoring cash generation.
- The analytically important variable is borrower self-generated repayment capacity after support fades, not whether the facility remained contractually current during support.
- A loan can move through normal -> special mention -> overdue -> restructured -> NPL in different sequences; the path itself is an Evidence-stage metric lead.
- A renewed facility can be economically riskier even when nominal maturity is extended and reported arrears fall.
- Genuine normalization should be tested through operating cash flow, interest servicing, collateral liquidity, guarantor capacity, and declining dependence on new credit.
- Counterexample: viable manufacturers or exporters may have used relief briefly and returned to ordinary repayment, so any blanket “forbearance hid losses” narrative would be too strong.
- Another counterexample: some borrowers may never have required formal relief because deposit/cash buffers or sector demand remained strong.
- Recognition-timing therefore looks like a cross-cutting measurement problem, not a separate city-bank business model.

## Stock-flow asset-quality semantics

- Period-end NPL stock can fall while gross new formation remains high if write-offs, sales, recoveries, or denominator growth are large.
- Special-mention and overdue balances may be leading indicators, but their usefulness depends on classification practice and relief treatment.
- Restructured exposures should be split between temporary maturity modification and deeper economic concession.
- Provision coverage can improve while reserve adequacy worsens if NPL recognition is delayed; denominator mechanics matter.
- Credit cost can fall after early reserve build even while underlying weak borrowers remain unresolved.
- “Stable NPL ratio” should be challenged against new-NPL formation, migration, write-off, recovery, and restructuring flows.
- A city bank with rapid policy-supported loan growth can dilute the NPL ratio mechanically.
- These are semantic/measurement discontinuities rather than evidence of a second structural category.

## Property-chain decomposition

- Developer exposure should be decomposed into direct developer lending, project companies, construction contractors, materials suppliers, mortgages, guarantees, and investment/legacy structures.
- Unfinished-project or delivery risk can affect mortgages and local confidence even where borrowers remain legally obligated.
- Construction-chain stress may first appear in SME/corporate receivables rather than a labeled “property” portfolio.
- Land collateral can lose liquidity before accounting values visibly adjust.
- Property-related local SOEs can blur the boundary between commercial developer risk and quasi-municipal support.
- Strong local governments may coordinate project completion or financing, reducing near-term loss but extending bank duration and policy exposure.
- Counterexample: home-city banks in diversified, supply-constrained, or stronger-demand markets may experience far less developer stress than national headlines imply.
- Property feedback therefore strongly conditions local concentration but still does not describe every city bank in the period.

## LGFV and local-fiscal challenge

- LGFV refinancing dependence matters more than a simple binary classification of “government-backed.”
- Land-sale weakness can reduce both fiscal flexibility and collateral confidence, linking property and platform risk.
- Municipal support can take forms such as asset transfers, refinancing coordination, stronger SOE guarantees, capital injection into banks, or project restructuring; exact forms require Evidence.
- A bank may extend duration or refinance to avoid disruption, shifting risk from immediate default to lower-yield / longer-duration balance-sheet lock-up.
- High-quality local-government-related assets may outperform private borrowers in some regions, so “LGFV = deterioration” is not a valid universal frame.
- Local-government ownership of the bank can align support and policy objectives but may also increase pressure to sustain local credit.
- The relevant city-bank mechanism remains regional fiscal concentration and support capacity, not a distinct universal second model.

## Deposit termization and repricing challenge

- A deposit franchise can grow while its economic value deteriorates if customers shift toward longer-tenor or higher-cost products.
- Pandemic/market volatility may push wealth money from WMPs or funds into deposits, creating temporary balance growth without proving durable primary-account relationships.
- Corporate deposits can be volatile around fiscal, property, and business cycles; retail term deposits may be stable but expensive.
- Public-institution and municipal deposits can be sticky yet sensitive to local relationships and policy allocation.
- Lower asset yields combined with rigid deposit costs create margin compression even if funding liquidity is ample.
- Wholesale funding may become relatively cheaper for strong issuers but remain reputation-sensitive for weak city banks.
- Strong-bank counterexample: a bank with payroll, settlement, card, government-service, or wealth ecosystems may maintain low-cost current-account balances despite sector pressure.
- Liability adaptation is a core differentiator within the same period-wide regime.

## WMP / AUM / deposit migration challenge

- Net-value transition can make customer balances more visibly market-sensitive, changing redemption and migration behavior.
- Customer money moving from WMP into deposits strengthens reported funding but can reduce fee/AUM economics.
- Customer money moving from deposits into funds or other products may weaken deposit balances while preserving client relationship.
- Legacy-product runoff may crystallize investment or client-communication challenges at some institutions but not others.
- AUM and deposits must not be summed as if economically identical; one is customer asset management/distribution, the other bank funding.
- Strong wealth franchises may retain customers through product migration; weaker banks may lose both AUM and deposits.
- This reinforces franchise heterogeneity, not a new structural category.

## Digital-origination vintage quality

- Rapid digital lending during/after lockdowns should be evaluated by vintage, acquisition channel, repeat borrower behavior, and fraud/control losses.
- Digital volume growth can conceal higher churn, smaller ticket size, or weaker pricing.
- Remote underwriting may improve efficiency for data-rich SMEs while weakening qualitative local-information advantages elsewhere.
- Platform or fintech partnerships can shift customer acquisition economics and model risk.
- Early delinquency by vintage is a stronger quality lead than app downloads or approved-customer counts.
- Digital acceleration therefore changes execution and operating leverage but remains an orthogonal channel within the same business model.

## Capital and provision mechanics

- Policy-supported loan growth can consume capital even before credit losses emerge.
- Provisioning and write-offs reduce earnings and internal capital generation, increasing dependence on external capital instruments or shareholders.
- Capital injection can mask weak recurring profitability if the franchise remains unable to earn through credit cost and margin pressure.
- Secondary-capital or perpetual-type instruments repair regulatory ratios differently from common-equity-like shareholder recapitalization.
- Local state-asset involvement can strengthen confidence but should be separated from balance-sheet cleanup.
- Capital restoration without governance repair can leave risk appetite unchanged.
- Governance repair without franchise repair can leave the bank dependent on high-cost funding.

## Weak-bank recapitalization and governance repair

- A useful four-part framework is: asset cleanup, capital repair, governance/shareholder repair, and funding/franchise repair.
- These components can occur in different orders and may take years.
- Opaque related-party exposures may become more visible under stress, but exact institution cases should remain Evidence-stage.
- Management replacement or state-asset participation is an intervention signal, not proof that legacy assets have been recognized.
- Recapitalization can reduce immediate solvency risk without restoring borrower quality or net interest profitability.
- Strong-city-bank counterexamples are essential: listed or well-governed banks with strong local franchises may face the same macro shocks without requiring structural rescue.
- Therefore weak-bank restructuring remains an extreme-tail/gap category, not a period-wide second business model.

## Regional counterexamples

- Manufacturing/export-oriented cities may see stronger corporate recovery than tourism/property-heavy cities.
- Wealthier municipalities may provide stronger fiscal and shareholder support than lower-income regions.
- Population inflow can support deposits and mortgages even during national property weakness.
- Population outflow can intensify local deposit, mortgage, collateral, and fiscal constraints.
- Resource, industrial, coastal, and service-heavy regions likely differ in both borrower mix and deposit behavior.
- Regional variation is so large that period-wide averages can obscure more than they explain.

## Contemporaneous expectation falsification leads

- “Relief borrowers will all normalize” should be tested against repeated extensions and later classification.
- “Property collateral protects the bank” should be tested against transaction liquidity and project completion.
- “Municipal links eliminate LGFV risk” should be tested against fiscal capacity and refinancing dependence.
- “Deposit growth proves stronger franchise” should be tested against termization, pricing, and WMP migration.
- “Digital lending lowers cost and risk simultaneously” should be tested by vintage loss and acquisition cost.
- “Capital injection completes repair” should be tested against recurring earnings, governance, and asset cleanup.
- “High provision coverage proves conservative recognition” should be tested against NPL denominator and restructuring flows.

## Negative-space decision

No independent second period-wide structural category emerged. Pandemic forbearance is primarily a recognition-timing and borrower-cash-flow measurement layer. Property-LGFV-local-fiscal feedback is a powerful regional concentration amplifier. Weak-bank recapitalization/governance repair is a tail mechanism. Deposit/WMP migration and digital acceleration alter funding/franchise execution. Together they remain dimensions of one broader 2020–2022 city-bank adaptation regime rather than a separate cohort-wide model.
