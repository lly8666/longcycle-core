## Period framing and institutional maturation

1. [new_category][high] 2000-2004 is the phase when many city commercial banks shift from “newly consolidated successor institution” toward an operating bank that must prove centralized governance, unified systems, recurring profitability and franchise quality.
2. [new_category][high] The central transition is from one-off municipal cleanup toward repeatable commercial-bank operating discipline: credit approval, treasury, ALM, risk classification, provisioning and internal controls.
3. [new_category] Inherited bad assets remain material but increasingly coexist with genuinely new loan vintages, making stock-versus-flow asset-quality analysis more informative than formation-era ratios alone.
4. [new_category] Municipal government remains a major governance and support actor, but the policy question shifts from formation support toward whether local influence can coexist with professional management.
5. [new_category] Some better-run city banks may begin to differentiate themselves through stronger local SME/customer knowledge, corporate settlement, technology and management rather than merely municipal backing.
6. [new_category] WTO-era competitive pressure from national banks and foreign-bank entry expectations likely pushes city banks to modernize service quality, systems, products and risk management.
7. [new_category] Geographic confinement remains structural, but ambitions to expand beyond the home city begin to matter as a strategy question even before broad cross-regional expansion becomes common.
8. [useful_refinement] “Regional bank” and “city commercial bank” are not interchangeable: the legal home city, operating geography and later cross-region footprint must be tracked separately.
