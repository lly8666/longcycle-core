## Broad blind leads

### 1. Financial deleveraging was not identical to a simple benchmark-rate tightening cycle
**Classification:** new_category  
**Importance:** high

A high-value reconstruction should separate a relatively prudent/neutral monetary stance from the simultaneous tightening of interbank, off-balance-sheet and asset-management channels. Market funding conditions could tighten because leverage, maturity transformation or interbank nesting was being constrained even when the central bank was also supplying liquidity to prevent disorderly stress. Search later for period-native phrases around `金融去杠杆`, `稳健中性`, `削峰填谷`, `流动性基本稳定`, and the later shift toward language closer to keeping policy appropriately tight/loose.

### 2. MPA broad-credit discipline increasingly mattered alongside traditional loan and money aggregates
**Classification:** new_category  
**Importance:** high

The macroprudential assessment framework appears to have become a material transmission layer: bank behavior was constrained not only by reserve requirements or loan guidance but by broader credit, leverage/capital, liquidity and pricing dimensions. Exact indicator sets and parameter vintages are uncertain and must be Evidence work. A later source task should distinguish MPA from microprudential supervision and from the earlier differentiated-dynamic-reserve mechanism.

### 3. Interbank certificates of deposit became a central wholesale-funding and policy-transmission object
**Classification:** useful_refinement  
**Importance:** high

NCDs likely remained especially important for joint-stock, city and smaller banks. Their issuance volume, maturity and pricing linked money-market conditions to balance-sheet expansion. Deleveraging pressure therefore transmitted through wholesale-funding cost and availability, not just through a headline policy rate. Do not assume all banks faced the same marginal funding curve.

### 4. Shadow-credit contraction and bank-loan substitution must be tracked separately
**Classification:** new_category  
**Importance:** high

A fall in trust/entrusted/non-standard financing could coexist with stronger formal RMB loan growth as credit was pushed back onto bank balance sheets. This means slower or differently composed Social Financing does not mechanically imply an equivalent fall in bank credit. Later Evidence should reconstruct which TSF components contracted and which bank-loan categories substituted, without importing a current TSF component list backward.

### 5. Social Financing stock, flow and component-vintage semantics became more important during deleveraging
**Classification:** useful_refinement  
**Importance:** high

Because non-loan channels were shrinking while government-bond or other components were changing in importance, researchers need point-in-time TSF definitions. A later series should preserve flow versus stock growth, current versus contemporaneous component coverage, and revision/backfill behavior rather than comparing differently defined aggregates as one timeless series.

### 6. Targeted reserve-requirement policy increasingly encoded borrower or institution eligibility
**Classification:** new_category  
**Importance:** high

Memory suggests targeted RRR reductions or preferential reserve treatment were used to support inclusive finance, small/micro borrowers, agriculture or selected banks. The research object is not only the reserve-ratio step but the eligibility perimeter, measurement window, institution class and intended transmission. Exact formulae and dates are uncertain.

### 7. Broad RRR easing could coexist with continued financial-deleveraging objectives
**Classification:** new_category  
**Importance:** high

Later in the period, reserve reductions appear to have released longer-term liquidity while authorities still resisted a return to unconstrained leverage expansion. Some actions may also have been framed as replacing maturing central-bank funding or supporting debt-equity conversion rather than pure net stimulus. Later Evidence must separate gross reserve release, MLF replacement, net liquidity and policy intent.

### 8. OMO, MLF and reserve tools jointly managed liquidity while structural tightening continued
**Classification:** useful_refinement  
**Importance:** high

Short-term reverse repos and medium-term lending likely functioned as distinct liquidity layers. A period narrative should not infer policy stance from one operation in isolation. Track maturity, counterparty access, collateral, rollover and whether an action offset tax payments, government deposits, cash demand, FX flows or maturing facilities.

### 9. TMLF or similar targeted medium-term liquidity created another structural-tool layer
**Classification:** new_category  
**Importance:** high

Toward the later part of the slice, a targeted medium-term facility appears to have been introduced or emphasized for institutions meeting small/private-enterprise lending conditions. Treat this as a research lead about conditional central-bank funding, not as a known exact facility design. Later work should distinguish it from ordinary MLF, relending and fiscal credit support.

### 10. Relending and rediscounting became increasingly visible in private/small-firm credit support
**Classification:** new_category  
**Importance:** high

As private and small firms faced refinancing pressure, central-bank relending/rediscount windows or quotas appear to have been expanded or redirected. These tools should be separated from direct central-bank lending to firms: commercial banks remained important intermediaries and credit-risk allocation may have stayed with them. Exact terms require Evidence.

### 11. Private-enterprise bond-financing support used credit-risk mitigation rather than only bank-loan quotas
**Classification:** new_category  
**Importance:** medium-high

Memory points to credit-risk-mitigation instruments/support facilities intended to improve private-enterprise bond issuance during 2018-era stress. This is a useful bridge between monetary-credit policy and capital-market transmission. Do not assume it was equivalent to a guarantee or fiscal bailout; recover the actual risk-sharing structure later.

### 12. Bank capital/liquidity support included instruments such as central-bank bills swaps around perpetual bonds
**Classification:** new_category  
**Importance:** medium-high

A late-period mechanism lead is the use of a central-bank bills-swap type arrangement linked to bank perpetual bonds. The key question is whether it improved collateral/liquidity acceptance and thereby supported bank capital issuance, rather than constituting a direct capital injection or outright purchase. Exact mechanics are deliberately left unresolved.

### 13. Small-bank counterparty-risk differentiation became a monetary-transmission issue, not only a resolution issue
**Classification:** new_category  
**Importance:** high

Late-period stress around smaller-bank counterparties appears to have widened funding spreads and reduced willingness to lend unsecured or accept weaker collateral. This should be researched as a money-market credit-tiering channel while keeping legal resolution details isolated in the sealed regulation/resolution shard. Bank size and ownership affected liquidity transmission.

### 14. Trade friction and growth slowdown shifted the policy mix toward stabilization
**Classification:** new_category  
**Importance:** high

The 2018-2019 external-demand/trade shock and domestic slowdown likely increased emphasis on countercyclical support. The shift did not necessarily mean abandoning deleveraging; policy may have moved from broad leverage suppression toward targeted stabilization and preventing an excessive credit contraction. Preserve the contemporaneous balance between growth, leverage, FX and financial-risk concerns.

### 15. RMB depreciation/capital-flow pressure affected domestic liquidity and policy room
**Classification:** new_category  
**Importance:** medium-high

FX pressure can change reserve-money conditions differently from the earlier persistent-inflow era. Outflows or reduced FX purchases may drain or cease supplying base money, increasing reliance on RRR cuts/OMO/facilities. Exchange-rate management, FX reserve changes and RMB liquidity operations are distinct objects; do not infer one-for-one causality without source-grounded accounting.

### 16. The 2019 LPR reform was a mechanism change, not the birth of the term `LPR`
**Classification:** new_category  
**Importance:** high

A first-generation Loan Prime Rate existed before 2019. The later reform materially changed how LPR was formed and how it was intended to anchor new loan pricing. This historical-vintage distinction is essential: do not back-project the post-2019 MLF-linked quotation mechanism into 2013-2018.

### 17. MLF → LPR → loan pricing became an explicit transmission chain
**Classification:** new_category  
**Importance:** high

The reformed LPR appears to have been designed to link the central bank's medium-term funding price more directly to bank lending rates, with quotation banks adding funding/risk/operating considerations. Treat this as a chain with frictions, not a mechanical equality. Deposit costs, credit risk, capital, competition and bank-specific funding all mediate pass-through.

### 18. New-loan pricing and legacy-stock repricing are separate transition states
**Classification:** new_category  
**Importance:** high

The 2019 reform likely affected newly originated lending before the broad conversion of existing floating-rate loans occurred later. A PIT timeline should therefore distinguish `new loans priced off reformed LPR`, `legacy loans still benchmark-linked`, and later stock-loan conversion. Do not let the 2020 conversion process leak backward into 2019.

### 19. Mortgage pricing required a separate long-tenor / add-point semantic layer
**Classification:** new_category  
**Importance:** medium-high

A longer-tenor LPR and mortgage add-point/floor structure became relevant near the reform boundary. Mortgage pricing should not be represented as merely `mortgage rate = LPR`; local policy floors, borrower type, first-home/second-home distinctions and fixed add-ons matter. Exact effective dates and floors are deferred.

### 20. Deposit pricing and liability competition constrained loan-rate transmission
**Classification:** new_category  
**Importance:** high

Even after formal loan-rate liberalization and LPR reform, deposit funding remained sticky and competitively priced. Banks with expensive deposits or wholesale liabilities could not reduce lending rates one-for-one with MLF/LPR changes. Liability beta and bank-type heterogeneity belong in the monetary-transmission story.

### 21. `Lower interbank rates` did not imply equal credit availability to all borrowers
**Classification:** duplicate  
**Importance:** medium

This confirms an existing heterogeneity principle: ample aggregate liquidity can coexist with tight financing for private, small, low-collateral or stressed borrowers. Liquidity, bank risk appetite, capital constraints and borrower credit risk must remain distinct.

### 22. Counterexample: easing reserve/liquidity conditions did not mean a return to the 2009-style broad credit boom
**Classification:** useful_refinement  
**Importance:** high

The later-period easing toolkit appears structurally different from crisis-era blanket credit expansion. The policy objective included stabilizing financing while preserving leverage/risk discipline and improving transmission to underserved borrowers. This counterexample guards against classifying all RRR cuts or liquidity injections as the same monetary regime.
