## Blind category expansion

1. **[new_category | high] Systemic-importance designation as an ex-ante preparedness trigger.** A bank can be designated systemically important and face extra supervisory/preparedness expectations without being distressed. Internal memory suggests both global and domestic systemic-importance layers are relevant to major PRC banks; exact lists and authorities are source-dependent.
2. **[new_category | high] Bank-led recovery planning.** A recovery plan is remembered as a menu/governance framework for restoring viability under severe stress while management and ordinary corporate control still operate. It should not be treated as a resolution event.
3. **[new_category | high] Authority-led resolution planning.** A resolution plan concerns how authorities could preserve critical functions and handle non-viability if recovery fails. Existence or approval of a plan is not evidence that resolution powers were activated.
4. **[new_category | high] Resolvability assessment.** Preparedness includes asking whether legal, operational, funding and information structures permit orderly handling. A resolvability problem is an ex-ante impediment, not itself insolvency or takeover.
5. **[new_category | high] Critical-functions and critical-shared-services continuity.** Resolution preparedness can distinguish economically critical banking functions from the survival of the whole legal entity and can require continuity of shared operations/services. Exact domestic definitions are uncertain.
6. **[new_category | high] Gone-concern loss-absorbing capacity / TLAC-like layer.** Internal memory strongly associates major PRC systemic banks with total-loss-absorbing-capacity concepts in the later period. This is pre-positioned capacity for non-viability/resolution, distinct from ordinary minimum capital. Exact ratios, phase-ins and covered institutions are Evidence work.
7. **[new_category | high] Eligible loss-absorbing liabilities/instruments.** A preparedness regime can care not only about amount of capital but also which liabilities or instruments can credibly absorb loss or recapitalize a group. Eligibility rules and contractual/statutory mechanics must not be guessed.
8. **[new_category | high] Operational, data and valuation readiness for resolution.** Orderly handling may require timely entity-level balance-sheet data, liability mapping, valuation capability and operational continuity. These are preparedness capabilities rather than realized loss outcomes.
9. **[new_category | high] Recovery indicators, escalation and option sets.** Recovery planning can include stress indicators, governance escalation and pre-identified actions such as capital raising, asset sales, balance-sheet reduction or funding actions. Exact triggers and mandatory plan contents are source-dependent.
10. **[new_category | high] Group/entity placement of loss-absorbing capacity.** For large banking groups, external versus internal or parent-versus-subsidiary placement of loss-absorbing resources is an important conceptual issue remembered from systemic-bank resolution frameworks. Whether and how PRC domestic rules adopt each layer is uncertain and should be verified later.
11. **[useful_refinement | high] Going-concern capital versus gone-concern resolution capacity.** Basel-style capital protects viability before failure; resolution loss absorbency is intended to support non-viability handling. Overlapping instruments do not erase the functional distinction.
12. **[useful_refinement | high] Additional systemic buffers versus resolution capacity.** Extra capital requirements attached to systemic importance are still prudential going-concern requirements unless a rule explicitly makes them resolution loss-absorbing resources.
13. **[useful_refinement | high] Issuance versus activation.** Issuing TLAC-like debt or other eligible instruments creates capacity; no creditor has necessarily lost money until an actual write-down, conversion or other loss-imposition event occurs.
14. **[useful_refinement | high] Recovery-plan activation versus legal resolution entry.** Management may execute recovery options during severe stress while the bank remains outside an authority-led resolution state.
15. **[useful_refinement | high] Recovery failure versus resolution trigger.** Exhausting or failing recovery options can be an antecedent to resolution, but the factual/legal trigger for resolution remains a separate question.
16. **[useful_refinement] Resolvability impediment versus supervisory deficiency.** Complex legal entities, operational dependencies, data gaps or funding structures may reduce resolvability even when ordinary prudential ratios are still compliant.
17. **[useful_refinement | high] Critical function versus legal entity.** Preserving payments, deposits, lending or other critical functions does not necessarily imply preserving the original bank legal entity or ownership structure.
18. **[useful_refinement] Contingent funding in a recovery plan versus resolution funding.** A recovery plan may pre-identify private/market liquidity or funding actions; this should remain separate from public/fund support actually used during resolution.
19. **[useful_refinement | high] Loss-absorbing capacity versus claimant hierarchy.** Capacity tells us what resources are intended/eligible to absorb losses; it does not by itself establish the legal order in which shareholders, capital instruments, wholesale claims or deposits would bear loss.
20. **[useful_refinement] Contractual loss-absorption terms versus statutory resolution powers.** An instrument may contain contractual write-down/conversion features, while an authority may separately possess statutory powers. ## Result

The reopened category is real and materially broader than one label. It contains at least four independent dimensions: **who is subject to preparedness (systemic-importance perimeter), what plans exist (recovery vs resolution), whether the institution is operationally resolvable, and what loss-absorbing capacity is pre-positioned**. None of those dimensions establishes that a resolution event occurred.

The highest-value orthogonal follow-up is a state challenge on **preparedness versus activation**: test whether designation, plan approval, resolvability work, TLAC issuance, contractual loss-absorption terms or stress-plan activation can be falsely promoted into actual resolution, creditor loss or public-cost outcomes.
