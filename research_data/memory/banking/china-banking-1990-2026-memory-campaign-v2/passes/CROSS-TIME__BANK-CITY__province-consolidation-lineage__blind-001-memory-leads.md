## Targeted gap-closure result

The measured gap is recoverable as a distinct cross-time lineage archetype. Blind recall separates three mechanisms that must not be collapsed:

1. **multi-municipal merger into a province/regional bank**;
2. **new or recapitalized regional platform that later absorbs weak city-bank predecessors**;
3. **single municipal bank rename / geographic repositioning without a true multi-bank merger**.

The first two are the material consolidation lineage identified by rev142; the third is a critical counterexample preventing false classification. No second material cross-time category emerged outside this lineage taxonomy. The gap can therefore close after this probe, with low-novelty confirmations still 0/3 and one fresh saturation-review frontier selected next.

## A. Multi-municipal merger lineage candidates

1. **[new]** Huishang Bank is strongly recalled as an early province-oriented consolidation case built from multiple Anhui urban commercial-bank / urban-credit-cooperative predecessors; exact predecessor roster and timing require Evidence.
2. **[new]** The Huishang lineage is useful because a current province-level franchise can contain customer, asset and branch histories older than the current legal brand.
3. **[new]** Bank of Jiangsu is strongly recalled as a multi-city Jiangsu consolidation rather than an organic expansion of one municipal bank; exact participating city-bank list requires Evidence.
4. **[new]** Bank of Jiangsu is a particularly useful counterexample to assuming the strongest provincial capital-city bank necessarily became the province-wide consolidation vehicle; some major city banks can remain separate.
5. **[new]** Hubei Bank is recalled as another province-level combination of several municipal city banks, while a major Wuhan-based city bank remained a separate franchise; exact identities require Evidence.
6. **[new]** This creates a recurring pattern: province-wide consolidation and a surviving strong metropolitan bank can coexist in the same province.
7. **[new]** Zhongyuan Bank is recalled as a major Henan province-wide combination of multiple city commercial banks, with a later phase of absorbing additional local banks; exact stages require Evidence.
8. **[new]** Zhongyuan therefore illustrates that province consolidation may be iterative rather than one-off: initial formation can be followed by a second consolidation wave.
9. **[new]** Bank of Guizhou is recalled as a province-oriented bank built from multiple local city-bank predecessors, while another large local city bank retained separate identity; exact formation mechanics require Evidence.
10. **[new]** Bank of Jiangxi is recalled as a consolidation in which Nanchang and another Jiangxi city-bank lineage were combined into a province-level brand; exact predecessor and legal sequence require Evidence.
11. **[refinement]** The recurring economic objective is not merely scale: consolidation can centralize capital, risk control, treasury, IT, audit and regulatory dialogue across previously fragmented municipal entities.
12. **[new]** A simultaneous political objective can be to reduce intra-province fragmentation and create a regional financing institution with broader provincial policy relevance.
13. **[new]** The commercial objective can conflict with the political one when the new bank is expected to preserve local service obligations across multiple cities.
14. **[new]** Pre-merger city banks may have had different underwriting norms, accounting cleanliness and local-government relationships, so the new legal entity inherits heterogeneous hidden state variables.
15. **[new]** Province-level branding can create apparent geographic diversification while economic exposures remain highly correlated to one provincial property/fiscal cycle.

## B. Resolution-platform / later absorption lineage candidates

16. **[new]** Sichuan Bank is recalled as a later provincial-state-backed restructuring platform involving weak regional/city-bank predecessors rather than the merger of several healthy peer city banks; exact legal sequence requires Evidence.
17. **[new]** This suggests a subtype in which consolidation is primarily a resolution/recapitalization vehicle rather than a strategic growth merger.
18. **[new]** Shanxi Bank is recalled as a later province-level consolidation of several city commercial banks under stronger provincial coordination; exact predecessor set requires Evidence.
19. **[new]** Shanxi's lineage is useful for testing how a province can retain another established regional bank while consolidating weaker municipal peers into a new platform.
20. **[new]** Liaoshen Bank is recalled as a Liaoning regional restructuring platform associated with absorption/reorganization of troubled city-bank predecessors; precise chronology requires Evidence.
21. **[new]** Liaoshen-type cases differ from classic merger arithmetic because some predecessor weaknesses may be addressed before, during or after legal absorption through capital injections and asset cleanup.
22. **[new]** A new platform can therefore present stronger opening capital ratios even while it inherits operating systems, people, branches and credit relationships from weaker predecessors.
23. **[new]** The economic successor may not equal the legal successor: deposits and branches can move more continuously than equity, subordinated capital or problem assets.
24. **[new]** Resolution-platform formation can deliberately break historical branding to restore confidence while still relying on inherited local distribution.
25. **[new]** New-bank branding therefore should not be interpreted as greenfield economics.
26. **[refinement]** In stressed provinces, consolidation can be part of a larger package involving provincial state capital, local AMCs, state-owned enterprises and fiscal coordination; exact participants are Evidence-stage questions.
27. **[new]** The state-capital sponsor may become both loss absorber and governance principal, changing incentives relative to predecessor municipal/private shareholder structures.
28. **[new]** A weak-bank platform can initially prioritize liquidity, asset cleanup and governance over loan growth, so early post-merger growth comparisons may misstate strategic success.

## C. Rename / regionalization counterexamples

29. **[new]** A bank adopting a province-level name does not prove multi-bank consolidation; some city banks can simply rename after expanding geographic scope or shareholder ambitions.
30. **[new]** Bank of Hebei is recalled as a useful candidate for this warning: its province-level identity may have evolved from a principal municipal-bank lineage rather than a peer merger of many city banks; exact history requires Evidence.
31. **[new]** Bank of Gansu is another candidate where the legal formation path should be checked carefully before classifying it as a multi-city merger.
32. **[new]** Bank of Lanzhou versus Bank of Gansu-like naming also illustrates that a province can host both a capital-city bank and a province-branded institution with distinct origins.
33. **[new]** A renamed bank's historical ratios may be far more continuous than those of a newly consolidated entity, even though the brand change looks equally dramatic in a dataset.
34. **[new]** Search systems must therefore distinguish `更名` from `合并组建`, `吸收合并`, `新设合并`, `重组设立` and `承接`.
35. **[new]** Province-level naming is a label; lineage is an economic and legal continuity question.

## D. Predecessor and age semantics

36. **[refinement]** Modern-bank founding dates can understate the age of branch networks and borrower relationships inherited from predecessors.
37. **[new]** Conversely, using the oldest predecessor date as the modern bank's age can overstate continuity of governance, capital and risk systems.
38. **[new]** A useful future Evidence schema should carry at least three dates: oldest material predecessor, current legal entity formation, and major consolidation/reorganization events.
39. **[new]** Historical branch-count series should tag whether each change is organic opening, merger inheritance, relocation or closure.
40. **[new]** Historical employee counts face the same arithmetic problem because merger integration can temporarily preserve duplicate teams.
41. **[new]** Historical customer counts and deposits can jump because of legal consolidation rather than franchise acquisition.
42. **[new]** A province-level bank can inherit city-specific deposit franchises with different pricing sensitivity; group-average deposit cost may hide these local differences.

## E. Inherited asset-quality stock versus new formation

43. **[new]** A consolidated bank needs an inherited-problem-asset bridge separating predecessor stock from post-merger credit formation.
44. **[new]** Pre-merger cleanup can move bad assets out before legal consolidation, making the opening NPL ratio look cleaner than the economic lineage.
45. **[new]** Post-merger recognition can do the opposite: centralized review may surface previously hidden predecessor problems, making reported NPLs rise even if new underwriting is improving.
46. **[refinement]** Write-offs, transfers to AMCs, restructurings and collateral realization can all break simple NPL stock continuity.
47. **[new]** Provision coverage can change through both numerator cleanup and denominator reclassification; it should not be treated as a pure operating-performance series around mergers.
48. **[new]** Capital ratios can improve because new state capital is injected and high-risk assets are transferred, but internal capital generation may remain weak.
49. **[new]** The first few years after consolidation are therefore an integration/normalization period rather than a clean through-cycle performance sample.
50. **[refinement]** For later Evidence, asset-quality vintage should be mapped to predecessor geography where possible, not only current bank legal entity.

## F. Shareholder and control transition

51. **[new]** A multi-city merger can convert several municipal control centers into one provincial control structure without eliminating municipal political influence over local branches.
52. **[new]** Municipal shareholders may be diluted economically while retaining informal influence through local borrower and government relationships.
53. **[new]** Provincial financial-holding or SOE capital can become the coordinating shareholder, changing board composition and capital access.
54. **[new]** A resolution-platform model can sharply dilute or eliminate problematic private shareholders from predecessor banks.
55. **[refinement]** Governance repair must be distinguished from capitalization: injecting state capital without changing approval culture or related-party behavior may not solve the core problem.
56. **[new]** Post-merger executive appointments and branch-president rotation can be leading indicators of whether control actually centralized.
57. **[new]** Internal audit reporting lines are another hidden integration variable: a formally unified bank can retain fragmented oversight if local audit teams remain locally captured.

## G. Operating integration mechanics

58. **[new]** Treasury is often one of the first functions that can be centralized because liquidity and market counterparties benefit immediately from a single balance sheet.
59. **[new]** Core banking / data migration can be slower and operationally risky because customer identifiers, product codes and historical data differ across predecessors.
60. **[new]** Credit approval integration can be slower still because it involves local relationship managers, collateral practice and tacit risk culture.
61. **[new]** HR grading and compensation harmonization can create retention risk if predecessor banks had materially different pay structures.
62. **[new]** Duplicate branches in overlapping cities may be retained temporarily for confidence and customer continuity before rationalization.
63. **[new]** A merger can therefore raise operating costs before synergies appear; short-horizon cost-income deterioration need not imply merger failure.
64. **[new]** Centralized procurement, technology and treasury synergies are easier to quantify than cultural integration, creating a bias toward declaring integration success too early.
65. **[refinement]** Branch-network optimization should be distinguished from ordinary digital-channel substitution because merger overlap is a separate driver of closures.
66. **[new]** Unified branding can precede unified product pricing, creating temporary customer confusion and deposit repricing risk.
67. **[new]** A province-wide bank may need internal transfer-pricing rules to prevent richer cities from subsidizing weaker predecessor regions invisibly.
68. **[new]** Without strong FTP and risk-adjusted performance measurement, provincial political objectives can obscure which predecessor franchises are economically viable.

## H. Diversification and local-fiscal correlation

69. **[new]** Province-level scale diversifies single-city idiosyncratic shocks but may leave high common exposure to province-wide property, LGFV and fiscal conditions.
70. **[new]** In a weak province, merging several correlated weak banks does not create diversification merely by combining them.
71. **[new]** In a stronger province, consolidation can create a more resilient deposit and SME network if local economies are sufficiently heterogeneous.
72. **[refinement]** The relevant diversification unit is economic factor exposure, not number of predecessor legal entities.
73. **[new]** Provincial state support can improve confidence while simultaneously coupling the bank more tightly to the same fiscal base supporting local borrowers.
74. **[new]** This is especially important during LGFV resolution: the bank and the borrower support system may depend on the same provincial resources.

## I. Strong/weak counterexamples and expectations

75. **[new]** A successful multi-city consolidation should not be benchmarked only against failed weak-bank platforms; strong organic city banks provide a different counterfactual for capital efficiency and culture.
76. **[new]** A weak predecessor can improve materially inside a provincial platform without the entire consolidated bank becoming a high-return franchise.
77. **[new]** Bigger province-level scale may improve market funding access while reducing return on equity if the merged bank carries public-service and cleanup burdens.
78. **[new]** The expectation `merge -> diversify -> safer -> higher profitability` can fail at each arrow independently.
79. **[new]** The expectation that legal merger immediately removes municipal influence can fail because local relationship networks persist.
80. **[new]** The expectation that one core system creates one risk culture can fail because data migration and decision rights are different integration layers.
81. **[refinement]** The expectation that a low opening NPL ratio proves successful cleanup can fail if difficult assets were transferred or recognition lagged.
82. **[refinement]** The expectation that high opening capital proves sustainable capitalization can fail if recurring profitability remains weak.

## J. Scope boundary and old search keys

83. **[new]** Province-level local-bank consolidation can mix city commercial banks and rural banks in public discussion; BANK-CITY analysis must retain entity-level lineage and exclude rural predecessors unless necessary only as a boundary note.
