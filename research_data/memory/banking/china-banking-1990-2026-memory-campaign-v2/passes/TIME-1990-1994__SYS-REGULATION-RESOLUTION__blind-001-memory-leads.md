## Contemporaneous expectation leads

1. Around 1993-94, policymakers likely expected that separating policy finance from commercial banking would make state banks more commercially accountable and reduce the conflict between policy mandates and bank profitability/asset quality.
2. Strengthening PBOC macro-control/supervision was expected to curb the 1992-93 credit/investment overheating and reduce local diversion of bank funds.
3. Emerging asset-liability ratio management appears to embody an expectation that prudential constraints could gradually replace some direct administrative credit management, but the transition was likely hybrid rather than abrupt.
4. Commercialization rhetoric probably assumed that banks could move toward self-managed balance sheets while interest rates, ownership and much credit allocation remained heavily state-influenced—an internal tension worth preserving rather than smoothing away.
5. The period does not trigger a clear model memory of an explicit “let weak banks fail” policy. Administrative stabilization and depositor confidence appear to have had higher priority than market-exit discipline.
