## Orthogonal leads: what public-market discipline may actually have changed

1. **Listing can be decomposed into several distinct control channels rather than treated as one reform event.** Candidate channels are recurring audited disclosure, minority-shareholder claims, investor/analyst questioning, market valuation, capital-raising access, director duties, related-party scrutiny and management reputation. Later Evidence should test which channels had practical bite at each bank.

2. **Public-market accountability did not replace the state-owner chain; it layered another principal onto it.** Management could face shareholder-value expectations while the state retained controlling ownership and broader policy/stability objectives. The useful question is how conflicts were resolved when commercial return and public-policy expectations pointed in different directions.

3. **A high valuation or successful offering may have become an internal reform scorecard.** Management and policymakers could interpret investor demand as confirmation that recapitalization and governance repair had worked. This is a contemporaneous-confidence lead, not proof that underlying risk selection was already durable.

4. **Annual-report and prospectus discipline may have increased the cost of hiding problems without eliminating recognition discretion.** More disclosure can force reconciliation of NPLs, provisions, capital and contingent exposures, yet management still retains choices over classification, restructuring, collateral valuation, growth and timing. Public-market discipline therefore changes the game rather than abolishing metric management.

5. **ROE pressure may have interacted ambiguously with stronger capitalization.** Raising equity can initially dilute return metrics, while rapid asset growth, fee expansion or greater leverage can restore ROE. A later source pass should ask whether market-facing performance targets encouraged growth precisely after the cleanup strengthened balance sheets.

6. **NPL-ratio targets could create denominator incentives under listing scrutiny.** Rapid high-quality growth can legitimately lower the ratio, while growth also makes old problem assets look smaller. Orthogonal analysis should pair ratios with new-NPL formation, migration, provision expense, write-offs and absolute balances.

7. **Provision coverage can rise because provisions increase, NPL recognition changes, or the recognized NPL denominator falls.** This is another metric-semantics warning: apparently conservative coverage should be read together with classification policy and credit cost.

8. **Cost-income and branch productivity targets may have accelerated centralization and channel reform.** Listing-era pressure could support outlet consolidation, back-office concentration, electronic channels and standardized processes. But a lower cost ratio might also reflect revenue-cycle strength rather than permanent efficiency improvement.

## Orthogonal leads: strategic investors — capability transfer versus signaling

9. **The key test for strategic investors is operational footprint, not announcement prestige.** Later Evidence should look for joint committees, seconded staff, risk models, card/retail partnerships, treasury processes, IT projects, training, governance procedures or product launches that demonstrate actual transfer.

10. **Board representation does not automatically imply control.** A minority investor may obtain observation, consultation or limited veto-like protections without directing core lending, personnel or policy decisions. Exact rights and reserved matters should be recovered bank by bank.

11. **Capability transfer may have been asymmetric.** Foreign partners likely possessed useful retail, risk, treasury or governance experience, while Chinese banks possessed branch scale, deposits, domestic clients and regulatory access. Cooperation could therefore be a two-way commercial alliance rather than a simple teacher-student model.

12. **Technical advice can fail to transplant when incentives and data differ.** Imported scoring models, risk frameworks or sales processes may require reliable borrower histories, centralized data, enforceable accountability and compatible customer behavior. The implementation gap is more informative than whether a cooperation agreement existed.

13. **Strategic-investor presence may have served a signaling function even before measurable operating gains.** A respected external institution could reassure public investors about due diligence and governance. This credibility channel should be distinguished from later operating performance.

14. **ABC is the strongest counterexample to the idea that strategic-investor/listing sequencing was a necessary precondition for all large-bank improvement.** Its delayed restructuring invites comparison of rural mandate, legacy assets, branch economics, data quality, policy burden and reform readiness. The point is heterogeneity, not a claim that ABC lacked reform during the slice.

## Orthogonal leads: risk selection during the pre-crisis expansion

15. **The post-cleanup years may have been a rare period when large banks simultaneously had stronger capital, falling legacy NPLs and strong demand for growth.** That combination can improve earnings while also making risk appetite harder to judge because new vintages have not seasoned.

16. **Mortgage expansion should be evaluated as a correlated macro bet, not only granular retail diversification.** Thousands of small loans can still share exposure to urban property prices, employment and household income. Period-native underwriting may nevertheless have viewed mortgages as unusually safe because of collateral and borrower equity.

17. **Card and consumer expansion introduced different loss timing from corporate lending.** Faster account turnover, unsecured exposures, fraud, collections and score-based underwriting create operational/data dependencies that traditional corporate credit systems may not capture well.

18. **Large corporate/infrastructure lending could look safer after SOE reform and strong macro growth while retaining concentration risk.** State affiliation, collateral and project importance may reduce perceived default probability but can also blur pricing and recovery assumptions.

19. **Fee and wealth businesses can be low-balance-sheet or can quietly carry reputational/market risk.** Agency sales, custody and simple fee services differ from products involving guarantees, principal protection or embedded market exposure. The exact contractual boundary must be source-recovered rather than inferred from “fee income.”

20. **Treasury modernization may have diversified earnings while opening new valuation and counterparty channels.** Foreign bonds/securities, derivatives, interbank claims and FX activity could matter more for BOC than peers, but bank-specific books should be verified before drawing any exposure ranking.

21. **Basel-style frameworks may have improved measurement before they altered business decisions.** More sophisticated risk weights, rating systems or operational-risk taxonomies can coexist with old incentives. Later research should distinguish implementation milestones from evidence that limits/pricing actually changed.

22. **Centralized IT/data systems may have reduced branch opacity yet increased model/system dependence.** Better group-wide customer and exposure views are a genuine control gain; at the same time, data definitions, overrides and model assumptions become new failure points.

## Orthogonal leads: 2008 shock transmission and bank heterogeneity

23. **The 2008 shock likely reached large Chinese banks through at least four separable channels: direct market assets, counterparties, trade/export clients, and domestic macro/policy response.** Treating “subprime exposure” as the whole story would miss the broader transmission mechanism.

24. **BOC is a likely outlier for direct external-market sensitivity because of its international/FX franchise.** Potential foreign securities, overseas subsidiaries/branches and cross-border counterparties make its shock map structurally different. Exact products, impairments and amounts must be source verified.

25. **CCB and ICBC may have been more insulated from foreign wholesale-funding stress because their domestic deposit franchises dominated funding.** That does not imply immunity: exporters, property borrowers, equity-market effects and policy-driven changes in credit demand could transmit the downturn indirectly.

26. **ABC’s delayed market transition may have made public-market valuation less direct while rural/domestic macro channels mattered more.** The comparison can help separate “listed-bank shock” from “large state-bank macro shock.”

27. **Market-value losses and credit losses should be kept distinct.** A foreign security can lose value without borrower default; a domestic corporate loan can deteriorate with no traded mark. Different accounting categories and recognition timing may produce different reported shock profiles.

28. **Counterparty exposure should be distinguished from funding dependence.** A bank can have little reliance on foreign wholesale funding yet still hold claims on, trade with, or settle through stressed global institutions. The balance-sheet direction of exposure matters.

29. **Trade finance can be both defensive and cyclical.** Short-tenor, transaction-linked structures may appear lower risk, but exporter/importer stress and collapsing trade volumes can still weaken clients and fee income. BOC’s franchise makes this an especially useful bank-specific lead.

30. **The late-2008 policy pivot may have changed management’s objective function before the full next-period credit boom arrived.** A bank recently disciplined around capital, risk-adjusted returns and market expectations could be asked to support stabilization more aggressively. This creates a bridge question for 2009–2012 without importing that later period’s volumes or outcomes.

31. **State ownership may have provided confidence and policy coordination while weakening the threat of market exit as a discipline mechanism.** Public shareholders can price risk, but a systemically important state-controlled bank may still be perceived as strongly supported. The coexistence of market pricing and implicit support is a core institutional tension.

32. **The most informative counterfactual is not “did reform work?” but “which pre-2008 controls survived a sudden objective change?”** Credit limits, underwriting independence, capital planning, board challenge, risk reporting and branch accountability can be tested against the pressure to support growth. That durability question belongs to later Outcome work, not this blind pass.

## Counterexamples to preserve

- More disclosure can improve comparability while still leaving classification and timing discretion.
- A strategic investor can be economically useful without possessing control rights.
- Strong ROE after recapitalization can indicate genuine efficiency or renewed balance-sheet expansion; the metric alone cannot decide.
- Low foreign wholesale funding reduces one crisis channel but not external-asset, counterparty, borrower or policy channels.
- Mortgage granularity reduces single-name concentration but not common housing/macro exposure.
- Public listing creates market discipline while state support can simultaneously weaken the threat of failure.
- ABC’s different sequence cautions against treating BOC/CCB/ICBC as a universal large-bank reform template.
