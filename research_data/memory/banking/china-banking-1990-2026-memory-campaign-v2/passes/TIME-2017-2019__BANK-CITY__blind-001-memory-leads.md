## Period frame

- 2017-2019 appears to reverse the prior city-bank growth regime: regulatory deleveraging, tighter interbank/shadow treatment and asset-management reform make funding quality, asset transparency and true capital more binding.
- The key transition is not simply slower growth. Credit and liquidity risk become harder to hide through rollover, WMP pools, non-standard channels and rapid denominator expansion.
- Strong and weak city banks likely diverge sharply according to local deposit franchise, governance, shareholder quality, market-funding dependence and ability to absorb asset recognition.

## Financial deleveraging and balance-sheet contraction

- Interbank asset/liability growth becomes less frictionless; banks relying on wholesale funding face higher marginal costs and tighter counterparty limits.
- Balance-sheet growth can slow because both funding supply and permissible asset structures tighten.
- Asset compression may occur via run-off of non-standard investments, reduced interbank assets, slower WMP-linked credit creation and tighter branch risk limits.
- Some banks may shift from shadow-like investments back toward standard loans/bonds, which changes reported loan growth and risk-weight composition.
- Deleveraging can temporarily weaken earnings because high-yield non-standard assets are replaced by lower-yield transparent assets.
- The same transition can improve long-run liquidity/credit transparency while exposing legacy losses.

## Asset-management-rule / WMP transition

- WMP pools and implicit-guarantee expectations face pressure from new asset-management rules and net-value-oriented direction.
- Legacy products may require grandfathering, run-off, restructuring or migration rather than immediate disappearance.
- Non-standard assets with long maturities create mismatch when old short-duration funding models can no longer roll indefinitely.
- Bank-sponsored WMP may become less able to serve as a hidden credit warehouse for local borrowers.
- Moving assets back on balance sheet can raise capital/provision requirements and reported credit exposure.
- Moving toward standardized securities can improve valuation transparency but expose mark-to-market volatility.
- Wealth-management transition affects fee income, customer retention and reputational obligations, not only shadow-credit volume.

## NCD / wholesale funding repricing

- NCDs remain important for city-bank marginal funding but become more credit-sensitive as investors differentiate issuers.
- Funding spreads can widen before deposit runs occur; market segmentation is an early-warning mechanism distinct from retail deposit behavior.
- Counterparty credit limits can tighten abruptly following a small-bank credit event.
- Collateral haircuts and repo eligibility can differ across bank-issued instruments, affecting liquidity even without outright default.
- Banks with strong local deposits may gain relative advantage as wholesale funding becomes more selective.
- Banks that previously relied on cheap interbank/NCD funding may need to shrink assets or pay up for deposits.

## Baoshang-era small-bank shock leads

- The Baoshang-type episode is a major search key for the market realization that small/medium bank liabilities were not uniformly risk-free in practice.
- The immediate mechanism for other city banks is likely counterparty/funding segmentation, not necessarily direct credit losses.
- Investors may distinguish large state-backed institutions, stronger listed city banks and weaker opaque regional banks more sharply.
- NCD issuance capacity, interbank lines, repo treatment and liquidity premiums can all transmit confidence shocks.
- Policy support can stabilize the system while still preserving more issuer differentiation than before.
- Exact event dates, haircuts, spreads and institution-specific funding effects belong to Evidence.

## Governance / shareholder stress

- Opaque private-shareholder control and related-party lending become increasingly important negative-space signals.
- Local-government ownership can support recapitalization or restructuring but may also delay recognition of local concentrated exposures.
- Shareholder liquidity problems can transmit to a bank if connected borrowers or pledged shares are involved.
- Industrial-group control can create concentration beyond conventional borrower-level limits through affiliated entities.
- Later-problem banks may reveal that formal capital ratios and board structures did not capture effective shareholder influence.
- Management/corruption cases can be both symptom and cause of weak controls, but should not be generalized across all city banks.

## Asset-quality recognition

- Slower asset growth makes denominator support for low NPL ratios weaker.
- Tighter classification and less easy refinancing can move overdue/special-mention/restructured exposures into NPLs.
- Legacy non-standard investments may require impairment/provision treatment that differs from ordinary loan NPL classification.
- Write-offs, transfers, restructurings and recapitalization can lower reported NPL stocks while raising credit costs or consuming capital.
- Provision coverage may fall mechanically when recognized NPLs rise faster than reserves.
- Capital adequacy can deteriorate from both RWA migration and loss recognition.
- Banks with years of aggressive growth may need external capital just as market confidence weakens.

## Private enterprise / SME credit

- Financial deleveraging can disproportionately tighten funding to private firms and SMEs reliant on non-bank/shadow channels.
- City banks with SME franchises face a tension between policy pressure to support private firms and rising credit risk.
- Guarantee chains and mutual guarantees can propagate stress among local private enterprises.
- Bond-market defaults among private firms may migrate risk back to bank loans, guarantees or wealth products.
- Strong relationship knowledge can help local banks distinguish borrowers, but local concentration can amplify regional downturns.
- Credit reallocation toward state/local-government-linked borrowers can reduce perceived credit risk while worsening structural concentration.

## Property / LGFV / local government

- Property and LGFV exposures remain central but may migrate from opaque channels toward loans, bonds or more standardized structures.
- Local-government debt-management changes can alter refinancing expectations without eliminating implicit-support assumptions.
- Land-finance dependence still links developer/property risk, local fiscal capacity and bank collateral values.
- Stronger local governments can support both borrowers and banks; weaker regions can create correlated asset/funding stress.
- Deleveraging may slow new platform credit while legacy projects still require refinancing.
- Property developer concentration can become more visible if shadow channels contract.

## Cross-region strategy

- Cross-region expansion may lose some appeal as funding conditions tighten and supervision emphasizes local risk containment.
- Weak remote branches can be candidates for slower growth, consolidation or stronger head-office control.
- Geographic diversification can prove illusory when remote branches were sourcing similar property/platform/private-enterprise risks.
- Home-market deposits regain strategic value relative to wholesale-funded expansion.
- Some stronger city banks may continue regional expansion, creating useful counterexamples to a universal retrenchment story.

## Liquidity and contingency management

- Liquidity contingency planning becomes more institution-specific as market counterparties differentiate banks.
- High-quality liquid assets matter more than total investment assets.
- Central-bank/system facilities may support market stability, but access/collateral and transmission to individual city banks can differ.
- Retail deposits can remain stable while wholesale funding becomes volatile; liquidity analysis must separate channels.
- Banks may increase long-term deposits or higher-cost certificates to reduce wholesale rollover risk.
- Funding concentration by institution or product becomes a more meaningful metric than total deposits alone.

## Capital paths and restructuring

- Listed or stronger city banks may raise common equity, preference shares, subordinated debt or other capital instruments to support transition.
- Capital raising can be defensive recognition of prior growth rather than evidence of fresh expansion opportunity.
- Weak banks may require local-government, state-asset, AMC or strategic-investor involvement.
- Mergers/ownership changes can occur before formal failure; institution name survival does not imply governance continuity.
- A bank can remain operating while effective control, shareholder base or balance-sheet quality changes materially.

## Failed / weak institutions and antecedent leads

- Bank of Jinzhou-type stress is a key city-bank search lead for opaque shareholders, market funding, non-standard assets and recapitalization/restructuring.
- Hengfeng-type governance/restructuring is another city-bank lead; exact facts/timing need Evidence.
- Other weaker regional city banks may have received local recapitalization, ownership cleanup or merger support without national visibility.
- Strong listed city banks provide counterexamples showing that the period is not synonymous with city-bank crisis.

## Contemporaneous expectations and counterexamples

- Expectation: deleveraging would simply reduce systemic risk by shrinking shadow assets.
- Counterexample: rapid contraction can expose rollover/liquidity stress and shift credit risk into recognized banking books.
- Expectation: asset-management reform would end implicit guarantees quickly.
- Counterexample: legacy products, reputational expectations and transition arrangements can persist.
- Expectation: NCD markets improve price discovery and funding efficiency.
- Counterexample: once credit differentiation rises, market funding can become sharply pro-cyclical for weak issuers.
- Expectation: local-government support prevents regional-bank stress.
- Counterexample: support capacity and willingness vary; shareholder/governance problems can overwhelm reputation.
- Expectation: lower shadow exposure means lower total credit risk.
- Counterexample: risk may reappear as loans, bonds, restructured assets or public-sector concentration.

## Metric-semantic cautions

- 'Shadow contraction' may mean WMP balance, non-standard underlying assets, interbank nesting or off-balance-sheet credit; definitions differ.
- NCD issuance volume and outstanding balance are not interchangeable and do not directly equal funding dependence.
- Interbank funding should distinguish unsecured placements, repos, NCDs and other institutional liabilities.
- NPL improvement can reflect write-offs/transfers rather than better new-vintage credit.
- Capital ratios can change because of RWA migration as well as equity/loss changes.
- WMP transition metrics may mix legacy guaranteed-like products, new net-value products and off-balance-sheet AUM.
- Cross-region retrenchment should be measured by assets/origination/funding, not only branch closures.

## Negative-space conclusion

The 2017-2019 city-bank story is a transition from expansion-through-market-funding/shadow structures toward forced transparency and credit differentiation. The main structural axis is deleveraging + WMP/non-standard transition + wholesale-funding repricing + asset-quality/capital recognition, with Baoshang-era counterparty segmentation and weak-bank governance cases acting as stress tests. An independent orthogonal pass should test whether weak-bank resolution/governance contagion forms a second material category, or remains an extreme tail/transmission channel of the same funding-and-recognition regime shift.
