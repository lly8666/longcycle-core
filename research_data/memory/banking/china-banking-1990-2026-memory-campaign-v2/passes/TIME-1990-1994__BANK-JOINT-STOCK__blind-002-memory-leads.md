## Funding and liquidity challenge

42. [N] Corporate deposits can look stable while the relationship is healthy but may be concentrated and transaction-sensitive rather than granular.
43. [N] Sponsor-group deposits may reduce acquisition cost while making funding and governance concentration the same underlying risk factor.
44. [N] Settlement relationships can create operational balances that are less rate-driven than pure treasury deposits, but exact segmentation terminology likely differed from modern operational-deposit concepts.

46. [N] 同业拆借/资金拆借 is a high-value liquidity lead because a fast-growing bank with a thinner deposit base could use interbank markets more intensively than a legacy state bank.
47. [N] Early interbank markets may have been less standardized and more relationship-driven; therefore counterparty and rollover risk may not resemble later repo/CD markets.
48. [N] "乱拆借" is best treated as an old search key for disorderly interbank/financial-company funding practices, not as a bank-specific accusation without evidence.
49. [N] Central-bank liquidity access, reserve requirements and interbank funding should be kept as external boundary conditions; the BANK-JOINT-STOCK question is how each bank's liability structure transmitted those conditions.
50. [N] A bank can report fast deposit growth from a small base while still having a fragile funding concentration. Absolute scale and concentration matter alongside percentage growth.

66. [N] Sponsor-related or locally supported borrowers can remain current longer because of refinancing/support, complicating comparisons of reported asset quality across institutions.
67. [N] Young banks may initially show lower legacy problem-loan stocks simply because their portfolios are newer; cohort age is a critical denominator for asset-quality comparison.
