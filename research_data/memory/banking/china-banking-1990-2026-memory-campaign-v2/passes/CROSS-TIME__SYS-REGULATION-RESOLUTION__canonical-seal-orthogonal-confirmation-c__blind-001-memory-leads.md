## Bounded independent scan

This pass does not retell chronology or enumerate named cases. It asks whether an independent memory scan of ex-ante systemic-importance/perimeter work, recovery/resolution planning, resolvability, activation/intervention entry, exercise of powers and realized outcomes changes the compact category map.

1. **useful_refinement — resolution/preparedness powers should use an explicit action-state ladder.** For any remembered authority or mechanism, record separately whether a power/framework merely exists or is available, whether a trigger/entry condition was reached, whether the power was actually invoked/exercised, when it became operationally effective, and what observable outcome followed. Importance: high. This is a representation refinement inside the existing preparedness/activation/outcome category, not a new top-level category.

2. **duplicate — systemic-importance designation is not intervention.** Being inside a systemic-importance or special-supervision perimeter says nothing by itself about distress, resolution entry or realized loss.

3. **duplicate — recovery planning and resolution planning remain distinct from actual execution.** A plan can exist, be updated or be reviewed without any resolution event occurring.

4. **duplicate — resolvability assessment is not resolution entry.** Work on operational continuity, separability, data, valuation capability or critical functions is preparedness unless a separate activation event occurs.

5. **duplicate — gone-concern loss-absorbing capacity is ex-ante capacity, not realized creditor loss.** Eligible instruments or capacity targets should not be converted into impairment outcomes without event-specific facts.

6. **duplicate — drills/tabletop exercises are not real interventions.** Simulation of operational or resolution processes is preparedness evidence only.

7. **duplicate — plan revision frequency is not a distress signal by itself.** Periodic updating can reflect governance or regulatory cycles rather than worsening institution-specific risk.

8. **duplicate — supervisory escalation and formal resolution entry are separate states.** Heightened supervision, remediation demands, management changes or restrictions can precede resolution without proving it.

9. **duplicate — resolution entry and exercise of a specific power are separate events.** A framework can authorize multiple tools; entry does not prove that any particular transfer, write-down, payout or closure tool was used.

10. **duplicate — exercise of a power does not by itself establish claimant loss.** A transfer, management intervention or restructuring tool can occur while the economic treatment of each claimant class remains unresolved.

11. **duplicate — operational continuity is not the same as legal-claim continuity.** Critical services can continue while legal claims are transferred, stayed, restructured or left in a residual entity; the two dimensions must remain separate.

12. **duplicate — temporary liquidity or capital support is not automatically a resolution trigger.** Support can occur before, during or without formal resolution and should not be used as a proxy for legal state.

13. **duplicate — public communication or contingency preparation is not proof of tool activation.** Announced frameworks, guidance or readiness measures remain ex ante until case-specific exercise is established.

14. **duplicate — legal entry time, operational effective time and economic-loss recognition time can differ.** A single timestamp should not be forced across these states when Evidence later shows staged implementation.

15. **duplicate — claimant settlement is downstream of intervention state.** Final payment, recovery, transfer or impairment can occur after the core resolution action and should be represented separately.

16. **duplicate — absence of visible terminal exit does not imply absence of intervention.** Conversely, intervention without entity exit remains a non-terminal or near-terminal state unless later facts support legal termination.
