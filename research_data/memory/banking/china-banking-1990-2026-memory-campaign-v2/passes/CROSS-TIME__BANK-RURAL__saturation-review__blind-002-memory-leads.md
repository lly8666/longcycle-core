## Independent cross-time negative-space review

### A. Provincial reform topology — material challenge

1. **[N | high] Province-level reform may have at least two legally different successor topologies.** A province-wide single legal-person rural commercial bank is not necessarily the only route replacing or reforming a provincial rural-credit union. A `联合银行` / united-bank route may create a new provincial banking/service legal person while preserving county rural-commercial-bank legal persons beneath or alongside it.
2. **[N | high] “United bank” must not be inferred to mean one consolidated balance sheet.** The key Evidence-stage questions are whether county banks remain separate legal persons, who owns shares in whom, whether capital and credit losses are mutualized, and which services/governance powers move to the provincial entity.
3. **[N | high] A united-bank route could be a successor to the old provincial-union management platform without being a successor to county-bank balance sheets.** This is structurally different from the rev168 compact note about provincial legal-person consolidation into a successor cohort.
4. **[N | high] Two-tier legal-person provincial reform can preserve local bank identity while centralizing selected capabilities.** Treasury, technology, payments, risk tooling, product development, audit or personnel influence may centralize while lending assets, deposits and regulatory capital remain at county-bank legal persons.
5. **[N | high] Provincial reform topology therefore needs a typed relation rather than one “consolidated/not consolidated” flag.** Candidate types include industry-management union, united-bank/two-tier legal-person platform, and province-wide single legal-person bank. Exact official categories require Evidence.
6. **[N | medium] Transition from provincial union to united bank may alter ownership even when county legal persons survive.** County banks might become shareholders/members of the provincial entity, the provincial entity might hold stakes in county banks, or another structure may apply; do not assume direction of ownership without sources.
7. **[N | medium] The economic meaning of capital support differs by topology.** Capital injected into a provincial united bank may not automatically recapitalize every county bank, while capital injected into a single consolidated successor may sit inside one legal perimeter.
8. **[N | medium] Local-service preservation can be an explicit design rationale for two-tier reform.** Keeping county legal persons may preserve local relationship lending and governance incentives while attempting to gain provincial scale in infrastructure; this is a contemporaneous-Judgment lead, not proof of outcome.
9. **[R | high] Shared provincial technology does not prove shared solvency.** Rev168 already preserves this general boundary; the new point is that a united-bank legal form could make that distinction institutionally durable rather than merely transitional.
10. **[R | high] Strong independent county RCBs remain a counterexample to universal province-wide consolidation.** A two-tier united-bank model strengthens this counterexample because local legal persons can remain intentionally preserved rather than merely not-yet-merged.

### B. Provincial infrastructure and service actors

11. **[R | medium] A national rural-credit clearing/payment network is a forgotten infrastructure layer.** Rural institutions can achieve nationwide payment/clearing reach through shared infrastructure without common ownership or solvency. Exact entity identity and formation date are Evidence-stage.
12. **[R | medium] Provincial shared core banking, data centers and product platforms should be typed as service relationships.** Shared systems can precede, survive or change across legal-form reform.
13. **[R | medium] Liquidity coordination is not equivalent to credit-loss mutualization.** A provincial platform may arrange liquidity, settlement or emergency support while county-bank capital remains separate.
14. **[R | medium] Personnel appointment/influence can differ from ownership control.** Provincial entities may influence executives or governance even where legal ownership is dispersed or county-based.
15. **[R | medium] Brand convergence is not legal-person convergence.** A common rural-credit/rural-commercial brand may mask multiple legal persons and different capital positions.

### C. Village-bank lineage and exit mechanisms

16. **[R | high] Sponsor-bank absorption is a distinct village-bank exit path.** A weak or subscale village bank may be absorbed by a sponsor or another bank and possibly converted into a branch; this should be represented as merger/absorption plus legal-form change rather than generic “closure.”
17. **[R | medium] Village-bank sponsor responsibility remains narrower than blanket guarantee unless Evidence says otherwise.** Governance, liquidity assistance, capital injection, acquisition and depositor protection are separate possible actions.
18. **[R | medium] Village-bank consolidation can reduce legal-person counts without reducing physical rural outlets.** Institution-count and outlet-count metrics remain distinct.
19. **[D | high] Village banks remain a sponsor-led tail distinct from RCC/RCB lineage.** Rev168 already preserves this broad boundary.
20. **[R | medium] The same sponsor may rationalize multiple village-bank holdings differently across regions.** This is franchise strategy, not a new BANK-RURAL system lineage.

### D. Failure, resolution and capital-support semantics

21. **[R | medium] Formal insolvency is only one rural-bank exit state.** Administrative merger, absorption, recapitalization, license cancellation, conversion to branch and successor formation should remain separate event types.
22. **[R | medium] Local-government special-purpose capital support is a mechanism, not a new bank type.** Where such support is used for small/rural banks, the instrument source and receiving legal person matter; exact program mechanics belong to Evidence.
23. **[R | medium] Deposit-insurance participation or resolution funding is a SYS-REGULATION-RESOLUTION relation, not proof of bank solvency.** Keep support source, trigger and legal authority separate from BANK-RURAL institution identity.
24. **[R | medium] High-risk-institution reduction is an outcome metric with denominator semantics.** Fewer “high-risk” institutions can reflect merger/removal/reclassification as well as improvement; exact classification belongs to the system/regulatory owner.
25. **[R | medium] Successor formation can hide predecessor dispersion in aggregate counts.** Replay needs predecessor-to-successor lineage and should not interpret fewer legal persons as equivalent to fewer problem balance sheets.

28. **[R | medium] Legal-person count, outlet count, employee count and service-village coverage can move in opposite directions during consolidation.** These are separate operational metrics.
