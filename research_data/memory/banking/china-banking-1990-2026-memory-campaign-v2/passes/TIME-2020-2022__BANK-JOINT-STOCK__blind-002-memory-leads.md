## Orthogonal challenge leads

### Temporary relief versus hidden impairment

1. A borrower-relief program can improve short-term contractual performance while worsening information quality: delinquency no longer maps one-for-one to economic stress. [new_category; high]
2. The most useful distinction is whether repayment relief solved a timing mismatch or merely postponed a solvency problem. [new_category; high]
3. Rescheduled principal, interest capitalization, covenant waivers and maturity extension should be treated as different mechanisms even if all lower immediate arrears. [new_category]
4. Renewal of SME loans without full principal repayment can preserve going-concern value, but repeated renewal may also obscure weak cash generation. [new_category]
5. Transaction-account turnover, tax/payroll flows and collateral utilization may have become more informative than simple days-past-due during policy-support periods. [new_category]
6. Stage-2 ECL migration can be a leading stress signal even where the account remains outside regulatory NPL. [new_category; high]
7. A loan can return to current payment status after reopening yet still carry higher lifetime expected loss because borrower leverage or sector outlook deteriorated. [new_category]
8. Conversely, conservative Stage-2 migration need not predict ultimate default if activity normalizes; forward-looking provisioning can intentionally front-load uncertainty. [new_category; counterexample]
9. NPL inflow, cure, write-off and disposal should be decomposed before reading a stable NPL ratio as stable credit quality. [useful_refinement]
10. Special-mention, overdue, restructuring and ECL stage migration are related but not nested in a simple universal hierarchy. [useful_refinement; metric_semantics]
11. Pandemic-support vintages should be separated from older corporate books because underwriting purpose, guarantee support and borrower cash-flow conditions differ. [new_category]
12. A bank with higher reported credit cost may be recognizing uncertainty earlier rather than experiencing structurally worse assets. [new_category; counterexample]

### Property, project, mortgage and retail transmission

13. Developer default risk and mortgage credit risk are connected through project completion but remain distinct borrower and collateral risks. [new_category; high]
14. Project-level escrow/cash management and developer-group exposure may create very different risk than unsecured parent-company funding. [new_category]
15. Financing suppliers, construction contractors and property-service firms can transmit developer stress even when direct development-loan exposure is limited. [new_category]
16. Residential mortgage books can remain low-loss while producing duration/NIM problems if yields reset downward or prepayment accelerates. [new_category; counterexample]
17. Consumer confidence affects card transaction volume and unsecured lending demand before formal delinquency necessarily rises. [new_category]
18. Credit-card receivables deserve vintage and channel segmentation because payroll-linked customers, long-standing bank customers and platform-acquired customers may behave differently. [new_category]
19. “Retail risk” should be decomposed into mortgages, cards, unsecured cash loans, auto, small-business-owner loans and wealth-linked credit. [new_category; metric_semantics]
20. Property stress can reduce corporate deposits and fee flows before appearing in developer NPLs, through weaker sales, escrow balances and supplier settlement. [new_category]
21. Local-government-linked infrastructure exposures may behave differently from developer exposures even where both depend indirectly on land markets. [new_category]
22. A counterexample to “property stress is all urban banks”: nationally diversified joint-stock banks can still carry concentrated client-level or region-level exposures. [new_category; counterexample]
23. A second counterexample: nationally diversified mortgage portfolios may cushion localized project stress even when a few developers dominate headlines. [new_category; counterexample]

### LPR, deposit termization and funding economics

24. The core repricing problem is timing mismatch: floating-rate corporate loans, mortgages, fixed/term deposits and market funding do not reset simultaneously. [new_category; high]
25. Front-book loan pricing can fall quickly as competition intensifies while back-book deposits remain expensive until maturity. [new_category]
26. Deposit termization can stabilize funding tenor while raising structural funding cost and reducing NIM flexibility. [new_category; high]
27. Demand-deposit share is meaningful only with behavioral stability; temporary transaction deposits are not equivalent to sticky payroll or household primary-bank balances. [useful_refinement]
28. Wealth-to-deposit migration during risk-off periods can temporarily improve liquidity but increase future repricing cost if customers prefer longer tenor deposits. [new_category]
29. Purchased corporate balances and genuine operating deposits should be distinguished; one may require pricing concessions while the other is embedded in transaction relationships. [new_category]
30. Interbank CDs can be used for tactical liquidity even by strong deposit franchises, so the ratio is not a standalone weakness indicator. [useful_refinement; counterexample]
31. FTP should reflect tenor and optionality; otherwise business lines may look profitable only because liquidity value is misallocated internally. [new_category]
32. Mortgage and consumer-loan prepayment options change effective asset duration and may matter more in falling-rate environments. [new_category]
33. NIM pressure can be partly offset by asset-mix shift toward higher-yield retail or SME lending, but this may raise credit cost. [new_category; mechanism]
34. A bank can defend NIM by raising risk appetite, making apparent spread resilience potentially ambiguous rather than unambiguously positive. [new_category; counterexample]

### Wealth/NAV transition and customer economics

35. NAV-based WMP conversion changes investor experience from expected return to visible volatility, creating customer-service and suitability risks. [new_category; high]
36. Wealth subsidiaries create clearer legal/organizational separation, but customer perception may still attach product losses to the parent bank brand. [new_category]
37. Wealth AUM, managed WMP AUM, distributed third-party products, custody assets and bank deposits should be separated; they represent different economics. [new_category; metric_semantics]
38. Customer AUM can rise while bank balance-sheet deposits fall, so total customer franchise may strengthen even as funding metrics weaken. [new_category; counterexample]
39. Conversely, falling risky-asset appetite can drive customers from WMPs/funds back into deposits, raising bank funding but depressing fee/AUM economics. [new_category]
40. Cash-management WMP products blur the psychological boundary with deposits for customers even if legal/accounting treatment differs. [new_category]
41. “破净” or temporary NAV below cost is a useful memory/search lead for customer reaction, but exact episodes and product scope require Evidence. [new_category; uncertain_fragment]
42. A strong distribution franchise can earn fees on third-party funds/insurance without assuming equivalent balance-sheet credit risk. [new_category]
43. A failed narrative to test is that wealth subsidiaries automatically remove reputational risk; customer expectations can survive structural reform. [new_category; failed_narrative]

### ECL, NPL and capital semantics

44. ECL is scenario-sensitive; macro assumptions can move provisions before realized default changes. [new_category]
45. Stage 3 and regulatory NPL may overlap substantially but should not be assumed identical in timing or scope. [useful_refinement; metric_semantics]
46. “Credit cost down” can result from prior front-loading of provisions rather than current-period risk improvement alone. [new_category; counterexample]
47. Provision coverage can rise while absolute allowance falls if NPL disposal shrinks the denominator. [useful_refinement; metric_semantics]
48. Write-off intensity is a flow metric and can signal cleanup rather than deterioration in new vintages. [useful_refinement]
49. Capital consumption depends on RWA mix and loan growth, so pandemic-policy lending can pressure capital even if accounting profits remain positive. [new_category]
50. Retail mortgages, corporate loans, off-balance-sheet guarantees and market investments consume capital differently; total assets are a poor proxy for capital intensity. [new_category]
51. Internal capital allocation can push banks toward fee/AUM businesses when loan growth has unattractive risk-adjusted returns. [new_category; mechanism]
52. Convertible or perpetual capital instruments may improve regulatory capital but do not directly solve weak core earnings; instrument economics should remain separate from franchise quality. [new_category; counterexample]

### Digital operating leverage and acquisition economics

53. Remote servicing reduces transaction friction but does not eliminate the cost of acquiring primary-bank relationships. [new_category]
54. App monthly-active users, registered users and profitable active customers are different metrics. [new_category; metric_semantics]
55. Digital acquisition may increase small-ticket lending volume faster than risk models season, making cohort/vintage monitoring especially important. [new_category]
56. Branch closures or consolidation can lower occupancy/staff cost but also reduce local deposit gathering and relationship depth in some markets. [new_category; counterexample]
57. Remote RMs and centralized wealth advisers can increase coverage ratios while potentially weakening face-to-face trust for older or high-net-worth customers. [new_category]
58. Fraud and operational-risk controls can offset some apparent digital cost savings; cybersecurity and authentication are part of unit economics. [new_category]
59. Ecosystem cross-sell is valuable only if it produces durable deposits, AUM, transaction flows or acceptable credit economics, not merely app traffic. [new_category]
60. A failed narrative to test is that technology-led retail necessarily produces lower credit cost; weaker adverse selection can move in the opposite direction. [new_category; failed_narrative]

### Institution-level adaptation

61. **CMB** remains a useful retail/wealth benchmark, but the clean blind test is franchise mechanics—deposits, cards, AUM, fee economics and credit quality—not direct use of preexposed 2023 management specifics. [useful_refinement; high]
62. **Industrial Bank** is a useful test of whether a bank with stronger market/investment/corporate capabilities could adapt to lower-shadow-banking growth through green finance, transaction banking and fee activities. [new_category]
63. **Minsheng Bank** remains a useful stress case for private-enterprise-oriented lending and legacy asset-quality cleanup. [new_category; high]
64. **Ping An Bank** is a useful test of group ecosystem plus retail/consumer exposure: cross-sell benefits and consumer-credit cyclicality can coexist. [new_category]
65. **SPDB** is a useful test of whether retail/digital transformation changed economics fast enough to offset legacy corporate and property exposures. [new_category]
66. **CITIC Bank** may sit between corporate/group-linked strengths and wealth/retail ambitions, making it a useful mixed-model comparator. [new_category]
67. **Everbright Bank** is useful for wealth/asset-management heritage and mid-tier franchise economics rather than only balance-sheet size. [new_category]
68. **Huaxia Bank** remains important negative space: smaller national peers can reveal deposit-franchise and corporate-concentration pressure hidden by stronger headline franchises. [new_category; long_tail]
69. **Guangfa/CGB** remains a useful card/retail and insurance-group-linked comparator; group affiliation should not be assumed to equal superior funding economics. [new_category; long_tail]
70. Peer adaptation should be scored by funding stability, customer AUM, asset mix, credit cost, capital consumption and operating efficiency together, not by one strategy label. [useful_refinement; high]
71. No major national joint-stock bank failure in 2020-2022 is itself a counterexample to reading severe sector stress as universal insolvency; institution-specific cleanup still mattered. [new_category; counterexample]
72. The orthogonal challenge does not reveal a second structural category beyond the broad regime: its strongest additions refine timing, metric semantics, customer migration, hidden impairment and peer adaptation. [duplicate]
