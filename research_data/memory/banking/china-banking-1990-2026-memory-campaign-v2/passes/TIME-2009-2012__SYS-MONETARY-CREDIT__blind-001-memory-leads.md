## Broad recall leads

### 1. “适度宽松的货币政策” was an explicit crisis-era regime label, not just a generic description
- **Novelty:** new_category
- Late-2008/2009 policy memory is of a formally easier stance framed around growth stabilization after the global crisis.
- The useful research object is the regime label and what instruments/credit behavior contemporaries associated with it, not a single interest-rate move.
- Avoid collapsing fiscal stimulus, bank credit expansion and central-bank easing into one actor or one policy instrument.

### 2. 2009 bank-credit expansion became a macro transmission mechanism in its own right
- **Novelty:** new_category
- Memory strongly associates 2009 with an extraordinary acceleration of RMB bank lending and broad money growth.
- Large state-owned banks appear to have been important early transmitters because balance-sheet capacity, government-linked projects and policy signaling aligned.
- Exact annual new-loan figures are Evidence work; preserve only the directional “very large / front-loaded” memory here.

### 3. “新增人民币贷款” and outstanding loan growth are different metric objects
- **Novelty:** useful_refinement
- Contemporary commentary often used the flow of new RMB loans as a policy/credit-pulse measure, while year-on-year outstanding loan growth described a different stock-growth object.
- A future historical series must not silently substitute one for the other or mix RMB-only with broader loan definitions.

### 4. M2 growth was a central contemporaneous monetary aggregate, but not equivalent to credit delivered to the real economy
- **Novelty:** useful_refinement
- Broad-money growth accelerated with the stimulus credit cycle, yet M2 included deposit creation and other balance-sheet effects beyond a simple “loans to productive firms” story.
- Research should preserve target/actual/stock-growth semantics separately and avoid treating M2 as a direct welfare or real-activity measure.

### 5. Local-government financing platforms became a major bank-credit transmission channel
- **Novelty:** new_category
- Crisis-stimulus implementation appears closely connected to local-government infrastructure financing vehicles/platform companies obtaining bank loans for transport, municipal and other projects.
- Monetary easing therefore propagated through local fiscal/investment structures, not only through private credit demand.
- This is a transmission lead, not a claim about later asset quality or resolution outcomes.

### 6. Infrastructure project lending created a maturity/cash-flow transmission problem that policy aggregates could hide
- **Novelty:** new_category
- A large loan pulse could support construction immediately while project cash flows, fiscal support and repayment capacity arrived on different horizons.
- Veteran-analyst gap to test later: how much “credit effectiveness” depended on implicit local support, land-related revenues or refinancing rather than project cash generation.
- Do not infer later default outcomes into 2009 knowledge-time.

### 7. Property and mortgage channels amplified the post-crisis easing cycle
- **Novelty:** new_category
- Easier credit conditions, investment demand and household mortgage availability likely reinforced property turnover/prices and developer financing in parts of the cycle.
- Property-specific administrative restrictions belong partly outside this shard, but their interaction with aggregate monetary-credit transmission matters.

### 8. Bill financing/discounted bills were an important early-stimulus credit composition signal
- **Novelty:** new_category
- Memory associates portions of the early credit surge with bill financing/discounting, which could expand quickly and did not always imply the same medium-term capital expenditure commitment as ordinary term loans.
- Subsequent compression or rotation from bill financing toward medium/long-term loans may be a useful composition lead.
- Exact monthly composition requires Evidence.

### 9. Loan “pace” guidance survived despite the absence of the old pre-1998 loan-limit regime
- **Novelty:** new_category
- PBOC/authorities appear to have used window guidance, credit-policy signaling and pressure for more balanced loan delivery across months/quarters.
- “信贷投放节奏 / 均衡投放” should be searched as period-native vocabulary.
- Counterexample: administrative influence over pace should not be mislabeled as restoration of the old formal loan-scale control system.

### 10. The policy stance shifted from crisis easing toward normalization before the period ended
- **Novelty:** new_category
- By 2010–2011, inflation, property/credit concerns and recovery changed the balance from emergency support toward liquidity withdrawal and tighter monetary conditions.
- The same 2009–2012 window therefore contains multiple knowledge-time regimes and must not be represented as one continuous stance.

### 11. Repeated required-reserve increases became a prominent normalization/tightening tool
- **Novelty:** new_category
- Memory is strong that reserve requirements were raised repeatedly during the tightening phase and reached historically restrictive territory for large banks.
- The mechanism was quantity/liquidity absorption through banks’ reserve obligations, not identical to changing benchmark lending or deposit prices.
- Exact ratios and step dates are intentionally deferred.

### 12. Benchmark deposit/lending-rate hikes remained part of the administered-rate framework
- **Novelty:** new_category
- Tightening also used benchmark-rate adjustments; bank retail/corporate pricing still operated around administratively defined benchmark structures rather than a modern single policy-rate transmission framework.
- Real borrowing/deposit conditions could differ materially from nominal benchmark moves when inflation was high.

### 13. “稳健的货币政策” marked a regime-label change and should be preserved as contemporaneous vocabulary
- **Novelty:** new_category
- Around the normalization phase, the official stance memory shifts from “适度宽松” to “稳健”.
- This label is a research key for contemporaneous intent and communication; it does not by itself prove the exact tightness of every instrument or bank balance sheet.

### 14. “差别准备金动态调整” looks like an early macroprudential-monetary hybrid
- **Novelty:** new_category
- Memory associates the 2011-era framework with differentiated/dynamic reserve requirements linked to institutions’ credit growth, capital adequacy and broader policy considerations.
- It should not be retroactively called the later MPA framework, but it may be an important antecedent showing movement from uniform aggregate tools toward bank-specific constraints.
- Exact formula, scope and implementation timing require Evidence.

### 15. Tight credit conditions transmitted unevenly by bank type and borrower class
- **Novelty:** new_category
- Large banks, joint-stock banks, city/rural institutions and policy banks did not face identical deposit bases, capital constraints, reserve burdens or access to projects.
- SMEs/private firms may have experienced materially different availability/pricing from large SOE or government-linked borrowers even when aggregate credit growth looked adequate.
- This heterogeneity is a key counterexample to reading aggregate loan growth as universal easing.

### 16. Interbank/repo market rates carried marginal-liquidity information not visible in benchmark loan/deposit rates
- **Novelty:** new_category
- Shibor and repo-market conditions appear increasingly useful for observing short-term liquidity stress/easing, especially around reserve changes, seasonal/fiscal flows and policy operations.
- Do not equate Shibor fixing, repo rates and a central-bank policy rate; they are different price objects.

### 17. Open-market operations and central-bank bills changed role as the liquidity problem changed
- **Novelty:** useful_refinement
- Earlier external-surplus sterilization mechanisms remained relevant, but the mix among central-bank bills, repos/reverse repos, reserve requirements and other operations likely changed across crisis easing and later normalization.
- A future timeline should recover direction (injection vs withdrawal), maturity and counterparty mechanics rather than treating “OMO” as one homogeneous instrument.

### 18. Social Financing / “社会融资规模” emerged because bank loans no longer captured the whole financing pulse
- **Novelty:** new_category
- Memory places the policy introduction/standardization of a broader financing aggregate around this period, especially as entrusted loans, trust loans, bankers’ acceptances, corporate bonds and other channels mattered.
- The key lead is the **vintage of the concept and its component definitions**, not a backcast assumption that today’s TSF series and terminology existed unchanged from 2009.
- Initial usage may have emphasized flow financing to the real economy; later stock series/definition revisions must remain separate.

### 19. Off-balance-sheet and non-loan financing could substitute for constrained bank lending
- **Novelty:** new_category
- When loan quotas/pacing, capital, reserve or property controls tightened, borrowers and banks had incentives to use entrusted loans, trust channels, acceptances or other financing structures.
- This means a fall in reported bank-loan growth did not necessarily imply an equal fall in system-wide financing.
- Specific shadow structures belong to another shard, but the substitution mechanism is material to monetary transmission.

### 20. 2012 brought an easing turn plus early additional pricing flexibility rather than a return to 2009 conditions
- **Novelty:** new_category
- Memory associates 2012 growth concerns with reserve cuts and benchmark-rate easing, alongside steps that widened banks’ ability to price loans/deposits around benchmark rates.
- The easing was therefore occurring inside a gradually changing pricing regime, not simply repeating the 2009 emergency framework.
- Exact permitted bands and dates are Evidence work.

### 21. “预调微调” is a useful period-native cue for the late-period policy posture
- **Novelty:** duplicate
- The phrase is remembered as signaling pre-emptive/fine-tuning rather than an all-or-nothing stance switch.
- It reinforces the already recovered point that this slice contains multiple policy states and lags; treat it as a search key, not a new mechanism category.

## Negative space / counterexamples

- A huge increase in bank loans is **not** equivalent to an equally large increase in private-sector productive credit; local-government/infrastructure, property, SOE and bill channels can dominate composition.
- Tight benchmark-rate or reserve settings are **not** equivalent to uniformly tight financing if non-loan channels substitute.
- “Social financing” must be vintage-scoped; do not use a later revised TSF component set as if it were contemporaneously fixed in 2009.
- Window guidance and loan pacing are **not** the same institution as the earlier formal loan-scale regime.
- Shibor/repo rates are **not** a modern single policy rate.
- Later LGFV/property stress, shadow-banking regulation or MPA outcomes must not leak backward into what was knowable during 2009–2012.
- No material pre-1990 antecedent surfaced from this broad pass.
