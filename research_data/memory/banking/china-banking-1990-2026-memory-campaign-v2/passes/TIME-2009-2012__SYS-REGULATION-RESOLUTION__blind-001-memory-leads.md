## Broad regime and supervisory architecture

1. **[new_category][H] Post-stimulus supervisory reversal.** 2009 begins with policy-driven credit acceleration, while 2010-2012 increasingly looks like a supervisory effort to contain the balance-sheet consequences of that acceleration rather than a classic bank-failure-resolution cycle.
2. **[useful_refinement][H] Macro credit policy ≠ prudential correction.** PBOC/State Council credit-support direction and CBRC microprudential responses should be recorded as separate mechanisms even when they affect the same loan books.
3. **[new_category][H] LGFV loans become a named supervisory object.** “地方政府融资平台/平台贷款” appears in memory as a period-native category that regulators asked banks to identify, classify and remediate rather than simply treat as ordinary corporate credit.
4. **[useful_refinement][H] LGFV risk was partly a look-through problem.** A platform borrower’s legal form, local-government support, project cash flow, land-sale dependence and repayment source could diverge; supervisory work therefore involved borrower-by-borrower or project-by-project reclassification.
5. **[new_category][H] Property concentration becomes a parallel supervisory channel.** Developer loans, land-related exposures and mortgages should not be collapsed into the LGFV category even when both depend on land/property cycles.
6. **[useful_refinement][M] Credit concentration may have been addressed through window guidance, loan quotas, sector limits and differentiated prudential expectations rather than one single rule.
7. **[new_category][H] “Three Measures and One Guideline” is a remembered native search key.** Fixed-asset, working-capital and personal-loan management rules plus a project-financing guideline may mark the move toward use-of-proceeds controls and loan-process discipline around 2009-2010; exact titles/dates require verification.
8. **[useful_refinement][H] Entrusted payment / 受托支付 is a mechanism lead.** Direct payment to transaction counterparties was intended to reduce diversion of loan proceeds; it is supervision of credit process, not resolution.
9. **[useful_refinement][M] Post-loan management and “实贷实付” are likely period vocabulary.** Exact regulatory wording and scope should be source-checked rather than inferred.
10. **[duplicate][M] Ordinary prudential tightening should remain distinct from owner recapitalization and actual institution exit; this repeats the state-separation rule recovered in earlier map summaries.

## Capital, provisioning, liquidity and Basel-oriented preparation

11. **[new_category][H] A four-tool prudential framework is recalled for the early Basel III transition.** Capital adequacy, provisioning, leverage and liquidity may have been framed as a coordinated set of supervisory indicators; exact document and timing are uncertain.
12. **[useful_refinement][H] Capital requirements increasingly differentiated by bank/systemic status and risk.** Large banks may have faced stronger buffers or earlier targets than smaller banks, but exact ratios should not be guessed.
13. **[new_category][H] Dynamic provisioning / countercyclical provisioning is a period concept.** China’s regulator appears to have used provisioning as a macroprudential-style buffer before full Basel III implementation.
14. **[useful_refinement][H] Provision coverage ratio and provision-to-loan ratio are separate metrics.** Memory associates both with strengthened loan-loss-buffer requirements; exact thresholds such as 150% or 2.5% are plausible but source-dependent.
15. **[useful_refinement][H] Regulatory capital raising became a recurring operational consequence.** Listed banks used rights issues, placements, subordinated debt or other capital instruments to support rapid RWA growth; capital raising is not itself stress resolution.
16. **[new_category][M] Capital-quality migration matters.** The period may include movement from old subordinated-debt-heavy structures toward Basel-compatible Tier 1/Tier 2 concepts, but instrument eligibility rules need later archaeology.
17. **[new_category][H] 2012 commercial-bank capital rules are a likely transition marker.** A “资本管理办法（试行）” memory lead may represent China’s Basel III implementation preparation, with effective date possibly later; exact issuance/effective dates must be verified.
18. **[useful_refinement][M] Liquidity supervision before formal LCR/NSFR implementation likely relied on legacy ratios plus emerging Basel concepts; do not back-project later LCR requirements into 2009-2012 without evidence.
19. **[useful_refinement][M] Rapid loan growth raised denominator effects.** Even profitable banks could face capital pressure because risk-weighted assets expanded faster than retained earnings.
20. **[duplicate][M] Capital pressure, rights issues and state/shareholder capital injections do not imply license impairment or bank resolution.

## Regulatory perimeter: trust cooperation, WMP and off-balance-sheet credit

21. **[new_category][H] Bank-trust cooperation / 银信合作 is a central perimeter lead.** Banks may have used trust plans to place credit-like assets outside ordinary loan books, prompting regulator concern over disguised credit and capital/provisioning treatment.
22. **[new_category][H] “表内转表外” is a native search phrase.** The supervisory problem was not merely shadow-bank growth but migration of economically bank-originated credit outside loan quotas or prudential metrics.
23. **[useful_refinement][H] Credit-asset transfer can be true sale, financing arrangement or risk-retention structure.** Later verification must distinguish legal transfer from continuing bank risk.
24. **[new_category][H] WMP funding pools / 资金池 may emerge as a regulatory concern by this period.** The memory lead is maturity mismatch and pooled management, but exact onset and formal rule dates are uncertain.
25. **[useful_refinement][H] WMP ≠ trust cooperation.** A bank WMP can fund a trust plan, bond, loan-like claim or other asset; the product wrapper and ultimate asset need separate fields.
26. **[new_category][H] Non-standard credit assets were forming before the later formal “非标” regime.** The later term may not yet have had its mature regulatory definition, so period-native vocabulary should be preserved rather than retrofitted.
27. **[useful_refinement][H] Regulatory arbitrage may have shifted among channels as individual rules tightened.** Trust cooperation clampdowns could push activity toward entrusted loans, bankers’ acceptances, wealth products or other channels; this is a hypothesis lead, not a verified sequence.
28. **[new_category][M] Bank acceptance bills and trade-finance instruments may have become quasi-credit channels during loan-control periods.** Need later evidence before assigning materiality.
29. **[useful_refinement][M] Off-balance-sheet status is an accounting/regulatory classification question, not proof that the bank had no economic risk.
30. **[useful_refinement][H] Supervisory perimeter actions are not bank resolution.** A rule forcing assets back on balance sheet or requiring capital/provisions changes prudential recognition without changing bank operating/license/entity status.

## Resolution, stress intervention and negative-space leads

41. **[new_category][H] Scarcity of terminal licensed-bank failures remains a substantive finding.** I do not confidently recall a routine completed judicial bankruptcy or depositor-loss bank liquidation in 2009-2012 despite major credit expansion.
42. **[useful_refinement][H] Absence of remembered failure does not mean absence of distress.** Administrative support, shareholder recapitalization, mergers, local-government assistance, asset transfers or prolonged regulatory forbearance could mask stress without formal exit.
43. **[new_category][H] Merger/absorption needs an entity-state test.** A weak bank or rural institution absorbed into another entity can be a resolution-like event even if depositors are transferred whole and no public “failure” label is used.
44. **[useful_refinement][H] License status and claimant treatment remain the decisive resolution discriminants.** Temporary business restriction, management replacement or capital injection are insufficient to infer resolution.
45. **[new_category][M] Deposit-protection expectations were implicit rather than backed by the later explicit national deposit-insurance regime.** Exact policy/guarantee mechanisms in this period remain a major gap.
46. **[useful_refinement][H] The statutory action ladder—takeover, suspension, revocation, dissolution, liquidation, bankruptcy—remains unresolved and should not be reconstructed from memory.
47. **[new_category][M] Resolution-adjacent use of local-government or state-owner support is likely more important than open depositor haircut cases in this slice; institution examples still need evidence.
48. **[useful_refinement][M] A bank can remain open while exposures are reclassified, provisioned, rolled over or transferred; asset-quality repair is not entity resolution.
49. **[duplicate][M] Listing, conversion, recapitalization and routine supervisory remediation must not be counted as market exit; this deliberately repeats the boundary rule because it is central to this slice.
