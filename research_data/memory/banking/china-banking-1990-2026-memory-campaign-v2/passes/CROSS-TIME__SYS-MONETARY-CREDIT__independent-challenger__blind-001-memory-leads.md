## Falsification tests

### 1. Instrument change does not uniquely identify monetary-policy stance

A reserve-requirement move, relending facility, MLF-style funding operation, LPR move, window guidance or credit-quantity instruction is an **instrument or transmission input**, not automatically the final policy stance. The same nominal instrument direction can coexist with offsetting liquidity factors, targeted allocation rules, prudential constraints, FX/fiscal flows or a different operative target.

The compact map already distinguishes many instruments and plumbing channels, but it does not explicitly prove a cross-time ontology for the hierarchy between policy objectives, intermediate targets, operating targets/signals, instruments and realized credit outcomes.

### 2. The hierarchy itself changes across regimes

Across the primary horizon, model memory suggests that the practical importance of direct credit quantities, monetary aggregates, reserve/base-money concepts, money-market rates, central-bank facility rates, LPR-style loan-pricing references and structural-credit tools changed materially. These objects cannot be flattened into one generic `policy rate / credit target / easing-tightening` field.

A robust long-horizon map needs period-native labels for what was being targeted, what was merely observed, what was used as an instrument, and what was an ex-post transmission outcome. Exact transition dates and official formulations remain Evidence work.

### 3. Operating target versus intermediate target versus final objective is a separate semantic question

Potential hierarchy to preserve as research questions, not asserted facts:

`final objective / policy objective set`
→ `intermediate target or nominal/credit anchor`
→ `operating target or short-horizon signal variable`
→ `instrument / facility / administrative control`
→ `market and bank balance-sheet transmission`
→ `borrower / sector / aggregate-financing outcome`

Some historical regimes may not fit a clean textbook hierarchy and may use overlapping quantitative and price controls. That is a reason to preserve overlap and ambiguity, not to collapse the layers.

### 4. Counterexample: easing-looking instrument with non-easing aggregate transmission

A targeted funding tool can ease one borrower or bank class while aggregate conditions remain restrained. A reserve-ratio cut can offset autonomous liquidity drain rather than represent a one-for-one easing in net stance. A benchmark or reference-rate cut can have weak pass-through when deposit costs, capital/risk constraints, borrower demand or administrative controls bind. Conversely, quantity guidance can tighten credit allocation even if money-market liquidity is ample.

Without the hierarchy above, these cases can be misclassified as contradictions rather than multi-layer transmission.

### 5. Communication and expectation need the same hierarchy

Period-native stance labels and central-bank communication may refer to broad objectives, liquidity conditions, credit structure, price signals or risk control at different layers. A future Evidence phase must be able to attach a statement to the layer it actually addresses instead of translating every phrase into a single retrospective `hawkish/dovish` axis.
