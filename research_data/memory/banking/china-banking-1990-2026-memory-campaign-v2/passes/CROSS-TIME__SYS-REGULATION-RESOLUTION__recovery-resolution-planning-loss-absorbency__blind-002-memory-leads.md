## Orthogonal state challenge

1. **[useful_refinement | high] Designation state.** Systemic-importance designation is a preparedness/perimeter fact. It should not be promoted to stress, non-viability or resolution.
2. **[useful_refinement | high] Plan-existence state.** A recovery or resolution plan can exist in normal times. Existence, filing, review or approval of a plan is not an intervention event.
3. **[useful_refinement | high] Recovery-trigger state.** A bank may cross internal or supervisory recovery indicators and begin management actions while remaining a going concern. Exact domestic triggers are source-dependent.
4. **[useful_refinement | high] Recovery-action state.** Capital raising, asset sales, deleveraging, liability management or contingency funding undertaken under a recovery plan remain distinct from authority-led resolution.
5. **[useful_refinement | high] Resolution-entry state.** A separate factual/legal threshold is required before calling an institution “in resolution.” Planning for that state does not establish that it has been reached.
6. **[useful_refinement | high] Power-exercise state.** Even after a resolution framework becomes relevant, takeover, transfer, write-down, conversion, bridge/successor use or other powers require event-specific evidence. The legal availability of a power is not its exercise.
7. **[useful_refinement | high] Loss-realization state.** Eligible loss-absorbing capacity does not equal realized creditor loss. Loss requires an actual write-down, conversion, haircut, transfer discount borne by a claimant or other evidenced economic impairment.
8. **[useful_refinement | high] Claimant-settlement state.** Final depositor/creditor/fund outcomes can occur after intervention and are not inferable merely from plan design or capital structure.
9. **[useful_refinement | high] TLAC issuance versus TLAC impairment.** Issuance, eligibility and stock of TLAC-like instruments are capacity facts; impairment/conversion is a separate event fact.
10. **[useful_refinement | high] Contractual non-viability features versus statutory resolution powers.** Contract terms may permit or require loss absorption under defined conditions, while statutory authorities may have separate powers. One cannot be inferred from the other without sources.
11. **[useful_refinement] Going-concern buffer breach versus resolution entry.** A capital or buffer shortfall can trigger remediation or recovery actions without proving legal resolution.
12. **[useful_refinement] Resolvability finding versus failure finding.** A bank may have resolvability impediments in normal times; “hard to resolve” is not “failing.”
13. **[useful_refinement] Resolvability remediation versus prudential remediation.** Legal-structure simplification, service continuity, data mapping or loss-absorbency placement can improve resolvability even when ordinary capital/liquidity remediation is a separate workstream.
14. **[useful_refinement | high] Critical-function continuity versus original-entity survival.** A plan can aim to keep deposits/payments/credit functions running while ownership, legal entity or parts of the balance sheet change.
15. **[useful_refinement] Planned transfer option versus executed transfer.** Identifying a successor, bridge-style option, asset/liability transfer path or sale strategy in a plan does not establish that a transfer occurred.
16. **[useful_refinement | high] Planned public/fund support versus actual draw.** A plan can model deposit-insurance, stability-fund, central-bank or fiscal options without proving any public money was used.
17. **[useful_refinement] Gross capacity versus net public cost.** Even if a facility is available or drawn, ultimate public/fund cost still depends on recoveries and loss allocation.
18. **[useful_refinement] Cross-border coordination versus domestic resolution event.** Crisis-management groups, colleges or home-host coordination may exist for large groups; their existence is preparedness context, not proof of a domestic intervention.
19. **[useful_refinement] Comparative “bail-in” language versus native PRC implementation.** Bail-in is useful conceptual vocabulary for creditor loss absorption, but internal memory is insufficient to infer a specific PRC statutory bail-in event or hierarchy from the term alone.
20. **[duplicate] Deposit-insurance membership and payout categories.** Already separated as safety-net/claimant layers.
21. **[duplicate] PBOC liquidity versus solvency capital.** Already separated in the funding/backstop stack.
22. **[duplicate] Shareholder recapitalization versus legal resolution.** Already covered.
23. **[duplicate] AMC/bad-asset transfer financing versus claimant transfer.** Already covered.
24. **[duplicate] Shareholders, capital instruments, wholesale claims and deposits as separate loss-bearing classes.** Already covered.
25. **[duplicate] Supervisory takeover versus license revocation/liquidation.** Already covered realized-state distinctions.
26. **[duplicate] Policy merger versus distress resolution.** Already covered consolidation motive/state separation.
27. **[duplicate] Exact ratios, rule titles, trigger wording and named-bank issuance or plan facts.** These remain Evidence work.

## Challenge result

The ex-ante preparedness gap is now broad plus orthogonally challenged. The useful state ladder is:

`systemic-importance / plan existence → recovery trigger → recovery action → resolution-entry fact → power exercise → realized loss → claimant settlement`.

Each transition requires its own evidence. No new top-level preparedness category was recovered beyond the broad pass; the main value is classifier stabilization and prevention of false promotion from capacity into event/outcome. Returns are still materially refining rather than low-novelty because the activation-state ladder changes how the newly reopened category must be represented.
