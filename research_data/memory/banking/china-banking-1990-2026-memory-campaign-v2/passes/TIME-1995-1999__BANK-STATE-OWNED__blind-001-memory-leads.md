## Broad blind-memory leads

1. **[new_category | high] Mid-1990s legal/institutional reform marks a transition from `国有专业银行` toward `国有商业银行`.** A remembered Commercial Bank Law milestone belongs in this slice, but exact articles/effective dates are Evidence work. The key lead is the new legal expectation of commercial operation, autonomy and risk responsibility while state ownership remained.

2. **[new_category | high] `一级法人` / unified legal-person governance is a major operating-model transition.** Head offices increasingly sought to centralize credit authority, risk standards, capital/fund allocation and branch accountability after years of local branch autonomy and local-government influence.

3. **[new_category | high] Branch credit authorization likely became more hierarchical and limit-based.** Large exposures and weak borrowers increasingly required higher-level approval; a useful later task is to reconstruct head-office/provincial/city branch authority tiers by bank and year rather than assume one uniform nationwide model.

4. **[new_category | high] SOE reform and state-bank reform were tightly coupled.** State banks carried working-capital and legacy loans to SOEs undergoing restructuring, mergers, layoffs or closure; improving bank discipline could conflict with employment/social-stability and enterprise-continuity objectives.

5. **[new_category | high] The Asian financial crisis reinforced a policy preference for domestic financial stability and exposed the danger of weak bank balance sheets, even though mainland state banks were not organized like the crisis-hit foreign systems.** External-crisis lessons likely accelerated prudential, NPL and recapitalization attention.

6. **[new_category | high] Domestic disinflation/deflation and weak enterprise profitability created a different credit problem from early-1990s overheating.** Credit demand quality, SOE cash flow and bank willingness to lend could weaken even as authorities wanted banks to support growth.

7. **[new_category | high] State-bank NPLs became an explicit systemic reform object rather than only scattered overdue/bad-loan bookkeeping.** The central research question is how recognition, classification and cleanup regimes changed the visible stock of problem loans without confusing recognition with new economic deterioration.

8. **[new_category | high] Transition toward a five-category loan-classification framework is a major metric-semantic breakpoint.** `正常/关注/次级/可疑/损失`-type categories are strongly remembered, but rollout timing and institution coverage require Evidence. Old `一逾两呆` and later five-category NPL ratios are not directly comparable.

9. **[new_category | high] Provisioning and interest-accrual rules likely tightened alongside classification reform.** Profit, nonperforming assets and recoverability can shift mechanically when recognition rules change, so later time-series work must preserve accounting-vintage effects.

10. **[new_category | high] A late-1990s capital injection via a special sovereign/government bond is a strong reform anchor for the four large state banks.** The precise amount, mechanics and allocation by bank must be sourced later; blind memory only supports a recapitalization-before-bad-asset-transfer sequence lead.

11. **[new_category | high] Four asset-management companies are a strong 1999-era cleanup anchor, with remembered one-to-one primary pairings roughly Cinda–CCB, Huarong–ICBC, Great Wall–ABC and Orient–BOC.** Exact legal transfer terms, face values, pricing and which assets qualified are deferred Evidence questions.

12. **[new_category | high] AMC transfers should not be interpreted as ordinary market sales at economic fair value.** They were part of a state-led balance-sheet repair architecture involving banks, AMCs and public-sector funding/claims; later research must separate accounting derecognition, economic loss allocation and ultimate recovery.

13. **[useful_refinement | high] Policy-bank separation from the prior slice continued to matter because commercial state banks still retained residual policy burdens and legacy assets.** The late-1990s cleanup may partly reflect the lag between formal mandate separation and actual commercial balance-sheet transformation.

14. **[new_category | high] Capital adequacy and asset-liability ratio management became more meaningful management constraints, but this was still a transition regime rather than fully modern Basel-style bank optimization.** Regulatory ratios, credit plans/guidance and state objectives could coexist.

15. **[new_category | medium] Branch/network rationalization is a likely reform lead.** Large state banks may have begun closing or consolidating inefficient outlets, reducing staffing and tightening organizational layers; exact scale/timing should not be guessed and likely varies materially by bank.

16. **[new_category | high] Internal control and authorization reform should be treated separately from legal commercial-bank status.** A bank could legally be a commercial bank while internal lending discipline, audit, collateral valuation, connected/local-government pressure and post-loan monitoring remained uneven.

17. **[new_category | medium] Deposit franchises and payment/settlement networks remained a core strength of the large state banks during cleanup.** Balance-sheet repair did not erase their nationwide household/enterprise account bases; franchise strength and asset weakness could coexist.

18. **[new_category | high] Bank-specific reform burdens were heterogeneous.** ICBC's large industrial/urban corporate book, ABC's rural/agricultural and RCC-adjacent environment, CCB's infrastructure/fixed-investment inheritance and BOC's FX/trade/overseas business imply different NPL origins and cleanup mechanics.

19. **[new_category | high] BOC's external/FX profile is a counterexample to treating the 1997 Asian crisis as only a domestic SOE-credit story.** Foreign-currency assets/liabilities, trade clients and overseas branches may have created distinct risk, liquidity and valuation issues even while the state cleanup framework was shared.

20. **[new_category | medium] `惜贷`/credit conservatism near the late 1990s may have a state-bank governance dimension as well as a monetary-policy dimension.** Stronger risk accountability after years of soft lending can make branch managers reluctant to originate new loans, especially to weak private/SOE borrowers, even when aggregate policy is supportive.

21. **[new_category | high] Bad-asset transfer creates a vintage break in bank performance metrics.** Post-transfer NPL ratios, profit and capital measures should not be compared to pre-cleanup figures without reconstructing denominator changes, transferred portfolios and accounting treatment.

22. **[useful_refinement | medium] The commercial-bank transition should be represented as a sequence of legal mandate → governance/authorization reform → recognition of legacy losses → recapitalization → asset transfer, not as one reform date.** Different elements overlap and may occur in different order at each bank.

23. **[duplicate | medium] The generic statement that policy and commercial functions remained partly mixed adds no new category by itself; its incremental value is in the specific legacy-asset, residual-obligation and cleanup mechanisms above.**

## Temporal traps / counterexamples

- Do not backcast later joint-stock restructuring, strategic investors, IPO/listing governance or post-WTO competition reforms into 1995–1999.
- A falling reported NPL ratio after cleanup is not automatically equivalent to organic credit-quality improvement.
- AMC asset transfer is not automatically a market-price sale or final loss realization.
- Five-category classification rollout can increase reported NPL recognition even if underlying borrower economics did not suddenly worsen.
- Commercial-bank legal status does not prove branch-level commercial incentives and controls were already mature.
