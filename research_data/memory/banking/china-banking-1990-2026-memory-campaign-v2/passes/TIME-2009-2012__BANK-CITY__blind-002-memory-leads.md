## Independent challenge result

The challenge does **not** elevate fraud/case risk into a second period-wide structural category. It is materially important negative space, but the remembered cases appear institution-specific and heterogeneous rather than a broad 2009-2012 city-bank business-model regime. Qilu-type case risk, related-party opacity and later-problem-bank antecedents fit the wider question of whether rapid balance-sheet/geographic growth outpaced governance, branch controls and group-exposure visibility. The period-wide structural transformation remains the stimulus/platform/property/refinancing/funding nexus, with operational cases serving as high-value counterexamples and control tests.

## New-category leads from the orthogonal challenge

N01 [HIGH] Platform-loan quality should be reconstructed by **repayment source hierarchy**: operating/project cash flow, fiscal budget support, land-related revenue, asset disposal and refinancing are economically different even if all sit under “platform” labels.

N02 [HIGH] A platform can appear solvent because refinancing is available; debt-service capacity from new borrowing should not be confused with project cash-flow coverage.

N03 Tentative supervisory vocabulary around “cash-flow full/basic/partial/no coverage” is analytically valuable even if exact category names require Evidence.

N04 [HIGH] Platform “exit” or reclassification from a supervisory list can change reported platform-loan balances without necessarily changing underlying local-government dependence.

N05 Search-key lead: `平台退出`, `名单制`, `分类处置`, `现金流覆盖`, `整改为一般公司类贷款` or similar phrases may reveal classification migration; exact wording is uncertain.

N06 [HIGH] Refinancing risk is a maturity-structure problem: long-lived infrastructure funded by shorter bank loans can require serial renewal even when ultimate projects are socially useful.

N07 `借新还旧`, `展期`, `续贷`, maturity adjustment and restructuring should be tested separately because they have different accounting/risk-recognition implications.

N08 [HIGH] A loan that remains “normal” after renewal can still contain economic weakness; vintage age should be measured from original project exposure, not only the most recent loan contract.

N09 Platform consolidation/merger or borrower renaming can interrupt historical time series while preserving economic exposure.

N10 [HIGH] City-bank local-government concentration can be hidden across platform companies, local SOEs, land-reserve entities and public-service borrowers rather than a single reported “platform” line.

N11 A group-level municipal-exposure map may therefore be more informative than regulatory borrower categories.

N12 [HIGH] Land finance ties multiple nominal sectors together: platform debt service, developer land purchases, collateral values, local fiscal revenue and infrastructure demand.

N13 Property tightening can weaken both developer cash flow and local-government land-sale capacity, making platform and property stress correlated.

N14 [HIGH] Local fiscal/public-sector deposits can soften liquidity stress precisely where asset exposure to the same municipality is high, creating asset-liability **wrong-way dependence** if fiscal conditions worsen.

N15 A city bank may look well-funded because of public deposits while its asset quality also depends on the public depositor’s fiscal health.

N16 [HIGH] Cross-region expansion transforms municipal concentration rather than eliminates it: the bank can accumulate exposures to several local governments and property cycles.

N17 If branch credit strategy is standardized, “different cities” can still create correlated infrastructure/property portfolios.

N18 [HIGH] WMP/trust structures need a four-part decomposition: customer liability promise, underlying asset, guarantee/recourse expectation and balance-sheet/accounting location.

N19 A product called `理财` can range from low-risk liquid investment to credit-linked trust exposure; product name alone is not a shadow-credit classification.

N20 [HIGH] `银信合作` can move credit risk outside ordinary loan lines while retaining reputational or implicit-support risk for the bank.

N21 Trust-company credit review may rely partly on the originating bank’s borrower relationships, so legal risk transfer may not equal economic independence.

N22 [HIGH] Bank guarantees, repurchase commitments, liquidity support or informal expectations are key Evidence questions when judging whether an off-balance-sheet structure truly transferred risk.

N23 `资金池` is a useful search key but should not be assumed universal or identical across city banks in this period.

N24 [HIGH] The later standardized label `非标` should be used cautiously: credit-linked trust/WMP assets can be economically “non-standard-like” before the mature regulatory taxonomy crystallizes.

N25 The period should be framed as a bridge toward later non-standard/interbank nesting, not as if the entire later architecture already existed.

N26 [HIGH] Negotiable certificates of deposit should remain excluded from 2009-2012 city-bank funding unless Evidence shows an exceptional instrument; mature interbank-CD funding is later.

N27 Period wholesale funding should focus on interbank deposits/borrowings, repos, bills, placements and other bank-counterparty liabilities.

N28 [HIGH] A bank can grow interbank assets and liabilities simultaneously, producing balance-sheet enlargement and liquidity transformation that net positions conceal.

N29 Repo maturity and collateral rules can create short-term rollover exposure distinct from traditional deposit funding.

N30 [HIGH] Bill business can serve as corporate credit, liquidity asset, interbank tradable exposure and policy-quota management mechanism; accounting location can shift across institutions.

N31 Bill and acceptance exposures also introduce document/fraud/operational risks, connecting the main funding/credit mechanism with case-risk controls.

N32 [HIGH] WMP customer migration can increase the apparent cost of conventional deposits even before WMP assets become deeply complex.

N33 Deposit “stability” should be tested after subtracting public-sector relationship deposits and money temporarily moved into/out of WMPs.

N34 [HIGH] Loan-to-deposit constraints may increase incentive for point-in-time liability management; later “冲时点” vocabulary may have antecedents here, but exact period prevalence requires Evidence.

N35 Fiscal deposits, corporate settlement deposits, retail deposits and interbank liabilities remain four distinct funding franchises.

N36 [HIGH] Low NPL ratios can coexist with rising **risk stock** when loan growth is fast and refinancing postpones recognition.

N37 The best orthogonal asset-quality measures would include overdue loans, special-mention loans, migration, restructuring, write-offs and new-vintage performance rather than aggregate NPL alone.

N38 [HIGH] Provision coverage can be high because the recognized NPL denominator is unusually low; reserves-to-total-loans or loan-loss reserve ratio provides a different lens.

N39 Remembered `拨贷比` / loan-loss reserves relative to loans becomes relevant around this regulatory period; exact thresholds/timing require Evidence.

N40 [HIGH] Capital pressure should include credit-risk RWA growth plus off-balance-sheet commitments and operational-risk requirements; exact rule transitions are Evidence questions.

N41 Rapid growth after a recapitalization can consume the apparent safety buffer faster than static ratios suggest.

N42 [HIGH] SME/private-enterprise stress around tighter conditions can expose **loan diversion**: bank working-capital loans may finance property, private lending or affiliates rather than stated business use.

N43 Private-lending stress can therefore contaminate bank asset quality even without direct bank participation in informal lending markets.

N44 [HIGH] Guarantee chains can propagate stress from one borrower’s private-lending or property losses into otherwise healthy guarantors.

N45 Wenzhou-like episodes are valuable stress laboratories but should remain regional counterexamples, not a national city-bank generalization.

N46 [HIGH] Qilu-type case risk is a reminder that deposit, bill, guarantee, seal, confirmation and authorization controls can cause losses or confidence shocks independent of borrower credit quality.

N47 Potential control points: official seals/chops, account confirmation, large-value payment authorization, bill authenticity, guarantee documentation, counterparty verification and branch exception reporting.

N48 [HIGH] External collusion with insiders can bypass formally centralized systems if branch-level documentation and authorization controls are weak.

N49 A case can also create liquidity/reputation stress if corporate depositors question account safety, even when credit NPL metrics remain unchanged.

N50 [HIGH] The case-risk mechanism is therefore highly material for governance research, but one or a few episodes do not establish a separate period-wide city-bank balance-sheet model.

N51 The correct classification is **orthogonal control failure / negative-space category**, unless later Evidence reveals widespread parallel cases across many city banks.

N52 [HIGH] Related-party lending is broader than classic operational fraud: owners can direct credit toward affiliates through formally valid loans and guarantees.

N53 Beneficial ownership and common-control mapping are necessary to detect related-party concentration in banks with private/corporate shareholders.

N54 [HIGH] Later-problem-bank cases such as Baoshang should be used only as backward research prompts: ask what ownership concentration, related lending, branch growth and risk controls looked like then; do not import later failure findings as contemporaneous fact.

N55 The same caution applies to other later troubled regional/city banks: later outcomes are hypothesis generators, not evidence for 2009-2012 state.

N56 [HIGH] Cross-region branch networks can facilitate affiliate borrowing in multiple locations if group customer identifiers and beneficial-ownership data are incomplete.

N57 Centralization of IT is necessary but insufficient if borrower groups deliberately fragment legal entities or use cross-guarantees.

N58 [HIGH] Governance should therefore be decomposed into ownership control, board/management oversight, branch operating controls and data/group-exposure visibility.

N59 Qilu-type operational cases and Baoshang-type later related-party concerns occupy different subdimensions; bundling them as one “fraud” category loses mechanism clarity.

N60 [HIGH] Weak-city-bank resolution can occur quietly through shareholder replacement, municipal recapitalization, bad-asset transfer and governance overhaul rather than closure.

N61 A bank that continues operating after repeated support may embody hidden resolution history; institutional continuity does not imply balance-sheet continuity.

N62 [HIGH] Regional heterogeneity remains critical: strong fiscal cities can refinance platform risk longer; export/SME regions can show private-enterprise stress sooner; property-heavy cities can transmit via land values.

N63 Cross-region banks may have both buffers and vulnerabilities because one region’s fiscal strength can offset another’s stress while central liquidity management becomes more complex.

N64 [HIGH] The period-wide common denominator is **growth outrunning risk visibility**: platform borrower complexity, off-balance-sheet structures, cross-region branches, guarantee networks and related parties all make aggregate exposure harder to see.

N65 The control response—centralized risk aggregation, stricter classification, platform cleanup, internal audit and capital/provision buffers—belongs to the same structural transformation.

N66 [HIGH] No independent second business-model category emerges from the orthogonal recall; case/operational risks are retained as mandatory negative-space leads inside the completed slice.

## Useful refinements

R01 Platform exposure should be measured before and after borrower reclassification to avoid false deleveraging conclusions.

R02 Platform repayment-source analysis should distinguish project cash flow, fiscal support, land revenue and refinancing.

R03 A borrower exiting a supervisory platform list does not necessarily lose economic local-government dependence.

R04 Original-vintage dating is better than renewal-contract dating for detecting evergreening.

R05 Platform loans and local-SOE loans may share the same fiscal/land repayment factor even if classifications differ.

R06 Asset-liability municipal dependence can create wrong-way risk when fiscal deposits and platform assets belong to the same locality.

R07 Multi-city expansion can diversify idiosyncratic risk but increase common property/infrastructure-factor exposure.

R08 WMP analysis must identify underlying assets and support/recourse, not just product volumes.

R09 Guaranteed and non-guaranteed/reputationally supported wealth products should not be merged analytically.

R10 Trust legal form does not establish economic risk transfer if bank-originated credit and implicit support persist.

R11 Mature `非标` taxonomy and later interbank nesting should not be back-cast wholesale into this slice.

R12 Mature NCD funding belongs later; 2009-2012 interbank funding should be reconstructed from period instruments.

R13 Gross interbank assets/liabilities reveal liquidity transformation better than net positions.

R14 Bill finance needs both credit and operational/fraud lenses.

R15 WMP/deposit migration can make conventional deposit-cost metrics structurally less comparable.

R16 Loan-to-deposit pressure may affect quarter-end funding behavior even if exact “冲时点” terminology belongs later.

R17 Aggregate NPL ratios should be paired with overdue/special-mention/restructured data.

R18 Provision coverage and reserves-to-total-loans answer different questions.

R19 Capital adequacy should be evaluated against forward asset growth and contingent/off-balance-sheet commitments.

R20 Loan diversion is an important bridge between formal bank credit and private-lending/property risk.

R21 Guarantee-network mapping should include private-lending exposure of guarantors, not only bank-loan borrowers.

R22 Regional private-lending stress is a counterexample, not a universal city-bank state.

R23 Operational case risk requires transaction-control evidence, not credit-file evidence.

R24 Related-party lending requires beneficial-ownership mapping, not only disclosed shareholder relationships.

R25 Later bank failures should remain Outcome prompts separated from contemporaneous Judgment/Reality.

R26 Quiet recapitalization/shareholder replacement can be a resolution event without legal closure.

R27 The period’s structural unit is risk visibility/control capacity relative to growth complexity.

R28 Case-risk negative space should remain explicitly in future saturation reviews even though it does not reopen this slice.

## Specific negative-space judgment: case risk

- Qilu Bank remains a high-value forgotten-actor lead for later Evidence.
- The remembered episode is insufficient by itself to claim a broad city-bank case-risk regime.
- Operational fraud, related-party lending and later problem-bank antecedents are different mechanisms and should remain separately coded.
- Their common connection to the period is that rapid growth, branch proliferation and complex counterparties can outpace centralized control and exposure aggregation.
- Result: **important orthogonal governance/control dimension, not second material structural category**.

## Counterexamples

- Platform loans can be refinanced successfully for years without proving underlying cash-flow sufficiency.
- A declining reported platform balance can coexist with unchanged economic local-government exposure after reclassification.
- A deposit-heavy bank can rely on wholesale funding at the margin.
- High provision coverage can coexist with hidden risk when recognized NPLs are low.
- Cross-region diversification can increase common-factor exposure.
- An operational fraud case can be severe without representing the dominant sector-wide risk regime.
- A bank can avoid formal failure while undergoing substantive resolution via recapitalization or ownership change.
