## Broad recall leads

1. **Low inflation / weak nominal-demand regime raises the real-rate question.** The period appears qualitatively different from earlier easing cycles because falling nominal loan rates could coexist with weak price growth, so the effective real financing burden may not fall proportionally. Research should separate nominal policy-rate cuts, loan-rate declines, inflation expectations and borrower cash-flow stress. `new_category`, high importance, medium confidence.

2. **Policy stance increasingly discussed as both price easing and quantity/liquidity support.** A single label such as “rate cuts” is too coarse: reserve-ratio changes, OMO liquidity, medium-term funding, structural relending and loan-pricing guidance can move in different combinations. `useful_refinement`, high importance, high confidence.

3. **Seven-day open-market reverse-repo rate may have become a more explicit policy-rate signal than the older MLF-centric shorthand.** Memory suggests an operating-framework communication shift in which the short OMO rate gained primacy while MLF’s signaling role was reduced or redefined. Exact transition timing and wording require later Evidence. `new_category`, high importance, medium-high confidence.

4. **MLF-to-LPR anchoring should not be assumed mechanically in the late period.** Even if MLF remained a funding instrument, LPR quotation behavior and policy communication may have increasingly referenced broader policy-rate signals and bank funding costs rather than a simple one-link formula. `new_category`, high importance, medium confidence.

5. **RRR cuts should be decomposed into gross reserve release, replacement of maturing central-bank funding, fiscal/seasonal absorption and net durable liquidity.** The same gross RRR move can have different balance-sheet meaning depending on what is simultaneously maturing or being absorbed. `useful_refinement`, high importance, high confidence.

6. **Large government-bond issuance may have become a first-order liquidity driver rather than merely a fiscal background variable.** Treasury and local-government bond issuance can move fiscal deposits, bank reserve demand, bond inventories and TSF composition; central-bank liquidity operations may partly smooth these flows without financing fiscal deficits in a QE sense. `new_category`, high importance, medium-high confidence.

7. **Central-bank government-bond trading is a separate operational state from Western-style QE.** Memory suggests PBOC secondary-market government-bond transactions became an explicit operating-tool discussion or practice. Research must distinguish liquidity management/yield-curve signaling from monetized deficit financing and avoid importing foreign labels. `new_category`, high importance, medium confidence.

8. **Outright/buyout reverse-repo operations may represent a new medium-tenor liquidity tool.** Period-native search vocabulary likely includes `买断式逆回购`; the analytical question is how its maturity, collateral/ownership treatment and replacement relation differ from ordinary pledged reverse repos and MLF. Exact mechanics are Evidence work. `new_category`, high importance, medium confidence.

9. **Temporary repo/reverse-repo corridor tools may matter for operating-framework interpretation.** Memory suggests temporary OMO instruments around the policy-rate corridor were introduced or emphasized to contain money-market deviations. Research should separate standing/corridor logic from routine liquidity injection. `new_category`, medium-high importance, medium confidence.

10. **DR007 and other repo rates remain better market-plumbing indicators than a generic “interbank rate.”** Overnight and seven-day repo benchmarks, exchange/interbank segmentation, collateral quality and tax/holiday effects can diverge. The policy question is whether market rates are clustering around a more explicit short policy rate. `useful_refinement`, high importance, high confidence.

11. **Deposit-rate repricing became an important transmission channel because asset yields were falling faster than or ahead of liabilities.** Bank NIM pressure can induce coordinated/self-discipline-guided deposit-rate reductions, repricing of time deposits and competition through non-rate features. The research unit should separate posted ceilings/guidance from realized deposit beta. `new_category`, high importance, high confidence.

12. **Deposit termization can weaken immediate funding-cost relief.** Even if newly quoted deposit rates decline, a stock of previously locked higher-cost term deposits can keep average liability cost elevated until rollover. This creates a lag between policy/loan repricing and NIM relief. `new_category`, high importance, high confidence.

13. **Loan-rate competition and weak credit demand can create “price down, quantity weak” transmission.** Lower quoted corporate or mortgage rates do not imply strong loan creation when borrowers are deleveraging or investment/property demand is weak. `useful_refinement`, high importance, high confidence.

14. **Existing-mortgage repricing became a distinct policy channel from new-mortgage pricing.** Memory suggests multiple rounds or mechanisms aimed at reducing the gap between legacy mortgage coupons and new-loan rates. Research must separate contractual benchmark reset, margin/add-point adjustment, refinancing/transfer possibilities and administrative negotiation; exact rules are deferred. `new_category`, high importance, medium-high confidence.

15. **Mortgage floors, city-level restrictions and LPR are different state variables.** A fall in longer-tenor LPR does not mechanically determine household mortgage pricing if local/national floors, add-points, borrower status or city policy also change. `useful_refinement`, high importance, high confidence.

16. **Developer/project financing and household mortgage demand should remain separate property-credit channels.** Property support may include project-completion or whitelist-style financing, developer refinancing, affordable housing acquisition finance and household mortgage easing, each with different bank risk and transmission. `new_category`, high importance, medium-high confidence.

17. **Affordable-housing / inventory-acquisition relending is a candidate structural tool.** Memory suggests central-bank support for lending related to purchasing existing housing stock or affordable housing. The key research distinction is central-bank funding support versus local/state purchaser balance-sheet risk and commercial-bank underwriting risk. `new_category`, high importance, medium confidence.

18. **PSL may have reappeared as a policy-financing channel for selected urban/property/public projects.** The analytical boundary is policy-bank credit supported by central-bank pledged funding versus ordinary commercial-bank monetary transmission. Exact restart timing and project scope require Evidence. `new_category`, high importance, medium confidence.

19. **LGFV/local-debt refinancing changes bank credit composition even when it is primarily fiscal/debt-management policy.** Special refinancing bonds, debt swaps or other replacement programs can convert opaque/local high-cost exposures into government-bond assets or alter loan repayment/refinancing behavior, affecting bank duration, yield and risk without being ordinary monetary easing. `new_category`, high importance, medium-high confidence.

20. **Government-bond-heavy TSF can make aggregate social-financing growth look stronger than private credit demand.** The period may require explicit decomposition of RMB loans, government bonds, corporate bonds, trust/entrusted financing and other components rather than reading TSF growth as one credit impulse. `new_category`, high importance, high confidence.

21. **“Credit idling” / fund-circulation concerns are a candidate counterexample to aggregate loan quantity as effective demand.** Memory suggests policy discussion about funds circulating within the financial system, loan-deposit arbitrage or weak transmission into productive investment. 24. **FX pressure can constrain or alter the composition of domestic easing without mechanically preventing it.** Interest-rate differentials, capital-flow expectations and RMB fixing/intervention tools may interact with rate cuts and liquidity easing. Research should avoid the false binary “FX pressure means no easing.” `useful_refinement`, high importance, high confidence.

25. **Bank heterogeneity remains material: large banks, joint-stock banks and smaller regional banks can face different deposit beta, wholesale funding, bond-book duration and loan-demand mixes.** A system-average policy move can therefore transmit unevenly. `useful_refinement`, high importance, high confidence.

## Counterexamples / negative-space reminders

- Lower LPR or loan quotes do **not** prove stronger credit demand or easier borrower cash flow.
- RRR release does **not** equal net liquidity if central-bank facilities mature or fiscal deposits absorb reserves.
- Government-bond purchases/trading do **not** automatically equal QE or deficit monetization.
- Local-debt swaps are not ordinary new credit creation; they may reclassify creditor, maturity and yield instead.
- Existing-mortgage repricing is not identical to new-mortgage floor changes.
- Structural relending is not direct central-bank lending to final borrowers and does not automatically transfer bank credit risk.
- Policy-bank lending is not interchangeable with commercial-bank loan supply.
- Aggregate TSF can rise because government bonds rise while private loan demand remains weak.
- Deposit-rate cuts do not immediately lower average deposit cost when term deposits reprice slowly.
- FX pressure and domestic easing can coexist; the interaction must be reconstructed rather than assumed.
