## Atomic recall ledger

### New-category leads

1. **[NEW] Bank legal entity versus banking group is a first-order historical distinction.** A veteran comparison of “ICBC/CCB/BOC/ABC/BoCom/PSBC” can silently move between the licensed commercial-bank balance sheet, consolidated group and parent-controlled financial subsidiaries. Metrics may therefore be non-comparable even when labels look stable.
2. **[NEW] Offshore investment-banking subsidiaries/platforms are a durable large-bank perimeter.** BOC’s offshore investment-banking lineage is especially salient in memory, with other large banks later operating Hong Kong/international securities or investment-banking platforms. Exact entity names/dates require Evidence.
3. **[NEW] The offshore investment-banking route may partly reflect domestic separation-of-banking-and-securities constraints.** Universal-banking ambitions could be expressed through geographically or legally separated subsidiaries rather than through the domestic bank itself.
4. **[NEW] Financial-leasing subsidiaries became a distinct balance-sheet/business-model extension for several large banks.** Leasing introduces asset residual-value, equipment-sector, duration and funding risks that do not map cleanly to ordinary corporate loans.
5. **[NEW] Bank-affiliated fund-management companies/JVs create another perimeter layer.** They matter for AUM, distribution, product manufacturing versus distribution, minority-partner governance and fee attribution; ownership forms may differ by bank and period.
6. **[NEW] Insurance ownership/affiliation is another possible large-bank group-perimeter channel.** The economic question is not simply “does the bank sell insurance?” but whether insurance manufacturing sits inside the consolidated group, in an affiliate/JV, or only as third-party distribution.
7. **[NEW] Post-deleveraging wealth-management subsidiaries/legal-person entities materially changed the perimeter of wealth business.** The product relationship moved from an on-bank/off-balance activity toward a separately governed asset-management entity, while customer acquisition and distribution often remained bank-linked.
8. **[NEW] Wealth-subsidiary creation changes the meaning of bank AUM and fee-income time series.** Product assets, management fees, distribution fees and valuation effects can sit in different legal entities or accounting lines across periods.
9. **[NEW] Wealth-subsidiary separation also changes risk-ownership semantics.** “Implicit guarantee broken” and “net-value transformation” should be tested separately from whether customers still expect reputational support from the parent bank.
10. **[NEW] Financial-asset-investment / debt-to-equity-swap subsidiaries are a distinct post-NPL-workout perimeter.** Large banks established specialized investment entities around the market-oriented debt-to-equity-swap era; exact naming/timing and consolidation treatment require verification.
11. **[NEW] Those asset-investment entities convert a credit-workout problem into an equity/investment-risk problem.** That changes valuation, exit horizon, capital consumption and the semantics of “NPL resolution” without necessarily eliminating economic exposure.
12. **[NEW] Bank-controlled financial-technology subsidiaries became an organizational experiment in the later digital era.** They potentially separate technology talent, procurement, external commercialization and data/product development from traditional bank HR and cost-center structures.
13. **[NEW] Fintech-subsidiary economics can distort simple cost-income comparisons.** A technology capability may appear as bank operating expense, group subsidiary expense, capitalization/intangible investment, or external service revenue depending on period/perimeter.
14. **[NEW] Group subsidiaries create cross-selling economics that branch-only narratives miss.** Corporate clients can be routed among lending, leasing, underwriting/advisory, custody, funds, insurance and wealth products, creating relationship economics outside loan spread alone.
15. **[NEW] Large-bank “comprehensive financial services” or similar historical vocabulary is a search key for perimeter expansion.** Exact contemporaneous wording must be source-verified rather than normalized into a modern “financial holding group” label.
16. **[NEW] Domestic branches, overseas branches and separately incorporated overseas subsidiaries should be distinguished.** They differ in capital, liquidity, governance, local regulation and resolution perimeter even when carrying one bank brand.
17. **[NEW] Cross-border RMB/trade-finance expansion can therefore occur through multiple perimeter forms.** Branch, subsidiary and correspondent channels produce different balance-sheet and risk-transfer signatures.
18. **[NEW] Group capital allocation is a hidden mechanism.** Parent banks may inject capital into subsidiaries or absorb dividends; subsidiary expansion can compete with core-bank capital needs even when consolidated capital looks adequate.
19. **[NEW] Minority-owned/JV subsidiaries create governance asymmetry.** “Controlled by the bank,” “strategic stake,” “joint venture” and “equity-accounted affiliate” are economically different and must not be collapsed.
20. **[NEW] Consolidation perimeter is itself a metric-semantic discontinuity.** Changes in whether a vehicle/subsidiary is consolidated can move assets, revenue, risk-weighted assets and profit without an equivalent underlying economic change.
21. **[NEW] Group-level versus bank-level NPL ratios can diverge conceptually.** Leasing, investment or other subsidiary credit-like exposures may not appear in the licensed bank’s loan NPL denominator even though the group bears related credit risk.
22. **[NEW] Group-level versus bank-level capital/RWA comparisons can also diverge.** Subsidiary equity holdings, deductions, minority interests and consolidated risk weights may alter capital consumption; exact rules are Evidence work.
23. **[NEW] Fee-income attribution is perimeter-sensitive.** Manufacturing fees earned in a fund/wealth/insurance subsidiary and distribution fees earned in the bank should be separated when reconstructing business-model shifts.
24. **[NEW] Intercompany funding and guarantees are a possible transmission channel between parent bank and subsidiaries.** The existence, scale and regulatory treatment of specific arrangements must be source-verified.
25. **[NEW] Reputational support may exceed legal support.** A separately incorporated product manufacturer can still inherit the parent bank’s name, distribution channel and perceived backing, creating contingent economic risk not visible in legal perimeter alone.
26. **[NEW] Specialized credit-card centers/business units are a useful counterexample to “subsidiary = specialization.”** Some important retail activities may be organizationally ring-fenced without being separately incorporated; legal form and managerial perimeter are distinct dimensions.
27. **[NEW] Centralized treasury, custody and asset-management capabilities can move organizationally without moving legal perimeter.** A cross-time study should track both legal-entity changes and internal operating-model changes.
28. **[NEW] Subsidiary proliferation can create internal-control complexity.** Related-party transactions, delegated authorities, valuation, model risk, IT/data access and audit coverage become group-governance questions rather than branch-only controls.
29. **[NEW] “One customer, one group” cross-selling ambitions can fail because incentives remain siloed.** Internal transfer pricing, referral-credit allocation and subsidiary minority partners may obstruct promised synergies.
30. **[NEW] Comprehensive-finance expansion can be cyclically pro-risk rather than purely diversifying.** Leasing, investment banking, wealth and investment subsidiaries may all load on the same property/SOE/local-government macro factor through different legal wrappers.
31. **[NEW] Post-crisis or post-regulatory reform may shrink, reclassify or re-govern perimeter rather than simply close businesses.** A “failed mechanism” can therefore appear as changed legal form, product migration or tightened parent-subsidiary firewall rather than an obvious institutional failure.
32. **[NEW] Group-perimeter changes complicate historical ROE decomposition.** Equity injected into subsidiaries, minority interests, retained earnings and dividend upstreaming can change group versus bank ROE mechanically.
33. **[NEW] Group-perimeter changes also complicate asset-growth narratives.** Some growth shifts from loans to investments, leasing assets, wealth AUM or off-balance/customer assets; “slower bank assets” need not mean slower financial intermediation by the group.
34. **[NEW] The large-bank peer set may converge in headline strategy while diverging in perimeter architecture.** Similar slogans—wealth, fintech, comprehensive finance, internationalization—can correspond to very different legal structures and earnings/risk transmission.

## Metric-semantic cautions

- Always identify **legal reporting perimeter** before comparing assets, revenue, profit, ROE, RWA, capital ratios, NPLs, provisions, AUM or fee income.
- “Group,” “bank,” “parent bank,” “consolidated,” “subsidiary,” “associate/JV,” “off-balance” and “customer assets” are not interchangeable.
- Wealth AUM is not bank balance-sheet assets; leasing/investment assets are not necessarily loans; product-manufacturing revenue is not identical to distribution revenue.
- A perimeter change can create a time-series break without a corresponding economic shock. Later Evidence should capture restatements/reclassifications where available.

## Counterexamples to a simple state-bank narrative

- BOC’s historical international/offshore platform depth may make its group architecture materially different from a purely domestic branch-bank model.
- PSBC demonstrates that a distribution network can be economically central while partly outside the bank’s ordinary owned-branch structure.
- BoCom demonstrates that state control, joint-stock governance and large-bank classification can coexist, so ownership taxonomy does not predict perimeter architecture mechanically.
- A bank with fewer headline subsidiaries may still achieve product breadth through internal divisions, external JVs or third-party distribution; subsidiary count is not itself diversification quality.
