## Falsification leads and residual negative space

1. [duplicate] National joint-stock banks are not one stable peer group through time; the legal label coexists with large differences in origin, shareholder influence, regional heritage, listing status, branch footprint and customer franchise.
2. [duplicate] CMB remains the canonical counterexample to the idea that joint-stock-bank advantage came mainly from wholesale funding: its durable retail/wealth/transaction franchise is economically distinct from a market-liability-led model.
3. [duplicate] Industrial Bank remains a counterexample to treating all successful joint-stock banks as retail-led; interbank, institutional and investment-book capabilities mattered more to its historical model.
4. [duplicate] Minsheng remains a counterexample to assuming private-enterprise orientation mechanically creates superior asset quality; borrower mix, governance, growth incentives and cycle timing can reverse the expected effect.
5. [duplicate] CITIC Bank, China Everbright Bank and Hua Xia Bank are useful controls against collapsing central-enterprise sponsorship, listed-bank governance and commercial franchise into a single ownership story.
6. [duplicate] Shanghai Pudong Development Bank is a reminder that an early strong home-market/corporate franchise and national expansion can coexist; regional origin is not equivalent to permanent regional concentration.
7. [duplicate] Shenzhen Development Bank to Ping An Bank remains a key rename/restructuring chain; legal-name continuity and economic-business-model continuity are not the same thing.
8. [duplicate] Guangdong Development Bank / China Guangfa Bank is another rename and ownership-restructuring chain where old vocabulary matters for period-correct retrieval.
9. [duplicate] Hengfeng, Bohai and Zheshang remain the main late-entry/troubled-governance falsification set; their trajectories already fit the targeted gap now considered closed rather than creating a second category.
10. [refinement] "全国性股份制商业银行" is a period-sensitive peer label; analyst peer sets may differ from legal-form labels and should not be projected backward mechanically.
11. [duplicate] A listed status is not evidence of governance immunity: shareholder influence, related-party exposure, management incentives and control failures can persist after public listing.
12. [duplicate] A recapitalization or ownership reset is not equivalent to franchise repair; deposit quality, client relationships, underwriting culture and staff incentives may lag the balance-sheet repair.
13. [duplicate] State-linked shareholders do not make a joint-stock bank equivalent to a large state-owned commercial bank; governance path, policy role, funding base and franchise remain separate dimensions.
14. [duplicate] Private or mixed shareholders do not automatically imply effective market discipline; concentrated owners can also intensify related-party or aggressive-growth risk.
15. [duplicate] Branch expansion is not itself evidence of franchise quality; it can buy loan growth faster than stable operating deposits or risk-management capacity.
16. [refinement] A more useful branch-era test is whether new outlets produced settlement flows, payroll, cash-management and recurring retail balances rather than only loan origination.
17. [duplicate] Corporate settlement deposits and transaction banking can create low-cost operating balances; this differs economically from rate-sensitive term deposits even if both appear in customer-deposit totals.
18. [duplicate] Deposit growth can hide termization and repricing; stable volume is not stable funding cost.
19. [duplicate] Structured deposits and other rate-enhanced products can defend reported deposit share while raising effective liability cost and weakening the meaning of "deposit franchise".
20. [duplicate] Negotiable certificates of deposit and other market liabilities improved funding flexibility but made some banks more exposed to wholesale spread and confidence cycles.
21. [duplicate] Interbank assets and liabilities should not be interpreted symmetrically: the same headline balance can represent liquidity placement, funding dependence, trading inventory or channel-business plumbing.
22. [duplicate] The 2013-era liquidity stress and later deleveraging episodes remain falsification events for the belief that wholesale funding is always a cheap substitute for deposits.
23. [duplicate] Deleveraging can be partly accounting or perimeter migration: shrinking receivables-type investments or channel balances does not prove equivalent economic-risk disappearance.
24. [duplicate] Trust beneficiary rights, receivables-type investments and other non-standard structures belong in look-through economic exposure, not merely accounting-line comparisons.
25. [duplicate] Loan growth versus investment growth can change apparent credit intensity without changing ultimate borrower exposure if non-loan credit structures are used.
26. [refinement] A bank can improve regulatory liquidity or capital optics while preserving duration, borrower or concentration risk through asset substitution; regulatory-metric improvement is not identical to business-model de-risking.
27. [duplicate] Property risk can migrate from developer lending into mortgages, bonds, wealth products, collateral values and local-government-linked exposures; category shrinkage is not necessarily system-level exit.
28. [duplicate] LGFV and local-government-linked exposures matter to joint-stock banks even without a state-owned-bank policy role; regional branch incentives and corporate franchises shape allocation.
29. [duplicate] Private-enterprise lending is not a single stable risk category: access, pricing, collateral, supply-chain links and cycle timing matter more than the ownership label alone.
30. [duplicate] Retail diversification is not automatically safer: cards, unsecured consumer credit and mortgages carry different seasoning, unemployment, property-price and prepayment sensitivities.
31. [duplicate] Fast retail growth can temporarily suppress observed NPL ratios because newer vintages have not seasoned; low stock NPL is not equivalent to low future loss content.
32. [duplicate] Mortgage prepayment is an earnings/duration issue as well as a household-credit issue; high prepayment can weaken yield without indicating borrower distress.
33. [duplicate] WMP growth can reduce balance-sheet deposit capture while increasing client AUM and fee opportunity; deposit outflow and franchise weakening are not synonymous.
34. [duplicate] Wealth-management AUM is not a substitute metric for customer deposits because liquidity, fee economics, risk bearing and balance-sheet treatment differ.
35. [duplicate] Net-value wealth transformation can move volatility toward clients and alter fee recognition without eliminating reputation or distribution risk.
36. [refinement] Custody, fund/insurance distribution and pension/annuity adjacency may deepen customer relationships but are better treated as franchise refinements than as a new BANK-JOINT-STOCK ontology category.
37. [duplicate] NPL stock, NPL formation, overdue loans, special-mention loans, restructured loans and write-offs answer different questions; period comparisons must not use them interchangeably.
38. [duplicate] IFRS9/ECL introduces forward-looking staging and provision timing that can change credit-cost recognition before comparable movement appears in NPL stock.
39. [duplicate] Provision coverage can rise because the NPL denominator changes; it is not a standalone measure of absolute loss-absorption strength.
40. [duplicate] Credit cost, reserve-to-loan and provision coverage can move in different directions; denominator and write-off effects matter.
41. [duplicate] Capital-adequacy improvement can reflect retained earnings, issuance, RWA optimization or slower asset growth; it is not automatically evidence of lower underlying risk.
42. [refinement] RWA density is a useful falsification lens for "asset-light" narratives: lower density may reflect mix and modeling changes rather than proportionate reduction in tail risk.
43. [duplicate] Digital customer acquisition is not proof of low-cost funding; acquired customers can be rate-sensitive, low-balance or weakly engaged.
44. [duplicate] App activity and remote-channel migration are not equivalent to monetized franchise quality; transaction depth, balances, cross-sell and retention matter.
45. [duplicate] Direct-banking, internet-finance partnerships and platform distribution can become dead ends if acquisition economics, regulation or risk ownership change.
46. [duplicate] Payments/card leadership can aid data and customer engagement, but interchange/fee economics, credit losses and competitive platform pressure can offset the apparent moat.
47. [refinement] Technology modernization should be separated into customer acquisition, transaction servicing, underwriting/risk control and operating-productivity effects; success in one does not imply success in all.
48. [duplicate] Cross-border trade finance and FX businesses are material for some joint-stock banks but do not make the cohort equivalent to BOC's structurally international franchise.
49. [duplicate] Offshore branches/subsidiaries can add funding and client capability while introducing currency, jurisdiction and group-liquidity complexity; overseas presence is not a one-directional diversification benefit.
50. [duplicate] RMB internationalization and trade-finance cycles should be treated as business-line opportunities whose importance varies by bank and period rather than a new cohort-defining regime.
51. [duplicate] "Scale first, risk later" was a recurring failed narrative: rapid balance-sheet and branch growth could precede NPL recognition, funding stress or capital needs.
52. [duplicate] "Strategic investor equals governance transfer" is a failed causal shortcut; ownership entry does not guarantee durable risk-culture or capability transfer.
53. [duplicate] "Wealth growth solves deposit weakness" is another failed shortcut because AUM and stable operating liabilities have different economics.
54. [duplicate] "Fintech solves branch disadvantage" is falsified when digital acquisition lacks transaction depth, cheap funding or disciplined underwriting.
55. [duplicate] "Deleveraging means risk gone" fails when exposure migrates across accounting lines, counterparties or customer products.
56. [duplicate] "Low reported NPL means low risk" fails when book seasoning, restructuring, write-offs, denominator growth or recognition timing differ.
57. [duplicate] "High provision coverage means conservative bank" fails without checking credit cost, reserve stock, write-offs and NPL recognition policy.
58. [duplicate] "High capital ratio means abundant loss capacity" fails without examining RWA density, asset growth, off-balance-sheet commitments and capital quality.
59. [duplicate] "Governance cleanup restores competitiveness" fails when customer franchise, staff culture, technology and product capabilities take longer to rebuild.
60. [duplicate] "All joint-stock banks converge to CMB-like retail/wealth models" fails because institutional, corporate, interbank, regional and mixed models remain economically distinct.
