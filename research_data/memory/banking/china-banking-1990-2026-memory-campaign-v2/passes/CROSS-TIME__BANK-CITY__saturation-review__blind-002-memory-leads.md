## A. Asset-quality stock-flow and classification semantics

1. **[refinement]** Reported NPL stock must be decomposed into opening stock + new formation + upgrades/cures - write-offs - disposals - recoveries/reclassifications; a low closing ratio can mask high formation plus aggressive cleanup.
2. **[duplicate]** Overdue, special-mention, restructured and NPL categories can move differently and should not be treated as synonyms.
3. **[duplicate]** Pandemic or policy forbearance can delay recognition without eliminating economic cash-flow weakness.
4. **[refinement]** A merger can create a one-time recognition reset if centralized review reclassifies predecessor assets, so post-merger NPL formation is not automatically new underwriting failure.
5. **[duplicate]** AMC transfers or bulk disposals can lower reported NPL stock without restoring borrower cash flows.
6. **[refinement]** Collateral-led cures and cash-flow-led cures are economically different even if both leave the NPL numerator.
7. **[duplicate]** Property/LGFV restructurings can reduce near-term default risk while extending duration and lowering yield.
8. **[refinement]** Repeated loan renewal can keep contractual delinquency low while borrower leverage remains elevated; renewal frequency is a useful future Evidence variable.
9. **[duplicate]** Special-mention and overdue trends may lead NPL ratios during stress periods.
10. **[refinement]** Post-consolidation historical series need predecessor-level bridges to distinguish inherited stock from new formation.

## B. Capital, provisioning and denominator effects

11. **[duplicate]** Capital-ratio improvement after recapitalization does not prove normalized internal capital generation.
12. **[refinement]** RWA migration can change capital ratios even when nominal assets are stable, particularly if asset composition shifts among loans, bonds and lower-risk exposures.
13. **[duplicate]** Provision coverage can rise because NPL denominator falls after disposal rather than because reserve buffers grow.
14. **[refinement]** Credit-cost comparisons around merger or cleanup years require normalization for one-off disposals and recognition changes.
15. **[duplicate]** Strong opening capital after a rescue/merger can coexist with weak recurring profitability.
16. **[new]** Bank-only versus consolidated-group capital/profit metrics can diverge where subsidiaries or wealth-management entities become material; future Evidence should pin metric perimeter before comparing peers.
17. **[refinement]** Dividend capacity is a separate question from headline capital adequacy when profitability is compressed and local-state shareholders expect capital conservation.

## C. Loans versus investment-credit migration

18. **[duplicate]** Economic credit risk can migrate from loans into investment/receivable structures without disappearing.
19. **[duplicate]** Falling non-standard assets can reflect repayment, sale, securitization, reclassification or conversion to loans.
20. **[refinement]** Loan-growth deceleration can therefore coexist with continued balance-sheet credit extension through bonds or investments.
21. **[duplicate]** Treasury/investment books can be used to compensate for weak local loan demand.
22. **[refinement]** Falling rates can generate valuation/carry benefits in treasury books that are cyclical rather than permanent franchise improvements.
23. **[new]** A low-rate period can create a false positive in peer comparisons if treasury gains offset weak core NIM; future Evidence should separate recurring spread income from market/valuation effects.
24. **[duplicate]** Duration extension in bonds can replace some credit risk with interest-rate/market-value sensitivity.

## D. Deposit, WMP and AUM measurement

25. **[duplicate]** Deposit growth does not by itself prove stronger franchise if the mix shifts toward expensive long-term deposits.
26. **[duplicate]** WMP-to-deposit migration can mechanically improve deposit growth while reducing fee/AUM economics.
27. **[refinement]** Deposit-to-asset ratio can rise because assets shrink or wholesale funding is cut, not only because deposits are won.
28. **[duplicate]** Government-related deposits can be concentrated, seasonal and relationship-dependent despite low headline cost.
29. **[refinement]** Payroll, merchant, public-service and settlement deposits are more informative franchise indicators than raw branch count.
30. **[new]** A useful future Evidence metric is deposit beta by customer segment rather than aggregate deposit cost, because municipal/corporate/retail term deposits reprice differently.
31. **[duplicate]** AUM retention inside the bank's wealth ecosystem can preserve customer franchise even when deposits leave the balance sheet.
32. **[refinement]** Wealth-subsidiary or distribution income may move between fee line items over time, so fee-income trend comparisons need classification checks.
33. **[duplicate]** Deposit termization can compress NIM while reported deposit balances remain strong.

## E. Wholesale funding and liquidity

34. **[duplicate]** NCD/interbank funding is both a growth tool and a market-confidence sensor.
35. **[duplicate]** Counterparty segmentation can show up in wholesale spreads before retail deposits move.
36. **[refinement]** A lower wholesale-funding share after a stress episode can reflect forced deleveraging rather than deliberate franchise improvement.
37. **[duplicate]** Province-level scale can improve market access without eliminating local economic concentration.
38. **[refinement]** Liquidity strength should distinguish stock liquidity buffers from stable recurring funding generation.
39. **[duplicate]** A bank can be liquid because it holds more low-yield assets, trading profitability for resilience.

## F. Branch/network and merger arithmetic

40. **[duplicate]** Branch growth from merger inheritance is not organic expansion.
41. **[duplicate]** Branch closures after consolidation can reflect overlap rationalization rather than digital substitution.
42. **[refinement]** Revenue-per-branch can jump mechanically after branch closures even without customer productivity gains.
43. **[duplicate]** Digital customer counts do not prove sticky deposits or good credit vintages.
44. **[refinement]** Network economics should include local public-service/SME relationship value that is not visible in transaction counts alone.
45. **[duplicate]** Core-system unification and risk-culture unification can occur on different timelines after mergers.
46. **[refinement]** A temporary post-merger cost-income deterioration can be consistent with longer-run synergy if duplicated systems and branches are being integrated.

## G. Governance repair versus franchise repair

47. **[duplicate]** State recapitalization can repair solvency without fixing underwriting culture.
48. **[duplicate]** Management replacement can change governance before local customer franchise or asset quality normalizes.
49. **[refinement]** Related-party exposure can decline while local-government concentration rises; governance repair and portfolio diversification are distinct dimensions.
50. **[duplicate]** Listing does not guarantee durable governance quality.
51. **[duplicate]** Strategic investors do not guarantee permanent capability transfer.
52. **[refinement]** Governance case frequency is not a clean cross-bank metric because detection, disclosure and enforcement intensity differ.

## H. Failed expectations and middle-cohort counterexamples

53. **[duplicate]** Bigger regional scale does not automatically create diversification.
54. **[duplicate]** Cross-region expansion does not automatically lower risk because adverse selection can offset geographic diversification.
55. **[duplicate]** Digitalization does not automatically reduce operating cost if legacy branches/systems remain.
56. **[duplicate]** Local-state ownership does not guarantee permanently cheap funding.
57. **[duplicate]** Low reported NPL does not equal low economic credit formation.
58. **[refinement]** Stable-low-return city banks remain important: low growth, high liquidity and conservative local books can be rational equilibrium rather than strategic failure.
59. **[duplicate]** Strong coastal winners and failed weak banks should not define the whole cohort.
60. **[refinement]** Local economic structure — public-sector intensity, export manufacturing, private SMEs, property dependence or resource industry — can explain persistent business-model differences without requiring a new bank-type ontology.
