## Mechanism frame

1. **[useful_refinement][high] Four-way funding-state split:** `liquidity support`, `capital/solvency support`, `asset-transfer financing`, and `final loss absorption` should be four separate fields. A single public-sector payer can appear in more than one field without making them equivalent.
2. **[useful_refinement][high] Funding actor is not legal state:** PBOC, deposit-insurance vehicle, fiscal/local-government entities, state shareholders, AMCs and successor banks can finance or facilitate a transaction, but actor identity alone does not prove takeover, closure, bankruptcy or payout.
3. **[useful_refinement][high] Gross funding versus ultimate loss:** temporary cash advanced to keep payments moving can later be repaid from recoveries; gross intervention funding must not be treated as ultimate taxpayer/fund loss.
4. **[useful_refinement][high] Asset-side versus liability-side support:** purchasing bad assets supports the balance sheet from the asset side; assuming deposits or other liabilities protects claimants from the liability side. They may occur together or separately.
5. **[useful_refinement][high] Capital injection versus creditor transfer:** new equity or other capital replenishes the institution/successor, whereas creditor/deposit transfer changes who owes the liability. These should not share one `recapitalization` label.
6. **[useful_refinement][high] Loss allocation must be claimant-specific:** shareholder loss, capital-instrument impairment, interbank/wholesale creditor loss, large corporate depositor loss, retail depositor loss and deposit-insurance fund loss are separate outcomes.
7. **[useful_refinement][medium] Ex ante safety net versus ex post transaction funding:** a standing fund may exist before a bank problem but not be used in a specific case; membership/availability is not the same as deployment.
8. **[useful_refinement][medium] Funding channel versus accounting bearer:** the entity providing cash can differ from the entity legally retaining impaired assets or recording final losses.

## Cross-time refinements and falsification tests

27. **[useful_refinement][high] Pre-deposit-insurance era:** before the formal national deposit-insurance regime, depositor protection in bank stress likely depended more on administrative transfer, state/local support, PBOC liquidity and successor assumptions; exact mechanisms should not be backfilled with post-2015 labels.
28. **[useful_refinement][high] Large-bank NPL cleanup is a counterfactual:** late-1990s/2000s AMC transfers and recapitalizations show large-scale public balance-sheet repair without ordinary depositor-resolution events. This helps separate systemic bank reform funding from bank failure resolution.
29. **[useful_refinement][high] Deposit insurance changes the option set:** after the deposit-insurance regime begins, a dedicated safety-net institution/fund becomes a potential participant, but the existence of that option does not mean every restructuring uses payout or fund money.
30. **[useful_refinement][high] Modern small-bank capital programs can be preventive:** special-bond or shareholder capital injection may strengthen weak banks before legal resolution action; classify trigger/state separately.
31. **[useful_refinement][high] Formal takeover can coexist with protected retail access:** a takeover case may require liquidity and transfer funding even while most ordinary depositors experience continuity. Funding intensity is not a proxy for depositor loss.
32. **[useful_refinement][high] Wholesale loss can coexist with deposit protection:** differentiated treatment of interbank/large institutional claims versus ordinary deposits is a plausible modern resolution feature; exact thresholds and instruments are source-dependent.
33. **[useful_refinement][high] Capital instruments can absorb loss before deposits:** subordinated or capital-like claims should be tracked separately from ordinary deposits; exact contractual/statutory treatment varies and must be verified.
34. **[useful_refinement][high] Public money can be recoverable:** a government/fund advance or asset purchase may create a recoverable claim. Final fiscal/fund cost cannot be inferred from initial cash deployed.
35. **[useful_refinement][high] AMC purchase price matters:** transferring bad assets at market-like, policy-supported or otherwise negotiated prices can shift loss between bank, seller shareholders, AMC and public sector; exact valuation mechanics are Evidence work.
36. **[useful_refinement][medium] New investor dilution is loss allocation:** incumbent shareholders can suffer dilution/control loss without formal creditor impairment. Governance loss is not claimant loss.
37. **[useful_refinement][medium] Merger consideration can conceal support:** an absorption merger may involve capital injections, guarantees or asset carve-outs, but the merger label alone does not show who financed the cleanup.
38. **[useful_refinement][medium] Guarantee versus funded support:** a guarantee/commitment can backstop confidence without immediate cash outflow; later drawdown must be separated from announced capacity.
39. **[useful_refinement][medium] Deposit transfer versus reimbursement:** customers whose deposits are assumed by a successor can retain access without receiving a statutory insurance payout; both protect depositors through different mechanisms.
40. **[useful_refinement][medium] Resolution funding does not determine license outcome:** the same financing stack could accompany continuation, merger, transfer or eventual legal-person termination. Funding and terminal state remain orthogonal.
