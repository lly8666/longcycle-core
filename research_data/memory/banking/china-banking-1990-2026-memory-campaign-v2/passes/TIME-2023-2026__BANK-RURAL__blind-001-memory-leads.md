## 1. Obvious landmarks

2. **L002 — [R/H] Deposit termization can worsen the asymmetry.** Households seeking yield and certainty may move toward longer fixed-term deposits while current-account/settlement franchises remain limited at many rural banks, locking in relatively costly liabilities as asset yields fall.
3. **L003 — [R/H] Provincial rural-credit reform and legal-person consolidation become much more visible.** The key research task is not a province list from memory but reconstructing each sequence: reform proposal/approval → capital/governance preparation → legal establishment → predecessor absorption/merger → system/brand/operations integration.
4. **L004 — [D/M] High-risk small-bank reduction and capital replenishment continue.** Rev163 already names this; the final slice should recover which mechanisms were used and whether they cured governance/franchise weaknesses or only created time for restructuring.
5. **L005 — [R/H] Property and local-government stress likely migrates into county banking through indirect channels.** Contractors, suppliers, collateral values, household confidence, local fiscal spending and guarantee networks may matter more than a uniform direct developer/LGFV book.
6. **L006 — [R/M] Rural revitalization / agriculture / inclusive micro-SME lending remains a policy mission while profitability weakens.** The structural tension is between local-service obligations and the need to preserve pricing, capital generation and credit discipline.

## 2. Liability, pricing and NIM leads

7. **L007 — [N/H] Deposit pricing discipline may shift from growth competition toward active cost control.** Search later for self-discipline mechanisms, reductions in quoted deposit rates, product repricing and restrictions on disguised high-rate practices rather than assuming all banks moved together.
8. **L008 — [N/H] `存款定期化` is likely a high-value search key.** A greater share of time deposits can raise funding beta persistence even when headline administered/posted rates fall.
9. **L009 — [N/M] `手工补息` / manual interest supplementation is a remembered industry phrase that may matter to liability-cost clean-up, but whether and how it applied to rural banks in-period is uncertain and must be source-verified.
10. **L010 — [N/H] Deposit maturity mismatch may become a franchise-quality indicator.** Two banks with equal average deposit cost can differ greatly if one relies on long-duration rate-sensitive term deposits while another has sticky settlement/current accounts.
11. **L011 — [N/M] Smaller rural banks may have less ability to offset NIM pressure with wealth-management, custody, cards or capital-markets fees.** This can make operating efficiency and low-cost local deposits more important than at diversified national banks.
12. **L012 — [N/H] NIM compression may create an incentive conflict with aggressive asset growth.** Chasing higher-yield small-business, consumer or investment assets to defend earnings can increase future credit/market risk; slowing growth preserves capital but weakens revenue.
13. **L013 — [N/M] Deposit competition may remain locally segmented even under national pricing discipline.** Affluent counties with strong household/corporate savings can have surplus deposits, while weaker regions may still compete intensely for stable funding.
14. **L014 — [R/M] Remote/internet deposit restrictions from the prior slice likely leave a legacy funding adjustment rather than a new post-2022 lineage.** The question is which institutions successfully replaced remote funding with local franchise deposits.

## 4. Provincial consolidation / legal-person reform leads

22. **L022 — [N/H] Province-level consolidation may be the most important organizational change of the final slice.** It potentially shifts governance from many county legal persons plus a provincial union toward fewer/larger legal persons with more centralized capital, risk, technology and management.
23. **L023 — [N/H] “Unified legal person” is not equivalent to “one operating bank on announcement date.”** Some reforms may proceed through staged merger, share conversion, asset/liability transfer, systems migration and branch renaming; each stage needs its own valid/known time.
24. **L024 — [N/H] Consolidation can create internal cross-subsidy and risk diversification that provincial shared-service coordination alone did not provide.** This is a candidate structural break and requires an orthogonal challenge before any shard seal.
25. **L025 — [N/H] Centralized capital allocation may reduce county-level autonomy.** Strong county franchises could effectively support weaker regions after merger, creating new internal transfer-pricing and governance questions.
26. **L026 — [N/H] Conversely, provincial consolidation can professionalize governance and reduce local shareholder capture.** Stronger appointment, audit, risk, data and credit-policy controls may be part of the intended mechanism, but actual effectiveness is an Evidence question.
27. **L027 — [N/M] Technology scale may be a major merger benefit independent of credit-loss resolution.** Unified core systems, anti-fraud, data governance, mobile channels and cybersecurity can be difficult for hundreds of small legal persons to maintain separately.
28. **L028 — [N/M] A province-level institution may inherit contradictory local business models.** Affluent manufacturing counties, agricultural counties, tourism regions and fiscally stressed areas cannot be assumed to converge economically merely because they share a legal parent.
29. **L029 — [N/H] Predecessor preservation is mandatory.** Later replay should allow a researcher to see which county/RCC/RCB generated a loss, deposit franchise or Judgment before consolidation; successor reporting alone can erase the causal history.
30. **L030 — [N/M] Provincial union roles may shrink, transform or be absorbed rather than simply disappear.** The exact legal/operational transition is likely province-specific and should be source-traced rather than inferred from a successor bank name.
31. **L031 — [N/M] Consolidation may be selective rather than universal.** Some strong rural commercial banks may remain independent, meaning the reform frontier itself is evidence of heterogeneity rather than proof of one national destination.

34. **L034 — [N/M] Guarantee-chain risk may reappear when low growth meets property/local fiscal stress.** Mutual guarantees among private firms can make geographically concentrated rural-bank portfolios more correlated than borrower-sector labels imply.
35. **L035 — [N/M] Weak local credit demand can be as important as credit losses.** In some counties, banks may have ample deposits but limited attractive loan demand, pushing money into bonds/interbank/investment books and further compressing returns.
36. **L036 — [R/M] Strong affluent-county banks are again a key falsifier.** Manufacturing/export clusters, entrepreneurship and deep household deposits can support resilient profitability despite national rural-bank stress narratives.

## 10. Metric semantics and counterexamples

52. **L052 — [N/H] Post-merger capital/NPL ratios are not directly comparable with predecessor ratios without perimeter mapping.** Consolidation can change denominator, provisioning, asset classification and internal eliminations.
53. **L053 — [N/M] “Number of high-risk institutions reduced” is an administrative/system metric, not proof that economic losses disappeared.** Resolution route and successor balance sheet must be preserved.
54. **L054 — [N/H] A strong independent county rural commercial bank is the decisive counterexample to “provincial merger is the universal efficient form.”** If such banks remain profitable, well-capitalized and locally dominant, consolidation is more likely a targeted response to weak/fragmented cohorts than a single inevitable lineage.
55. **L055 — [N/M] A newly consolidated provincial bank can also be a counterexample to “merger only hides weakness” if it demonstrably improves governance, technology, funding and risk control.** This is a future Evidence/Judgment/Outcome question, not a blind conclusion.
