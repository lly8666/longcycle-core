2. **[N | high] Central-bank/local-branch supervisory role likely became more important after the separation.** Memory suggests the People's Bank apparatus had a direct role in supervising or administering RCCs before the later creation of a dedicated banking regulator. 3. **[R | high] Management reassignment may have been layered rather than cleanly centralized.** County/local government, central-bank branches and county unions may each have exercised different forms of influence. “Who managed RCCs” should therefore be decomposed by prudential supervision, personnel, business guidance and ownership.

5. **[N | medium] The period may mark a transition from bank-affiliated cooperative finance toward a more explicitly separate cooperative-financial system.** Search later for how contemporaneous documents described the legal/organizational identity of RCCs immediately after separation.

### B. County unions and grassroots legal persons

6. **[R | high] County union and grassroots RCC should not be collapsed into a simple parent-subsidiary hierarchy.** A county union could coordinate, settle, inspect or represent grassroots cooperatives without automatically owning their balance sheets.
7. **[R | high] Two-level legal-person structure is a plausible central organizing concept.** County union and individual grassroots cooperatives may both have had legal-person status, making later “unified legal person” reform a genuine perimeter change rather than a naming exercise.
8. **[N | medium] Intra-county liquidity mutual assistance may precede legal consolidation.** A union/network could redistribute temporary liquidity among members while credit losses and capital remained legally local; this distinction is important for later provincial reform comparisons.
9. **[N | medium] County-union service functions may include accounting, clearing, inspection, training or policy transmission.** These are forgotten operating functions worth verifying separately from lending and deposit-taking.
10. **[R | medium] Grassroots cooperative autonomy could coexist with strong administrative guidance.** “Independent accounting” or “self-responsibility for profits/losses” phrases may appear in old material, but should be treated as search keys until exact meaning and enforcement are verified.
11. **[N | medium] Legal-person fragmentation can create weak internal diversification.** A township-level cooperative exposed to one local industry or borrower cluster may have had less capacity to absorb shocks than a county-wide legal person, providing a mechanism for later consolidation reform.
