## Independent negative-space review

### A. Urbanization and the metropolitan rural-commercial-bank cohort

1. **[N | high] `Rural commercial bank` does not imply a small or predominantly rural operating model.** In highly urbanized municipalities and metropolitan regions, RCC-derived successors can become city-scale banks while retaining rural-commercial legal lineage and naming.
2. **[N | high] A metropolitan/urbanized RCB cohort is structurally different from the compact map's strong-county counterexample.** The key transition is not only strength but the transformation of the underlying service geography from rural county economy toward a large urban/suburban economy with diversified households and enterprises.
3. **[R | high] Urbanization can change the asset mix without changing legal lineage.** Corporate, property, municipal, retail and wealth-related business can become more important even though the institution remains in BANK-RURAL by predecessor/legal history.
4. **[N | high] Consolidation of suburban or municipal rural-credit entities into one large metropolitan RCB can create a legal person comparable in scale and product breadth to city or national commercial banks.** This is a bank-type heterogeneity category, not province-wide rural-credit consolidation.
5. **[R | medium] Direct-administered-municipality or large-city RCBs can sit awkwardly between BANK-RURAL and BANK-CITY analytical intuitions.** The ontology should follow legal/predecessor lineage while allowing business-model tags to reflect urbanized reality.
6. **[N | high] Rural-to-urban franchise transformation is a mechanism in its own right.** Land conversion, suburbanization, local enterprise growth, household wealth and municipal infrastructure can shift borrower/depositor composition without a formal bank-type change.
7. **[R | medium] Rapid urban balance-sheet growth can dilute agriculture-related loan shares even if rural-service obligations continue.** Mission metrics and overall asset mix must therefore be stored separately.
8. **[N | high] Large metropolitan RCBs are a counterexample to treating local embeddedness as synonymous with narrow agriculture exposure.** A locally concentrated franchise can be economically diversified if the locality itself is a major city.
9. **[R | medium] Municipality-level ownership or local-government influence can coexist with commercialized governance and large-bank operations.** Formal ownership and business-model sophistication are independent axes.
10. **[N | high] Metropolitan RCB scale can alter peer selection.** Comparing such an institution only with small county RCBs may misstate NIM, fee income, capital-market access, asset mix and operating efficiency; peer sets need scale/geography tags.

### B. Capital-market access and public-company evolution

11. **[R | medium] Stronger RCB cohorts may gain access to external equity and capital instruments that are unrealistic for small county institutions.** This changes recapitalization options and governance incentives without changing rural lineage.
12. **[N | high] Public listing is a meaningful later-stage differentiator for some RCBs.** A listed RCB becomes subject to public disclosure, market valuation and investor governance constraints that can make its data quality and capital access unlike the long tail of rural banks.
13. **[R | medium] Listing should be modeled as a financing/governance event, not a new bank lineage.** It can follow an earlier metropolitan or strong-regional transformation and should preserve predecessor continuity.
14. **[R | medium] H-share/A-share or other venue differences are precision fields rather than separate BANK-RURAL categories.** Exact venues and dates are Evidence questions.
15. **[N | high] Public-market disclosure can create selection bias in historical research.** Large/listed RCBs are easier to study than weak nonlisted county banks, so source richness must not be mistaken for representativeness.
16. **[R | medium] Listed-bank accounting and investor metrics can be more standardized than cooperative-era measures.** Cross-time replay still needs explicit semantic transitions rather than splicing modern series backward.
17. **[R | medium] Capital-market access can support expansion and technology investment, but cannot be assumed to cause superior credit quality.** Starting-region strength and borrower mix are competing explanations.
18. **[N | high] The later success of some listed/metropolitan RCBs is a falsifier of a distress-only narrative of rural-bank reform.** Some legal-form reforms can create growth platforms from economically strong regions rather than merely rescue weak institutions.

### C. Business-model and geographic boundaries

19. **[R | medium] Large RCBs may serve urban SMEs, private enterprises and households at scale while retaining legacy agriculture/rural mandates.** Asset-purpose and institution-lineage taxonomies must remain orthogonal.
20. **[R | medium] Cross-district or limited cross-region expansion can matter for the strongest RCBs.** Exact branch permissions and periods require Evidence; do not assume nationwide freedom comparable to joint-stock banks.
21. **[N | high] Urbanized RCBs may develop meaningful wealth, custody, cards, payments or treasury capabilities.** Fee/AUM and technology sophistication are therefore not exclusive to national or city-bank shards.
22. **[R | medium] Deposit franchise in affluent suburban/urban districts can produce funding economics very different from poorer agricultural counties.** This is a geography overlay, not a new legal form.
23. **[R | high] Property and local-government exposures can become more material as rural land and suburbs urbanize.** The asset themes belong to ASSET-PROPERTY/LGFV boundaries even when BANK-RURAL explains institution lineage.
24. **[R | medium] Urban land conversion can create concentrated local wealth and collateral cycles.** That can initially strengthen deposits/collateral and later amplify property-linked risk; exact causal claims need Evidence.
25. **[R | medium] Large RCB scale may permit centralized risk, treasury and technology internally without any provincial network merger.** Internal scale economies should be distinguished from shared provincial-service arrangements.
26. **[R | medium] Metropolitan RCB customer concentration can still be geographically local despite business diversification.** Large asset size does not automatically imply national diversification.
27. **[R | high] A metropolitan RCB can look like a city commercial bank in products while differing in predecessor ownership, local networks and historical mission.** This is a key cross-shard counterexample for future stitching.
28. **[R | medium] Naming alone is especially hazardous in urbanized regions.** `农村商业银行` can be a historical lineage label after the surrounding economy has become overwhelmingly urban.
29. **[R | medium] Rural-service branch/outlet obligations can persist inside a large diversified institution.** Outlet geography and asset mix should be tracked separately from legal-person scale.
