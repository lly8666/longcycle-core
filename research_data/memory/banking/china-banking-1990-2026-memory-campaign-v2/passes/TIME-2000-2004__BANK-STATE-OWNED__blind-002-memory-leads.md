## Orthogonal leads: ownership and decision rights

1. **The central reform problem may have been less “who owns the bank?” than “which state role is speaking?”** A large state-owned bank could simultaneously face the state as shareholder, macro/policy principal, regulator, personnel appointing authority, and implicit guarantor. A useful later Evidence question is which decisions were actually delegated to a shareholder channel and which remained administrative or political.

2. **Central Huijin is worth treating as a decision-rights experiment rather than merely a recapitalization vehicle.** The research lead is whether its emergence created a more legible owner-capital relationship—capital contribution, board nomination, performance expectations, preservation of state equity value—while other state controls continued in parallel. Formal ownership change should not be assumed to equal immediate behavioral change.

3. **A “shareholder → board → management” chain may have been a target architecture before it became an effective operating reality.** The period likely involved building or rehearsing shareholder-meeting, board, board-committee, supervisory and executive-management responsibilities in preparation for joint-stock conversion. Later source work should recover which bodies existed when, what authority they held, and whether decisions still bypassed them.

4. **Board architecture itself may have been used as a reform instrument.** Audit, risk, remuneration/nomination or strategy-type committees, outside/independent directors, and clearer management reporting could have been introduced or prepared unevenly across pilot banks. Do not assume later listed-company committee structures were fully present throughout this slice.

5. **The board-of-supervisors / supervisory function should be separated from internal audit and external state inspection.** Internal memory suggests several overlapping control layers could coexist. Their mandates, reporting lines and practical independence are a long-tail governance lead because nominally multiple monitors can still leave accountability gaps.

6. **Personnel authority may have remained a core boundary of commercialization.** Senior-management appointment, cadre evaluation, Party/state personnel channels and company-style board nomination could coexist. A later source pass should ask how this duality affected executive incentives, tenure, risk appetite, and accountability rather than forcing a simple public-company governance template onto the period.

7. **Reform objectives may have been translated into explicit management targets before market listing supplied external discipline.** Banks may have been judged against compact bundles of asset-quality, capital, profitability, cost, control and governance indicators. The important lead is the target-setting mechanism: who set the targets, what definitions were used, and what happened if a bank missed them.

## Orthogonal leads: recapitalization, recognition and opening balance sheets

8. **Recapitalization and NPL recognition were mechanically intertwined.** A capital injection can look large or small depending on how aggressively problem loans are recognized and provisioned before or after it. The research question is the sequencing among asset verification, reclassification, provisioning, write-off/disposal and capital contribution—not merely the headline injection.

9. **The “opening balance sheet” for a restructured bank may have been a negotiated accounting boundary.** Reform credibility required deciding which legacy losses were acknowledged before joint-stock conversion, which remained recoverable claims, and which obligations stayed with the bank or another state vehicle. Exact treatments need source recovery; the lead is that balance-sheet cleanliness is partly a recognition rule.

10. **Residual post-AMC NPLs should be split into at least three cohorts.** Potentially useful categories are legacy assets not included in earlier transfers, loans that deteriorated after transfer, and genuinely new post-reform lending that later became problematic. Mixing these cohorts would obscure whether governance reform reduced fresh NPL formation.

11. **Provisioning can expose losses without removing the loan, while write-off can remove accounting carrying value without ending collection.** Period vocabulary such as `拨备`, `核销`, `账销案存`, recovery and disposal should be recovered with bank-specific accounting semantics. This distinction is central to interpreting “cleanup” metrics.

12. **Capital adequacy improvement could come from both numerator and denominator actions.** State capital, retained earnings and subordinated-like instruments may strengthen the numerator, while asset disposal, growth restraint, portfolio mix and risk-weight treatment affect the denominator. A later Evidence pass should not treat reported capital ratios as pure evidence of fresh external capital.

13. **Foreign-exchange-reserve-linked state capital is a likely technical lead, but the exact vehicle/currency/accounting path must not be guessed.** The later research task is to reconstruct how a state-controlled foreign-currency resource became bank equity and how exchange-rate/accounting treatment was handled.

14. **A pre-listing asset-quality audit could create an apparent one-time deterioration before improvement.** If due diligence or stricter classification revealed hidden problems, reform-period metrics may worsen when measurement improves. This is a metric-semantics counterexample to reading every worsening ratio as economic deterioration.

15. **Legacy policy-directed lending and commercially originated credit may have required different accountability treatment.** A bank could be asked to become commercially responsible while still carrying exposures created under older policy mandates. Research should recover how loss responsibility, pricing, provisioning and performance evaluation distinguished—or failed to distinguish—these vintages.

## Orthogonal leads: branch control and risk architecture

16. **“一级法人” or head-office legal-person control only matters if authorization actually moved.** The key operational lead is the authorization matrix: which branch levels could originate, approve, price, restructure, write off, guarantee, open accounts, or commit the bank, and how those limits changed through the slice.

17. **Separation of marketing/origination from approval may have been a foundational control reform.** Large banks likely experimented with independent credit-review functions, credit committees, risk departments and post-lending inspection to reduce the old branch model in which one local hierarchy could source and approve a loan. Actual independence and override routes require later verification.

18. **Vertical reporting for internal audit/risk functions may have been intended to weaken local capture.** A branch auditor or risk officer accountable primarily to the same local branch leadership has limited independence. The long-tail question is whether reporting lines shifted upward to provincial/head-office control and how quickly that happened.

19. **Credit accountability may have moved from collective ambiguity toward named responsibility.** Loan-file signatures, approval records, post-event responsibility reviews, NPL accountability and disciplinary mechanisms are possible period-native implementation details. Research should test whether these reduced arbitrary lending or instead encouraged excessive risk aversion/document formalism.

20. **Guarantees, collateral valuation and connected local borrowers were likely important control weak points even when formal approval rules improved.** A nominally collateralized loan can remain risky through inflated values, weak enforceability, reciprocal guarantees or local administrative pressure. This is a mechanism lead for why stronger paperwork might not immediately prevent new losses.

21. **Fraud/corruption cases are most useful as control-system stress tests, not scandal anecdotes.** Later source work should classify failures by segregation of duties, account reconciliation, seal/document control, unauthorized guarantees, fake deposits/collateral, off-book activity, treasury/FX operations, or management override. Names and loss amounts should be recovered only from Evidence.

22. **BOC likely had a distinct control problem because cross-border and foreign-currency operations widened the control perimeter.** Overseas branches/subsidiaries, correspondent relationships, trade finance and treasury activity could require stronger consolidated reporting and audit than a predominantly domestic network. This may make BOC governance reform non-comparable with peers in important ways.

23. **ICBC’s scale could turn centralization into an information-processing problem.** A huge branch network means that tighter headquarters control requires standardized data, classification and systems; otherwise approvals simply migrate upward without improving information quality. Scale therefore links governance reform to IT architecture.

24. **CCB’s project/infrastructure heritage may have favored specialized sector/project appraisal but also concentrated large-ticket risk.** A useful later comparison is whether its restructuring emphasized project cash-flow discipline, concentration limits and corporate-client segmentation differently from banks with other legacy franchises.

25. **ABC’s rural network likely created the hardest branch-economics and mandate problem.** Commercial branch profitability, rural financial access, agriculture-related policy objectives and legacy organizational scale can point in different directions. This heterogeneity may explain why a governance template proven at other large banks could not simply be copied.

## Orthogonal leads: operating reform, data and incentives

26. **Outlet and staffing reform should be studied as a control redesign, not only a cost-cutting story.** Closing weak outlets, merging back offices, changing managerial spans, rotating staff, and moving transactional work to centralized processing can reduce both costs and opportunities for local control failure.

27. **Performance evaluation likely had to migrate away from pure balance-sheet growth.** Possible additions include risk-adjusted profitability, NPL formation/recovery, funding cost, fee income, operating cost and control compliance. A key contemporaneous-Judgment lead is whether managers believed a new scorecard would change behavior before long performance histories existed.

28. **Incentive reform could create a new failure mode: target gaming.** Once NPL ratios, profits, capital or cost targets become salient, branches may restructure, roll over, reclassify, defer recognition, shift assets, or accelerate denominator growth. This is a counterexample requiring metric-by-metric source scrutiny.

29. **IT/data centralization was probably prerequisite infrastructure for genuine group-wide governance.** Consolidated general-ledger data, centralized customer identifiers, loan-classification fields, authorization logs and management reporting would allow headquarters to see exposures that had previously been fragmented across branches. Research should recover system milestones only from later sources.

30. **A single-customer / group-borrower view may have been especially important.** Without consolidated customer and connected-party data, multiple branches could each remain within local limits while the bank accumulated a large aggregate exposure. This mechanism connects data architecture directly to concentration risk.

31. **External audit and listing-oriented financial reporting may have served as an adversarial check on internal numbers.** International or market-facing auditors, due diligence and prospectus-style disclosure could force reconciliation among accounting profit, provisions, asset classification and contingent liabilities. The lead is what discrepancies or control weaknesses they surfaced, not an assumption that an auditor automatically solved them.

32. **The strongest test of reform durability is fresh-loss formation after the cleanup.** A bank can satisfy an initial NPL/capital target through state support and disposal while preserving the incentives that generated losses. The period-native evaluation framework should therefore pair opening-balance-sheet repair with flow measures: new NPL formation, migration, recoveries, overrides, concentration, control incidents and risk-adjusted earnings.

## Counterexamples and uncertainty to preserve

- A cleaner legal governance chart does not prove decision rights changed in practice.
- A lower NPL ratio does not isolate recovery from disposal, write-off, reclassification or denominator growth.
- More centralized approval can reduce local capture but can also worsen information loss and bureaucracy.
- More performance metrics can improve discipline or create gaming and delayed recognition.
- External audit can improve measurement without immediately improving underlying borrower quality.
- Pilot-bank success cannot be generalized automatically to ICBC or ABC because scale, franchise and policy burdens differ.
