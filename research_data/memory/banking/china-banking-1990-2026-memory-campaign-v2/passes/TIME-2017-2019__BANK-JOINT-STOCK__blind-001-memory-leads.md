### Deleveraging and balance-sheet transition
1. [new_category][high] Financial deleveraging changed joint-stock-bank strategy from “how fast can assets be scaled through interbank/nonstandard channels?” toward “which assets and funding can survive tighter look-through, liquidity and capital constraints?”
2. [new_category][high] Banks with large interbank-investment or receivable-type books likely faced greater pressure to shrink, reclassify, standardize or move exposures, creating strong peer divergence in reported asset growth.
3. [new_category] A fall in nonstandard assets can reflect genuine deleveraging, accounting migration, repayment or transfer; balance-sheet labels alone cannot identify the mechanism.
4. [new_category] Slower asset growth can improve liquidity/capital while depressing fee, carry or spread income associated with legacy structures.
5. [new_category] The transition can temporarily increase standard bond holdings or ordinary loans as banks replace opaque credit-like investments with recognized assets.
6. [useful_refinement] Gross asset contraction, risk-weighted-asset contraction and credit contraction are different events.
7. [new_category] Treasury/interbank teams may lose balance-sheet autonomy as group risk, liquidity and compliance constraints become tighter.

### Asset management and WMP transition

10. [new_category] Existing products and new-rule products can coexist, making headline WMP balances an imperfect measure of economic transition.
11. [new_category] Wealth-management subsidiaries or preparations for separate licensed entities became a bank-strategy lead late in the slice; exact institution dates require evidence.

13. [new_category] Net-asset-value products transfer more market volatility to customers, increasing suitability, valuation and retention challenges.
