## Broad period-native recall leads

### Pandemic operating regime, borrower support and credit allocation

1. **Pandemic operating continuity became a bank-business-model issue, not merely a macro backdrop.** Joint-stock banks with stronger mobile banking, remote onboarding, online customer service, digital signatures and centralized operations could preserve more customer interaction when branch traffic was disrupted. [new_category; high]
2. Branch-network economics likely changed unevenly: flagship and advisory branches retained value, while routine transactions migrated faster to apps, remote relationship managers and self-service channels. [new_category]
3. Corporate transaction-banking franchises may have become more valuable because cash-management visibility gave banks earlier information on customer stress than periodic loan review alone. [new_category]
4. Payment flows, payroll, merchant acquiring and settlement activity became useful contemporaneous operating signals for SME and private-enterprise borrowers. [new_category]
5. Loan-renewal, principal/interest deferral, repayment rescheduling and emergency liquidity support should not be read as identical credit phenomena; some were temporary relief, some were genuine restructuring, and some simply delayed recognition. [new_category; high]
6. The period likely widened the semantic gap between reported NPL stock and underlying borrower stress because policy-support tools could keep accounts contractually current or restructured for a time. [new_category; high]
7. “无还本续贷”, renewal without principal repayment, and similar SME continuity language are useful period-native search keys; exact institutional use and timing need Evidence later. [new_category]
8. Inclusive-finance and micro/small-business lending became more central to franchise narratives, but growth in those balances did not by itself prove attractive risk-adjusted returns. [useful_refinement]
9. Banks with stronger transaction deposits from operating companies may have been better positioned to lend through the shock without purchasing as much high-cost funding. [new_category]
10. A counterexample to “pandemic = immediate bad debt”: temporary cash-flow interruption in otherwise viable firms could cure after reopening, especially when bank support bridged a short operating gap. [new_category; counterexample]
11. A counterexample in the other direction: a loan remaining outside NPL after relief did not prove that credit risk had normalized; borrower viability and collateral cash flow still mattered. [new_category; counterexample]
12. Pandemic-era support likely altered vintage analysis: 2020 originations and renewed loans should be separated from pre-2020 vintages when interpreting later credit cost. [new_category]
13. Sector mix mattered sharply; tourism, offline retail, restaurants, transport-linked services and some consumer-facing SMEs faced different cash-flow paths from technology, healthcare, logistics or export-manufacturing beneficiaries. [new_category]
14. Export and trade-finance clients experienced both disruption and later periods of strong external demand; treating cross-border corporate books as uniformly weak would be misleading. [new_category; counterexample]
15. Remote due diligence and collateral monitoring became practical risk-management questions when travel restrictions impeded physical inspection. [new_category]

### LPR, repricing, deposits and NIM

16. Existing loans repricing toward LPR-linked structures made asset yield reset timing more important; NIM pressure depended on asset repricing speed versus deposit and wholesale funding repricing. [new_category; high]
17. The analytically useful distinction is **front-book pricing versus back-book repricing**: new loans could price differently from legacy portfolios even under the same policy-rate environment. [new_category]
18. Mortgage repricing and corporate-loan repricing should be separated because contractual reset frequency and borrower sensitivity differ. [new_category]
19. Loan yield compression could coexist with stable or rising reported NIM for a time if funding cost fell faster, so “lower loan rates = immediate lower NIM” is not a safe one-step inference. [new_category; counterexample]
20. Deposit beta remained heterogeneous by customer: payroll/settlement deposits, retail savings, negotiated corporate deposits and purchased/structured balances likely behaved differently. [useful_refinement]
21. Deposit “termization” is a useful lead: households and corporates could migrate from demand deposits toward longer-tenor time deposits when risk appetite fell or wealth products looked less certain. [new_category; high]
22. Average balance and period-end deposit balances should not be conflated; quarter-end or year-end balance gathering can distort franchise interpretation. [new_category; metric_semantics]
23. Structural deposits/structured deposits remained an important funding-economics lead, but normalization or tighter treatment likely reduced their usefulness as an unlimited balance-purchasing tool; exact path requires Evidence later. [useful_refinement]
24. Wealth AUM growth and deposit growth can be substitutes rather than complements when customers move cash into funds, WMPs or other investment products. [new_category]
25. Conversely, risk aversion can send wealth assets back into deposits, improving liquidity while raising future deposit-cost pressure if customers prefer term products. [new_category]
26. Wholesale funding still mattered for some joint-stock banks, but the post-deleveraging lesson likely increased management emphasis on core deposits and more stable funding. [useful_refinement]
27. Interbank CDs should be interpreted together with deposit franchise, not in isolation: a bank may use CDs tactically without having a structurally weak customer franchise, while another may depend on market funding more persistently. [new_category]
28. FTP remained crucial because business-line profitability could change even if headline NIM moved little; retail deposits, corporate transaction deposits and long-duration loans carry different internal liquidity values. [new_category]
29. The spread between customer acquisition cost and accounting deposit interest cost became more important as digital marketing and wealth distribution intensified. [new_category]
30. “Low-cost demand deposits” as a franchise claim should be challenged by stability and behavioral duration, not only point-in-time coupon cost. [new_category]

### Property developers, mortgages, consumer and cards

31. The property-developer downturn became a direct differentiator among joint-stock banks because exposure varied by developer quality, project geography, collateral, loan structure and whether the bank financed developers, homebuyers, suppliers or all three. [new_category; high]
32. Developer credit and residential mortgages should not be merged analytically: one can deteriorate while the other remains comparatively resilient for a period. [new_category; high]
33. Presale/project cash-flow disruption created possible transmission from developers to mortgages, construction contractors, suppliers and local transaction deposits even where direct developer-loan exposure looked manageable. [new_category]
34. “房地产开发贷 / 按揭 / 经营性物业贷 / 并购融资” are economically distinct property-related buckets and useful search keys for later Evidence. [new_category; old_vocabulary]
35. Property stress could raise drawdowns on corporate credit lines or reduce settlement deposits before formal loan delinquency appeared. [new_category]
36. Mortgage prepayment behavior is a useful uncertain lead for late-period NIM and duration analysis; it should not be assumed uniformly material before Evidence. [new_category; uncertain_fragment]
37. 2022 mortgage-payment boycotts/停贷 discussion may be relevant as a confidence and project-delivery stress signal, but the cohort-level balance-sheet effect should not be inferred without verification. [new_category; uncertain_fragment]
38. Consumer lending and credit cards faced an unusual cycle: transaction volumes could fall during restrictions while unemployment/income uncertainty could raise delinquencies for some borrower cohorts. [new_category; high]
39. Credit-card balances, transaction volume, revolving balances and card fee income are different metrics; a bank can lose transaction fees without proportional credit loss, or vice versa. [new_category; metric_semantics]
40. Vintage and borrower-acquisition channel matter in cards/consumer finance; digitally acquired borrowers can have different risk and engagement characteristics from payroll or long-tenure bank customers. [new_category]
41. Retail “customer count” should not be treated as franchise quality without active usage, deposits/AUM, cross-sell and risk-adjusted credit economics. [new_category; metric_semantics]
42. Mortgage books can provide relatively stable credit performance yet still pressure NIM if repricing reduces yields faster than deposit costs. [new_category; counterexample]
43. A counterexample to “retail is safer”: unsecured consumer and card exposures can reprice and deteriorate faster than secured mortgages when household income weakens. [new_category; counterexample]

### WMP, wealth subsidiaries and customer-asset migration

44. The legacy-WMP-to-NAV transition became operational rather than merely regulatory: valuation, product design, duration matching, investor communication and distribution behavior all changed. [new_category; high]
45. Wealth-management subsidiaries changed the economic boundary between bank balance sheet, subsidiary AUM and customer assets; AUM should not be mistaken for bank assets. [new_category; metric_semantics]
46. Product migration from expected-return/legacy formats toward NAV products could initially lower customer comfort and change retention behavior even if long-run economics improved. [new_category]
47. Breaking implicit-guarantee expectations is not a binary event; customer beliefs, distributor behavior and reputational support can change more slowly than legal/product structures. [new_category]
48. Bank wealth economics increasingly depended on management fees, distribution, custody and customer retention rather than spread capture inside opaque legacy structures. [new_category]
49. Deposit migration into WMPs can weaken bank funding while strengthening fee/AUM economics; the opposite can happen in risk-off periods. [new_category; high]
50. Public funds, bank WMPs, insurance products and deposits competed for the same household wallet; franchise analysis should use total customer assets when possible rather than deposits alone. [new_category]
51. Wealth-product mark-to-market volatility became more visible to bank customers, making drawdown communication and suitability a franchise issue. [new_category]
52. “净值化”, “破刚兑”, “理财子公司”, “现金管理类” and “母行代销” are period-native vocabulary leads; exact rule dates and product definitions remain Evidence-stage work. [new_category; old_vocabulary]
53. A strong wealth franchise may improve customer stickiness while simultaneously reducing cheap deposits; this is a useful counterexample to simple deposit-growth rankings. [new_category; counterexample]

### ECL, NPL, restructuring, provisioning and capital

54. IFRS9/ECL accounting remained central to interpreting 2020-2022 stress: Stage 1, Stage 2 and Stage 3 migration should not be equated mechanically with performing, special-mention and NPL regulatory classifications. [new_category; high; metric_semantics]
55. Stage-2 migration can reveal rising lifetime expected loss before NPL formation, making it potentially important during borrower-relief periods. [new_category]
56. NPL formation flow, ending NPL balance, NPL ratio and credit cost answer different questions; pandemic relief can distort their timing relationships. [useful_refinement]
57. “关注类 / 逾期 / 重组 / 核销 / 清收 / 转让” remain distinct asset-quality states or flows and should not be collapsed into one distress bucket. [useful_refinement; old_vocabulary]
58. Write-offs can reduce ending NPL balance while increasing evidence of realized loss; falling NPL stock is not necessarily improving underlying credit. [new_category; counterexample]
59. Provision coverage can rise because provisions increase, because NPL denominator falls through disposal, or both; it is not a standalone measure of conservatism. [useful_refinement; metric_semantics]
60. Credit cost is a flow through the income statement, while loan-loss allowance is a stock; comparing banks requires consistent denominators and accounting scope. [useful_refinement; metric_semantics]
61. Pandemic borrower relief created an especially important distinction between contractual delinquency and economic impairment. [new_category]
62. Property stress could create concentrated Stage-2 migration before formal default if management revised forward-looking scenarios. [new_category]
63. Capital pressure depends not only on losses but also on RWA growth and asset mix; rapid inclusive/corporate lending can consume capital even with low observed NPLs. [new_category]
64. Capital instruments such as preference shares, perpetual-style instruments or convertibles may have remained part of joint-stock-bank capital planning; exact issuance by institution belongs to later verification. [new_category; uncertain_fragment]
65. Risk-weight density can fall because the asset mix genuinely becomes lower risk, or because growth shifts toward categories with lower regulatory weights; this is not the same as lower economic risk. [new_category; counterexample]
66. Pre-provision operating profit remains a useful buffer concept: banks with stronger fee income and low funding costs can absorb higher credit cost without identical bottom-line damage. [useful_refinement]

### Interbank, liquidity, markets and treasury

67. The 2019 counterparty-differentiation lesson likely carried into 2020-2022, reinforcing name-specific limits and collateral/liquidity discipline even when system liquidity was supportive. [useful_refinement]
68. Pandemic liquidity support could reduce market funding stress without eliminating differences in wholesale-funding dependence among joint-stock banks. [new_category]
69. Treasury books benefited or suffered differently across bond-yield movements; trading income, investment income and banking-book interest income should not be merged. [new_category; metric_semantics]
70. Bond holdings can improve liquidity buffers while introducing duration and mark-to-market sensitivity. [new_category]
71. Corporate bond defaults could transmit through direct investment, WMP portfolios, collateral values and corporate client confidence even where bank-loan exposure was modest. [new_category]
72. Repo, interbank placements, interbank borrowings and negotiable CDs have different tenor/collateral/counterparty structures; wholesale funding should not be treated as one line item. [new_category]
73. The pandemic created a possible “liquidity abundant but credit selective” regime: system liquidity could be comfortable while some private firms still faced high risk premiums. [new_category; counterexample]
74. Trade finance and FX flows could become more volatile with supply-chain disruption and exchange-rate moves; joint-stock banks with strong transaction-banking or cross-border franchises may have gained fee opportunities alongside risk. [new_category]

### Digital service, payments, fintech and operating model

75. Remote service accelerated from convenience to operational necessity: app uptime, digital servicing, e-signature, remote RM coverage and online lending workflows became resilience capabilities. [new_category; high]
76. Fintech value should be separated into customer acquisition, servicing cost, risk decisioning, fraud control and cross-sell; “digital” is not one economic mechanism. [new_category]
77. Pure online acquisition can lower branch cost but raise marketing expense and adverse-selection risk, so lower physical distribution cost does not automatically improve unit economics. [new_category; counterexample]
78. Ecosystem/scene finance narratives around merchants, autos, housing, travel or platforms should be tested for durable primary-bank relationships rather than user traffic alone. [new_category]
79. Payment-platform competition continued to make the bank account less visible at the customer interface, increasing the value of embedded finance, open APIs and corporate settlement integration. [new_category]
80. Fraud, account takeover, remote identity and transaction-monitoring risks likely became more salient as online usage rose. [new_category]
81. Digital transformation could lower routine branch workload while increasing demand for centralized data, model governance and cyber/operational resilience. [new_category]
82. A failed narrative to test: “pandemic permanently eliminates branches.” Advisory, complex corporate, wealth and high-value relationship functions remained plausible reasons for physical presence. [new_category; failed_narrative]

### Institution differentiation and forgotten/negative-space leads

83. **China Merchants Bank** remained the clearest joint-stock-bank example of retail deposits, cards, wealth/private banking and customer-AUM economics; period analysis should avoid using any directly preexposed 2023 management-detail overlap as clean blind evidence. [useful_refinement; high]
84. For CMB, the key memory lead is not simply “retail leader” but whether a large wealth franchise cushioned spread pressure through fee/AUM economics while risk-off migration sometimes brought assets back into deposits. [new_category]
85. **Industrial Bank (兴业银行)** remained differentiated by corporate/financial-market capabilities, green-finance branding and a historically stronger interbank/investment orientation than retail-heavy peers; the 2020-2022 question is how that model adapted after earlier deleveraging. [new_category]
86. “商行+投行” or investment-banking-style corporate service is a useful Industrial Bank-era search key, but exact management slogan timing should be verified later. [new_category; uncertain_fragment]
87. **Minsheng Bank** remained an important private-enterprise-oriented counterexample: a franchise built around private firms could create both relationship advantages and greater sensitivity to SME/property/corporate stress. [new_category; high]
88. Minsheng’s 2020-2022 story likely includes continued adjustment from earlier aggressive growth and asset-quality cleanup; exact problem exposures or governance events require Evidence. [new_category; uncertain_fragment]
89. **Ping An Bank** remained differentiated by group ecosystem, retail/consumer/credit-card emphasis, technology narrative and cross-selling potential; pandemic consumer-credit behavior is therefore especially relevant. [new_category]
90. Ping An group affiliation may support customer acquisition and cross-sell, but intra-group ecosystem advantage should not be assumed to equal low-cost deposits or low credit losses. [new_category; counterexample]
91. **SPDB** retained a major corporate/urban franchise and had been pursuing retail and digital transformation; the analytical question is whether transformation changed funding quality and fee mix faster than legacy corporate risks changed asset quality. [new_category]
92. **CITIC Bank** is a useful mixed corporate/retail/wealth case with group-linked corporate relationships; compare transaction-banking and wealth cross-sell rather than classifying it as purely corporate. [new_category]
93. **China Everbright Bank** should be kept distinct from its broader financial-group associations; wealth/asset-management heritage and retail/corporate balance are useful leads, but exact subsidiary relationships need verification. [new_category]
94. **Huaxia Bank** is a useful long-tail peer because a less celebrated retail franchise can reveal whether deposit competition and corporate concentration affected smaller national joint-stock banks differently from CMB or Ping An. [new_category; long_tail]
95. **China Guangfa Bank (CGB/广发银行)** remains a national joint-stock peer with insurance-group linkage and meaningful cards/retail history; ownership/group distribution may influence cross-sell but should not be treated as automatic franchise superiority. [new_category; long_tail]
96. Institution ranking by total assets can obscure business-model differences in low-cost deposits, wealth AUM, card receivables, nonstandard cleanup and corporate concentration. [new_category; metric_semantics]
97. Joint-stock banks should be compared on **funding composition + customer franchise + asset mix + fee/AUM + credit cost + capital consumption**, not asset growth alone. [useful_refinement; high]
98. A bank shrinking or slowing risky assets in 2020-2022 may look weaker on growth while actually improving balance-sheet resilience; low growth is not automatically franchise deterioration. [new_category; counterexample]
99. Conversely, fast inclusive or retail growth can be strategically positive but may hide immature vintages; early delinquency metrics and seasoning matter. [new_category; counterexample]
100. Governance remains negative space: incentive changes after prior deleveraging, risk-appetite limits, centralized approval and model overrides may explain peer divergence more than headline strategy labels. [new_category; negative_space]
101. Restructuring/failure negative space: no major national joint-stock bank failed in this period, which itself differentiates the cohort from stressed smaller-bank cases; resilience should not be interpreted as absence of institution-specific cleanup. [new_category; counterexample]
102. Capital-market valuation narratives around “retail king”, “wealth bank”, “investment bank”, “private-enterprise bank” and “digital bank” should be treated as contemporaneous hypotheses rather than realized outcomes. [new_category; contemporaneous_judgment]
103. A veteran analyst would likely ask whether apparent NIM resilience came from temporary deposit-cost relief, asset mix, accounting classification, or genuine franchise power. [new_category; mechanism]
104. Another veteran question: did wealth AUM growth create durable fee economics after NAV transition, or did market volatility expose customers to losses that weakened trust? [new_category; mechanism]
105. Another negative-space lead is staff productivity and compensation: digitalization may reduce transaction workload but relationship managers, wealth advisers, technology staff and risk teams can keep operating costs high. [new_category; negative_space]
106. Another old-vocabulary lead: “零售转型”, “财富管理”, “大财富管理”, “轻型银行”, “交易银行”, “投商行一体化/商行+投行”, “普惠金融”, and “无还本续贷” should be verified institution-by-institution rather than treated as cohort-wide identical programs. [new_category; old_vocabulary]
107. Another uncertain fragment is whether some peers increasingly emphasized “财富管理银行” or “生态银行” language during this slice; preserve as a search lead, not a fact. [new_category; uncertain_fragment]
108. Do not import post-2022 property-resolution, later mortgage-repricing, later deposit-rate cuts or later NIM outcomes backward into 2020-2022 management expectations. [useful_refinement; counterexample]
