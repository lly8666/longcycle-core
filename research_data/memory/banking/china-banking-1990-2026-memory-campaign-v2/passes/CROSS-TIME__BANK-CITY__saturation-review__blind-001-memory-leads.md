## Saturation challenge result

The negative-space challenge does **not** support a low-novelty confirmation. A material cross-time lineage gap emerged: a repeated **multi-city / province-level consolidation archetype** in which several municipal city-commercial-bank predecessors are combined into a wider regional bank, sometimes followed by later absorption of additional weak city banks. This lineage differs structurally from both (a) one municipal bank organically becoming a strong regional franchise and (b) one weak bank receiving a standalone recapitalization. It changes the meaning of institution age, branch footprint, historical NPL stock, shareholder structure, local-government coordination, operating integration and later consolidation statistics. The follow-up should therefore target this archetype only and keep the low-novelty counter at 0/3.

## B. Failed, restructured and absorbed institutions

21. **[new]** Baoshang Bank remains a central city-bank failure/resolution counterexample: it should be treated as a governance/related-party/funding-confidence tail case, not representative of all city banks.
22. **[refinement]** Its market importance is larger than its balance-sheet size because the event changed counterparty assumptions in wholesale funding and exposed heterogeneity among smaller banks.
23. **[new]** Bank of Jinzhou is recalled as a severe governance/asset-quality/recapitalization case in which strategic/state-linked support and balance-sheet repair became central; exact sequence requires Evidence.
24. **[new]** Shengjing Bank is recalled as another governance/shareholder/property-era stress case relevant to the boundary between weak franchise, related-party exposure and local-state support.
25. **[new]** Harbin Bank is recalled as a useful Northeast counterexample where regional macro weakness, governance, cross-border/Russia adjacency and asset quality can interact differently from coastal strong-bank narratives.
26. **[new]** Dalian Bank is recalled as another Northeast/local-governance case worth testing for ownership changes, asset-quality repair and strategic repositioning.
27. **[new]** Troubled Liaoning local banks illustrate that “city bank” weakness can become a province-level restructuring problem rather than remain a municipal problem.
28. **[new]** Some absorbed city banks may vanish without a dramatic failure label; merger into a regional platform can be a resolution mechanism even when formal insolvency language is absent.
29. **[refinement]** “Merged” must therefore not be coded automatically as “healthy strategic combination”; it may embed recapitalization, bad-asset transfer, governance repair or regulatory pressure.
30. **[refinement]** Conversely, recapitalization does not imply failure if a growing regional bank raised capital to support expansion; context is essential.
31. **[new]** Local AMC involvement, state-owned enterprise capital and provincial financial-holding structures are plausible recurring counterparties in weak-city-bank repair, but exact cases and roles need Evidence.
32. **[new]** Resolution may split legacy assets, deposits, branches, employees and legal claims across different receiving entities; successor-bank continuity is not always one-to-one.
33. **[new]** A failed institution's deposit franchise can survive while equity and subordinated capital are restructured, so “bank disappearance” is not equal to “customer franchise disappearance.”
34. **[new]** Predecessor and successor names are therefore essential search keys for later Evidence work.

36. **[new]** Bank of Nanjing is recalled as another strong regional counterexample with capital-market/treasury sophistication and cross-regional expansion distinct from weak-bank repair cases.
37. **[new]** Bank of Hangzhou is recalled as a strong Yangtze-delta regional franchise useful for testing whether high local private-enterprise density can support differentiated SME capabilities.
38. **[new]** Bank of Chengdu is recalled as a strong western-city-bank counterexample with local public-sector and regional-economy advantages that do not map neatly onto coastal private-enterprise models.
39. **[new]** Bank of Changsha is recalled as another locally strong franchise whose profitability/risk outcomes may reflect a favorable home-region deposit and public-sector ecology.
40. **[new]** Bank of Qingdao is recalled as a listed coastal city bank with foreign/strategic-investor history and a different governance path from inland municipal banks.
41. **[new]** Xiamen Bank is recalled as a useful ownership/governance counterexample because Taiwan-related/foreign strategic shareholder participation may have been more salient than in many peers; exact history requires Evidence.
42. **[new]** Bank of Beijing and Bank of Shanghai remain essential early large-city-bank archetypes: metropolitan scale, government/corporate deposits and early modernization gave them paths different from later province-level rollups.
43. **[refinement]** Strong-bank status should not be inferred from listing alone; listing can coexist with concentrated local exposures, valuation pressure and governance episodes.
44. **[new]** A “stable-low-return” city bank can be neither distressed nor elite: conservative local public-sector lending, high liquidity and limited fee businesses may produce resilience with modest ROE.
45. **[new]** Such middle-cohort banks are important negative-space cases because analyst narratives often over-sample listed winners and failed outliers.
46. **[refinement]** Regional macro beta can dominate management quality in some periods; strong governance does not immunize a bank from weak local credit demand or falling collateral values.

## D. Shareholders, governance and control discontinuities

47. **[new]** City-bank ownership often combines municipal/provincial state entities, local SOEs, private industrial groups and employee/legacy shareholders; the balance among them can shift sharply across recapitalizations.
48. **[new]** A private industrial shareholder can create related-party/concentration risk even when formal board structures look conventional.
49. **[new]** A municipal state shareholder can stabilize funding/confidence yet also reinforce policy-directed lending and soft-budget expectations.
50. **[refinement]** State ownership is not a single governance category: municipal SASAC-type capital, provincial financial-holding entities and centrally owned investors can imply different control incentives.
51. **[new]** Strategic investors can be temporary governance bridges before listing or recapitalization; their later dilution/exit can change incentives without changing the bank name.
52. **[new]** Management turnover following governance cases may matter more than headline ownership percentages for credit-culture repair.
53. **[new]** Discipline/anti-corruption investigations can signal governance discontinuity but cannot be mapped mechanically to asset-quality deterioration without Evidence.
54. **[refinement]** Related-party lending should be separated from ordinary local concentration: both can involve the same corporate groups, but the control mechanism differs.
55. **[new]** Province-level merger platforms can dilute any one municipal shareholder while increasing provincial coordination; that is a real control transition, not just bigger geographic scope.
56. **[new]** Integration of compensation, approval authorities, internal audit and branch-president appointment rules is a key post-merger variable that legal-entity data will not show.

## E. Cross-region expansion and retrenchment

57. **[refinement]** The earlier wave of city-bank cross-region expansion created a tension between growth diversification and loss of local information advantage.
58. **[new]** Out-of-province branches can import competition for deposits but also raise funding costs if the bank lacks a recognizable brand outside its home market.
59. **[new]** Cross-region corporate lending can reduce one-city concentration while increasing adverse selection because the bank competes without local informational rents.
60. **[new]** Later regulatory preference for returning to local service can make historic branch maps misleading: the relevant story may be expansion, freeze, retrenchment and re-localization rather than monotonic growth.
61. **[refinement]** Branch closure/reduction after digitalization should be separated from regulatory geographic retrenchment; both reduce physical footprint for different reasons.
62. **[new]** Province-level consolidation is another way to “expand geography” without classic cross-region branching, reinforcing why it deserves a separate lineage category.

## F. Deposit, wholesale funding and wealth transitions

63. **[refinement]** City-bank liability models range from sticky municipal/corporate settlement deposits to aggressive retail time deposits and wholesale NCD/interbank funding; averages conceal this dispersion.
64. **[new]** Government-related fiscal and quasi-fiscal deposits can look cheap but may be concentrated, seasonal and relationship-dependent rather than permanently core.
65. **[new]** Strong local payroll/card/merchant ecosystems can turn physical regional density into durable low-cost deposits; this is a franchise variable, not simply branch count.
66. **[refinement]** NCD and interbank access became both a growth enabler and a market-confidence sensor; weak-bank events can widen spreads before retail deposits visibly move.
67. **[new]** Wealth-management migration can either weaken deposits or strengthen customer retention depending on whether AUM stays within the bank group/distribution ecosystem.
68. **[refinement]** Net-value WMP transition changes fee/AUM economics and should not be read as a one-for-one decline in customer franchise.
69. **[new]** Province-level merger banks may initially inherit heterogeneous deposit pricing and product systems, creating internal transfer-pricing and customer-migration frictions.

## G. Treasury, non-standard assets and asset mix

70. **[refinement]** Some city banks used investment/treasury books to compensate for limited local loan demand or to accelerate balance-sheet growth; economic credit exposure may exceed reported loan exposure.
71. **[new]** Bond investment, funds, trust-beneficiary-type exposures and other receivable/investment categories can migrate across accounting labels while preserving similar underlying credit risk.
72. **[refinement]** A fall in “non-standard” assets can reflect repayment, sale, securitization, reclassification or on-balance-sheet loan conversion; those outcomes are economically different.
73. **[new]** Post-merger regional banks may centralize treasury more quickly than they centralize credit culture, creating an uneven integration sequence.
74. **[new]** Treasury competence can be a genuine strong-bank differentiator, but duration gains in falling-rate periods should not be mistaken for permanent franchise superiority.

## H. Property, LGFV and local-fiscal feedback

75. **[refinement]** City banks face a structural correlation problem: local government, LGFV, property developers, construction firms, SME suppliers and household mortgages can all load on the same regional land/property cycle.
76. **[new]** Province-level diversification reduces single-city concentration only partly because provincial property and fiscal cycles remain correlated.
77. **[refinement]** Debt resolution can lower near-term default probability while extending duration and reducing yield, turning a credit event into an ALM/profitability problem.
78. **[new]** Local-state recapitalization of a bank and local-state support of its borrowers can be financially linked through the same constrained fiscal base.
79. **[new]** This creates a feedback loop in weak regions: fiscal stress weakens borrowers, weak banks need support, and support capacity itself depends on the local fiscal economy.

## I. Branch, network, digital and operating-model semantics

80. **[new]** Branch count should be decomposed into inherited merger branches, organic new branches, relocated branches and digitally substituted branches.
81. **[new]** Post-merger duplicate outlets can create a temporary cost overhang even when strategic consolidation is sound.
82. **[refinement]** Digital acquisition can lower unit servicing cost while worsening customer stickiness or credit vintage quality if pricing is the primary acquisition tool.
83. **[new]** City banks may use local-government service portals, payroll, social-security or merchant ecosystems as digital distribution moats; the moat is institutional connectivity, not app quality alone.
84. **[refinement]** Wealth/digital transformation should therefore be judged on deposit retention, cross-sell, risk-adjusted acquisition and service cost, not downloads or registered-user counts.
