## Pricing, deposits and FTP

1. **[useful_refinement][high] Rate liberalization should be tested through realized customer pricing, not announcement chronology.** The useful bank-level question is whether new and repricing loans actually moved toward risk/relationship pricing and whether branches gained or lost discretion as benchmark constraints weakened.

2. **[new_category][high] Deposit beta was probably segmented by customer and product.** Payroll/transaction retail balances, affluent wealth customers, large corporates and public-sector deposits can respond differently to rate competition; one average deposit cost can hide major franchise differences.

3. **[new_category] FTP must price more than a nominal funding rate.** Term, liquidity value, behavioral maturity and optionality of deposits matter when internal transfer prices determine whether a branch deposit or loan adds economic value.

4. **[new_category][high] Asset mix can offset or amplify pricing reform.** A shift toward lower-risk mortgages or high-quality corporate clients can compress asset yield even if risk pricing improves within each product, so yield decline is not direct proof of poor pricing discipline.

5. **[useful_refinement] Legacy benchmark language can persist after formal liberalization.** Customer negotiations, contracts and management reporting may continue using “benchmark plus/minus” conventions while LPR or market-reference concepts develop alongside them.

6. **[new_category] Relationship pricing can deliberately underprice a loan to win deposits, settlement, payroll, custody or fee business.** Loan spread alone therefore may understate total client profitability, especially for strategic SOE and institutional customers.

7. **[new_category][high] WMP competition can raise the economic cost of deposits without appearing entirely in deposit interest expense.** A bank may retain customer assets by moving them into wealth products, paying higher product yields and earning fees while losing conventional deposit funding.

8. **[new_category] Average daily deposits versus reporting-date deposits is a stronger liability-quality test than point-in-time balances alone.** Period-end gathering can distort apparent franchise stability even without any accounting violation.

9. **[new_category][high] Counterexample: perceived state-bank safety can create a structural funding premium even in a liberalizing market.** Customers may accept lower yields for convenience, payment functionality or perceived security, allowing large state banks to defend NIM better than institutions reliant on rate-sensitive wholesale funds.

## Asset-quality recognition and loss-flow challenge

10. **[new_category][high] NPL formation is a flow, ending NPL is a stock.** If a bank recognizes large new NPLs and simultaneously writes off, disposes or cures loans, year-end NPL balance can look stable while the credit-cost burden is materially higher.

11. **[useful_refinement][high] Special-mention loans are a leading-state category, not merely a weaker version of NPL.** Migration from normal to watch/special-mention and onward to NPL can reveal deterioration before headline NPL ratios peak.

12. **[new_category][high] Overdue status and regulatory classification can diverge.** Payment delinquency, borrower cash-flow weakness, collateral, guarantees and restructuring can place economically similar loans in different reported buckets; historical ratios require definition-aware interpretation.

13. **[new_category][high] Restructuring creates a recognition fork.** Extending tenor, reducing rates, changing repayment schedules or adding sponsor support may be economically sensible or may postpone loss recognition. The key Evidence question later is how the bank classified and provisioned the modified exposure.

14. **[new_category][high] Gross disposal activity is an independent measure of stress-management capacity.** Write-offs, cash recoveries, collateral realization and asset sales can reduce reported NPL stock but also demonstrate that the bank is actively crystallizing losses rather than indefinitely rolling them.

15. **[useful_refinement][high] Provision coverage and current-period credit cost can move in opposite directions.** A bank can consume a large historical reserve buffer while current impairment expense rises, or rebuild reserves after accelerated recognition; reserve stock and provision flow must stay separate.

16. **[new_category][high] Pre-provision operating profit is a loss-absorption concept.** Strong core revenue and cost control can allow a bank to recognize more credit losses without immediate capital distress, so slower net-profit growth can coexist with improving balance-sheet transparency.

17. **[new_category] RWA density can change even without balance-sheet shrinkage.** Moving from corporate loans into mortgages, government bonds or different investment assets can alter capital intensity, meaning asset growth and capital consumption need not move one-for-one.

18. **[new_category][high] Regional and sector NPL formation can be more informative than a national bank average.** Overcapacity provinces, export/private-enterprise clusters or property-sensitive regions may deteriorate while stronger regions dilute the headline ratio.

19. **[new_category][high] Cohort analysis separates legacy-vintage cleanup from current underwriting.** A rising NPL ratio driven mainly by older stimulus-era borrowers implies a different control diagnosis from rising defaults among newly originated 2014–2016 loans.

20. **[new_category] Local and national bad-asset buyers create price-discovery as well as disposal capacity.** Sale prices, retained servicing, guarantees or recourse terms determine whether a transfer genuinely removes risk; the existence of a disposal channel alone does not prove full risk transfer.

## Overcapacity, SOE and private-enterprise restructuring

21. **[new_category][high] Capacity-reduction policy turns banks into restructuring creditors, not merely lenders.** For steel, coal and other overcapacity borrowers, banks may need to coordinate repayment extensions, asset sales, mergers, closures and new working-capital support while avoiding support for nonviable capacity.

22. **[new_category][high] Creditor-committee / bank-consortium coordination is a likely period-native mechanism.** Multiple banks exposed to the same large borrower can destroy value by racing to withdraw; coordinated creditor arrangements may stabilize viable firms while allocating restructuring burdens. Exact formal terminology and timing require Evidence.

23. **[new_category][high] Debt-to-equity conversion changes the form of risk more than it automatically eliminates risk.** Credit exposure may become equity, fund or implementation-vehicle exposure; loss absorption, valuation, control rights and exit timing determine whether economic risk truly declines.

24. **[new_category] Group support can make subsidiary default data misleading.** Strong SOE parents may inject funds, transfer assets or refinance weak subsidiaries, so stand-alone borrower weakness may surface as lower group returns rather than formal bank default.

25. **[new_category] Zombie-firm risk can manifest as low-return capital lock-up.** Continuous interest servicing through subsidies/refinancing may keep NPLs low while preventing balance-sheet reallocation to more productive borrowers.

26. **[useful_refinement][high] Private-enterprise guarantee chains are network exposures.** A bank that screens each obligor separately can still underestimate concentration if several borrowers guarantee one another or depend on the same local property/collateral cycle.

27. **[new_category][high] Branch geography can dominate legal borrower industry.** The same nominal sector may perform very differently across provinces because local fiscal capacity, commodity prices, export demand and property conditions affect borrowers and guarantors together.

## Local-government debt and loan-to-bond migration

28. **[useful_refinement][high] A local-debt swap is both a credit event and an asset-allocation event for the bank.** Replacing a platform loan with a government bond can improve legal seniority/transparency while changing yield, duration, liquidity and capital treatment.

29. **[new_category][high] Duration extension can be the hidden cost of risk reduction.** A safer local-government bond may mature later than the original loan and expose the bank to more interest-rate/duration risk even if expected credit loss falls.

30. **[new_category][high] Yield compression is not equivalent to economic loss.** Lower coupon income may be the price of moving from an opaque corporate/platform exposure to a more standardized government claim; profitability and credit-quality effects can move in opposite directions.

31. **[new_category] Local-government bonds can crowd into treasury portfolios rather than corporate-loan books.** A bank’s reported loan concentration may decline while its securities portfolio gains local-fiscal exposure, so cross-period exposure comparisons require asset-class aggregation.

32. **[new_category] Explicit local-government obligations and implicit LGFV support should remain separate.** A debt-swap program may formalize some liabilities while leaving other platform debts outside the converted perimeter; “local government exposure” is not one legal category.

33. **[new_category][high] Residual LGFVs after swaps may be the harder tail.** Exposures not converted could include commercially viable platforms, ineligible debt, projects with weak repayment sources or debts intended to remain corporate; their selection effect may matter more than aggregate platform balances.

34. **[duplicate][high] Counterexample: loan-to-bond migration can genuinely lower credit risk.** It is a mistake to interpret every reduction in loan yield or platform-loan balance as concealed stress; some conversion can improve claim quality and transparency even while returns fall.

## Property, mortgages and four-bank heterogeneity

35. **[new_category] Falling market rates create mortgage prepayment/repricing optionality.** Borrowers can seek cheaper financing or repay early when alternatives improve, so a long-duration mortgage’s realized yield depends on behavior as well as contractual rate.

36. **[new_category][high] Developer and mortgage books can decouple before they reconverge in a severe downturn.** Developers may face sales/refinancing stress while household mortgage performance remains strong; treating them as one property-risk pool can obscure the early warning sequence.

37. **[useful_refinement][high] CCB is a useful housing-finance sensitivity test, not an automatic risk outlier.** Historical expertise can mean better underwriting and operational scale as well as greater concentration; both hypotheses need later bank-specific Evidence.

38. **[new_category] City-tier and regional mortgage vintages may matter more than national averages.** Down-payment behavior, price cycles, local income and transaction liquidity vary materially, so retail granularity does not eliminate geographic correlation.

## WMP, nonstandard and interbank role decomposition

39. **[new_category][high] A state bank can participate in shadow/nonstandard credit through several distinct roles.** Direct investor, WMP manager, distributor, custodian, originating bank, trust/securities counterparty and wholesale fund provider carry different credit, liquidity, capital and reputational exposures.

40. **[new_category][high] Look-through to the WMP/nonstandard underlying asset is essential.** A bond, trust beneficiary right, loan-like claim or money-market instrument can have very different duration and credit behavior even if all sit inside the same wealth-product label.

41. **[new_category] Distribution risk can exist without principal credit exposure.** A bank may sell a third-party or separately managed product while customers still infer endorsement from the branch/channel; legal nonrecourse and reputational expectations need separate fields.

42. **[new_category][high] Interbank-investment labels can conceal credit-like duration.** If an ostensibly interbank or investment claim ultimately funds a corporate/local project through another vehicle, counterparty form alone may understate the underlying credit concentration.

43. **[useful_refinement][high] Large state banks may be system liquidity providers more often than marginal wholesale-funding seekers.** Their deposit base can make them net lenders or stable counterparties to more market-funded banks, creating indirect exposure to the same ecosystem without the same funding fragility.

44. **[new_category] Receivable/investment-account labels require accounting-semantic caution.** A credit-like asset outside “loans and advances” may follow different disclosure, impairment or capital conventions; exact line-item names by bank/year should be sourced later.

45. **[new_category][high] WMP maturity transformation and implicit support can create a bank-liquidity tail even when the asset is off balance sheet.** If customers expect repayment and underlying assets are illiquid, reputational rescue pressure can reconnect product liquidity to the sponsoring bank.

46. **[new_category] Wealth-product yield competition can transmit market rates into retail relationships faster than deposit-rate repricing.** WMP advertised yields may therefore be an economically relevant funding/customer-retention benchmark even when they are not deposit interest rates.

47. **[duplicate] Counterexample: state-bank involvement does not imply joint-stock-bank-style leverage.** Large deposit franchises and different growth incentives can make the same legal channel economically smaller or less funding-critical at ICBC/CCB/BOC/ABC than at faster-growing peers.

## Digital-channel economics and dead ends

48. **[new_category][high] Payment and internet-money platforms competed for the customer interface before they necessarily displaced the bank balance sheet.** A customer could keep funds ultimately within the banking system while the technology platform captured transaction frequency, data and front-end loyalty.

49. **[new_category] Bank-owned e-commerce or platform experiments are useful failed-strategy leads.** Large banks could try to replicate marketplace ecosystems using merchant/client relationships, but branch trust and balance-sheet scale did not guarantee consumer-platform network effects.

50. **[new_category] Branch transformation changes cost allocation rather than simply closing branches.** Moving routine transactions to mobile channels can turn branches toward sales, advice, SME relationship work and wealth service; productivity should be measured per customer/revenue, not only transaction count.

51. **[useful_refinement] ABC’s rural network makes digital economics unusually two-sided.** Mobile channels may cut servicing cost across dispersed counties while physical outlets still carry inclusion, cash and local-information functions that cannot be removed as quickly as urban branches.

52. **[new_category] Early fintech data competition challenges proprietary-information advantages.** Payment/e-commerce platforms can observe transaction behavior that traditional banks may not see, potentially improving small-borrower screening outside the large-bank branch model even before platform lending reaches later scale.

## Capital, liquidity, cross-border and heterogeneity checks

53. **[new_category][high] Capital-instrument diversification changes loss-absorption hierarchy.** Preference-share/other additional-capital instruments can support regulatory buffers without the same economics or voting rights as common equity; total capital should not be treated as homogeneous.

54. **[useful_refinement][high] Liquidity regulation makes asset liquidity and runoff assumptions explicit.** A deposit-rich bank can still face maturity or stress constraints; high-quality liquid assets, wholesale outflows and operational deposits matter beyond an old loan-to-deposit lens.

55. **[useful_refinement][high] BOC’s overseas/FX book is a structural heterogeneity test.** Currency, country, offshore funding and trade-finance channels can change both earnings and risk even when domestic state-bank policy exposure looks similar to peers.

56. **[new_category] ICBC’s scale can make operational and model governance a first-order risk-control issue.** Centralized standards must still work across enormous branch, subsidiary and overseas footprints; scale benefits in data/technology coexist with implementation complexity.

57. **[useful_refinement] ABC’s rural mission remains a counterweight to purely market-priced allocation.** County deposits, agriculture-related borrowers and inclusion goals can alter pricing, cost and risk economics relative to urban peers even under the same formal capital/prudential framework.

58. **[new_category][high] Cross-bank asset-quality differences may reflect portfolio mix, geography, recognition speed and disposal capacity simultaneously.** A lower NPL ratio is not automatically better underwriting; it must be decomposed alongside special-mention migration, credit cost, write-offs, sector mix and reserve buffers.
