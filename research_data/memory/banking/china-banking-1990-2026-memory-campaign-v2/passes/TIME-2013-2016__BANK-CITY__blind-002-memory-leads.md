## Orthogonal question

Test whether liquidity/maturity transformation or ownership/related-party control failures constitute a second period-wide structural category, rather than amplifiers inside the same city-bank rapid-growth model.

## Funding decomposition

- A city bank with nominally strong deposit growth may still have weak marginal funding quality if growth is bought through high-rate term deposits or market liabilities.
- Sticky payroll/settlement/public-service deposits should be separated from promotional deposits and institutional money that can move quickly.
- NCD and interbank funding can be complementary rather than substitutes; both widen marginal balance-sheet capacity while increasing market-price sensitivity.
- Repo dependence introduces collateral and haircut mechanics that differ from unsecured interbank borrowing.
- Wholesale funding concentration by counterparty can matter as much as total wholesale share.
- Large-bank and policy-bank counterparties may function as liquidity anchors, but this is relationship-dependent and should not be assumed universal.
- 2013 money-market stress tests not only funding cost but intraday liquidity, collateral mobilization and treasury centralization.
- Head-office treasury control may be decisive for whether branches can create hidden liquidity mismatches through local products or off-balance-sheet promises.

## Maturity transformation

- Shorter-duration market funding paired with longer-duration credit-like investment assets creates rollover risk even when accounting liquidity ratios look acceptable.
- WMP maturities can disguise longer underlying borrower duration if products are rolled repeatedly.
- Expected bank support can economically reconnect nominally off-balance-sheet products to the bank during stress.
- Investment-receivable or trust-beneficiary-right assets can be harder to monetize than high-quality bonds despite being booked within investment portfolios.
- Liquidity buffers should be assessed by unencumbered quality, not simply asset volume.
- Market liquidity and funding liquidity can reinforce each other adversely when credit-like investments lack transparent prices.
- A funding shock can force asset compression before credit losses crystallize, so liquidity stress need not imply immediate insolvency.

## Credit migration across forms

- Direct loan ceilings or risk limits can coexist with continued economic credit creation through trust, securities, WMP and investment-account channels.
- Borrower-level aggregation is therefore more informative than product-level accounting categories.
- Property/LGFV concentrations can be fragmented across loans, bonds, trust plans, receivables and guarantees.
- Credit migration can weaken conventional NPL early-warning systems if non-loan assets are not subject to identical classification routines.
- Channel counterparties can obscure ultimate borrower concentration and create multiple layers of guarantees or repurchase commitments.
- Repurchase or liquidity-support clauses may transform nominal investments into bank-like credit exposure.
- Wealth-management distribution can create reputational obligations even without formal guarantees.

## Ownership and control

- Local-government ownership can provide recapitalization/support capacity while simultaneously encouraging local concentration and policy-sensitive lending.
- Private/industrial shareholders can diversify capital sources but create related-party lending, tunneling or influence risks if governance is weak.
- Mixed ownership should be decomposed by effective control, board influence, connected borrowers and management appointment power.
- Formal board structures do not guarantee independent credit decisions if shareholder/local-government influence persists informally.
- Strategic investors may improve systems/processes without resolving incentive conflicts at branches or shareholder-related borrowers.
- Listing disclosure can increase transparency while rapid growth targets still create pressure to stretch risk limits.
- Executive discipline/corruption cases may indicate control failures, but case incidence is not equivalent to balance-sheet-wide fraud.

## Later-troubled-bank antecedent challenge

- Later-problem city banks may show early combinations of opaque ownership, connected lending, fast market-funded growth and non-standard assets.
- Baoshang-, Jinzhou- and other later stress cases are useful antecedent search keys, but exact institution facts, timing and causal links belong to Evidence.
- These cases may be extreme tails of a broader governance/funding continuum rather than proof that all city banks shared a separate fraud-driven model.
- Qilu-type case risk reinforces that operational fraud can bypass conventional credit metrics, but it remains a control dimension rather than a universal period-wide earnings model.
- Regional banks with conservative local franchises provide counterexamples to any claim that shadow/wholesale expansion was universal.

## Capital and recognition

- RWA growth may lag or differ from economic-risk growth when exposures migrate across accounting forms.
- Headline capital ratios can be preserved through fresh equity, subordinated instruments, slower RWA recognition or retained earnings; these mechanisms have different quality.
- Low reported NPLs can coexist with rising overdue/special-mention/restructured exposures.
- Provision coverage can remain high mechanically when the recognized NPL denominator is small.
- Credit costs may appear benign until refinancing conditions tighten or asset classifications catch up.
- Capital pressure from rapid growth is therefore a stock-flow issue: fresh capital can support continued expansion without proving underlying asset quality.

## Deposit-insurance / franchise angle

- Deposit insurance reduces tail fear for covered retail deposits but does not make wholesale funding indifferent to bank credit quality.
- Strong local franchise and government reputation may continue to dominate depositor behavior during this period.
- Deposit competition after rate liberalization can increase liability cost even while deposit insurance reduces perceived default risk.
- Banks without sticky transaction accounts may respond with higher-priced funding or shadow-credit retrenchment.

## Operational integration / branch controls

- Cross-region branches can create data-lag and authorization problems if core systems are not truly centralized.
- Central credit committees can be circumvented through product reclassification, guarantees or channel structures unless exposure aggregation is borrower-based.
- Treasury centralization becomes more valuable as products span deposits, interbank, WMP and investment books.
- Internal transfer pricing is a key hidden mechanism: cheap internal liquidity allocation can encourage branch growth even if group-level funding is expensive.
- Risk limits need to cover economic substance across on/off-balance-sheet products; siloed limits invite migration.

## Regional heterogeneity / counterexamples

- Rich coastal or provincial-capital banks may possess genuinely sticky corporate/retail deposits and better absorb liberalization.
- Resource- or investment-heavy regions may face concentrated platform/corporate cycles and weaker diversification.
- Some city banks may remain mostly local and traditional, providing a counterexample to a universal cross-region/NCD/shadow model.
- Strong listing status is not a sufficient proxy for low risk; weak banks can also be locally well supported for long periods.
- Conversely, reliance on market funding is not automatically distress if liquidity buffers, asset quality and governance are strong.

## Metric-semantic cautions

- Wholesale-funding share must define whether NCDs, interbank placements and repos are all included.
- WMP and non-standard measures can be issuance, outstanding balance, underlying assets or bank-retained exposure; they are not interchangeable.
- 'Investment assets' can range from government bonds to opaque credit-like receivables.
- Liquidity ratios may not capture encumbrance, collateral eligibility, maturity ladders or committed reputational support.
- Ownership concentration should distinguish nominal shareholder percentage from effective control.
- Cross-region diversification should be measured by actual risk/funding distribution, not branch count alone.

## Contemporaneous expectations / falsification

- Expectation: market-based funding would make strong city banks more scalable and sophisticated.
- Falsification lead: scalability can come with rollover and margin sensitivity.
- Expectation: shadow/channel structures diversify products and earnings.
- Falsification lead: the same local credit risk can merely migrate into harder-to-read forms.
- Expectation: listing and diversified ownership improve governance.
- Falsification lead: effective control and related-party incentives can survive formal reform.
- Expectation: low NPL plus high provision coverage demonstrates superior quality.
- Falsification lead: delayed recognition, refinancing and denominator growth can produce the same optics.

## Decision

No second period-wide structural category emerged. Liquidity/maturity transformation is a core transmission mechanism of the same rapid-growth funding model; ownership/related-party failures are an important governance/control negative-space dimension with extreme-case tails, but not a separate period-wide city-bank business model. TIME-2013-2016 can be treated as broad+orthogonal complete for blind-memory planning, with institution-specific cases reserved for Evidence and later cross-time saturation challenges.
