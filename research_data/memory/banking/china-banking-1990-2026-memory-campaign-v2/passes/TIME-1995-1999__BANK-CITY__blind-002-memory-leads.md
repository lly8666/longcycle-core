## Orthogonal challenge: legal form and lineage

1. [new_category][high] “城市合作银行” and “城市商业银行” should be treated as distinct legal/name stages where applicable; the transition may occur at different times by city and must not be collapsed into one national switch.
2. [new_category] The legal successor bank may inherit some but not all urban credit cooperatives; failed, closed, carved-out or separately resolved entities can create lineage gaps.
3. [new_category] A preparation group or municipal consolidation office can be an important actor even when it never appears in later bank branding.
4. [new_category] The economic predecessor perimeter may be broader than the legal merger perimeter because excluded cooperatives can still leave depositor, borrower or local-government obligations around the new bank.
5. [new_category] Renaming from cooperative-bank language to commercial-bank language can signal governance/legal standardization without proving a clean change in risk culture.
6. [useful_refinement] Founding/opening/rename/approval dates should be independently tagged; one “established in” date is analytically unsafe.
7. [new_category] A city’s urban-credit system may include neighborhood cooperatives, union/federation entities, specialized local finance units and municipal support vehicles; later genealogies can simplify this diversity.
8. [new_category] Some later city-bank institutions may have crossed the 1999 boundary in legal form, meaning the 1995-1999 slice should include transition work even when mature branding came later.

## Municipal support versus soft-budget constraints

11. [new_category][high] Municipal support should be decomposed into capital injection, public-sector deposits, borrower coordination, asset transfers, political reassurance and liquidity facilitation; these mechanisms have different balance-sheet effects.
12. [new_category] A local government can strengthen depositor confidence while simultaneously encouraging credit to weak local enterprises, creating a support-risk feedback loop.
13. [new_category] Local SOE shareholders can function as nominal market shareholders while remaining administratively linked to the same municipal authority that influences lending.
14. [new_category] Related-party risk may arise through common local-government control even where direct equity ownership by a borrower is absent.
15. [new_category] Local capitalization quality should distinguish cash capital from asset contributions, receivables, land/property-linked assets or other less-liquid contributions; exact forms require later Evidence.
16. [new_category] A newly capitalized bank may still be economically undercapitalized if inherited losses are not fully recognized.
17. [new_category] Government-linked deposits can mask franchise weakness: funding stability may depend on administrative relationships rather than broad customer preference.
18. [new_category] Municipal rescue expectations can reduce near-term run risk but may weaken creditor discipline and borrower selection.
19. [new_category] City-bank governance in this period may combine company-law organs with cadre-style appointment, making formal board structure an incomplete indicator of control.
20. [useful_refinement] Governance reform should be evaluated by credit approval, related-party limits, branch authority and loss-recognition behavior—not only by articles of association.

## Inherited assets versus post-consolidation NPL formation

21. [new_category][high] The central measurement problem is stock versus flow: inherited problem loans can dominate early reported asset quality even if post-formation underwriting improves.
22. [new_category] Conversely, restructuring or rollover of inherited loans can delay recognition, making early new-bank vintage performance appear stronger than underlying borrower economics.
23. [new_category] New lending to refinance or support old local-enterprise exposures can blur inherited versus new NPL formation.
24. [new_category] Loan-growth denominator effects can temporarily reduce the reported bad-loan ratio without reducing the inherited bad-loan stock.
25. [new_category] Asset transfers before legal opening can make successor-bank reported ratios non-comparable with predecessor-system economic losses.
26. [new_category] A loan can move from urban cooperative books to the new bank after restructuring, changing legal creditor while preserving underlying risk.
27. [new_category] Local-enterprise reorganizations can break borrower-name continuity; debt may migrate to successor enterprises, asset-management entities or municipal holding companies.
28. [new_category] Guarantee substitution can make a weak borrower appear supported without extinguishing expected loss.
29. [new_category] Mutual guarantees among local enterprises may amplify correlated risk even where each borrower appears individually collateralized.
30. [new_category] Problem-asset work should separate principal delinquency, interest arrears, capitalized interest, restructured loans and administratively supported loans.
31. [useful_refinement] Any historical “NPL improvement” narrative should test whether classification became stricter at the same time, which can obscure genuine economic improvement.
32. [useful_refinement] Vintage analysis should distinguish predecessor-originated loans, transition-period bridge loans and post-consolidation new underwriting.

34. [new_category] Direct foreign-currency or offshore funding exposure should not be assumed material for most city banks; indirect borrower and confidence channels may dominate.
35. [new_category] Deflation and weak commodity/industrial prices can raise real debt burden for local SOEs and manufacturers even when nominal interest rates fall.

## Deposits, settlement franchise and liquidity

43. [new_category][high] Deposit confidence during consolidation is an independent mechanism: customers must accept account migration, new legal identity and branch/system changes while preserving access to funds.
44. [new_category] Municipal public-sector deposits can act as a confidence signal that attracts private deposits, not merely as a funding source.
45. [new_category] Corporate settlement business may be more durable than term-deposit price competition because payroll, collections and payments create operational switching costs even in the 1990s context.
46. [new_category] A fragmented predecessor network can have strong neighborhood deposits but weak centralized treasury management.
47. [new_category] Centralizing liquidity after consolidation may reveal branch-level funding deficits that were previously obscured by local bookkeeping or administrative support.
48. [new_category] Deposit concentration by municipal entities or a few local enterprises can make apparently stable funding vulnerable to fiscal or enterprise stress.
49. [new_category] Interbank borrowing should be separated from emergency/administrative liquidity support; the institutional channels and pricing signals differ.
50. [new_category] A solvent but illiquid city bank and an insolvent but administratively liquid bank require different diagnosis; historical narratives may blur them.
51. [new_category] A local government may protect depositor confidence without explicitly guaranteeing all liabilities; wording and legal status require Evidence.
52. [useful_refinement] Deposit growth should distinguish inherited balances transferred from cooperatives from genuinely new franchise acquisition.
53. [useful_refinement] Funding cost comparisons should not use later marketized-deposit concepts mechanically because administered-rate regimes and relationship services dominate.
