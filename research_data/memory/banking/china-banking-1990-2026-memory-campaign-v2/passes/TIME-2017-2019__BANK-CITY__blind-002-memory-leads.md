## Orthogonal question

Test whether weak-bank governance/resolution forms a second period-wide structural category in 2017-2019, or remains an extreme-tail and transmission manifestation of the same wholesale-funding, shadow-asset unwind and recognition regime shift.

## Wholesale funding segmentation

- A weak-bank event can change pricing and availability of NCDs before retail deposit behavior changes materially.
- Counterparty limits are institution-specific: banks can lose unsecured interbank lines while still retaining repo access against sufficiently accepted collateral.
- Repo funding depends on collateral eligibility, haircut and counterparty appetite; aggregate interbank-liability data can hide this hierarchy.
- NCD spread widening can be a market-confidence signal rather than a direct indication of deposit outflow.
- Stronger listed city banks with transparent capital, diversified deposits and credible local support can remain well funded even as weaker regional issuers are repriced.
- The Baoshang-type shock therefore appears more like a segmentation catalyst than proof of universal city-bank insolvency.
- Wholesale market segmentation can become self-reinforcing when funding costs reduce profitability and force asset sales or slower refinancing.

## Retail and local-franchise contrast

- Local payroll, settlement, public-service and retail deposits can remain sticky while institutional funding becomes volatile.
- Home-city franchise value rises when market funding becomes credit-sensitive.
- Administrative/public-sector deposits can be stable but concentrated; their stability depends on local fiscal and political relationships.
- Banks with broad household/SME transaction accounts have a different liquidity profile from banks whose apparent deposits are mostly large corporate or institutional money.
- Deposit insurance can limit retail panic without preventing wholesale investors from differentiating issuers.

## Weak-bank ownership and governance tails

- Opaque private or industrial-group control can create connected borrowers, pledged-share complications and influence over credit decisions.
- Local-government ownership can increase rescue capacity but also create incentives to delay recognition of local borrower stress.
- Mixed ownership is not equivalent to independent governance; effective control, management appointments and related-party networks matter more than nominal shareholder diversity.
- Fraud/case-risk can bypass normal borrower underwriting and create losses not visible in standard sector-concentration metrics.
- Later restructuring of a bank can reveal that prior capital, profit or NPL measures did not capture effective shareholder extraction or hidden connected exposure.
- However, these governance failures are concentrated tails: conservative or strong city banks provide clear counterexamples.

## Resolution and support mechanics

- Support can arrive through local-government capital, state-asset shareholders, strategic investors, asset transfers, management replacement or combinations thereof.
- Formal failure is not necessary for economic resolution; a bank can remain open while ownership/control and balance-sheet quality are substantially reset.
- Local-government willingness and fiscal capacity to support a bank vary across regions.
- Resolution can protect depositors while imposing more differentiation on shareholders, creditors or market counterparties; exact treatment is an Evidence question.
- Market participants may infer support strength from ownership and regional importance, but those expectations are not guarantees.
- A weak-bank rescue can temporarily stabilize its funding while still raising funding premia for opaque peers.

## WMP / non-standard unwind versus recognition

- Shadow-asset contraction does not make underlying credit disappear; exposures can run off, refinance, move onto balance sheet or crystallize as losses.
- Long-duration non-standard assets are difficult to shrink quickly if borrower cash flow is weak.
- WMP transition can expose valuation and liquidity gaps when old pooled-product rollover assumptions weaken.
- On-balance-sheet migration can raise reported loans, RWA and provisioning even without new economic credit creation.
- Asset sales/transfers can reduce balance-sheet exposure but may embed discounts or deferred loss recognition.
- Standardization can improve transparency while lowering yields and challenging earnings models built around high-spread credit-like investments.

## NPL / provision / capital stock-flow mechanics

- Slower denominator growth can mechanically raise NPL ratios even if gross new-NPL formation is unchanged.
- Tightened recognition can simultaneously increase NPLs and provisions, making a one-period deterioration partly semantic/timing-related.
- Write-offs reduce NPL stock but consume reserves and can hide the scale of gross formation if only ending balances are observed.
- Asset transfers can lower recognized NPLs while shifting economic loss to sale discounts or external vehicles.
- Fresh equity can restore capital without resolving weak governance or poor asset quality.
- Capital instruments of different loss-absorption quality should not be treated as equivalent.
- RWA migration from shadow/investment structures back to ordinary credit exposure can make capital ratios fall even before cash losses rise.

## Private-enterprise and guarantee-chain transmission

- Deleveraging can tighten refinancing for private firms that previously used trusts, WMP channels or mutual guarantees.
- Guarantee-chain defaults can create nonlinear local contagion across otherwise unrelated SME borrowers.
- City banks with genuine relationship information can outperform broad credit tightening, but local concentration still raises tail correlation.
- Risk migration from private borrowers toward SOE/LGFV borrowers can lower near-term default anxiety while increasing public-sector concentration.
- Private-enterprise stress therefore interacts with the funding/recognition regime rather than forming a separate bank-type story.

## Property and LGFV migration

- Shadow contraction may move property/LGFV exposure toward loans, bonds or standardized investments.
- Legacy projects still require refinancing even when new non-standard funding is curtailed.
- Strong local fiscal support can improve refinancing expectations but increases bank dependence on one local ecosystem.
- Property collateral values and land-related fiscal revenues remain correlated, so local bank asset and support capacity can weaken together.
- Recognition pressure can reveal previously rolled-over platform/property exposures as restructured or impaired assets.

## Cross-region retrenchment and strategy

- Remote branches that depended on wholesale funding or unfamiliar borrowers are more vulnerable to retrenchment than deposit-rich home-market operations.
- Head-office capital/liquidity allocation can favor core regions during stress.
- Cross-region diversification may prove shallow if exposures were concentrated in similar platform/property/private-enterprise themes.
- Stronger city banks can continue selected regional growth, offering a counterexample to universal retreat.
- Home-market depth becomes a strategic asset precisely because market funding no longer scales uniformly across issuers.

## Strong-bank counterexamples

- Some listed and well-governed city banks can preserve low funding stress, good asset quality and continued franchise growth through the same period.
- Strong local economies, diversified deposit franchises and more transparent ownership can materially differentiate outcomes.
- Market-funded growth is not automatically unsafe if matched by liquid assets, capital and credible controls.
- City-bank heterogeneity therefore increases rather than collapses into a single weak-bank category.

## Failed expectations and contemporaneous judgments

- Expectation: system support means bank-issued wholesale liabilities remain effectively homogeneous.
- Falsification lead: post-stress counterparty and NCD pricing can differentiate issuers sharply.
- Expectation: deleveraging automatically lowers bank risk.
- Falsification lead: recognition, refinancing and liquidity stress can rise during the transition.
- Expectation: local-government support is sufficient evidence of safety.
- Falsification lead: support capacity, governance quality and fiscal strength differ materially.
- Expectation: weak-bank cases reveal the true model of all city banks.
- Falsification lead: strong-bank counterexamples show they are tails of a heterogeneous sector, not a universal business model.

## Metric-semantic cautions

- NCD issuance rates should be compared by tenor, date and issuer quality; simple averages can obscure stress.
- Interbank exposure must separate unsecured lines from repos and security holdings.
- 'Deposit stability' requires customer and concentration decomposition, not aggregate deposit growth alone.
- Resolution support can change ownership/effective control without changing the legal bank name.
- NPL stock, gross formation, write-offs and transfers are distinct flows.
- Capital raising and capital adequacy are not synonymous with economic solvency.
- Shadow-asset contraction must distinguish run-off, on-balance-sheet migration, sale and true borrower repayment.

## Decision

No second period-wide structural category emerged. Weak-bank governance and resolution are crucial extreme-tail mechanisms and trigger market credit segmentation, but strong city-bank counterexamples show they do not define the whole sector. The period-wide structural transition remains wholesale-funding differentiation plus WMP/non-standard unwind plus delayed-credit recognition/capital repair under deleveraging. TIME-2017-2019 can therefore be treated as broad+orthogonal complete for blind-memory planning; institution-specific resolution facts remain for Evidence and later cross-time negative-space review.
