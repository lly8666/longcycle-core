## 1. Material negative-space finding: the actor set is too narrow

The compact coverage map repeatedly uses ICBC, CCB, BOC and ABC as the heterogeneity frame. That is historically useful but materially incomplete for a shard defined as **large state-owned commercial banks**.

Two actors require explicit cross-time recovery:

- **Bank of Communications / 交通银行 / BoCom**: a state-controlled large commercial bank whose joint-stock and mixed-shareholder path differs materially from the original Big Four reform template.
- **Postal Savings Bank of China / 中国邮政储蓄银行 / PSBC**: a later large state-owned bank with a postal-savings lineage, nationwide postal/agency network and deposit-heavy/rural-retail business-model antecedent unlike either ABC or the other large banks.

This is not merely a missing bank name. It changes the comparative logic of ownership, reform path, branch/network economics, funding, strategic investors, listings, rural inclusion and later large-bank taxonomy. The shard therefore cannot count this pass as a low-novelty saturation confirmation.

## 2. Evolving taxonomy itself is a semantic gap

- The period likely moves from language centered on the **four state-owned commercial banks / 四大国有商业银行** toward **five** and later **six large state-owned commercial banks / 国有大型商业银行 / 大型商业银行**.
- “State-owned,” “state-controlled,” “joint-stock,” and “large commercial bank” are not mutually exclusive categories. BoCom is the strongest counterexample to treating joint-stock form as synonymous with the BANK-JOINT-STOCK shard.
- A bank-type taxonomy based only on legal corporate form can therefore misclassify BoCom relative to its state-control and large-bank role.
- PSBC creates a second taxonomy discontinuity: it became a commercial bank from a postal-savings institutional lineage rather than the professional-bank lineage of the Big Four.
- Future stitching needs point-in-time labels. A contemporary analyst in the 1990s would not use a 2020s “six large state-owned banks” frame unchanged backward.

Search-key leads: `四大国有商业银行`, `五大行`, `六大行`, `国有控股大型商业银行`, `大型商业银行`, `交通银行`, `邮政储蓄银行`.

## 3. Bank of Communications: selective antecedent and reform-path counterexample

- A selective pre-1990 antecedent appears necessary: BoCom was re-established before the primary horizon, plausibly in the late 1980s; the exact institutional date/wording should be verified later.
- Its early nationwide joint-stock structure is a counterexample to the idea that large state-bank commercialization began only with the Big Four's 2000s joint-stock conversions.
- State control and non-state/foreign shareholders can coexist. Ownership concentration, board rights and strategic-investor influence need to be distinguished.
- BoCom's reform/listing chronology appears earlier/different from some Big Four pathways; exact H-share/A-share dates are intentionally not asserted here.
- A major foreign strategic investor—HSBC is strongly recalled—belongs to the actor map. Exact stake, transaction date and governance rights require evidence.
- This makes BoCom a particularly useful test of the 2000s claim that strategic investors supplied governance, technology, risk-management or product capabilities rather than only certification/signaling.
- Mixed ownership did not necessarily mean foreign control. Board representation, minority rights and operational transfer are separate from ultimate control.
- Because BoCom was smaller than ICBC/CCB/ABC/BOC, scale economics, funding mix and corporate/retail franchise comparisons may differ from the dominant Big Four narrative.
- Shanghai and Yangtze-delta/corporate-commercial associations are plausible memory leads but require evidence before use as claims.

## 4. BoCom as a cross-time mechanism test

- **1990s:** already-commercial/joint-stock characteristics may make it a useful counterexample to the Big Four's first-level legal-person and policy/commercial separation narrative.
- **2000s:** mixed ownership, foreign strategic investment and listing can test whether governance reform mechanisms worked differently without the same Big Four sequencing.
- **2009–2012:** policy-credit expansion may still reach BoCom, but its balance-sheet scale and customer mix could make stimulus transmission different.
- **2013–2016:** rate liberalization and deposit competition may expose a different funding franchise from the largest deposit-rich banks.
- **2017–2019:** deleveraging/interbank/wealth transitions may be useful for testing whether “large state bank” automatically meant low wholesale-funding sensitivity.
- **2020–2026:** low-rate/NIM pressure could be more acute or differently buffered at a smaller large state bank; exact comparative margins are evidence questions.

These are targeted questions, not conclusions.

## 5. Postal savings is a missing institutional lineage

- PSBC requires a selective antecedent in **postal savings / 邮政储蓄**, whose deposit-mobilization network predates the commercial-bank entity.
- The key mechanism is not merely “another rural bank.” Postal outlets can provide an enormous geographic distribution network with economics different from bank-owned branches.
- A distinction between **self-operated bank outlets / 自营网点** and **agency/postal outlets / 代理网点** is a high-value future evidence key.
- If deposits are collected through an affiliated postal network, transfer pricing and agency fees/revenue sharing can materially affect the apparent cheapness of the deposit franchise.
- “Many deposits” therefore does not automatically imply “very low all-in funding/distribution cost.”
- Postal-savings origins may have initially produced a conservative asset-allocation model—large deposits with comparatively limited lending capability and more funds placed in safer/wholesale assets—before later expansion into a fuller loan franchise. Exact stages require evidence.
- This trajectory differs structurally from ABC's agricultural-policy/professional-bank lineage even though both have extensive county/rural reach.

Search-key leads: `邮政储蓄`, `邮储银行`, `邮银协同`, `代理网点`, `自营网点`, `代理费`, `县域`, `三农`.

## 6. PSBC institutional transition and ownership questions

- The commercial bank entity is strongly recalled as being established in the 2000s, with later joint-stock restructuring and public listings; exact dates and transaction terms are intentionally deferred.
- China Post / 中国邮政集团 is a central connected actor. The parent-bank relationship matters for governance, distribution and agency economics.

## 7. PSBC as a business-model counterexample

- **Funding:** a huge retail deposit base can coexist with material distribution/agency expenses.
- **Asset mix:** a deposit-heavy institution may initially have lower loan-to-deposit intensity and more investment/interbank assets than mature universal banks; exact ratios require evidence.
- **Credit expansion:** moving from safer assets into retail, micro, agriculture or corporate lending changes credit-risk seasoning and operating requirements.
- **Rural inclusion:** postal reach can serve places where a conventional full-service bank branch is uneconomic, challenging a simple branch-closure/digitalization narrative.
- **Digitalization:** digital channels may complement rather than replace the postal physical network, especially for older or rural customers.
- **Profitability:** network scale can create customer reach while agency payments and servicing complexity reduce the apparent cost advantage.
- **Risk:** a historically conservative asset book is not proof of permanently lower risk once loan mix expands.
- **Counterexample:** PSBC can be state-owned and large without sharing the Big Four's 1990s NPL-cleanup/restructuring origin story.

## 8. Why ABC cannot substitute for PSBC in the coverage map

- ABC's rural/county role originates in a banking institution with corporate/agricultural credit functions; PSBC's distinctive antecedent is retail savings mobilization through the postal system.
- ABC branch assets and employees are not economically identical to postal agency outlets serving PSBC customers.
- Their deposit sources, agency-cost structure, credit capabilities and governance histories can therefore diverge even in the same county.
- Treating “rural footprint” as one category risks missing institutional architecture, not merely bank-level degree differences.

## 10. Forgotten governance/control mechanisms to retain

- “Mixed ownership” should not be used as a causal synonym for “better governance.” Decision rights, incentives, minority-shareholder protections, board practice and state strategic objectives remain separate.
- Foreign strategic-investor participation can deliver capability transfer, certification, market access or signaling in different proportions; BoCom offers an independent case to test this.
- Postal parent/agency dependence can create a different related-party/service-contract governance issue from ordinary bank branch control.
- A network controlled partly outside the bank legal entity creates operational-risk and customer-service accountability questions that a wholly bank-owned branch network does not.

## 11. Metric-semantic discontinuities exposed by the missing actors

- Branch count is not comparable if one bank reports/uses large numbers of agency outlets alongside self-operated outlets.
- Deposit cost should distinguish interest paid to customers from distribution/agency expense where relevant.
- Loan-to-deposit ratio can be especially misleading when comparing a historically deposit-mobilizing institution with universal banks.
- Asset quality comparisons need seasoning: a newer loan book can display low NPLs before vintages mature.
- Group customer reach, bank-owned outlets and affiliated-network access are different metrics.
- State ownership percentage, state control and shareholder diversity should not be collapsed into one ownership variable.
- “Big Four,” “Big Five” and “Big Six” are time-dependent peer sets, not timeless categories.

## 12. Contemporaneous expectations worth recovering later

- BoCom's foreign strategic investor may have been expected to accelerate governance/risk/product modernization; later evidence should distinguish expectations from realized outcomes.
- PSBC's postal deposit franchise may have been viewed as unusually stable/low-cost, while later commercial-bank transformation required finding profitable assets and building lending/risk capability.
- Public listing may have been expected to strengthen market discipline for both institutions; the interaction with continuing state control is a useful Judgment-versus-Outcome question.
- Digital finance may have been expected to make physical networks less valuable, while postal/rural service could provide a counterexample where physical reach retains strategic value.

## 13. Failed shortcuts and counterexamples

- `joint-stock bank` does not automatically mean BANK-JOINT-STOCK if the period-native institution is a state-controlled large commercial bank; BoCom forces a taxonomy check.
- `rural large bank` does not make PSBC equivalent to ABC; their institutional lineages and network contracts differ.
- `large retail deposits` do not prove low all-in funding cost when agency/distribution economics are material.
- `foreign strategic investor` does not prove transfer of control or successful capability transfer.
- `later entrant` does not imply small-bank economics: PSBC can become systemically large through a pre-existing postal network.
- `all state banks underwent the same AMC/Huijin/listing sequence` is an invalid universalization; BoCom and PSBC are structural counterexamples.
