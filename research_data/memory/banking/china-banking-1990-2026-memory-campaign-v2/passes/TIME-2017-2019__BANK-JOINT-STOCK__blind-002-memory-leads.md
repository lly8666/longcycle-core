## Independent orthogonal challenge

### Genuine deleveraging versus migration
1. [new_category][high] The first question is whether shrinking interbank/nonstandard balances reduced borrower credit risk, reduced liquidity transformation, or merely moved exposure into loans, bonds or other standardized investments.
2. [new_category] True deleveraging should be tested through gross credit exposure, leverage, maturity mismatch, funding tenor and RWA rather than one accounting caption.
3. [new_category] Legacy assets can run off naturally while new standardized credit replaces them, producing apparent structural change without total credit contraction.
4. [new_category] Asset-sale or transfer economics differ from simple runoff because retained servicing, guarantees or reputational expectations may remain.
5. [useful_refinement] Falling “nonstandard” share and falling total assets are separate signals.
6. [new_category] The cost of de-risking can appear as lower asset yield, weaker fee income or one-off valuation/impairment effects.
7. [counterexample] A bank that preserves total assets while reducing opaque investments may still have materially deleveraged if liquidity and credit complexity fall.

### WMP legacy transition and wealth subsidiaries
8. [new_category][high] Legacy WMP runoff should be separated from migration into NAV products, funds, bank wealth subsidiaries and ordinary deposits.
9. [new_category] Customer AUM retention is the most important franchise test; product-label contraction can hide successful migration.
10. [new_category] NAV transition increases visible market volatility and customer suitability risk, especially for clients accustomed to implicit principal stability.
11. [new_category] Wealth-subsidiary formation changes governance, staffing, capital/perimeter and product-manufacturing economics even before the new entity reaches mature scale.
12. [new_category] Bank distribution can remain valuable even if asset management is legally separated.
13. [new_category] Legacy pooled products may require asset disposal, rollover management or balance-sheet support during transition; exact practices require evidence.
14. [useful_refinement] Wealth fees, custody fees, distribution fees and investment gains should not be treated as one income line.
15. [counterexample] Lower WMP balances can coincide with stronger wealth franchise if customer assets migrate to compliant products.

### Structured deposits and purchased funding
16. [new_category][high] Structured deposits should be treated as a hybrid funding-cost question: reported deposits may rise while economic funding cost remains high because of embedded option/derivative economics.
17. [new_category] A bank using structured deposits heavily may be defending customers from wealth-product migration rather than gaining new primary relationships.
18. [new_category] Corporate structured balances may behave differently from payroll/retail deposits in stress or repricing periods.
19. [new_category] Deposit franchise quality therefore requires relationship depth, transaction activity and renewal behavior, not volume alone.
20. [useful_refinement] Average deposit cost can lag changes in marginal acquisition cost.
21. [counterexample] Expensive deposits may still be rational if they support profitable transaction, wealth or lending relationships.

### 2019 interbank credit differentiation
22. [new_category][high] The 2019 small-bank stress episode tests whether joint-stock banks were treated as homogeneous bank credit or stratified by perceived franchise, ownership, capital and liquidity strength.
23. [new_category] Counterparty limits can tighten before actual default losses, reducing interbank asset opportunities and increasing liquidity hoarding.
24. [new_category] Haircuts, collateral preferences and tenor shortening are potential transmission channels even when headline funding remains available.
25. [new_category] Stronger joint-stock banks may act as liquidity providers while weaker peers become more defensive wholesale-funding users.
26. [new_category] NCD pricing can transmit market perceptions of bank credit faster than traditional customer-deposit pricing.
27. [useful_refinement] Gross interbank exposure and net liquidity-provider status should be separated.
28. [counterexample] A temporary rise in liquidity assets can depress earnings while signaling stronger risk management.

### Private-enterprise/default transmission

30. [new_category] Bond default can trigger bank credit-line withdrawal and accelerate liquidity stress for an issuer even when bank loans were previously current.
31. [new_category] Conversely, banks may refinance viable private firms precisely because market bond access disappears, increasing loan exposure while reducing systemic refinancing risk.
32. [new_category] Guarantee-chain contagion remains relevant for smaller private firms and regional industrial clusters.
33. [new_category] Inclusive-finance growth may shift portfolios toward granular borrowers, changing operating cost, data use and portfolio-loss distribution.
