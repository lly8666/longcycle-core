## Orthogonal challenge leads

### Is NIM defense franchise strength or risk-taking?

1. A slower NIM decline can reflect superior low-cost deposits, but it can also reflect delayed asset repricing or a shift into higher-risk lending; margin resilience needs causal decomposition. [new_category; high]
2. A faster NIM decline can be consistent with prudent de-risking if a bank replaces high-yield risky assets with lower-yield mortgages, government bonds or high-grade corporates. [new_category; counterexample]
3. Front-book corporate pricing is a cleaner competitive signal than average loan yield because legacy assets can mask current price pressure. [useful_refinement]
4. Retail spread defense through unsecured consumer lending can support yield but increase future credit cost and capital volatility. [new_category; high]
5. Mortgage repricing lowers asset yield yet can improve borrower affordability and reduce default probability; NIM and credit quality can move in opposite directions. [new_category; counterexample]
6. Fee/AUM growth can offset some spread compression, but lower fee take-rates can make headline AUM growth economically weaker than it appears. [new_category]
7. Treasury income can temporarily cushion earnings without solving structural lending-spread pressure. [new_category; high]
8. “以量补价” can preserve net interest income only if volume growth exceeds spread compression and incremental capital/credit costs remain acceptable. [new_category; mechanism]
9. A failed narrative to test is that balance-sheet growth alone proves successful margin defense; growth can destroy value at sub-hurdle spreads. [new_category; failed_narrative]

### Deposit termization and repricing lag

10. Quoted deposit-rate cuts should not be equated with immediate average funding-cost relief because the legacy time-deposit stock reprices at maturity. [new_category; high]
11. Deposit termization can persist even after quoted rates fall if households value certainty over liquidity or risky investment products. [new_category]
12. Long-term deposit maturity walls can make funding-cost improvement lumpy across banks depending on vintage composition. [new_category]
13. Corporate operating deposits and negotiated rate-sensitive deposits should be separated; both may be booked as deposits but have different price elasticity. [new_category]
14. Curtailing manual/off-book interest supplements, if material, may improve average pricing discipline while risking corporate balance outflow; exact timing remains Evidence-stage. [new_category; uncertain_fragment]
15. Demand-deposit share can rise because customers genuinely deepen primary-bank relationships or because time deposits mature temporarily; average balance behavior is needed. [new_category; metric_semantics]
16. Wealth/fund outflows back into deposits can improve liquidity while worsening termization and future deposit cost. [new_category]
17. Deposit growth should be judged together with marginal cost, maturity and customer concentration. [useful_refinement]
18. Interbank CDs can remain a flexible liquidity tool even in a deposit-focused strategy; market funding use is not binary franchise weakness. [useful_refinement; counterexample]
19. A bank with strong corporate transaction banking can gain low-cost deposits without necessarily having the strongest retail franchise. [new_category; counterexample]

### Mortgage and LGFV duration/yield trade-offs

20. Existing-mortgage repricing and prepayment both shorten expected interest cash flows, but prepayment also returns principal sooner; they have different duration and reinvestment consequences. [new_category; high]
21. Low mortgage defaults do not make mortgages economically costless if spreads compress below funding and capital hurdles. [new_category]
22. LGFV refinancing or debt replacement can lower default probability while locking banks into longer-duration, lower-yield assets. [new_category; high]
23. “化债” should be decomposed into cash repayment, refinancing, maturity extension, interest reduction, asset transfer and conversion into government-linked instruments. [useful_refinement; metric_semantics]
24. Moving exposure from LGFV loans to local-government bonds changes liquidity, capital treatment and yield even if local fiscal economics remain related. [new_category]
25. A lower coupon after restructuring may be economically equivalent to partial concession even without headline principal loss. [new_category]
26. Longer duration can create opportunity cost if rates or credit spreads later rise; lower credit risk can coexist with higher duration risk. [new_category; counterexample]
27. Joint-stock banks may participate selectively in local-debt resolution rather than with the same intensity as large state banks. [new_category]
28. Regional concentration remains essential: the same legal instrument can have very different cash-flow confidence across localities. [new_category]

### Property resolution: genuine risk reduction or migration?

29. Project-level completion financing can genuinely reduce loss severity if ring-fenced cash flows deliver homes and restore sales, but it does not erase parent-developer liabilities. [new_category; high]
30. White-list/project-support mechanisms should be analyzed at project rather than developer-group level; exact program mechanics require Evidence. [new_category]
31. Property risk can migrate from development loans into restructuring receivables, bonds, supplier exposures or longer-dated facilities rather than disappear. [new_category; high]
32. A falling development-loan balance can reflect repayment, write-off, disposal, reclassification or weak new origination; each has different meaning. [new_category; metric_semantics]
33. Mortgage credit and developer credit can decouple for long periods, especially in nationally diversified portfolios. [useful_refinement; counterexample]
34. Supplier/contractor transaction deposits provide an underappreciated transmission channel from housing construction to bank funding. [new_category]
35. Property cleanup can release management attention and capital over time, but early-stage resolution may require higher provisions and lower yields. [new_category]
36. A failed narrative to test is that property exposure is fully captured by labeled “real-estate loans”; bonds, WMPs, guarantees and corporate counterparties can broaden the perimeter. [new_category; failed_narrative]

### Wealth, funds, insurance and customer-asset competition

37. In a low-rate environment, the customer choice among deposits, WMPs, bond funds and savings-type insurance can drive both funding cost and fee income. [new_category; high]
38. A bank may intentionally allow some deposits to migrate to fee-generating products if total customer AUM and relationship depth improve. [new_category; counterexample]
39. The reverse can occur during volatility: customers move from NAV products to deposits, strengthening funding but hurting fee economics. [useful_refinement]
40. Insurance distribution can generate fees while competing with long-tenor deposits for household savings. [new_category]
41. Lower distribution fee rates can reduce revenue even if product sales volumes remain high; “sales/AUM” and “fee income” are not interchangeable. [new_category; metric_semantics]
42. Wealth subsidiary performance should be separated from parent-bank balance-sheet NIM; consolidated earnings may include both but economic drivers differ. [new_category]
43. Customer loss aversion after NAV volatility can alter product mix for years, affecting both deposits and wealth AUM. [new_category]
44. A strong wealth franchise requires adviser productivity and suitability/risk controls, not only digital product shelves. [new_category]

### Bond duration and treasury earnings

45. Falling yields can create mark-to-market or trading gains while lowering reinvestment income; current earnings and future carry can move oppositely. [new_category; high]
46. Banks extending duration to preserve yield increase sensitivity to later yield reversals. [new_category]
47. Government and policy-related bond accumulation can improve liquidity quality but compress asset yield. [new_category]
48. Trading-book gains are less durable than recurring customer spread/fee income and should not be annualized mechanically. [useful_refinement; counterexample]
49. Banking-book bond duration can hide economic sensitivity even where accounting volatility is muted. [new_category]
50. Credit-bond allocations to property/LGFV/private issuers can transmit sector stress outside conventional loan classifications. [new_category]
51. A failed narrative to test is that a bond bull market makes low rates unambiguously positive for banks; reinvestment yield and NIM remain under pressure. [new_category; failed_narrative]

### ECL, cleanup quality, capital and cost discipline

52. Stage-2 migration, special mention, overdue and restructured balances can reveal stress hidden by a stable NPL ratio. [useful_refinement; high]
53. Falling NPL stock can coexist with high cleanup intensity if write-offs/transfers are large. [useful_refinement]
54. Lower credit cost after years of provisioning can reflect prior front-loading, not necessarily a sudden improvement in borrower fundamentals. [new_category; counterexample]
55. Property/LGFV restructuring may reduce near-term default risk but extend uncertainty, making scenario assumptions in ECL especially important. [new_category]
56. Capital generation weakens mechanically when NIM and fee revenue compress, increasing the importance of RWA discipline and retained earnings. [new_category; high]
57. Asset-mix changes toward lower-risk-weight assets can stabilize capital ratios while reducing return on assets/equity. [new_category]
58. Cost cutting can improve efficiency ratios, but cuts to risk, technology or relationship capacity may damage future franchise quality. [new_category; counterexample]
59. Branch optimization should be judged by customer retention, deposits/AUM and relationship revenue rather than closure count alone. [new_category]
60. AI automation may reduce selected processing/service costs, but model governance, data security and human review create offsetting costs. [new_category; uncertain_fragment]
61. “Cost-to-income improvement” can result from cost cuts or revenue recovery; its denominator matters. [new_category; metric_semantics]
