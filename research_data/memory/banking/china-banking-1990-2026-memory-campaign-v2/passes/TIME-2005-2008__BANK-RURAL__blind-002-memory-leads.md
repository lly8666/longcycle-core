## Orthogonal question

Can the 2005–2008 rural-bank picture be falsified by finding a second period-wide licensed BANK-RURAL structural model, rather than one dominant RCC-lineage commercialization regime plus smaller parallel entrants and cross-entity service/control layers?

## 1. Village-bank sponsorship lineage versus RCC/RCB lineage

- Village banks should be treated as separate legal ancestry from RCC-to-RCB conversion even when both operate in the same county.
- The strongest discriminator is sponsor-led formation: governance, initial systems, staffing and risk methods can be imported from an incumbent sponsor rather than inherited from a county RCC network.
- Sponsor lineage can create a “network fragment” relationship even when the village bank is legally local and separately capitalized.
- Sponsor-bank reputation may ease wholesale relationships or supervisory confidence but does not automatically create a household deposit franchise.
- A new village bank can therefore face the inverse problem of a mature RCC: lending demand without the inherited village deposit/customer network.
- Deposit cold start may push early village banks toward narrow customer clusters, sponsor support or aggressive local relationship building.
- Narrow geographic scope increases concentration in a few crops, employers, industrial clusters or local property markets.
- Small balance sheets create high fixed costs for compliance, core systems and risk staff unless the sponsor provides shared infrastructure.
- Sponsor standardization may improve controls yet misfit agricultural cash-flow seasonality or informal borrower information.
- Local directors/managers still need local information, so “imported governance” does not eliminate relationship underwriting.
- Village-bank expansion is therefore a distinct institution type but not necessarily a second period-wide system model in 2005–2008 because scale and geographic coverage are still limited relative to the legacy RCC/RCB network.
- Rural mutual/cooperative pilot entities and loan-company experiments should remain separate from village-bank balance sheets; “new-type rural financial institution” is a policy umbrella, not one accounting category.
- Microcredit companies may compete for similar borrowers but belong outside licensed BANK-RURAL unless later Evidence shows bank status.
- A useful counterexample is a county with no meaningful new entrant: the entire rural-bank story there can still be explained through RCC/RCB conversion, showing entrant lineage is not period-wide necessary.

## 2. Provincial-union authority versus county legal autonomy

- Provincial unions/associations can make legally independent county entities behave operationally like a federation.
- Personnel appointment or approval is especially important because it can dominate formal shareholder-board autonomy in practice.
- Shared IT, payments, settlement, audit and liquidity create operational dependence even when assets and liabilities stay on county legal entities.
- Province-level branding can make depositors perceive a network guarantee that may not match legal liability boundaries.
- Centralized risk rules can reduce extreme local discretion, but credit approval may remain decentralized enough for local capture to persist.
- Liquidity coordination can pool surplus county deposits and support deficit institutions without creating a formal consolidated balance sheet.
- Internal placements within the rural-credit system can look like interbank assets/liabilities at entity level but disappear in aggregate reporting.
- Province-wide procurement and technology can generate scale economies that standalone small banks cannot reproduce.
- This quasi-group behavior is a governance architecture, not a second bank lineage: the underlying legal entities remain RCC/RCB/cooperative-bank descendants.
- A fully converted RCB may still depend heavily on provincial infrastructure, so legal conversion does not imply operational exit from the provincial-union ecosystem.
- Conversely, some stronger banks may gain greater practical autonomy despite nominal provincial membership, creating a continuum rather than a categorical second model.
- The main falsification result is therefore heterogeneity of control intensity, not a separate period-wide institutional class.

## 3. Post-cleanup NPL and capital seasoning

- A very low reported NPL ratio soon after restructuring can be consistent with legacy bad-asset disposal plus rapid denominator growth rather than fully proven underwriting quality.
- “Historical burden” should be separated from “newly generated bad loans” by vintage whenever later Evidence permits.
- New loan vintages from household microcredit, SME clusters and local-enterprise expansion may not have seasoned through a full downturn before 2008.
- Support instruments can improve solvency while introducing contingent policy expectations or incentive conditions that ordinary capital does not carry.
- Share capital raised during conversion may be concentrated among local firms, creating both loss-absorption capacity and connected-credit risk.
- Member shares, qualification shares and investment shares may have different withdrawal/governance semantics; they cannot be treated as one stable capital measure.
- Legal-entity consolidation can mechanically change NPL ratios because bad assets and loan denominators are re-pooled.
- A write-off, transfer, recovery and classification upgrade all reduce reported problem assets through different economics.
- Provision coverage can rise from stronger earnings and lower NPL denominator simultaneously; the ratio alone does not identify risk improvement.
- Rapid asset growth can suppress NPL ratio while increasing absolute expected-loss exposure.
- 2008 stress is therefore an early seasoning test of post-reform loan books rather than proof that cleanup either succeeded or failed universally.

## 4. Household microcredit, joint guarantees and county clusters

- Small household loans reduce single-name concentration but can remain highly correlated by village, crop, weather, commodity price or migrant-income source.
- Joint-guarantee lending transforms missing collateral into social/peer enforcement, but group members can fail together under a common shock.
- Guarantee chains can transmit distress beyond the original borrower and make legal obligor counts understate economic concentration.
- County manufacturing RCBs may have nominally diversified SME portfolios that are actually one industrial-cluster bet through shared suppliers, exporters and collateral values.
- Local private-enterprise relationships can strengthen information advantages while raising related-party or insider-influence risk.
- County-level real estate collateral can correlate household, SME and local-government-linked exposures even when loan categories differ.
- Agriculture-oriented institutions can face harvest-season liquidity and income volatility that annual NPL snapshots miss.
- Early standardized scoring can improve consistency but sparse borrower data may preserve heavy reliance on local loan officers.
- Growth targets after recapitalization can weaken underwriting if management equates expansion with reform success.
- The risk mechanism is therefore correlated local economy exposure, not a new institutional model.

## 5. Postal-bank transformation and deposit/funds-flow competition

- Postal financial transformation changes the rural liability landscape even if postal-bank balance sheets remain outside BANK-RURAL lineage.
- A postal network can compete strongly for household deposits because distribution reach and trust may predate full commercial lending capability.
- If more postal funds are recycled into lending, the old “pure drain” narrative weakens, but recycling may not occur in the same county where deposits were collected.
- RCC/RCB advantage remains local relationship depth and credit information; postal advantage may be payments/remittance and network coverage.
- State-bank selective return to profitable county markets can intensify competition for better SMEs and affluent households.
- Deposit competition under administered rates can be expressed through service, convenience, gifts, relationship bundling and branch density rather than only interest rates.
- Migrant remittances can become a contested payment/deposit gateway between postal and rural-credit networks.
- The result is a change in franchise competition around the dominant rural-bank system, not a second BANK-RURAL model.

## 6. 2007 tightening and 2008 external shock

- Rural banks with granular deposits may avoid the direct wholesale-market funding shock seen elsewhere, but loan-demand and borrower-cash-flow channels remain powerful.
- Tightening can create rationing pressure because county SMEs have fewer alternative funding channels than large corporates.
- Local banks may face political or relationship pressure to maintain credit even as system conditions tighten.
- Export-oriented county SMEs can experience 2008 through orders, receivables, factory utilization and employment rather than through banks’ direct foreign exposures.
- Migrant job losses can reduce remittances, increase precautionary withdrawals or change rural household repayment behavior.
- Industrial-cluster collateral values can fall together, defeating nominal collateral diversification.
- Inland institutions may see a different path: weaker export shock but stronger policy-driven rural lending or returned migrant-worker financing needs.
- Late-2008 easing/stimulus can reverse the direction of credit conditions quickly, so full-year 2008 averages mix two regimes.
- A bank that appears resilient in 2008 may simply have delayed recognition into later periods; vintage tracking matters.
- This regional transmission heterogeneity remains a factor-exposure story within BANK-RURAL rather than evidence of a second institution model.

## Failures and counterexamples

- Village-bank sponsor governance can fail if sponsor systems are too centralized for local decisions or too weak to support a remote small subsidiary/affiliate.
- A village bank may be formally local but economically dependent on sponsor technology, capital reputation and management pipeline.
- Provincial-union standardization can reduce fraud in one dimension while creating common-model risk or slow decision making across diverse counties.
- Strong coastal RCBs can outgrow the stereotype of a protected rural cooperative and compete directly with city/commercial banks for SMEs and retail customers.
- Weak RCCs can remain dependent on public support despite the same national reform framework, showing legal reform does not equal economic convergence.
- A converted RCB with concentrated local shareholders can retain related-party problems even after cooperative governance labels disappear.
- A high deposit share is not automatically evidence of strong franchise economics if deposits are expensive to service through dense low-productivity outlets.
- A low NPL ratio after cleanup is not automatically a success metric if new loan vintages are immature.
- A new village-bank license is not automatically evidence that local credit constraints were solved; liability franchise and risk staffing can remain bottlenecks.

## Metric/perimeter cautions

- “Rural financial institution count” can mix RCC legal persons, RCBs, cooperative banks, village banks and nonbank pilots; lineage-specific counts are required.
- Province-wide rural-credit data are not necessarily consolidated-group accounts.
- Conversion breaks time series for assets, deposits, capital and NPLs.
- Village-bank sponsor exposure can sit partly outside the village bank’s own balance sheet through shared services or support arrangements.
- Deposit franchise comparisons must distinguish owned rural-bank branches from postal agency outlets.
- 2008 annual growth/NPL data can conceal pre- and post-stimulus regime changes.
- Capital and NPL ratios should be read with vintage, cleanup, denominator and perimeter effects together.

## Orthogonal disposition

- Village banks/new-type institutions are a genuinely distinct legal/sponsorship lineage, but in 2005–2008 they are a smaller parallel entrant layer rather than a second period-wide BANK-RURAL structural model.
- Provincial unions create federation/quasi-group operating characteristics, but these are cross-entity governance and shared-service layers over RCC/RCB lineage, not a separate bank type.
- Post-cleanup seasoning, joint-guarantee correlation, postal competition and 2008 shock transmission are risk/franchise/cycle mechanisms within the same broader rural-bank system.
- No second period-wide structural category is recovered.
- TIME-2005-2008__BANK-RURAL can therefore be marked broad+orthogonal complete and the campaign can advance to one TIME-2009-2012 broad frontier.
