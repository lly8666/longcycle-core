## Broad trajectory

This period feels like the point at which the city-bank model became tightly entangled with the post-crisis local investment cycle. Many city commercial banks entered 2009 with newly strengthened capital, improving headline asset-quality ratios and expanding regional ambitions. The stimulus-era surge then linked their growth to local-government financing platforms, infrastructure, property, land-related collateral, SME demand and increasingly complex funding. Apparent NPL stability could coexist with refinancing, maturity extension, platform restructuring and unseasoned loan vintages. At the same time, WMP/trust cooperation and interbank activity began to create a bridge toward the more mature 2013-2016 shadow/wholesale-funding model.

## New-category leads

N01 [HIGH] The stimulus response created a **local-government-credit accelerator** especially relevant for city banks because municipal relationships, fiscal deposits and local project knowledge were already embedded in their franchises.

N02 [HIGH] Local-government financing platforms / financing platform companies become period-appropriate vocabulary rather than merely a later analytic label; platform loans are a central city-bank asset-quality lead.

N03 [HIGH] Platform lending should be split into urban infrastructure, transport, utilities, development zones, land-related projects and other municipal investment rather than treated as one exposure bucket.

N04 Land-sale and land-collateral expectations can connect platform credit and property credit even where legal borrowers differ.

N05 [HIGH] The economic repayment source of a platform loan may be project cash flow, fiscal transfer, land sale, refinancing or implicit local-government support; legal borrower form alone is insufficient.

N06 Search-key lead: `地方政府融资平台`, `融资平台公司`, `政府融资平台贷款`, `城投公司`, `城市建设投资`, `开发区投资`, `土地储备`, `平台贷款`.

N07 [HIGH] Period supervisory language may have classified platform loans by cash-flow coverage or repayment-source sufficiency; remembered terms like “全覆盖/基本覆盖/半覆盖/无覆盖” are uncertain but valuable Evidence search keys.

N08 Tentative old-control vocabulary: `名单制管理`, `逐笔排查`, `逐户核查`, `整改`, `分类处置` may appear in platform-loan clean-up work; exact official phrases require Evidence.

N09 [HIGH] Platform-loan risk can migrate without default through extension, refinancing, borrower reclassification or conversion to more conventional corporate/project structures.

N10 The apparent maturity of a municipal project can obscure refinancing dependence if original cash flow never matched debt service.

N11 [HIGH] City banks may have had particularly high local-government concentration because they were both local policy partners and commercial banks competing for municipal deposits/business.

N12 Fiscal/public-sector deposits and platform credit create a two-sided municipal relationship: local government can be both funding source and borrower ecosystem.

N13 [HIGH] Rapid 2009-era loan growth likely consumed capital faster than pre-stimulus plans assumed, creating repeated needs for retained earnings, shareholder injections, subordinated capital or market issuance.

N14 Capital stress should be measured against risk-weighted asset growth, not just nominal asset growth; platform/property/corporate mix matters.

N15 [HIGH] Strong headline capital after prior recapitalization can be temporary if balance-sheet expansion is exceptionally fast.

N16 The end of the period may already involve transition toward newer capital rules; exact regulatory timing/definitions should remain Evidence-stage.

N17 [HIGH] City-bank cross-region expansion continued during/after the stimulus boom, but asset growth in new branches could be amplified by abundant credit conditions, masking weak branch economics.

N18 New branches may find platform/property/large-corporate deals easier to scale than the granular SME relationship model, changing portfolio composition outside the home city.

N19 [HIGH] Geographic expansion plus local-government credit can create **multi-local-government exposure**: a bank moves from one municipality’s political/fiscal risk to several municipalities’ risks.

N20 The home-city implicit-support narrative may weaken as the bank’s balance sheet becomes geographically dispersed.

N21 [HIGH] Interbank liabilities become a more important growth bridge, but negotiable certificates of deposit should **not** be back-cast into this slice as the mature instrument; NCDs belong later.

N22 Period-appropriate wholesale plumbing includes interbank borrowing, placements, repos, bills and other bank/counterparty funding.

N23 [HIGH] A deposit-heavy average balance sheet can coexist with wholesale-funded marginal growth, especially where rapid loan expansion outruns branch deposit acquisition.

N24 Loan-to-deposit constraints may have encouraged competition for point-in-time deposits, WMP-like customer retention tools, bills and balance-sheet management; exact regulatory mechanics require Evidence.

N25 Search-key lead: `存贷比`, `同业拆借`, `同业存放`, `存放同业`, `买入返售`, `卖出回购`, `票据转贴现`, `同业业务`.

N26 [HIGH] Bill financing may be especially important at the start of stimulus because banks can deploy credit quickly through discounted bills; later policy tightening can reverse or reclassify this mix.

N27 Bankers’ acceptances, discounted bills and trade finance can blur loan/fee/interbank boundaries.

N28 [HIGH] WMP activity becomes more material during this period as banks compete for customers/yield and manage funding, but mature 2013-2016 non-standard-asset structures should not be assumed yet.

N29 `银信合作` is a strong period search key: bank wealth-management funds and trust structures may have transmitted credit outside ordinary on-balance-sheet loans.

N30 [HIGH] Trust cooperation can serve multiple functions—asset transfer, credit channel, customer wealth product, regulatory-balance-sheet management—so “trust exposure” needs transaction-level interpretation.

N31 `信托贷款`, `信托受益权`, `银信理财`, `理财资金`, `资金池` are candidate terms, but the exact maturity of each structure in city banks needs Evidence.

N32 [HIGH] The important transition is from **simple customer wealth products toward credit-linked asset management**, not the mere existence of a product called “理财”.

N33 WMP liabilities may alter deposit behavior even before they become a major shadow-credit channel: customers can move money between deposits and wealth products around yield differentials.

N34 [HIGH] Apparent low NPL ratios after 2009 can reflect benign liquidity, refinancing and rapid denominator growth as much as genuinely low lifetime credit loss.

N35 Platform borrowers in particular can avoid near-term NPL recognition when projects are rolled over or debt is refinanced.

N36 [HIGH] Loan extension/renewal should be separated from true cash repayment; “借新还旧”, `展期`, `续贷`, `重组贷款` are critical old-vocabulary leads.

N37 Special-mention and overdue trends may reveal deterioration earlier than formal NPL ratios, especially as 2009 vintages season.

N38 [HIGH] Provision coverage can appear exceptionally strong when recognized NPL denominators are low; this does not necessarily measure hidden platform/property risk.

N39 Loan-loss provision ratio / reserves relative to total loans may become a second prudential metric alongside provision coverage; exact period thresholds belong to Evidence.

N40 [HIGH] Property is not separate from platform risk: municipal land finance, developer land purchases, collateral values, construction activity and household mortgages form a local feedback loop.

N41 City banks may have higher exposures to smaller local developers that lack access to national capital markets or top-tier banks.

N42 Property-control tightening after the initial stimulus can shift financing toward trusts, WMP-linked structures, guarantee companies or less transparent borrowers.

N43 [HIGH] Development-zone and land-reserve entities can sit between municipal and property categories; classification choices can change reported sector exposures without changing economic risk.

N44 Search-key lead: `房地产开发贷款`, `土地储备贷款`, `开发区`, `房地产调控`, `项目资本金`, `土地出让收入`.

N45 [HIGH] SME/private-enterprise credit has a two-phase period story: post-crisis stimulus can initially ease funding, while later tightening/inflation/slowdown can expose guarantee chains and private-lending dependence.

N46 The 2011-era Wenzhou/private-lending stress is a strong regional counterexample lead for Zhejiang-area banks and local SME credit culture; exact city-bank exposures require Evidence.

N47 [HIGH] Informal/private lending can interact with bank credit through borrower leverage, guarantee networks and diverted loan proceeds even when banks have no direct private-lending asset.

N48 Guarantee companies may become both credit enhancers and contagion nodes as SME leverage rises.

N49 Search-key lead: `民间借贷`, `资金链断裂`, `企业互保`, `联保`, `担保圈`, `担保公司`, `小微企业融资`.

N50 [HIGH] Qilu Bank is a strong memory candidate for a major fraud/forged-instrument or case-risk episode around this period; exact facts/timing must be Evidence-verified.

N51 The Qilu-type lead matters because city-bank risk is not only macro credit risk: operational fraud, internal controls, forged instruments, collusion and case risk can be material independent of NPL cycles.

N52 [HIGH] Fraud/case losses can reveal weak branch authorization, seal/document controls, deposit-account controls or insider collusion that normal credit metrics miss.

N53 Old search-key lead: `案件风险`, `票据案件`, `伪造金融票证`, `内外勾结`, `操作风险`, `内部控制`.

N54 [HIGH] Baoshang Bank is a later-known failure that should be back-tested as a contemporaneous city-bank counterexample: rapid growth, ownership/related-party concentration and cross-region ambitions may have antecedents here, but later failure facts must not be projected backward without Evidence.

N55 Other later troubled regional banks (for example Jinzhou/Shengjing-related trajectories) may have antecedents in this or later slices; exact period relevance is uncertain and should be tested rather than assumed.

N56 [HIGH] Related-party lending may be especially difficult to observe when corporate shareholders control networks of affiliates or use nominally separate borrowers.

N57 Cross-region branching can enlarge the space for hidden related-party or connected lending if group-level customer identification is incomplete.

N58 [HIGH] Centralized core systems and credit registries improve visibility, but legal-entity fragmentation and guarantees can still hide economic groups.

N59 The period’s expansion therefore raises a governance question: did headquarters know the bank’s true aggregate exposure by local government, property group, guarantor network and shareholder affiliate?

N60 [HIGH] Local-government support can delay resolution of weak city banks through recapitalization, asset transfers, public deposits or shareholder restructuring rather than formal failure.

N61 “No failures” is therefore a poor negative-space conclusion; repeated recapitalization, name change, ownership change and asset cleanup can be resolution outcomes.

N62 Search-key lead: `风险处置`, `重组`, `增资扩股`, `地方政府注资`, `不良资产处置`, `股权重组`, `托管`.

N63 [HIGH] Strong regional heterogeneity intensifies: coastal export/SME cities, property/land-led cities, inland infrastructure hubs, resource-heavy regions and old industrial bases experience different stimulus benefits and later stress.

N64 A city bank’s growth rate during stimulus can partly measure local-government investment ambition rather than bank-specific franchise strength.

N65 [HIGH] Regional fiscal strength affects both sides of the balance sheet: stronger local governments can provide deposits/support and sustain platform refinancing longer.

N66 Weak fiscal regions may rely more heavily on land sales or repeated refinancing, making platform-credit quality more cyclical.

N67 [HIGH] Cross-region banks can arbitrage regional cycles—moving toward faster-growing cities—but also import several correlated property/infrastructure booms at once.

N68 The post-crisis period can therefore increase nominal geographic diversification while increasing common-factor exposure to land/property/infrastructure.

N69 [HIGH] Wealth/interbank/platform growth is not yet the fully mature shadow-banking ecosystem, but this period creates the **institutional bridge**: assets seek structures when loan, capital, property or platform constraints tighten.

N70 Trust companies, securities firms and other nonbank counterparties begin to matter as channel actors; detailed channel taxonomy belongs the later shadow shard/Evidence stage.

N71 [HIGH] Regulatory tightening can shift risk form rather than eliminate it: from direct platform loan to trust/wealth/bond/other structure, or from developer loan to upstream/downstream borrower.

N72 City-bank financial statements may thus show sector improvements while economic exposure migrates across accounting categories.

N73 [HIGH] Contemporaneous analysts may have regarded strong city-bank ROE and low NPLs as evidence of superior local franchises; later vintage outcomes could reveal substantial cyclical tailwind.

N74 Listing and capital access can reinforce growth incentives when market valuation rewards asset/profit expansion.

N75 [HIGH] Public-sector relationship advantages can become soft-budget expectations: borrowers may believe refinancing/support will persist because the bank and borrower share local-government ties.

N76 The moral-hazard channel can operate without an explicit guarantee from the government.

N77 [HIGH] A central negative-space question for this slice is whether the city-bank “success story” actually rests on a three-way loop: **local fiscal investment → bank credit → land/property activity → fiscal/support capacity**.

N78 Breaking that loop requires later Evidence on cash flow, land dependence, refinancing and local fiscal strength rather than headline borrower labels.

## Useful refinements

R01 “Stimulus growth” should be decomposed into ordinary corporate loans, platform loans, property/development-zone credit, bills and off-balance-sheet/channel credit.

R02 Platform classification should distinguish legal entity, economic repayment source and supervisory classification.

R03 Mature LGFV terminology may coexist with older names such as urban-construction/development companies; both should be searched.

R04 Platform risk is better measured by cash-flow coverage and refinancing dependence than by legal government affiliation alone.

R05 Fiscal deposits and platform loans create correlated municipal funding/asset relationships.

R06 Cross-region diversification needs a common-factor test because multiple cities can share the same property/infrastructure cycle.

R07 Interbank funding should be analyzed gross and marginally; average deposit ratios can miss marginal wholesale dependence.

R08 Do not use NCD data for this slice unless Evidence confirms the instrument timing; mature NCD funding is later.

R09 WMP existence is not equivalent to shadow-credit maturity; identify underlying assets and guarantee/return structure.

R10 `银信合作` can be on-balance-sheet-adjacent, off-balance-sheet or customer-wealth activity depending on transaction design.

R11 A decline in direct platform loans may reflect reclassification/refinancing rather than economic deleveraging.

R12 NPL ratios should be paired with overdue, special-mention, restructuring, migration and write-off data where available.

R13 High provision coverage with low NPL recognition can give a misleading impression of resilience.

R14 Platform/property credit should be linked through land collateral and local fiscal revenue expectations.

R15 SME stress should include bank loans plus private lending and guarantee obligations to measure total leverage.

R16 Wenzhou-style stress is a regional mechanism lead, not evidence that every Zhejiang city bank had the same exposure.

R17 Operational/case risk can produce losses independent of borrower default and therefore requires separate controls/metrics.

R18 Later failure of a bank is a prompt to back-test earlier governance, not permission to label earlier-period exposures as already impaired.

R19 Related-party risk should follow beneficial control/economic groups, not only disclosed legal related parties.

R20 Recapitalization can be a resolution event even when the bank remains open and grows afterward.

R21 Local macro quality must be controlled when comparing city-bank ROE, NPL and growth.

R22 Strong local fiscal capacity can delay loss recognition without eliminating ultimate credit risk.

R23 Policy tightening can cause credit migration into trust/wealth/channel structures rather than simple contraction.

R24 Listed-bank disclosure may make stronger banks easier to remember, creating selection bias against weak/unlisted city banks.

R25 2012 apparent stabilization may still contain unseasoned or refinanced 2009-2010 vintages that emerge later.

## Metric-semantic cautions

- Platform loan balance versus economically platform-dependent credit after borrower/reclassification changes.
- Direct loan versus trust/WMP/channel economic credit.
- Deposit versus WMP customer funds; interbank is a separate liability source.
- NPL ratio versus overdue/special-mention/restructured/refinanced exposures.
- Provision coverage versus reserves-to-total-loans and credit cost.
- Capital adequacy versus capital source and post-stimulus RWA growth.
- Geographic diversification versus common-factor property/infrastructure exposure.
- Parent-bank versus consolidated/off-balance-sheet perimeter.
