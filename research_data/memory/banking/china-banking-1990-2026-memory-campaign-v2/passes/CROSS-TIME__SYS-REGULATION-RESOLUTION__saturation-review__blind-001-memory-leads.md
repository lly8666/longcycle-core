## Material missing blind category: resolution funding and loss allocation architecture

11. **[new_category][high] Resolution funding stack:** the compact map is strong on *state classification* but weaker on the independent question of *who funds the intervention and who ultimately absorbs loss*. This is a material cross-time mechanism category rather than a source-detail footnote.
12. **[new_category][high] Deposit-insurance fund participation versus insured-deposit payout:** fund participation in risk disposal, transfer support or other backstop activity should be modeled separately from a statutory payout to insured depositors; exact powers and transaction mechanics remain to be verified later.
13. **[new_category][high] Financial Stability Guarantee Fund / `金融稳定保障基金`:** internal memory places this in the post-2022 financial-stability architecture as a potentially important system-risk disposal backstop. Its precise legal status, funding base, activation conditions and bank-case use are uncertain and must not be truth-promoted blind.
14. **[new_category][high] PBOC liquidity or relending versus resolution funding:** central-bank liquidity, emergency liquidity, relending or other funding support can stabilize funding without itself recapitalizing an insolvent bank or allocating final losses.
15. **[new_category][high] Local-government special bonds for small-bank capital replenishment:** remembered use of special local-government bond channels to replenish small-bank capital is a distinct fiscal/ownership support mechanism. It should not automatically be labeled resolution funding or a depositor bailout.
16. **[new_category][high] State/local shareholder recapitalization versus dedicated resolution fund:** public or state-linked capital can absorb losses and change ownership while leaving the operating bank intact; funding source and legal resolution state are orthogonal.
17. **[new_category][high] AMC/bad-asset transfer financing:** purchase or transfer of impaired assets can move losses, financing needs and recovery rights without necessarily transferring deposits or terminating the banking license.
18. **[new_category][high] Successor/new-bank capitalization versus transfer consideration:** when assets and liabilities move to a successor or newly formed bank, the capital placed into the successor and the consideration paid for transferred assets/liabilities are distinct accounting/economic questions.
19. **[new_category][high] Loss-absorption ordering:** shareholder equity, capital instruments, uninsured wholesale claims, interbank claims, large corporate deposits and insured household deposits should not be collapsed into a single `creditor loss` field. Exact statutory or transaction ordering is source dependent.
20. **[new_category][high] Bad-asset bearer versus liability successor:** a resolution can split the entity that receives customer liabilities from the vehicle/entity that retains or acquires impaired assets. Modeling only the successor bank can hide where losses actually reside.

## Refinements and falsification tests for the missing category

21. **[useful_refinement][high] Modern takeover cases:** a remembered formal takeover is not fully characterized until the funding/backstop side is separated into liquidity support, capital support, asset purchase/transfer funding, deposit-insurance involvement and final loss allocation.
22. **[useful_refinement][high] Early closure cases:** an early bank closure can be a valid terminal-state anchor while the source of depositor repayment, successor assumption and fiscal/central-bank support remains unknown. Those unknowns must stay explicit.
23. **[useful_refinement][medium] Policy consolidation:** capital injection plus merger or branch conversion can be funded heavily by public owners without implying a statutory resolution-fund event.
24. **[useful_refinement][high] Distress support versus ordinary owner support:** the same funding source can appear in ordinary recapitalization and in a distress transaction; the trigger and legal action determine the resolution label, not the identity of the funder alone.
25. **[useful_refinement][medium] Deposit-insurance institution versus payout event:** memory suggests the deposit-insurance institution may have broader risk-disposal relevance than simple depositor reimbursement, but the exact boundary is a future Evidence question.
26. **[useful_refinement][medium] Financial-stability backstop semantics:** `金融稳定保障基金` should be tracked separately from the deposit-insurance fund, PBOC liquidity facilities, fiscal capital injection and ordinary shareholder support unless authoritative sources show a specific relationship.
27. **[useful_refinement][medium] Special-bond semantics:** using local-government special bonds to inject bank capital is best represented as a financing/capital channel first; whether it belongs to a resolution transaction is case-specific.
28. **[duplicate][medium] Delisting is not funding or loss allocation:** stock-market listing exit says little by itself about depositor protection or who bears bank losses.
29. **[duplicate][medium] Ordinary NPL sale is not resolution funding:** an AMC purchase or bad-loan sale outside a distress-resolution process remains an asset-management transaction.
30. **[duplicate][medium] P2P/platform reimbursement is not bank resolution:** claimant reimbursement in a non-bank platform failure should not populate bank-resolution funding fields.
