## Cross-time negative-space and saturation leads

### A. Forgotten lineage, actors and institution classes

1. **[N | high] Agricultural Bank administrative lineage before RCC institutional separation.** Memory suggests rural credit cooperatives were historically managed in a close administrative relationship with the Agricultural Bank before a later separation. This is a lineage mechanism, not a claim that RCC balance sheets were Agricultural Bank balance sheets. Search later for the exact management relationship, separation sequence and what functions moved to county/local authorities.
2. **[N | high] Post-separation governance vacuum / reassignment.** The important question is not merely that a separation occurred, but who then exercised personnel, prudential, liquidity and business-management authority over county and grassroots cooperatives. A temporary or layered governance arrangement could explain later provincial-union design.
3. **[N | high] County union versus grassroots cooperative legal-person structure.** Older RCC systems may have had county unions plus township/grassroots cooperatives with separate legal-person status. Search keys may include `县联社`, `基层信用社`, `两级法人`, `一级法人` and `统一法人`; do not assume these phrases had identical meaning in every province.
4. **[N | high] County-level unified-legal-person reform as a structural breakpoint.** Converting multiple grassroots legal persons into one county legal person could change capital pooling, internal liquidity, governance and loss mutualization before later conversion into a rural commercial bank. This deserves its own chronology rather than being collapsed into generic “RCC reform.”
5. **[N | medium] Rural cooperative bank as a transitional legal form.** `农村合作银行` may be a distinct reform destination between traditional RCC structures and rural commercial banks rather than a synonym for either. Its rise and later decline/disappearance is a legal-form lineage question.
6. **[N | high] Rural cooperative foundations as negative-space antecedent.** `农村合作基金会` is remembered as a non-bank/para-financial rural institution class whose cleanup in the late 1990s may have transferred liabilities, customers, political pressure or bad assets into the formal rural financial system. It must not be conflated with RCCs, but its failure/cleanup may be necessary to explain the 1995-1999 BANK-RURAL environment.
7. **[N | medium] New-type rural financial institution trio as boundary case.** Village banks were introduced alongside other rural-finance experiments such as loan companies and rural mutual/cooperative fund institutions. Even if only village banks belong in the BANK-RURAL bank lineage, the other types are useful negative-space controls for why legal form and deposit-taking authority matter.
8. **[R | high] Sponsor-bank role in village banks.** The compact map already preserves village banks as a sponsor-led tail. A later Evidence pass should distinguish the sponsor bank's formal governance/liquidity/brand role from other shareholders and from any assumption of blanket liability support.
9. **[N | high] Provincial union (`省联社`) as industry-management actor rather than ordinary parent bank.** The cross-time category may be “administrative/industry management over separate county legal persons,” not ownership consolidation. This would explain why shared rules, executives, technology or liquidity coordination can coexist with legally separate balance sheets.
10. **[N | medium] Rural-credit clearing/network infrastructure.** `农信银` / a rural-credit funds-clearing network is remembered as a shared payments/clearing actor that helped local rural institutions interoperate nationally without turning them into one bank. This is a forgotten infrastructure actor worth verifying.

### B. Old vocabulary, ownership and governance semantics

11. **[N | high] `资格股` versus `投资股`.** Older cooperative-capital reform may have distinguished qualification/member shares from investment shares. Search later for voting, withdrawal, transfer and dividend rights; these terms could explain why “capital” before commercialization is not directly comparable with ordinary bank equity.
12. **[N | high] Withdrawable member shares versus permanent loss-absorbing capital.** Cooperative-member exit rights may have created a tension between member ownership and prudential capital permanence. This is a mechanism lead, not a claim about any institution's actual redemption policy.
13. **[N | medium] `社员代表大会` to shareholder-governance vocabulary.** Changes from member/cooperative governance to shareholder meetings, boards and commercial-bank company governance may reflect a real institutional transition rather than cosmetic renaming.
14. **[N | medium] “Commercialization by legal-form conversion” as a contemporaneous expectation.** A recurring Judgment lead is that converting an RCC into a rural commercial/cooperative bank was expected to improve governance and capital discipline; an orthogonal counter-Judgment is that legal-form change alone might not remove local-government or insider influence.
15. **[R | high] Related-party/local-elite capture as a persistent mechanism.** Rev165 already preserves local heterogeneity and predecessor risk. The saturation question is whether ownership reform sometimes changed labels while concentrated connected lending or local influence persisted.
16. **[N | medium] Province-level personnel influence as distinct from legal ownership.** Appointment, evaluation or management influence by provincial structures may matter even when the county institution remains a separate legal person. This should be separated from capital ownership and legal consolidation.

### C. Asset-quality and capital metric semantics

17. **[N | high] `一逾两呆` as an old asset-quality search key.** Older rural-bank bad-loan language may have classified overdue, sluggish and bad/dead loans differently from later five-category loan classification. Exact definitions and transition timing require Evidence.
18. **[N | high] Five-category classification transition breaks NPL comparability.** A move from older arrears-style categories to five-category loan classification could cause measured NPL ratios to change even without identical economic deterioration/improvement. Historical series should therefore preserve the classification regime.
19. **[R | high] Overdue, special-mention and NPL are not interchangeable.** The compact map already flags NPL/overdue semantics in later periods; cross-time replay should retain distinct definitions and avoid using one as a proxy for another without source support.
20. **[R | medium] Write-off, transfer, restructuring and cash recovery alter reported NPL paths differently.** Falling NPL ratios can reflect disposal mechanics, denominator growth or reclassification rather than only borrower cure.
21. **[N | high] Central-bank special bills (`专项票据`) as RCC reform instrument.** Memory strongly suggests a reform-era mechanism in which central-bank special bills or similar support were linked to rural-credit reform and bad-asset/capital repair. The key research question is the conditional mechanism and eligibility, not an assumed blanket bailout.
22. **[N | medium] `花钱买机制` as an uncertain old search phrase.** Memory associates a phrase like “spending money to buy mechanisms/incentives” with rural-credit reform support. Treat only as a search key until source text verifies who used it and what it referred to.
23. **[N | high] Capital replenishment source taxonomy.** Member/shareholder capital, local-government fiscal resources/assets, central-bank reform support, retained earnings and later market instruments should not be merged into one “recapitalization” category.
24. **[N | medium] Capital adequacy before/after legal-form reform may use changing rule sets.** Apparent improvement can reflect new capital injections, asset disposal, denominator changes or regulatory-definition changes; exact ratios should be normalized only after Evidence.
25. **[N | medium] Legal-person count versus operating-outlet count.** Consolidation can sharply reduce the number of bank legal entities while branches/outlets remain. “Number of rural banks fell” is therefore semantically incomplete unless the metric is specified.
26. **[N | medium] Institution count versus predecessor count versus successor count.** A province-wide successor can absorb many predecessors; replay must preserve merger lineage rather than treating the successor as a greenfield entrant and predecessors as unexplained disappearances.

### D. Mechanisms and business-model memory beyond a timeline retell

27. **[N | medium] County deposit franchise and local recycling expectation.** Rural institutions historically may have been evaluated not only on profitability but on keeping locally mobilized funds available for local agriculture/household/SME credit. Search later for precise policy or supervisory metrics instead of assuming a fixed loan-to-deposit rule.
28. **[N | medium] `信用户` / `信用村` / household credit-rating vocabulary.** Relationship-based rural lending may have used borrower/household/village credit-status programs as a low-cost information technology before modern data platforms. These are old search keys and not proof of credit quality.
29. **[N | medium] `农户小额信用贷款` as a characteristic rural-lending mechanism.** Small unsecured/relationship loans to farm households may be a persistent franchise category. Keep it at the BANK-RURAL mechanism boundary rather than allowing it to replace the separate ASSET-CORPORATE-SME or retail asset taxonomy.
30. **[N | medium] `联保贷款` / mutual-guarantee chains as both access mechanism and contagion mechanism.** Group or joint-guarantee lending could initially reduce information/collateral constraints but later transmit correlated stress across borrowers. Exact prevalence and periods are Evidence questions.
31. **[N | high] `涉农贷款`, `农户贷款`, and later inclusive-rural metrics are not synonyms.** The policy mission can be measured with different borrower, geography or purpose definitions; a bank may serve a county without every loan qualifying under the same “agriculture-related” metric.
32. **[N | medium] Mission metric versus profitability metric tension.** Contemporaneous management expectations may have treated rural service obligations, micro-loan pricing, branch density and local relationship banking as strategically necessary even when scale economics favored centralization.
33. **[R | medium] Shared provincial technology does not prove shared solvency.** Rev165 already preserves this boundary. Cross-time review should separately track shared core systems, payments, risk models and data platforms from legal balance-sheet mutualization.
34. **[R | medium] Centralization can trade efficiency for local information loss.** A larger successor may improve treasury, technology and controls yet weaken local credit discretion or relationship knowledge; this is a testable operating-model countermechanism.
35. **[N | medium] Legacy claims can survive a legal conversion.** Conversion from RCC/cooperative bank to rural commercial bank does not itself prove that legacy bad loans, related-party exposures, ownership disputes or guarantee obligations disappeared.
36. **[R | medium] Market exit has multiple forms.** Absorption, merger, legal conversion, administrative restructuring, license cancellation and formal insolvency/resolution should remain separate event types rather than one generic “failure.”

Later province-wide mergers should not be projected backward into that earlier expectation.
39. **[D | high] Strong independent county rural commercial banks remain a falsifier of universal consolidation.** Rev165 already records this explicitly; retain it as the leading counterexample when testing a province-wide successor model.
40. **[D | medium] Village banks remain a distinct sponsor-led tail.** Rev165 already records this and it should not be used to redefine RCC/RCB lineage.
41. **[D | high] Provincial consolidation is real but not a universal replacement lineage.** Rev165 already contains this conclusion; this saturation pass does not count it as new.
