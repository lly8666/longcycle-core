## Bounded independent scan

This pass does not retell chronology or enumerate named cases. It asks whether an independent memory scan of intervention funding roles, recoverability, backstop channels, transfer financing, claimant classes and final loss bearing changes the compact category map.

1. **useful_refinement — support instrument recoverability/recourse should be recorded as its own state.** A cash outflow or committed support amount does not by itself establish final loss. The analytical record should separately preserve whether the provider appears to receive a claim, repayment right, collateral, recovery/subrogation right or other recourse, and whether that right is outstanding, recovered, impaired or unknown. Importance: high. This refines the existing recoverability/final-loss category rather than adding a new mechanism category.

2. **duplicate — liquidity funding and solvency support remain distinct.** Short-term liquidity provision can create a recoverable claim and should not be normalized as recapitalization or subsidy merely because it occurs during stress.

3. **duplicate — capital injection is not automatically realized fiscal loss.** New capital can remain an investment whose eventual gain/loss depends on valuation, exit and recovery.

4. **duplicate — gross funding and ultimate loss are different measures.** Amount committed, amount drawn, amount outstanding and net realized loss should not be treated as interchangeable.

5. **duplicate — backstop availability is not the same as deployment.** A fund, fiscal channel, central-bank facility or guarantee capacity can exist without being drawn in a particular resolution.

6. **duplicate — depositor protection outcome does not identify the funding source.** Continued access or claim transfer does not prove deposit-insurance payout, fiscal support, successor-bank funding or central-bank support.

7. **duplicate — deposit-insurance participation is broader than a cash payout category.** Memory of risk-disposal involvement should not be forced into a payout-only interpretation without Evidence.

8. **duplicate — public recapitalization, deposit-insurance resources, central-bank liquidity and financial-stability backstops remain separate analytical channels.** They may interact but should not be collapsed into one `state rescue` bucket.

9. **duplicate — local public capital support is distinct from dedicated resolution funding.** A local-government or local-SOE capital channel does not by itself establish a resolution-fund mechanism.

10. **duplicate — asset-transfer financing is separate from liability succession.** Financing the purchase or warehousing of impaired assets does not determine who assumes deposits or other customer claims.

11. **duplicate — transfer consideration is distinct from successor capitalization.** Capitalizing a successor institution and paying consideration for transferred assets/liabilities are analytically different flows even when both occur in one restructuring.

12. **duplicate — the first payer need not be the final loss bearer.** Guarantees, advances, indemnities or recoverable support can shift timing and liquidity burden without fixing ultimate incidence.

13. **duplicate — claimant classes must remain separate.** Equity, capital instruments, wholesale claims, interbank claims, corporate deposits and household deposits can face different economic outcomes; a single `creditor loss` field is insufficient.

14. **duplicate — asset-side loss recognition and liability-side protection can diverge.** A transaction can crystallize losses on transferred/impaired assets while customer liabilities continue elsewhere.

15. **duplicate — a public support channel can redistribute rather than eliminate loss.** Final incidence may sit with shareholders, an asset seller, a residual estate, a public entity, a fund, a successor investor or another claimant; the intervention-day payer is not enough to decide.

16. **duplicate — pre-deposit-insurance periods require period-native classification.** Post-2015 institutional labels should not be retroactively assigned to earlier interventions simply because later frameworks provide familiar categories.
