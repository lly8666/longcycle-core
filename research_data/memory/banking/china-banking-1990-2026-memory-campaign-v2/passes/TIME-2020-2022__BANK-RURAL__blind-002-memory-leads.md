## 1. Structural test propositions

1. **L001 — [R/H] Funding geography looks like a balance-sheet/risk dimension, not a legal-institution lineage.** A rural commercial bank, RCC-derived bank or village bank can differ radically in how local versus remote its deposits are, so “internet/off-site funding” should probably sit beside institution type rather than replace it.
2. **L002 — [R/M] Village banks remain the most plausible place where sponsor control and remote-deposit geography intersect structurally.** Their sponsor-led form and smaller local governance base make the interaction unusually salient, but this does not automatically generalize to RCC/RCB lineage.
3. **L003 — [D/M] Provincial shared services do not imply pooled credit losses.** Rev162 already states this separation; retain it as a falsification guard because shared IT, payments or liquidity coordination can coexist with separate legal-person solvency.
4. **L004 — [R/H] Depositor confidence is best treated as a transmission mechanism.** A governance/fraud problem can become a funding problem quickly when customers are remote and digitally connected, but the confidence channel itself is not evidence of a new bank type.
5. **L005 — [D/M] Capital injection or merger is not proof that the same weakness existed across all rural banks.** Rev162 already warns against this inference; resolution choice may reflect province-specific policy capacity and institution-specific legacy problems.
6. **L006 — [R/M] The orthogonal hypothesis should fail if strong local-deposit rural banks show materially different behavior.** Their existence would imply cohort heterogeneity rather than a period-wide structural break.

## 2. Funding-geography challenge

7. **L007 — [R/M] Internet-platform deposits likely mattered most where local franchise deposits were insufficient or growth ambitions exceeded the home-market deposit base.** This suggests dependence was endogenous to strategy rather than universal to small-bank status.
8. **L008 — [N/H] Remote depositors may have been less relationship-sticky and more rate-sensitive than local household deposits.** If true, funding cost and run sensitivity would depend on acquisition channel even at the same legal bank type.
9. **L009 — [R/M] “Local bank” can describe lending geography while funding geography is much wider.** Historical reconstruction should separately track where loans are originated, where depositors reside, and where digital acquisition occurs.
10. **L010 — [D/M] Restrictions on off-site/internet deposits are already a rev162 lead.** The orthogonal value is not repeating the rule but testing bank-level reliance and substitution after the channel was curtailed.
11. **L011 — [N/H] Replacement funding after remote-deposit restrictions may have pushed some banks toward higher-cost local term deposits, interbank funding, certificates or slower asset growth.** The exact substitute differs by institution and is a later Evidence task.
12. **L012 — [R/M] Strong county banks could have the opposite problem: abundant local deposits and limited high-quality local loan demand.** For them, the key issue may be asset allocation rather than liability scarcity.
13. **L013 — [N/M] Funding geography can interact with regulatory perimeter through branchless acquisition.** A bank can stay legally local while economically serving a dispersed national depositor base, weakening the intuition that local supervisors observe the whole franchise through local networks.
14. **L014 — [R/M] Remote funding does not necessarily imply weak governance.** Some well-managed small banks may have used digital distribution opportunistically; the risk rises when aggressive growth, opaque ownership and weak controls combine.

## 3. Sponsor / shareholder-control challenge

15. **L015 — [R/M] Sponsor-bank identity matters more for village banks than for RCC/RCB lineage.** The sponsor may provide branding, systems, management or oversight, but effective control can still be diluted by local shareholders and operating distance.
16. **L016 — [N/H] Formal sponsor status and practical control should be separate variables.** A sponsor can satisfy a legal or ownership role without continuously controlling customer acquisition, related-party lending or local management behavior.
17. **L017 — [R/M] Related-party risk is not uniquely rural.** What may be distinctive is the density of local commercial/political relationships and smaller governance capacity, not a special rural-bank accounting category.
18. **L018 — [N/H] Shareholder qualification and source-of-capital questions are likely important in troubled small banks.** Later Evidence should distinguish genuine paid-in capital from leveraged/connected funding and separate shareholder misconduct from ordinary credit losses.
19. **L019 — [R/M] Local ownership can be either information advantage or capture risk.** The same embeddedness that supports relationship lending can also facilitate concentration, connected lending and resistance to recognizing losses.
20. **L020 — [D/M] Henan-type failures remain an episodic-tail candidate, not a representative BANK-RURAL template.** The map already requires this caution.
21. **L021 — [R/M] Sponsor-bank reputation may create depositor expectations beyond legal obligations.** A failure could therefore test whether customers distinguish sponsor support, deposit insurance, local-government support and the bank’s own balance sheet.
22. **L022 — [N/M] Sponsor withdrawal, replacement or forced governance intervention would be a useful later search family.** It could reveal whether weak village banks were resolved through sponsor mechanisms or through broader provincial/regulatory channels.

## 4. Provincial support / resolution challenge

23. **L023 — [R/H] Provincial unions appear to be infrastructure/governance coordinators first, not automatic fiscal backstops.** Their practical ability to coordinate liquidity or management may exceed their ability to absorb credit losses.
24. **L024 — [N/H] Resolution capacity probably depends on province fiscal strength and the quality of stronger local banks available as merger partners.** This could explain why similar weak-bank problems produce different restructuring forms across regions.
25. **L025 — [R/M] Public capital can be a bridge rather than a cure.** Capital replenishment may buy time for governance clean-up, NPL disposal and merger, so historical replay should separate the capital event from recurring franchise viability.
26. **L026 — [R/M] A province-wide legal-person merger can improve control while obscuring predecessor heterogeneity.** Any later consolidated institution should preserve the exact predecessor lineage and not retroactively homogenize earlier balance sheets.
27. **L027 — [N/H] Stronger provincial integration may be partly an organizational response to supervision and IT complexity, not merely insolvency.** The research question is whether the reform motive was risk disposal, operational efficiency, governance centralization, or a mix.
28. **L028 — [D/M] Later provincial rural-commercial-bank formations must not be projected backward into 2020-2022.** Rev162 already states this temporal guard.
29. **L029 — [R/M] Local-government support expectations are likely uneven.** Fiscal strength, political salience, depositor base and systemic importance can influence intervention without creating an explicit common guarantee.

## 5. Depositor-confidence / run mechanics challenge

30. **L030 — [R/M] Digital access can increase both convenience and panic speed.** An access problem or rumor can reach geographically dispersed customers faster than branch-based communication, but this remains a channel effect.
31. **L031 — [N/H] A confidence event can expose whether customers understand deposit ownership versus platform distribution.** If deposits were acquired through third-party apps, some customers may perceive the platform as part of the protection chain even when legal liability sits with the bank.
32. **L032 — [R/M] Deposit insurance is a confidence institution, but its existence does not make payout/resolution instantaneous.** The distinction between insured status, temporary access restrictions, administrative verification and eventual repayment matters for historical Judgment.
33. **L033 — [D/M] Confidence stress is not proof of system-wide insolvency.** Rev162 already requires this boundary.
34. **L034 — [N/H] Operational access failures, fraud allegations and credit insolvency should be separated.** A depositor may experience the same symptom—cannot access funds—even when the underlying mechanism and legal remedy differ materially.

## 6. Counterexamples and falsifiers

35. **L035 — [R/M] Strong Jiangsu/Zhejiang-style county franchises are a conceptual falsifier even without naming institutions.** Affluent private-enterprise counties can support deep local deposits, fee/payment activity and relationship SME lending that behaves unlike distressed inland village-bank cases.
36. **L036 — [N/H] A rural bank with surplus deposits and conservative local lending may face low-yield asset allocation rather than a funding-confidence problem.** This is the mirror image of remote-deposit-dependent growth.
37. **L037 — [R/M] Provincial shared-IT success can coexist with legally independent banks of very different credit quality.** Technology standardization therefore should not be mistaken for balance-sheet convergence.
38. **L038 — [R/M] A sponsor bank with strong controls may make a village bank more stable than a poorly governed RCC-derived bank.** Institution form alone cannot rank risk.
39. **L039 — [D/M] Agricultural or essential-economy regions may be more resilient than urban-service regions.** This repeats rev162’s anti-uniformity reminder and adds no new structural category.
40. **L040 — [N/H] Deposit stickiness may come from social/local relationships, payroll/payment ecosystems and branch density rather than higher rates.** This can make some rural franchises unusually resilient to market-wide deposit competition.

## Orthogonal conclusion and stop decision

This challenge does **not** recover a second period-wide BANK-RURAL structural category. It instead sharpens a cohort model:

- `funding geography` is an overlay on legal institution type;
- village banks are a distinct sponsor-led lineage where remote deposits and control gaps can become unusually salient;
- RCC/RCB-derived institutions remain highly heterogeneous, including strong local-deposit county franchises;
- provincial unions/shared services are cross-cutting coordination mechanisms rather than pooled-solvency entities;
- depositor confidence is a transmission mechanism whose speed can be amplified by digital channels;
- public capital, merger and stronger provincial integration are responses/organizational reforms whose exact in-period timing must not be backfilled from later outcomes.
