## Blind-memory leads

1. **[useful_refinement | high] Period-native actor class was closer to `国有专业银行` than the later mature `大型国有商业银行` concept.** The four large institutions carried sectoral/professional mandates inherited from the planned-credit system. Search later should preserve the vocabulary transition rather than backcast today's bank taxonomy.

2. **[new_category | high] Industrial and Commercial Bank of China had a material pre-1990 institutional antecedent in the separation of ordinary commercial banking functions from the central-bank apparatus.** This helps explain why early-1990s ICBC still looked like a recently separated nationwide state credit machine rather than a modern shareholder-value bank. Exact organizational dates/details require Evidence.

3. **[new_category | high] China Construction Bank's early-1990s balance-sheet role likely retained strong fixed-asset/capital-construction financing legacies, including the earlier shift from fiscal appropriation toward loan financing often remembered under `拨改贷`-type vocabulary.** The relevant lead is the fiscal-to-bank migration of investment risk, not an exact rule title.

4. **[new_category | high] Bank of China was not merely another interchangeable domestic state bank.** Its foreign-exchange, trade-finance, international-settlement and overseas-network inheritance likely made its asset/liability mix, customer base and policy interface structurally different from the more domestically specialized banks.

5. **[new_category | high] Agricultural Bank of China should be kept distinct from rural credit cooperatives.** ABC was a nationwide state bank with an agriculture/rural mandate, while RCCs were a separate institutional layer with different ownership/governance histories. Collapsing them would erase an important state-bank versus rural-finance boundary.

6. **[new_category | high] The four specialized banks still combined policy/administrative credit functions with emerging commercial-accounting incentives.** Loan allocation could reflect state plans, sector priorities, SOE continuity and local policy objectives even while reform language increasingly stressed enterprise-style operation and financial discipline.

7. **[new_category | high] Large state-bank branches were important agency nodes, not passive head-office pipes.** Provincial/city branches likely faced simultaneous vertical instructions from the bank system and horizontal pressure from local governments and major local SOEs; this could shape project approval, rollover behavior and the geography of credit risk.

8. **[new_category | high] Soft-budget-constraint lending to state enterprises is a core balance-sheet mechanism for this period.** A loan could function partly as working-capital support, employment/social-stability finance or quasi-fiscal continuation rather than as an arms-length risk-priced commercial asset. This is a mechanism lead, not a claim that every SOE loan was noncommercial.

9. **[useful_refinement | high] The creation of separate policy banks around the end of this slice was a structural attempt to peel policy finance away from state specialized banks.** The key research question is what assets/functions actually migrated and how incomplete the separation remained, rather than assuming a one-day switch to pure commercial banking.

10. **[new_category | high] Policy/commercial separation likely remained incomplete after the institutional redesign.** State commercializing banks could still face directed lending, legacy policy assets, government-mandated support and implicit expectations to stabilize key borrowers. Later research should distinguish legal/institutional separation from realized balance-sheet separation.

11. **[new_category | high] Asset-quality vocabulary itself is a period-semantic issue.** Before later five-category loan classification became standard, historical materials may use categories such as overdue, doubtful/sluggish or bad/dead loans and the remembered phrase `一逾两呆`. These should not be mechanically mapped into later NPL ratios without definition reconciliation.

12. **[new_category | high] Modern capital-adequacy, provisioning and risk-weighted-asset concepts should not be backcast as the dominant internal management frame of 1990–1994.** Capital buffers, provisioning discipline and bad-loan recognition were likely less standardized, so apparent profitability could coexist with economically weak legacy assets.

13. **[new_category | medium] Deposit mobilization and the nationwide branch network were strategic operating capabilities in their own right.** State banks competed to gather household and enterprise deposits even while loan quantities/prices were heavily administered; branch deposit surpluses and deficits could matter for internal fund allocation.

14. **[new_category | medium] Sector specialization created different maturity structures and credit-risk concentrations.** Construction/fixed-investment finance, industrial-commercial working capital, agricultural/rural credit and foreign-trade/FX business should be treated as distinct starting balance-sheet archetypes rather than one homogeneous `big four` model.

15. **[new_category | high] The PBOC–state-bank funding relationship was still operationally important.** Central-bank relending/refinancing and planned credit arrangements could support or discipline bank balance sheets, meaning deposit funding alone may not explain marginal lending capacity. Exact instrument mechanics belong in later Evidence work.

16. **[new_category | medium] `企业化经营`, `自主经营` and related reform language may be important contemporary search keys for the transition from administrative specialized banks toward commercial banks.** These terms should be treated as reform aspirations/operating principles, not proof that modern governance had already been achieved.

17. **[new_category | high] Corporate-governance architecture remained very different from later joint-stock/listed banks.** Ownership was state-based, modern boards/shareholder accountability were limited or absent, senior appointments were administrative/political, and profitability/risk incentives coexisted with public-policy obligations.

18. **[new_category | medium] Bank heterogeneity likely existed strongly below the institution label.** Coastal/high-deposit branches, inland policy-heavy branches, commodity/industrial regions and major project centers could have very different deposit bases, borrower quality and pressure to expand or roll over credit.

19. **[new_category | high] A useful counterfactual is the later recapitalization/AMC/listing sequence: those major cleanups belong after this slice and should not be backcast into 1990–1994.** For this period the research object is the accumulation and partial recognition of legacy risk, not the later solution architecture.

20. **[new_category | medium] State banks were also core payment/settlement and enterprise-cash-management infrastructure.** Their relationship with state enterprises included account administration and settlement flows, so bank–enterprise dependence was broader than a loan contract alone.

## Counterexamples / temporal traps

- Do not use today's `六大行` or listed-bank governance as the 1990–1994 actor model.
- Do not collapse ABC and RCCs into one rural-bank institution class.
- Do not assume the establishment of policy banks instantly removed policy obligations from the large state banks.
- Do not translate old bad-loan categories directly into later five-category NPL ratios.
- Do not backcast later AMCs, large recapitalizations, Basel-style capital management, listings or 2019-era loan-pricing concepts into this slice.
