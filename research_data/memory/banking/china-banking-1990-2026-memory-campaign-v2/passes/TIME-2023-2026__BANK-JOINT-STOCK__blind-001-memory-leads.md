## Broad period-native recall leads

### Low-rate regime, NIM compression and asset repricing

1. **NIM compression became a central business-model constraint for joint-stock banks.** The analytical question shifted from whether margins would fall to how fast asset yields repriced relative to deposits and market funding. [new_category; high]
2. Low LPR/loan pricing and competition for high-quality corporate borrowers likely reduced front-book yields faster than some legacy liabilities could reprice. [new_category; high]
3. Existing mortgages repriced downward through policy/contractual resets and later adjustments, creating a material asset-yield issue distinct from new-mortgage pricing. [new_category; high]
4. “存量房贷利率调整/下调” is a useful period-native search key; exact rounds and mechanics belong to Evidence. [new_category; old_vocabulary]
5. Mortgage prepayment likely surged at points when household deposit/investment returns fell below mortgage coupons or refinancing expectations changed; this affects asset duration as well as credit risk. [new_category]
6. Mortgage prepayment is not automatically negative for capital: faster principal return can reduce RWA/duration while hurting interest income. [new_category; counterexample]
7. “Asset shortage / 资产荒” is a useful contemporaneous shorthand for competition for acceptable-yield, acceptable-risk assets, but it should be interpreted as relative pricing scarcity rather than literal absence of loan demand. [new_category; contemporaneous_judgment]
8. Banks could defend NIM by increasing higher-yield retail/SME exposure, but that trades spread support for potentially higher credit cost. [new_category; mechanism]
9. A bank with a slower decline in NIM may have better deposit franchise, slower asset repricing, riskier asset mix, or hedging/treasury effects; the metric is not self-explanatory. [new_category; metric_semantics]
10. Front-book/back-book analysis remains crucial: new corporate loans can price at thin spreads while older assets still carry higher yields for a time. [useful_refinement]
11. Loan repricing frequency differs across mortgages, corporate floating-rate loans, fixed-rate consumer loans and bond investments; “asset yield” is an aggregate outcome of multiple clocks. [new_category]
12. The spread contribution of fees and treasury income became more important as pure lending margins compressed. [new_category]
13. “以量补价” / volume-for-price thinking is a useful management-narrative lead: asset growth can partially offset lower spreads but increases capital and operating demands. [new_category; contemporaneous_judgment]
14. A failed narrative to test is that lower rates automatically stimulate enough loan growth to preserve interest income; weak borrower demand or risk appetite can break that mechanism. [new_category; failed_narrative]

### Deposits, termization and liability repricing

15. **Deposit termization remained a major NIM problem:** customers preferred time deposits or higher-yield savings products while banks wanted more current-account and settlement balances. [new_category; high]
16. Repeated downward repricing of deposit products is a major memory lead, but the exact sequence of rate adjustments should be verified later. [new_category]
17. Long-tenor deposits can provide stability yet lock in expensive funding when market rates fall; stability and cost can move in opposite directions. [new_category; high]
18. “定期化/长期化” and “存款成本刚性” are useful analyst phrases for the period. [new_category; old_vocabulary]
19. Corporate settlement deposits became especially valuable because they combine low coupon cost with transaction data and cross-sell potential. [new_category]
20. Some high-cost corporate deposit practices may have been curtailed or normalized during the period; “手工补息” is a useful uncertain search key, but exact regulatory timing and cohort effects require Evidence. [new_category; uncertain_fragment]
21. Eliminating off-book/manual interest supplements can lower funding cost but may also drive rate-sensitive corporate balances away. [new_category; mechanism]
22. Deposit rate cuts do not pass through instantly because legacy time deposits mature gradually; average funding cost can lag quoted rates materially. [new_category; high]
23. New deposits versus average deposit cost should be separated; marginal repricing can look dramatic before it materially changes the stock. [new_category; metric_semantics]
24. Wealth products, bond funds, money-market funds, insurance savings and deposits compete for the same household wallet; bank funding analysis should be linked to total customer assets. [useful_refinement]
25. Falling market yields can push customers into longer deposits or savings-type insurance, potentially weakening demand deposits even when total customer assets rise. [new_category]
26. A bank may lower deposit cost by losing expensive deposits, so improved funding cost can coexist with weaker balance growth. [new_category; counterexample]
27. Conversely, rapid deposit growth can be low quality if purchased at high marginal rates; volume is not franchise quality. [new_category; counterexample]
28. Digital channels can accelerate deposit migration because customers compare rates/products more easily, reducing traditional branch-local pricing power. [new_category]

### Property resolution, project financing and mortgages

29. Property-developer stress remained a key asset-quality and capital question, but the focus increasingly shifted from broad developer default headlines toward project-level completion, restructuring and loss allocation. [new_category; high]
30. “保交楼/保交房”, project white-list/“白名单”, mergers, restructuring and special project financing are useful period-native leads; exact program chronology belongs to Evidence. [new_category; old_vocabulary]
31. A bank can support project completion while reducing parent-developer risk if funding is ring-fenced to viable projects; project and group credit should be separated. [new_category]
32. Property exposure should be decomposed into development loans, mortgages, construction/supplier finance, operating-property loans, bonds, WMP holdings and guarantees. [useful_refinement; metric_semantics]
33. Developer loan NPLs can rise while mortgage credit remains relatively resilient; portfolio-level property risk is not a single curve. [new_category; counterexample]
34. Residential mortgage repricing can reduce borrower burden and future defaults while simultaneously compressing bank asset yields. [new_category; counterexample]
35. Mortgage prepayment can be driven by falling mortgage rates, weak investment opportunities, household deleveraging preference or home-sale transactions; no single cause should be assumed. [new_category]
36. Property-linked transaction deposits can shrink with presales and construction activity even before bank credit losses crystallize. [new_category]
37. Urban/regional concentration remains critical because property cycles and local fiscal dependence vary sharply across cities. [new_category]
38. A failed narrative to test is “developer cleanup solves all housing exposure”; mortgages, suppliers, land-related LGFV cash flow and local consumption can remain linked. [new_category; failed_narrative]

### LGFV/local-government exposure and debt replacement

39. **LGFV/local-government debt resolution became a distinct yield-versus-credit trade-off for banks.** Refinancing or debt replacement can lower near-term credit risk while extending duration and compressing asset yield. [new_category; high]
40. “化债”, “置换”, “再融资债券” and “平台退出/转型” are useful search keys; exact programs and accounting treatments require later verification. [new_category; old_vocabulary]
41. Bank exposure should be separated into explicit government bonds, LGFV loans, bonds, nonstandard investments and project finance; moving from one bucket to another can alter risk weights and yield without eliminating economic exposure. [new_category]
42. A debt swap from high-yield LGFV loan to lower-yield government-linked instrument can improve credit quality while worsening NIM. [new_category; counterexample]
43. Longer maturity and lower coupon can reduce refinancing risk but create duration and opportunity-cost risk for banks. [new_category]
44. LGFV resolution can also affect deposits: local platforms, contractors and fiscal accounts may change cash balances as financing structures shift. [new_category]
45. Distinguish “risk resolution” from “cash repayment”: restructuring, maturity extension, interest reduction and asset transfer have different economics. [new_category; metric_semantics]
46. Banks with stronger local-government or infrastructure relationships may receive both franchise benefits and lower-yield asset allocations. [new_category]
47. Joint-stock banks likely differ from large state banks in policy-role intensity; treating all banks as equally exposed to local debt resolution would be misleading. [new_category; counterexample]

### Corporate, private-enterprise and inclusive credit

48. Weak private-sector demand can coexist with strong competition for top-quality corporates, producing both sluggish credit appetite and thin spreads. [new_category; high]
49. Manufacturing, green, strategic-emerging-industry and technology-finance narratives likely grew in importance as banks searched for new asset pools; exact category definitions need Evidence. [new_category]
50. “科技金融/绿色金融/普惠金融/先进制造” became business-development labels as well as policy categories; their risk-adjusted economics should be tested separately. [new_category; old_vocabulary]
51. Inclusive micro/small-business growth can diversify credit but may require more data, guarantee support, pricing discipline and collection infrastructure. [new_category]
52. Private-enterprise-oriented banks remain especially sensitive to weak business confidence and property-adjacent corporate networks. [useful_refinement]
53. Transaction banking, supply-chain finance and payroll can provide low-cost deposits and data while generating lending opportunities. [new_category]
54. Supply-chain finance can reduce information gaps if anchored to strong buyers, but can concentrate risk around a dominant anchor. [new_category; counterexample]
55. “High-quality asset competition” can push banks toward state-linked corporates even if management publicly emphasizes private or SME lending; strategic narrative and marginal pricing can diverge. [new_category]
56. A counterexample to “lower rates help SMEs”: if weak demand reflects confidence rather than funding cost, cheaper credit may not generate profitable borrowing. [new_category; counterexample]

### Wealth/AUM, funds, insurance and fee economics

57. Wealth management became more important as a defense against NIM compression, but fee rates and customer risk appetite matter as much as AUM volume. [new_category; high]
58. Wealth-subsidiary AUM is economically different from parent-bank deposits and loans; customer AUM should be decomposed by product and fee rate. [useful_refinement; metric_semantics]
59. Falling bond yields can boost mark-to-market performance of fixed-income wealth products for a time, supporting customer migration from deposits; later yield reversals could expose duration risk. [new_category]
60. Bank-distributed savings-type insurance can compete with deposits for long-duration household assets while generating distribution fees. [new_category]
61. “银保” fee economics may have faced pressure/normalization in this period; preserve as a lead, not a verified chronology. [new_category; uncertain_fragment]
62. Funds, WMPs and insurance can deepen customer relationships but reduce balance-sheet funding if money leaves deposits. [new_category; counterexample]
63. AUM growth with lower fee take-rate can produce weaker revenue than headline asset growth suggests. [new_category; metric_semantics]
64. Wealth advisers and private-banking RMs remain labor-intensive, limiting the idea that digitalization makes wealth distribution nearly costless. [new_category]
65. “大财富管理” and “财富管理银行” remain useful strategy phrases, but exact institutional slogans should be verified and not treated as cohort-wide. [new_category; old_vocabulary]

### Bonds, investment books and treasury

66. Falling market yields can generate valuation/trading gains on bond books while compressing reinvestment yields and future interest income. [new_category; high]
67. A bond bull market can therefore improve current treasury income and worsen future asset yield simultaneously. [new_category; counterexample]
68. Duration extension is a key hidden trade-off when banks chase yield in a low-rate environment. [new_category]
69. Banking-book versus trading-book bond exposures should be distinguished because accounting volatility and earnings recognition differ. [new_category; metric_semantics]
70. Government/local-government bond growth may support liquidity and low credit risk while lowering average asset yield. [new_category]
71. Credit-bond exposure to property/LGFV/private issuers can transmit stress outside the loan book. [new_category]
72. “债券牛市/长久期/票息下行” are useful analyst search keys; exact yield levels/dates are Evidence-stage work. [new_category; old_vocabulary]
73. A failed narrative to test is that treasury gains permanently offset NIM compression; gains can be cyclical and non-repeatable. [new_category; failed_narrative]

### ECL, NPL, provisions, capital and cost discipline

74. Low credit demand can reduce RWA growth, but property/LGFV cleanup can increase provisions and capital uncertainty; slow asset growth is not necessarily low risk. [new_category]
75. ECL Stage 2 remains useful for tracking watch-list migration before formal NPL, especially in restructured property/LGFV exposures. [useful_refinement]
76. Write-offs, transfers and restructuring can reduce NPL stock while credit cost remains elevated; ending NPL ratio alone is insufficient. [useful_refinement]
77. Capital planning becomes harder when margins compress because retained earnings replenish capital more slowly. [new_category; high]
78. Dividend policy, AT1/perpetual-style capital instruments, convertibles or other capital tools may matter for different banks; exact issuance details require verification. [new_category; uncertain_fragment]
79. Lower RWA density can result from growth in mortgages/government bonds or from migration away from riskier corporate assets; economic interpretation depends on composition. [new_category; metric_semantics]
80. Cost-to-income pressure can rise even during cost cutting if revenue falls faster than expenses. [new_category; counterexample]
81. Branch consolidation, headcount mix, centralized operations and technology spending should be analyzed together rather than assuming “digital = lower cost.” [new_category]
82. Credit cost and pre-provision profit become especially important together: a low-margin bank needs operating efficiency to absorb cleanup costs. [useful_refinement]

87. Branch productivity should be measured in deposits/AUM/revenue/customer retention, not only transactions per employee. [new_category; metric_semantics]
88. Remote RM coverage can raise customer reach but also create relationship dilution if too many customers are assigned per adviser. [new_category]
89. Open-banking/API and embedded corporate finance remain potential ways to defend transaction deposits against platform competition. [new_category]
90. Digital marketing cost and incentive spending can offset branch savings; acquisition economics remain central. [new_category]
