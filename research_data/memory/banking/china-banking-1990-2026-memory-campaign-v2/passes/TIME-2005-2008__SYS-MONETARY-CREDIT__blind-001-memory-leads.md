## Broad regime picture

My memory of 2005-2008 is a high-liquidity regime in which the exchange-rate framework became more flexible while persistent external surpluses and appreciation expectations complicated domestic monetary control. The PBOC repeatedly used reserve requirements, central-bank bills/open-market operations and benchmark-rate adjustments alongside window guidance and credit controls. Bank reform/listings changed lending incentives; property, equity and fixed-investment cycles amplified transmission. The period then split sharply inside 2008: inflation/overheating concern early, global-crisis support and credit easing later.

- **Confidence:** high at regime level; exact implementation details unresolved
- Around the middle of the decade the RMB moved away from the prior tightly fixed dollar relationship toward a managed floating/reference-basket framework.
- Monetary relevance: exchange-rate flexibility did not eliminate intervention; appreciation expectations and continued FX purchases could still create domestic liquidity.

## Failure/dead-end and counterexample set

1. **RMB appreciation did not automatically tighten domestic money.** Intervention and FX settlement could inject RMB liquidity even under a stronger currency.
2. **Repeated reserve hikes do not prove credit contraction.** Strong deposits, recapitalized banks, borrower demand and external liquidity could offset part of the mechanical restraint.
3. **Nominal rate hikes do not equal high real rates.** Inflation and administered deposit/loan pricing matter.
4. **Shibor was not a modern policy rate.** It was a reference/market-rate development inside a multi-layer framework.
5. **Credit guidance did not disappear with bank listings.** Commercialization and administrative pacing could coexist.
6. **2008 cannot be represented by one stance label.** The policy problem shifted materially within the year.
7. **Short-term money-market volatility need not equal policy intent.** IPO/fiscal/settlement flows can create autonomous liquidity shocks.
8. **Property/equity moderation cannot be assigned solely to PBOC tools.** Administrative, fiscal, regulatory and global-market channels coexist.

## Contemporaneous intent/judgment leads

- 2005-2007 policymakers likely viewed abundant liquidity, excessive fixed investment/credit, asset prices and later inflation as increasingly material risks while still managing exchange-rate reform and bank restructuring.
- The tension was structural: stronger external surpluses and appreciation expectations added liquidity while domestic policy was trying to restrain credit and overheating.
- By late 2008, the dominant contemporaneous risk perception appears to have flipped toward external-demand collapse, financial stress and growth/employment downside; easing intent should be preserved as a new Judgment state rather than overwriting the preceding tightening state.
