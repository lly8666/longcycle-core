## 1. Period frame and contemporaneous judgment

- The state-owned-bank problem appears to shift from the prior period's acute pandemic/property shock toward a slower-burn combination of weak nominal growth, persistent property adjustment, local-government debt resolution and structurally lower asset yields.
- A central contemporaneous tension is likely: large state banks are expected to transmit lower financing costs and provide stabilization credit while remaining listed commercial banks accountable for NIM, ROE, capital generation and asset quality.
- Scale and deposit franchise can become less obviously advantageous when safe asset yields fall faster than liability costs; a very large balance sheet may amplify small spread changes.
- Credit growth can look healthy in volume terms while marginal risk-adjusted returns deteriorate because policy-priority lending is aggressively priced.
- The balance-sheet question is increasingly not only “will borrowers default?” but also “at what yield and duration can a large bank redeploy abundant funding without accumulating concentration or duration risk?”
- State support is a funding/franchise advantage but does not neutralize profitability, duration, operating-cost or recognition risks.

## 2. Low-rate transmission, pricing and NIM mechanics

- Loan/LPR-linked assets can reprice downward faster than the full deposit book, especially when legacy time deposits mature gradually; this produces a deposit-beta timing problem rather than a single policy-rate effect.
- Existing mortgage repricing is a distinct asset-yield channel from new-mortgage pricing and new corporate-loan pricing.
- NIM, net interest income, average asset yield, loan yield, deposit cost and total liability cost should not be treated as interchangeable metrics.
- Asset growth can partly offset a lower spread in net-interest-income terms even when NIM continues to decline; whether this creates value depends on credit cost, capital consumption and operating cost.
- FTP remains a useful internal vocabulary lead: branch/product profitability can look different after transfer pricing assigns the value/cost of deposits and duration.
- Deposit repricing can lag loan repricing because time-deposit cohorts mature at different dates; this creates a “stock deposit” versus “newly priced deposit” distinction analogous to loan-vintage analysis.
- Low-yield policy lending may be commercially rational if expected loss and capital intensity are low, but headline pricing alone cannot establish profitability.
- A loan-rate cut can help borrower solvency while simultaneously compressing bank margin; macro stabilization and bank earnings effects can move in opposite directions.
- The relevant contemporaneous debate may include whether NIM has a practical floor given capital, operating-cost and provision requirements, but any exact threshold should be evidence-verified later.

## 3. Deposit termization, repricing and customer migration

- “存款定期化” is an important period-native search lead: customers may move from demand deposits into time deposits when risk appetite is weak and deposit returns remain attractive relative to volatile alternatives.
- Deposit termization can increase average funding cost even while posted deposit rates are being cut.
- Corporate settlement deposits, retail demand deposits, ordinary time deposits, large-denomination certificates and structured/market-linked deposits should be separated; their pricing and stability differ.
- A deposit-rate reduction affects new money and maturing cohorts first, not the entire stock instantaneously.
- Customer funds can migrate among deposits, bank wealth products, bond/fund products, insurance and securities accounts; “deposit loss” can sometimes be group-level customer-asset migration rather than franchise loss.
- Large state banks may defend deposits less aggressively than funding-constrained smaller banks because of franchise depth, but scale means even modest changes in deposit mix matter materially.
- “手工补息” is recalled as a potentially period-native deposit-pricing/search term; exact regulatory wording and timing are uncertain and must not be promoted without evidence.
- Off-balance customer wealth and on-balance deposits can substitute for each other, but they carry different NIM, fee, liquidity and conduct economics.
- Deposit competition is not only quoted rate: payroll, settlement, custody, ecosystem access, wealth service and digital convenience can affect retention.
- A cheap deposit franchise can become more valuable under low rates, yet deposit termization can erode that advantage without causing an outright funding shortage.

## 4. Mortgage repricing and household behavior

- Mortgage prepayment is a distinct profitability and duration lead: borrowers may prepay higher-rate legacy mortgages when alternative funding or savings economics make repayment attractive.
- Mortgage prepayment reduces outstanding high-yield assets and can accelerate reinvestment into lower-yield assets.
- Adjusting rates on outstanding mortgages can reduce prepayment incentives and borrower burden while also lowering bank asset yield.
- Mortgage credit risk, mortgage prepayment risk and developer/project risk should remain separate even though all are linked to housing.
- Falling house prices can weaken collateral buffers without immediately creating mortgage NPLs if household cash flow remains adequate.
- CCB may remain a useful heterogeneity check because housing finance and related customer ecosystems are relatively salient to its franchise; exact exposure shares require later evidence.

## 5. Developer, project-completion and property-resolution boundaries

- Developer-group credit, project-company loans, construction/completion funding and household mortgages are different claims on different cash flows; broad “property exposure” is too coarse.
- A project can be economically viable or socially prioritized even if its parent developer is distressed; lending to complete a project does not equal recapitalizing developer equity.
- “保交楼”/project-completion support remains a period-native bridge lead because completion protects homebuyer confidence, mortgage performance and collateral values.
- “房地产融资协调机制” and “白名单” are recalled as relevant search vocabulary for project-level financing coordination; exact scope, dates and bank obligations require evidence later.
- A white-list or coordination mechanism does not by itself prove a loan is low-risk; project presales, construction cost, seniority, collateral, sales pace and sponsor support still matter.
- New project financing can coexist with recognition/disposal of legacy developer exposures, so aggregate property lending growth may conceal large vintage differences.
- Restructuring, maturity extension, interest adjustment or project transfer can reduce near-term default but complicate comparisons of headline NPL ratios.
- State banks may be asked to distinguish “support reasonable financing needs” from indiscriminate developer rescue; the exact official phrase should be source-verified rather than reconstructed from memory.
- Counterexample: project-completion finance can preserve mortgage/project value while developer shareholders and some creditors still suffer losses.

## 6. LGFV/local-government debt resolution

- The local-debt problem appears increasingly to involve refinancing, maturity extension, lower coupons/rates and debt-form conversion rather than a simple binary default/non-default frame.
- “化债”, “债务置换”, “展期”, “降息”, “统筹化解” and related phrases are useful search keys, but exact program boundaries and dates should be verified later.
- Extending maturity can reduce near-term liquidity/default risk while increasing bank duration and lowering reinvestment flexibility.
- Lowering loan rates can improve local borrower debt service but reduce bank asset yield.
- Replacing a loan with a government or quasi-government bond changes legal form, accounting/market-risk treatment, liquidity, yield and duration; it should not automatically be labeled equivalent risk elimination.
- Local fiscal concentration can persist after refinancing even if the immediate borrower or instrument changes.
- Land-sale weakness remains a plausible repayment-source stressor for entities whose cash flows or local support depend indirectly on land/fiscal conditions.
- Some LGFV borrowers have operating assets and genuine cash flows; “LGFV” is not a uniform credit category and contains counterexamples to a pure implicit-guarantee story.
- A state bank can rationally accept lower yield in exchange for improved seniority, clearer repayment arrangements or lower tail-default risk, but the trade needs claim-level evidence later.
- Debt resolution can convert credit-tail risk into spread, duration and opportunity-cost risk rather than making the exposure disappear economically.

## 7. Asset quality, recognition and capital semantics

- A stable or improving headline NPL ratio can coexist with material stress if denominator growth, write-offs, disposals, restructurings and recoveries are moving simultaneously.
- NPL stock, new NPL formation, NPL ratio, overdue loans, special-mention loans, restructured loans, write-offs and credit cost should remain distinct.
- “特殊提及”, “重组贷款”, “不良生成率”, “核销” are useful period vocabulary/search leads.
- Property and local-debt restructurings can change timing of recognition; restructuring should not automatically be interpreted as either concealment or cure.
- Provision coverage can remain high while absolute provisions or credit costs change; coverage ratio alone is not a flow measure.
- Margin compression can weaken internal capital generation even if credit costs fall.
- RWA optimization matters more when low-yield assets consume capital; asset growth and capital adequacy should be read together.
- TLAC/loss-absorption funding is a salient state-bank capital/funding lead for the period, but exact bank applicability, issuance timing and requirements must be evidence-verified.
- Capital instruments, retained earnings and RWA management are different routes to preserving capital ratios.
- Counterexample: a safer low-yield sovereign/local-government bond can reduce credit-risk intensity but still create duration/reinvestment economics that matter to profitability.

## 8. Treasury, bonds and duration

- Falling market yields can create mark-to-market gains on existing longer-duration bonds while simultaneously lowering future reinvestment income.
- Banking-book versus trading-book treatment matters; “bond gains” cannot be generalized across accounting classifications.
- Abundant deposits plus weak loan demand can push more balance-sheet capacity toward bonds, making duration choices more important.
- Extending duration to defend yield can increase sensitivity to a later rate reversal.
- Local-government debt conversion can increase holdings of longer-duration government-related instruments even if loan balances decline.
- Treasury income can temporarily offset NIM pressure, but unrealized/realized valuation effects and recurring carry are different.
- Bond duration, credit spread and liquidity risk should be separated; a low-credit-risk asset can still have large market-value duration risk.

## 9. Wealth-management subsidiaries and customer assets

- Bank wealth subsidiaries and NAV-based products are now more mature than in the shadow-banking era, making customer-asset migration a franchise/fee issue rather than simply hidden bank credit.
- Parent-bank distribution, wealth-subsidiary asset management, custody and balance-sheet exposure are separate roles.
- Deposit inflows after weak wealth/bond performance can later reverse as risk appetite or yields change; deposits and wealth AUM can be substitutes at the customer level.
- NAV volatility creates conduct/reputation risk even when the investment loss is contractually borne by customers.
- Wealth fee income can help diversify revenue but cannot mechanically offset NIM compression if fee rates or AUM mix fall.
- Legacy expectations of implicit support may fade unevenly; actual support obligations require product-level evidence.

## 10. Policy-priority credit and asset-mix economics

- Inclusive/micro, advanced manufacturing, green, technology/strategic-sector and other policy-priority lending are salient business-mix leads.
- “普惠小微”, “科技金融”, “绿色金融”, manufacturing lending and similar labels should be preserved as search vocabulary without assuming identical official taxonomies across years.
- Large state banks can use branch networks, transaction data and scale to penetrate SME/micro segments once dominated by smaller banks, potentially changing industry pricing.
- Policy-priority growth targets can create tension between volume, pricing, expected loss and operational servicing cost.
- A lower nominal loan rate does not necessarily imply lower risk-adjusted profitability if loss rates, collateral or RWA treatment differ.
- Digital underwriting can reduce unit servicing cost but introduces model/data risk and may perform differently across economic regimes.
- State banks may play both commercial allocator and stabilization channel; these objectives can be aligned in some sectors and in tension in others.

## 11. Digital, AI, fraud and operating model

- Mobile banking and remote service continue to reduce dependence on routine branch transactions, but branches retain roles in complex sales, corporate relationships, rural access and trust-intensive services.
- Branch reduction/transformation should not be equated with branch irrelevance; the economically relevant metric is productivity and customer acquisition/service mix.
- AI/large-model use is a plausible period-native lead in customer service, coding/operations, document processing, anti-fraud and risk analysis; exact deployments and dates require verification.
- Model hallucination, explainability, customer privacy and data governance become operational/control concerns as AI use expands.
- Anti-fraud controls can reduce financial-crime losses while creating false positives, account restrictions and customer-friction costs.
- Remote-channel fraud, telecom/network fraud and mule-account controls can shift operating burden toward identity, behavior and transaction monitoring.
- Cyber resilience and third-party technology dependencies remain relevant even for large banks with substantial in-house infrastructure.
- Platform/payment competition is increasingly about ownership of the customer interface, data and transaction context rather than merely payment fee revenue.

## 12. Small-bank/system-support boundary

- Small-bank consolidation or stress matters to state banks mainly through interbank counterparty exposure, liquidity provision, payment/settlement links, asset transfers or broader confidence channels.
- A deposit-rich state bank can be a receiver of flight-to-quality funds during localized stress, which may improve liquidity while worsening reinvestment pressure.
- System-support activity should not be imported into BANK-STATE-OWNED as a regulatory-system claim unless it changes bank balance-sheet behavior.
- Counterexample: a liquidity-support role can be low credit loss but low return, so “beneficiary of flight to quality” is not equivalent to stronger profitability.

## 13. Cross-border, trade and FX

- BOC remains the clearest heterogeneity check for overseas branches, trade finance, FX and cross-border RMB business.
- RMB internationalization can create settlement, clearing, custody and corporate-service opportunities while increasing sanctions/compliance/geopolitical operating complexity.
- FX translation effects, trading/market risk and borrower credit risk are distinct.
- Weak or shifting global trade can affect fee/transaction flows even without direct overseas credit losses.
- Cross-border compliance cost and correspondent relationships can affect profitability in ways not visible in domestic NIM.

## 14. Four-bank heterogeneity checks

- ICBC: scale, broad corporate/retail franchise and very large deposit base make small changes in spread and asset mix economically important.
- CCB: housing/mortgage and infrastructure associations make property/project-completion and local-investment channels useful stress lenses.
- BOC: overseas/FX/trade channels make external rates, currencies and cross-border compliance a more material differentiator.

## 15. Metric and accounting semantics to preserve

- NIM ≠ net interest income.
- Loan yield ≠ total earning-asset yield.
- Deposit cost ≠ total liability cost.
- Posted deposit rate ≠ average stock deposit cost.
- NPL ratio ≠ NPL formation flow.
- Write-off ≠ economic recovery.
- Restructured loan ≠ automatically NPL, and performing ≠ automatically economically cured.
- Provision coverage ≠ reserve-to-loan ratio ≠ credit cost.
- Mortgage balance change combines new originations, scheduled amortization, prepayment and write-off/transfer effects.
- Property exposure must separate developer-group, project, mortgage, bonds/investments and off-balance commitments.
- Local-government exposure must separate direct government bonds, LGFV corporate credit, project finance and other public-sector claims.
- Wealth AUM ≠ parent-bank on-balance-sheet assets.
- Bond valuation gain ≠ recurring interest carry.
- Capital ratio ≠ absolute capital amount; RWA growth matters.
- Deposit growth ≠ lower funding cost; mix can worsen while volume rises.

## 16. Failed mechanisms, counterexamples and negative-space leads

- Cutting deposit rates may fail to deliver immediate funding-cost relief if expensive time-deposit cohorts reprice slowly.
- Mortgage-rate relief may stabilize borrowers but accelerate bank NIM compression.
- Property project support may preserve completion value without restoring developer solvency.
- LGFV refinancing may avert near-term default while entrenching low-yield long-duration assets.
- Stable headline NPLs may coexist with rising restructurings or special-mention exposures; alternatively, genuine cash-flow recovery can also make stable NPLs real rather than cosmetic.
- State-bank deposit inflows during risk aversion can be a mixed blessing when high-quality asset yields are falling.
- Rapid policy-sector loan growth can be commercially sound if risk and capital consumption are low; “policy lending equals bad lending” is an invalid shortcut.
- Digital channel growth can reduce transaction cost but does not eliminate relationship-intensive branch economics.
- AI can reduce operating cost yet create new governance/model risk; adoption is not equivalent to realized productivity.
- A larger bond book may diversify credit risk but increase duration and mark-to-market sensitivity.
- Wealth-product recovery can pull funds out of deposits while strengthening group customer economics; deposit decline is not necessarily customer loss.
- Low reported credit cost can reflect improved vintages, recoveries or prior provisioning, not necessarily weaker recognition.
