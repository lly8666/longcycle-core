## Orthogonal decision first
The six-angle falsification does **not** produce a second period-wide BANK-RURAL structural model for 1990-1994.

The licensed-bank core remains the rural credit cooperative system, with fragmented grassroots/cooperative units embedded in county-township economies and materially shaped by Agricultural Bank of China administrative/operational relationships, local-government and collective-sector influence, local deposit franchises, TVE/agricultural/household production credit, weak hard-capital and risk-recognition semantics, and a dispersed village-level service network.

Rural cooperative foundations, private/informal lending, postal savings and other adjacent channels are important because they alter competitive funding, credit rationing and failure dynamics, but they are not a second licensed BANK-RURAL bank archetype. The other challenged dimensions are mechanisms or reporting/governance layers inside the broader RCC model.

## 1. Rural cooperative foundations versus licensed RCCs

### Lead 1 — 农村合作基金会 is a parallel local-finance form, not synonymous with RCC
Rural cooperative foundations are recalled as local quasi-financial organizations that could mobilize funds and extend credit/invest in local projects. Their exact legal status, sponsoring authority and permitted activities varied and require Evidence. The critical scope rule is that they must not be silently merged into the licensed rural credit cooperative balance-sheet series.

Classification: new category, high importance.

### Lead 2 — foundations can compete for the same deposits and borrowers
Even if outside the bank perimeter, a rural foundation can attract household/collective funds and lend to township enterprises or local projects, affecting RCC deposit growth, loan pricing pressure and borrower selection.

Classification: new category.

### Lead 3 — foundation growth can reveal rationed formal credit
Where RCC/ABC credit controls, policy priorities or local risk limits restrict lending, quasi-financial foundations can emerge as an institutional response to unmet local credit demand.

Classification: new category.

### Lead 4 — local-government backing can create deposit-like confidence without bank-grade loss absorption
Local political sponsorship or township affiliation may make fund contributors expect implicit protection even when the vehicle lacks formal bank capital, deposit insurance or central-bank liquidity access.

Classification: new category.

### Lead 5 — later cleanup does not retroactively make these institutions banks
A foundation that later requires closure, merger, repayment support or administrative cleanup remains a boundary/dead-end mechanism, not evidence of a second BANK-RURAL bank species.

Classification: useful refinement.

### Lead 6 — informal finance is even more clearly outside the bank perimeter
Private lending, rotating associations, family credit and trade credit can substitute for RCC loans, but these are external channels. Their importance changes the completeness of a rural-credit picture, not the atomic-bank classification.

Classification: useful refinement.

**Orthogonal verdict on angle 1:** material adjacent category, but outside the licensed BANK-RURAL bank core; no second bank model.

## 2. RCC–ABC administrative, settlement and liquidity hierarchy

Classification: new category, high importance.

### Lead 8 — county ABC branch versus county RCC union is a key archival distinction
Historical materials may mention county agricultural-bank branches and county credit-cooperative unions in the same local system. Analysis must preserve separate legal/accounting perimeters even where management relationships are close.

Classification: new category.

### Lead 9 — settlement dependence can create functional hierarchy without full balance-sheet consolidation
Grassroots cooperatives likely needed access to payments, cash logistics, reserve settlement or inter-institution transfers through higher-level structures. Exact arrangements need Evidence, but operational dependence should not be mistaken for consolidated ownership.

Classification: new category.

### Lead 10 — liquidity support can obscure unit-level weakness
A small cooperative with seasonal funding stress may receive transfers or support from higher layers. System-level stability can coexist with economically weak grassroots units.

Classification: new category.

### Lead 11 — liquidity pooling can also redistribute rural savings geographically
If funds are pooled upward and reallocated, a county with strong deposits can export liquidity even without postal savings. The 'rural funds outflow' debate therefore may include multiple mechanisms.

Classification: new category.

Classification: useful refinement.

### Lead 13 — dual-principal governance creates accountability gaps

Classification: new category, high importance.

**Orthogonal verdict on angle 2:** hierarchy is a structural mechanism within the RCC model, not a separate rural-bank model.

## 5. Postal savings / local funds outflow

### Lead 28 — postal savings is a liability-side competitor with different asset recycling
Postal outlets can collect household savings in rural areas while the collected funds are not necessarily lent back through the same local economy. This creates a structurally different funds-flow channel from RCC deposit intermediation.

Classification: new category, high importance.

### Lead 29 — rural deposit growth does not equal locally available credit
A county can exhibit strong aggregate household saving while local enterprises and households remain credit constrained if savings migrate through postal or higher-level banking channels.

Classification: new category.

### Lead 30 — funds outflow can be rational, not merely extractive
Centralized allocation may reflect risk diversification, monetary arrangements or lack of bankable local projects. '资金外流' is a contemporaneous political-economy label that requires mechanism-specific interpretation.

Classification: new category.

### Lead 31 — RCC deposit franchise therefore has a public-policy dimension
Maintaining local savings within local cooperative intermediation can be framed as supporting county/rural development, which can strengthen pressure on RCCs to lend locally even when local risk is concentrated.

Classification: new category.

### Lead 32 — local lending pressure can worsen concentration
If policymakers respond to funds outflow by emphasizing local recycling, the result may improve access while increasing exposure to a narrow county economy.

Classification: new category.

### Lead 33 — regulated rates shift competition toward network and trust
When price competition is constrained, postal versus RCC competition may depend on convenience, safety perception, cash access, government credibility and outlet coverage.

Classification: useful refinement.

**Orthogonal verdict on angle 5:** external liability/funds-flow mechanism; no second BANK-RURAL bank model.
