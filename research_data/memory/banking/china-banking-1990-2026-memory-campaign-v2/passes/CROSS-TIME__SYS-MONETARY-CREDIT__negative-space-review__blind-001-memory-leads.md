## Material omission reopened: monetary-aggregate metric semantics

A long-horizon monetary-credit memory cannot safely treat `M0`, `M1`, `M2`, “money supply”, deposits, reserve money or adjacent monetary aggregates as timelessly comparable labels. The current compact map contains strong coverage of base money, reserve requirements, TSF vintage/perimeter, deposits and liquidity tools, but it does **not** yet demonstrate a dedicated blind audit of how monetary-aggregate definitions, component classifications and signal content changed across 1990-2026.

The reopened frontier should recover research leads around:

- period-native meanings and component boundaries of `M0` / `M1` / `M2` and related “货币供应量” vocabulary;
- deposit-classification and institutional-sector boundaries that can move an item between aggregates or change what growth means without an equivalent behavioral change;
- distinctions among fiscal/government deposits, commercial-bank deposits, non-bank-financial deposits, reserve/base money and broad money;
- statistical reclassification, series-break and publication-vintage risks that can make nominally identical growth rates non-comparable across periods;
- payment technology, deposit termization and financial deepening as reasons the signal content of narrow versus broad money may change even when the label persists;
- the boundary between monetary aggregates and already-covered `社会融资规模 / TSF` stock-flow/perimeter semantics, so the next pass does not simply duplicate TSF work;
- contemporary analytical vocabulary that treated money/credit divergences as meaningful, without inventing exact historical dates, formulas, thresholds or source wording.

This is material because Longcycle's long-cycle comparison goal fails if a recurring headline monetary metric is assumed semantically stable when its measurement universe or economic signal may have changed. Exact revision dates, official component rules, published series breaks and document language remain deferred Evidence work after a future valid seal.
