## 1. Obvious landmarks

1. **L001 — [R/H] Pandemic relief and repayment accommodation.** Rural and small banks were an important delivery channel for temporary repayment extensions, renewal/rollover, inclusive-micro support and other borrower relief. The research question is how much genuine borrower recovery versus delayed recognition sat behind apparently stable asset-quality metrics.
2. **L002 — [R/H] Property downturn and county-economy spillover.** From the later part of the slice, weaker property activity plausibly transmitted to county banks through developers, mortgages, construction suppliers, land-dependent local spending and household income rather than through one uniform direct-property exposure.
3. **L003 — [D/M] Margin pressure.** Lower asset yields, policy pressure to reduce borrower financing costs and sticky deposit costs likely compressed NIM for many rural banks; this is already explicit in rev161 and is retained only as a baseline landmark.
4. **L004 — [D/M] Digital-channel acceleration.** Pandemic-era remote service increased mobile/online adoption, but rev161 already names this theme; the useful work is to recover who depended on third-party platforms versus proprietary local channels.
5. **L005 — [R/M] WMP/net-value transition reached small-bank balance sheets unevenly.** Rural banks with legacy wealth-management, trust/channel or investment-book exposures may have faced valuation, customer-retention and clean-up pressures even where they lacked the scale to build dedicated wealth subsidiaries.
6. **L006 — [R/M] Small-bank consolidation became a more visible policy direction.** The period appears to contain early signals that provincial coordination, mergers, capital replenishment and stronger legal-person integration would become a preferred response to weak rural institutions, while the exact provincial sequences remain Evidence-stage questions.

## 2. Long-tail leads

7. **L007 — [N/H] Internet-platform deposit funding created a geography mismatch.** Some small local banks appear to have gathered deposits well outside their physical franchise through third-party internet platforms. This could transform a nominally local bank into a nationally funded balance sheet without corresponding local relationship advantages.
8. **L008 — [N/M] Curtailment of third-party internet deposits may have produced a liability shock that was highly bank-specific.** Banks that had priced aggressively for remote deposits could face slower growth or higher replacement funding costs after restrictions, whereas locally deposit-rich rural commercial banks might barely notice.
9. **L009 — [N/H] “Off-site deposit” restrictions are likely a better research key than generic fintech regulation.** Search later for terms such as `异地存款`, `互联网存款`, `第三方互联网平台存款`, and small-bank liability management rather than assuming a single platform-specific story.
10. **L010 — [N/H] Local-government special-bond or other fiscal-capital channels may have been used to replenish capital at selected small banks.** The important rural-bank question is not the aggregate program but which weak institutions received public capital, through what vehicle, and whether the capital came with governance/restructuring conditions.
11. **L011 — [R/M] Pandemic forbearance probably interacted with pre-existing “renewal without principal repayment” practices.** `无还本续贷`, rollover, extension and restructuring should not be treated as equivalent: some were policy-supported continuity tools, some may have obscured borrower deterioration, and accounting/regulatory treatment could differ.
12. **L012 — [N/H] Asset-quality stability may have been partly a timing phenomenon.** Rapid loan growth, moratoria, restructuring, write-offs, transfers and delayed migration from overdue/special-mention into NPL could all make reported NPL ratios look calmer than underlying borrower stress.
13. **L013 — [N/M] Agriculture and food-supply borrowers may have had a different pandemic path from service-sector micro firms.** Rural banks serving grain, livestock, agricultural inputs or protected supply chains could see countercyclical demand or policy support while tourism, restaurants, transport and small retail weakened.
14. **L014 — [N/M] Export-manufacturing counties are a possible positive counter-cycle pocket.** Some affluent county rural commercial banks may have benefited from resilient manufacturing/export customers and strong household deposits, making “rural bank” an especially misleading aggregate label in this slice.
15. **L015 — [N/H] Guarantee-chain and mutual-guarantee structures may have amplified SME stress.** In county/private-enterprise networks, one borrower failure could transmit through guarantees and related local firms even when direct collateral initially appeared sound.
16. **L016 — [N/H] Local land-finance weakness could affect rural banks through more than LGFV loans.** Channels may include local-government contractors, village/township projects, property collateral values, public-sector payroll ecosystems and implicit expectations of local support.
17. **L017 — [R/M] Mortgage stress should be separated from developer stress.** Rural banks could have small developer books but meaningful household mortgage or construction-supply exposure; the later mortgage-payment controversy may matter more as a confidence/income signal than as a direct rural-bank balance-sheet event.
18. **L018 — [N/H] Sponsor-bank incentives are a distinctive village-bank risk axis.** Village banks were typically founded with a sponsor-bank relationship, but local minority shareholders, remote operations and weak oversight could create a gap between formal sponsorship and effective control.
19. **L019 — [N/M] Village banks using online channels may have outgrown the monitoring assumptions of their local franchise model.** Remote deposit gathering plus dispersed customers could weaken the practical role of local social information and make depositor runs/confidence events more digitally transmissible.
20. **L020 — [N/M] Provincial rural-credit unions likely provided shared IT, payments, clearing, training, liquidity coordination and policy transmission, but also created principal-agent tensions.** Strong centralized services could coexist with weak governance of member legal persons.
21. **L021 — [N/M] Provincial-union technology centralization may have accelerated during pandemic remote-service demand.** This could be an underappreciated reason some tiny legal persons were able to offer sophisticated digital service despite limited stand-alone technology budgets.
22. **L022 — [N/M] Early provincial integration signals may have been partly a response to duplicated systems and fragmented governance, not only insolvency.** Later province-wide rural commercial bank structures should not be projected backward as if already complete during 2020-2022.

## 3. Forgotten actors / institutional surfaces

23. **L023 — [N/H] Provincial rural-credit union / rural financial-service platform itself is a first-class actor.** Research should recover its legal status, governance authority, liquidity-support role, shared-services role and limits in each province rather than treating “the province” as a unitary owner.
24. **L024 — [N/M] Local financial bureaus and provincial governments may have become more active capital/restructuring actors.** Their role can differ from banking regulators and from sponsor banks; later Evidence should preserve who actually decided capital injections, mergers or shareholder clean-ups.
25. **L025 — [N/M] Local AMCs and other asset-disposal channels may matter for rural-bank NPL clean-up.** The lead is to distinguish genuine sale/transfer, restructuring and write-off from simple reclassification, and to preserve whether risk remained locally concentrated after transfer.
26. **L026 — [N/M] Fintech/consumer-internet platforms were not merely technology vendors.** For some small banks they could be deposit-distribution, customer-acquisition, data or loan-channel partners, creating dependency and regulatory perimeter questions even without naming any platform at blind stage.

## 4. Failed / restructured institution leads

27. **L027 — [N/H] Henan village-bank depositor-access episode is a high-priority blind lead, not a verified fact bundle.** Recall suggests a cluster of village banks suffered severe depositor access/confidence problems linked in public discussion to shareholder/control, fraud or online-channel issues. Exact banks, actors, dates, amounts and legal findings must be recovered only from authoritative sources later.
28. **L028 — [N/M] The Henan episode may demonstrate that deposit insurance, local resolution, criminal investigation and ordinary bank supervision can overlap without being the same process.** A later trajectory should preserve which mechanism addressed liquidity, depositor repayment, governance and legal accountability.
29. **L029 — [N/M] Some weak rural institutions elsewhere may have been merged or absorbed into stronger rural commercial banks during or just after this slice.** Search later for mergers, new provincial-level legal persons and “high-risk institution” reduction, while avoiding backward-dating post-2022 restructurings.
30. **L030 — [R/M] Capital replenishment without full legal-person merger is a separate restructuring path.** Weak banks might receive new local/provincial state capital, shareholder replacement or balance-sheet clean-up while keeping their original legal entity for a time.

## 5. Mechanism leads

31. **L031 — [N/H] Forbearance can create a recognition cliff rather than remove credit loss.** If repayment relief expires while cash flow has not recovered, special-mention, overdue and NPL migration can bunch later; longitudinal analysis should compare cohorts by relief vintage rather than only year-end NPL ratios.
32. **L032 — [R/M] Deposit-rate rigidity can make policy easing asymmetric.** Asset yields may reprice down faster than household time deposits, especially where small banks compete for stable local funding and lack low-cost current-account franchises.
33. **L033 — [N/H] Digital deposit acquisition can worsen both funding cost and run velocity.** The same channel that cheaply expands geographic reach can create rate-sensitive customers with little local relationship stickiness, making funding more confidence-sensitive.
34. **L034 — [N/H] Local-support expectations can weaken market discipline until they suddenly fail.** Depositors and creditors may assume local government, provincial union, sponsor bank or deposit insurance will prevent losses, but those backstops differ in legal scope and timing.
35. **L035 — [N/M] Provincial shared-service arrangements may socialize operational resilience without socializing credit losses.** A member bank can share IT/clearing and receive liquidity coordination while still owning its credit and governance problems.
36. **L036 — [R/M] Consolidation can improve governance but also obscure heterogeneity.** Merging weak and strong county institutions into a larger legal person may diversify funding and professionalize control, yet historical replay should preserve the predecessor banks and local loss origins rather than backfill the successor as if it always existed.

## 7. Contemporaneous Judgment leads

41. **L041 — [N/H] Early-pandemic judgment may have been that repayment problems were temporary liquidity shocks.** Policy accommodation and rollover support plausibly rested on an expectation that viable micro/SME borrowers would normalize after activity resumed; later repeated outbreaks and property weakness challenge that assumption.
42. **L042 — [N/M] Small/rural banks were simultaneously framed as essential local-credit channels and as a concentration of governance risk.** This tension matters because policy could push them to lend more to vulnerable borrowers while also requiring faster risk clean-up and capital strengthening.
43. **L043 — [N/M] Internet platforms may initially have been viewed by small banks as a rational way to overcome branch/geography disadvantages.** Subsequent restrictions would then represent a change in the accepted boundary of local-bank funding, not merely a technical rule change.
44. **L044 — [R/M] Consolidation expectations may have strengthened as isolated fixes appeared insufficient.** The hypothesis is that policy thinking shifted toward provincial coordination/legal-person integration for some regions, but the timing and official rationale require Evidence.

## 8. Metric-semantic leads

45. **L045 — [N/H] Reported NPL ratio is especially unsafe as a single stress measure in this slice.** Denominator loan growth, moratoria, restructuring, special-mention migration, write-offs and transfers can move the ratio independently of borrower recovery.
46. **L046 — [N/M] “Overdue” and “NPL” can diverge by recognition timing.** A bank with improving overdue-to-NPL recognition discipline may report rising NPLs even while underlying governance improves; cross-bank comparison needs classification policy and vintage context.
47. **L047 — [N/M] Capital adequacy can be temporarily improved by injections without proving franchise repair.** Capital ratio, ownership clean-up, asset disposal and recurring pre-provision profitability should be tracked separately.

## 10. Counterexamples / anti-uniformity leads

50. **L050 — [N/H] Affluent-county rural commercial banks can be structurally stronger than many urban peers.** Strong local private-enterprise ecosystems, sticky household deposits and disciplined SME underwriting can produce high-quality franchises; `rural` is not a synonym for weak.
51. **L051 — [N/M] Agricultural exposure is not automatically a pandemic loss channel.** Food security, commodity conditions, policy support and essential production can make some agricultural borrowers more resilient than urban service-sector borrowers.
52. **L052 — [N/M] Physical local networks may have remained a competitive advantage despite digital acceleration.** Relationship information, village/township coverage and older-customer trust can coexist with growing mobile usage rather than being simply displaced by fintech.
53. **L053 — [N/M] Village-bank sponsor/control failures should not be generalized to RCC/RCB lineage.** Village banks are a distinct sponsor-led institutional form; a confidence episode in several village banks does not prove province-wide rural commercial bank insolvency.
54. **L054 — [D/M] Property exposure is heterogeneous.** Rev161 already requires regional counterexamples; retain the reminder that some rural banks may have minimal direct developer exposure even when county economies suffer indirect property spillovers.

## Negative space and stop decision

The broad pass still leaves several important negative spaces: exact in-period provincial reform sequences; the relative importance of internet deposits versus traditional local deposits; whether Henan-type confidence failures reveal a distinct village-bank structural category or an extreme governance/channel failure; how pandemic relief translated into later recognition by bank cohort; and which rural-bank groups were resilient counterexamples.

The pass adds materially more than the rev161 compact frontier: 39 `new_category`, 11 `useful_refinement`, 4 `duplicate`, with 18 high-priority novel/refinement leads. The strongest new cluster is not a new rural-bank lineage but an interaction among **funding geography, sponsor/shareholder control, depositor confidence, provincial support and resolution boundaries**. Because broad recall remains materially novel, this slice should **not** advance to 2023-2026 yet.

Recommended single next probe: an independent same-slice orthogonal challenge focused on whether the 2020-2022 funding-geography / shareholder-control / depositor-confidence / provincial-support cluster is genuinely structural across BANK-RURAL or only an episodic village-bank tail. It should deliberately avoid re-running the broad pandemic-credit list.
