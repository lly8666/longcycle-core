## Broad blind leads

### 1. The early-2020 response combined system liquidity support with directed credit support rather than relying on one headline rate
**Classification:** new_category  
**Importance:** high

The pandemic shock appears to have produced a layered response: open-market/MLF liquidity, reserve relief, LPR transmission, relending/rediscount and targeted support for firms hit by shutdowns. A later reconstruction should separate reserve supply, bank funding cost, credit-risk incentives and borrower relief rather than label the entire episode as one generic easing cycle.

### 2. Special relending or preferential funding for epidemic-related production was a distinct emergency-credit channel
**Classification:** new_category  
**Importance:** medium-high

Memory suggests early emergency funding was directed through banks toward epidemic-control or essential-production firms. This should be treated as a conditional refinancing/intermediation mechanism, not assumed direct PBOC lending to enterprises. Exact eligible lists, rates and risk-bearing remain uncertain.

### 3. `直达实体经济的货币政策工具` marked a new policy vocabulary around bank-mediated small/micro support
**Classification:** new_category  
**Importance:** high

Two broad families are remembered: incentives around deferring principal/interest for small/micro loans and support for extending unsecured/credit loans. The important semantic is that the central bank created incentives or funding linked to commercial-bank behavior; the commercial bank still originated/managed borrower credit. Exact purchase/incentive percentages or legal form must be sourced later.

### 4. Principal-and-interest deferral is not the same object as loan forgiveness or credit-risk transfer
**Classification:** new_category  
**Importance:** high

Pandemic borrower relief could delay debt service while preserving principal claims and bank credit exposure. A research timeline must separate `payment deferral`, `maturity extension`, `renewal`, `interest concession`, `write-off` and `guarantee/risk-sharing`; collapsing them would misread both policy generosity and bank asset quality.

### 5. Credit-loan support to small/micro firms should distinguish central-bank incentive from bank credit decision
**Classification:** new_category  
**Importance:** high

A direct-to-real-economy tool may have rewarded banks for increasing qualifying unsecured small/micro lending, but this does not imply the central bank chose each borrower. Later Evidence should recover qualifying loan type, participating bank classes, incentive/funding formula and who retained default loss.

### 6. 2019 LPR reform and 2020 legacy floating-rate loan conversion are separate regime transitions
**Classification:** new_category  
**Importance:** high

In 2020, existing floating-rate loans reportedly faced a conversion choice or process involving LPR-linked repricing versus a fixed-rate alternative. This is distinct from the 2019 new-loan pricing reform. Point-in-time history needs separate states for new LPR-linked loans, old benchmark-linked stock, conversion election, and post-conversion repricing.

### 7. Existing mortgage conversion required a fixed spread/add-point concept that could persist after benchmark migration
**Classification:** new_category  
**Importance:** high

For legacy mortgages, moving from benchmark-rate linkage to LPR linkage did not necessarily reset the economic spread each repricing period. A fixed add-point derived from the conversion basis appears relevant. Exact calculation and dates are uncertain; preserve the state distinction without inventing formulas.

### 8. The pandemic did not push China into zero or negative policy rates, creating a distinctive `normal monetary policy` narrative
**Classification:** new_category  
**Importance:** high

Compared with several advanced economies, China retained positive conventional policy-rate space and repeatedly emphasized a normal monetary-policy framework. This is a contemporaneous policy-intent lead, not a normative judgment. Later sources should recover when that vocabulary appeared and how it coexisted with strong structural easing.

### 9. 2020 easing was followed relatively quickly by normalization in money-market operations and emergency tools
**Classification:** new_category  
**Importance:** high

After the first shock passed, interbank liquidity and emergency support appear to have normalized earlier than in some economies. This creates a within-slice regime change: emergency stabilization → normalization while targeted support persisted. Do not infer continuous maximum easing from full-year aggregates.

### 10. `跨周期调节` became an important distinction from purely countercyclical policy around 2021
**Classification:** new_category  
**Importance:** high

Memory suggests policy language increasingly emphasized cross-cyclical design—smoothing policy across current and future pressures rather than reacting only to the immediate output gap. This terminology deserves separate contemporaneous-vintage recovery and should not be equated mechanically with a particular instrument setting.

### 11. Reserve-requirement reductions in 2021-2022 likely mixed broad liquidity release with structural intent
**Classification:** useful_refinement  
**Importance:** high

As in the prior slice, gross RRR release, replacement of central-bank funding and net reserve effect are distinct. The later context also included efforts to lower bank funding costs and support the real economy. Bank-type reserve requirements may differ, so a single system RRR remains insufficient.

### 12. Carbon-emission-reduction support introduced a structural facility linked to qualifying green lending
**Classification:** new_category  
**Importance:** high

A carbon-reduction support tool appears to have provided low-cost central-bank funds to financial institutions based on qualifying carbon-reduction loans. The key mechanism is conditional reimbursement/funding after bank lending, not direct central-bank project lending. Exact coverage, percentage, tenor and rate must be sourced later.

### 13. Clean-coal or other structural facilities complicate any single `green tool` category
**Classification:** new_category  
**Importance:** medium

Memory suggests another structural tool around cleaner/efficient coal use or energy supply appeared near this period. This is a useful counterexample to overly simple green taxonomy. Research should preserve policy objective, eligible assets and whether facilities were separate or later merged/extended.

### 14. Inclusive small/micro support tools transitioned from emergency arrangements toward more regular structural instruments
**Classification:** new_category  
**Importance:** high

Some 2020 temporary direct tools appear to have been converted, extended or succeeded by standing support arrangements for inclusive small/micro lending. Historical state should preserve temporary emergency phase, extension, successor mechanism and final expiration/replacement rather than treating the support program as timeless.

### 15. Deposit-pricing self-discipline reform changed the liability side of monetary transmission
**Classification:** new_category  
**Importance:** high

Around 2021, the deposit-rate self-discipline mechanism or cap-setting approach appears to have changed from a simple multiple-style convention toward a benchmark-plus-basis-point style structure. Exact formula and bank categories are uncertain. This matters because LPR cuts transmit differently when deposit costs reprice slowly or under separate discipline rules.

### 16. Deposit liability beta and term competition remained a constraint on asset-side easing
**Classification:** useful_refinement  
**Importance:** high

Loan and bond yields could fall faster than sticky retail deposit costs, pressuring bank margins and weakening pass-through. Larger banks, joint-stock banks and smaller local banks may have faced different deposit franchises and wholesale funding dependence. Monetary transmission remains institution-specific.

### 17. 2022 shifted toward stronger growth support as property weakness and recurrent pandemic disruptions accumulated
**Classification:** new_category  
**Importance:** high

The policy stance appears to have become more supportive through rate/reserve/structural tools while authorities also faced property-sector stress and weak credit demand. This should be reconstructed as a transition from pandemic normalization to renewed cross-cyclical/countercyclical support, not as one uninterrupted easing trajectory.

### 18. The longer-tenor LPR became especially important for mortgage transmission in 2022
**Classification:** new_category  
**Importance:** high

Mortgage pricing and property support were increasingly connected to the longer-tenor LPR. Yet the effective borrower rate also depended on first-home/second-home rules, national/local floors and fixed add-points. Do not equate a longer-tenor LPR change with a uniform mortgage-rate change.

### 19. Mortgage-rate floors and city-level differentiation became a separate policy lever from the LPR itself
**Classification:** new_category  
**Importance:** high

Authorities could adjust national or local minimum spreads/floors while the LPR remained a reference. This creates at least two dimensions: benchmark reference and administrative/local floor. Exact city eligibility and floor changes need Evidence; broad memory is sufficient only to flag the mechanism.

### 20. Property-credit support must distinguish buyer mortgages, developer financing and broader monetary easing
**Classification:** useful_refinement  
**Importance:** high

A lower mortgage rate does not equal easier developer credit, and liquidity support to banks does not guarantee property lending. The 2022 period needs separate borrower-side mortgage demand, developer/project financing, bank risk appetite and broad system liquidity states. Regulation/resolution legal details remain outside this shard.

### 21. Policy-bank infrastructure financing and central-bank liquidity are distinct balance-sheet channels
**Classification:** new_category  
**Importance:** high

Infrastructure stabilization may have involved policy-bank credit or special financing instruments. That is not the same as central-bank lending to projects. Later reconstruction should trace Treasury/fiscal capital, policy-bank balance sheets, commercial-bank co-financing and PBOC liquidity support separately.

### 22. Central-bank profit remittance to the fiscal authority created an unusual fiscal-liquidity interaction without being QE
**Classification:** new_category  
**Importance:** medium-high

Memory suggests the PBOC remitted accumulated profits to the central government around 2022. Fiscal spending of those funds could affect base money/liquidity, but the transaction is different from central-bank asset purchases or monetizing new government debt. This is a valuable accounting lead and false-positive guard.

### 23. FX depreciation pressure and foreign-currency reserve tools should remain separate from RMB monetary easing
**Classification:** new_category  
**Importance:** medium-high

In 2022, exchange-rate pressure may have prompted adjustments to foreign-exchange reserve requirements or macroprudential FX tools. These affect foreign-currency liquidity and exchange-rate conditions differently from RMB RRR/OMO/MLF operations. Do not collapse them into one monetary stance measure.

### 24. Government-bond financing became more important to Social Financing composition
**Classification:** useful_refinement  
**Importance:** high

TSF during 2020-2022 can be heavily influenced by government bond issuance alongside bank loans and corporate financing. Flow, stock and component definition vintages must be preserved. Strong TSF can therefore reflect public-sector financing composition rather than equivalent private credit demand.

### 25. Counterexample: abundant bank liquidity did not guarantee strong private/property credit demand in 2022
**Classification:** duplicate  
**Importance:** medium

This confirms the established separation between liquidity supply and credit demand/risk appetite. Weak borrower demand, property uncertainty, collateral quality and bank risk management can block pass-through even when system liquidity is ample.
