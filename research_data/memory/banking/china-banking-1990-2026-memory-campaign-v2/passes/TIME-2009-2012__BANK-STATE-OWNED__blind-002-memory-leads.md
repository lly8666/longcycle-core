## Boundary note

This is an independent orthogonal blind-memory challenge, not a verification pass. It deliberately attacks the rev78 control-map gaps from governance, recognition, balance-sheet, liability, metric and counterexample angles. Exact figures, official category wording, transaction terms and dates are omitted when uncertain.

## Governance and decision-rights challenge

1. **[useful_refinement][high] Dual-principal governance was operational, not merely conceptual.** A listed state bank could face a state shareholder asking for macro stabilization and public/minority shareholders expecting risk-adjusted returns. The important research lead is where conflicts were actually resolved: board, senior management, credit committee, party/state-owner channel, or branch quota.

2. **[new_category][high] State backing can lower funding risk while increasing asset-allocation agency risk.** A strong sovereign franchise may make deposits and counterparties more stable, allowing a bank to carry policy-linked exposures longer. Funding resilience can therefore coexist with weaker marginal pricing or prolonged asset duration.

3. **[new_category][high] Credit quota transmission may bypass normal bottom-up risk appetite without literally bypassing approvals.** Head office can preserve formal credit process while changing sector limits, branch targets, strategic-client designations or hurdle rates so that policy-preferred lending clears the same formal workflow.

4. **[new_category] Accountability horizon mismatch.** Loan officers/branch managers could receive immediate credit for volume and client acquisition while the project’s first meaningful cash-flow test occurs years later. Later rotation of personnel can weaken ex-post accountability unless lifecycle responsibility is explicit.

5. **[new_category][high] The normalization phase is an underwriting stress test of the boom phase.** Once credit growth slows or policy tightens, borrowers that had relied on refinancing become easier to distinguish from genuinely self-liquidating projects. Asset quality should therefore be read by origination vintage and refinancing environment.

6. **[new_category][high] Aggregate bank ratios can hide vintage composition.** A bank with low overall NPLs may simultaneously have old cleaned-up books, newly originated unseasoned stimulus loans and rapidly growing retail assets. Aggregate ratios blend cohorts with radically different age and risk observability.

## Recognition, provisioning and metric semantics

7. **[new_category][high] Special-mention migration may be more informative than headline NPL at this stage.** Loans showing weakened repayment capacity can enter watch/special-mention categories before formal NPL migration; if the research record only captures NPL ratios it may miss the leading deterioration signal.

8. **[new_category][high] Overdue and NPL are separate dimensions.** An overdue loan is a payment-status concept; an NPL is a credit-classification concept. Their relationship can vary with cure, restructuring, collateral/support and classification policy. Historical comparison should not treat overdue balances as a synonym for NPLs.

9. **[new_category][high] Restructured/extended loan semantics are crucial.** A maturity extension can represent sensible project matching, temporary borrower relief or economic evergreening. Whether a restructured loan stays performing, migrates classification or carries extra provisioning determines how quickly economic weakness appears in reported asset quality.

10. **[new_category][high] Write-offs and disposals can mask gross formation if only ending NPL balance is observed.** A flat NPL balance can coexist with meaningful new NPL formation if recoveries, write-offs, transfers or upgrades offset inflows. A veteran analyst would want migration/formation and disposal flows, not only stock.

11. **[useful_refinement][high] Provision coverage is doubly path-dependent.** It depends on accumulated reserves and the recognized-NPL denominator; delayed recognition can make coverage appear exceptionally strong. High coverage is genuine loss-absorption capacity but is not a direct estimate of all latent expected loss.

12. **[useful_refinement][high] Reserve-to-total-loan measures challenge the NPL-denominator problem.** They are still affected by loan growth and provisioning policy, but they provide a different lens from provision coverage. Exact regulatory names and thresholds should remain an Evidence-stage question.

13. **[new_category][high] Credit cost, provision expense and reserve stock are not interchangeable.** A bank can have a high reserve stock from prior years but low current provision expense, or raise current credit cost before NPL balances spike. Time-series analysis needs flow-versus-stock separation.

14. **[new_category] ROE can remain strong while economic capital consumption worsens.** Regulated spreads, scale growth and operating leverage may support profits; rapid RWA growth can simultaneously demand more capital. ROE without capital-generation/RWA context can overstate sustainability.

## LGFV / infrastructure repayment-source challenge

15. **[useful_refinement][high] “LGFV loan” is not one economic category.** The legal borrower may be a platform company, but repayment can depend on project tolls/fees, land income, fiscal transfers, SOE operating cash flow, asset sales or refinancing. Credit analysis should classify repayment source rather than only borrower label.

16. **[new_category][high] Cash-flow coverage categories may have been a practical supervisory/management triage tool.** Memory suggests platforms were distinguished by whether operating/project cash flow fully, partially or inadequately covered debt service. Exact labels are uncertain; the mechanism should be searched later rather than reconstructed as fact now.

17. **[new_category][high] Land support is nonlinear.** Rising land prices can increase collateral values and local fiscal revenue together; a property slowdown can weaken both at once. That creates correlated PD/LGD deterioration rather than a simple collateral cushion.

18. **[new_category] Fiscal support can be indirect.** A platform may not receive an explicit guarantee yet can obtain subsidies, capital injections, land, asset transfers, fee rights or payments from another local entity. The economic sponsor can therefore matter more than legal guarantee wording.

19. **[new_category][high] Refinancing is part credit event, part policy choice.** If a weak infrastructure borrower is repeatedly refinanced because abrupt default would disrupt public projects, observed default rates understate the duration/capital cost of the exposure. But refinancing may also be rational for long-lived assets with initially mismatched maturities.

20. **[new_category] Platform “exit” or normalization can change reporting categories without changing underlying project economics.** A borrower may later be treated as an ordinary corporate borrower if cash flows strengthen or government linkage changes. Time-series exposure data may therefore shift due to classification boundary changes.

21. **[duplicate][high] Counterexample: some infrastructure/platform borrowers were economically sound.** Toll roads, utilities or other projects with durable cash flows and strong fiscal regions can repay without hidden bailout. A blanket “platform = bad debt” interpretation is not admissible.

22. **[new_category] Concentration can create underwriting expertise as well as risk.** A bank historically strong in infrastructure may know project finance, construction economics and government counterparties better than peers. Sector concentration does not mechanically imply worse underwriting; it can produce both expertise and correlated loss exposure.

## Four-bank heterogeneity and ABC challenge

23. **[new_category][high] ICBC’s scale may reduce idiosyncratic regional exposure while increasing macro beta.** Broad geographic/customer diversification can absorb local failures, but a nationwide policy-credit push means the bank remains highly exposed to the national credit cycle.

24. **[useful_refinement] BOC’s international franchise changes the denominator of comparison.** Cross-border trade, FX, overseas assets and RMB internationalization-related businesses may diversify domestic loan income while adding market/country/counterparty risk that CCB/ABC carry less intensely.

25. **[new_category][high] ABC’s late reform may make headline asset-quality improvement partly a balance-sheet-reset story.** Recapitalization/restructuring and later market discipline can rapidly improve starting metrics, but county/rural credit processes, historical burdens and policy mission may season differently from already-listed peers.

26. **[new_category][high] ABC’s county business may require differentiated governance rather than simple replication of urban branch models.** Small borrowers, agriculture cycles, local information, collateral limits and dispersed outlets make centralized scoring/approval harder to apply uniformly.

27. **[new_category] Rural deposit franchise can be an asset and a cost.** A wide county network can provide sticky granular deposits and cross-selling reach while carrying high branch operating costs and lower average transaction size. Cost-to-income and deposit economics should therefore be decomposed geographically.

28. **[new_category] Listing can improve disclosure without erasing mandate.** ABC’s public-market transition could strengthen capital, governance and reporting while the state continued to expect agriculture/rural support. “Listed” should not be coded as “fully commercial objective.”

## Early wealth / off-balance / contingent boundary challenge

29. **[useful_refinement][high] WMP growth can represent customer-asset migration rather than bank funding in a legal sense.** If a customer buys a non-deposit wealth product, the bank may earn fees and retain the relationship while the asset/liability treatment differs from deposits. Economic liquidity/reputation can still reconnect the product to the bank.

30. **[useful_refinement][high] Implicit-support expectations are a separate risk channel from contractual guarantee.** Even where the bank did not legally guarantee principal, customers might expect rescue because the product was sold through branches under the bank brand. Reputational support can create contingent balance-sheet risk after losses emerge.

31. **[new_category][high] Early bank–trust cooperation can be understood as a quota/balance-sheet boundary experiment.** Banks could connect clients to trust financing or credit-like assets outside conventional loan books. The analytical task is to determine who ultimately bore credit risk and liquidity risk, not to project later multi-layer nesting structures backward.

32. **[useful_refinement] Entrusted lending is primarily agency administration unless the bank adds separate exposure.** The entrusted principal is supplied by another entity, so it should not be treated as ordinary bank lending; however, credit screening, settlement, fee and relationship incentives may still matter.

33. **[new_category][high] Bank acceptances and guarantees have conversion risk.** A contingent commitment can become funded exposure if the customer cannot perform. Off-balance notional amounts therefore need conversion/credit-quality semantics rather than simple addition to loans.

34. **[useful_refinement] Fee income linked to financing is not fully independent of credit cycle.** Arrangement, guarantee, settlement or wealth fees may rise alongside customer financing activity and fall when regulation or credit demand changes. “Capital-light” should be tested product by product.

35. **[new_category] Product maturity and reporting dates can interact with deposit competition.** Short WMP maturities or product redemptions near month/quarter/year ends may move customer funds between wealth products and deposits, complicating point-in-time deposit figures. This is a search lead, not a claim about deliberate accounting manipulation.

## Funding, pricing and treasury challenge

36. **[useful_refinement][high] Loan-to-deposit pressure operates through branch incentives, not just a bank-level ratio.** Head office can translate a binding funding constraint into branch deposit targets, client bundling and internal transfer-pricing signals; the behavioral effects may appear before aggregate liquidity stress.

37. **[new_category] “冲时点”/period-end deposit gathering can make liability stability look stronger at reporting dates than on daily averages.** Exact vocabulary, rule changes and prevalence require Evidence, but average-versus-point-in-time balances are a useful metric-semantic distinction.

38. **[useful_refinement] Bill financing can act as a quota/liquidity valve.** Because discounted bills are short-duration and linked to trade/payment instruments, their share may swing quickly when banks need to use credit quota, manage yields or respond to tightening. Ordinary-loan growth and total-credit growth can therefore diverge.

39. **[duplicate] Large state banks were not primarily wholesale-funded institutions in this period.** Their interbank/treasury activity mattered, but large deposit bases reduce the relevance of the later “wholesale funding run” template for interpreting 2009–2012.

40. **[new_category] Early pricing liberalization increases the importance of internal transfer pricing.** As permitted loan/deposit rates became less mechanically tied to a single benchmark, branch/product profitability increasingly depended on FTP, customer risk pricing and liability scarcity rather than posted rate alone.

41. **[duplicate][high] NIM and net interest income can move differently.** Asset growth can offset margin compression in absolute earnings, so strong net interest income does not prove unchanged pricing power.

## Hidden concentration, controls and stress archetypes

42. **[new_category][high] Guarantee-chain contagion can convert granular private-enterprise loans into a correlated portfolio.** Mutual guarantees among firms can create network exposure that borrower-level limits miss; one failure can trigger calls across otherwise performing companies.

43. **[new_category][high] Steel-trade / warehouse-receipt problems are a collateral-governance archetype.** Duplicate pledges, weak warehouse control, commodity-price volatility or financing of speculative inventory can defeat apparently secured short-term lending. The value of this lead is the control failure, not an asserted aggregate loss amount.

44. **[new_category] Wenzhou/private-credit stress may affect banks through collateral and guarantee networks even when direct informal-lending exposure is absent.** Falling property/business values and entrepreneur defaults can impair bank-secured loans indirectly.

45. **[useful_refinement][high] Developer loans and mortgages should be stress-tested differently but can become correlated in a property downturn.** Developers face liquidity/refinancing and sales risk; households face income/payment and collateral risk. A severe property shock can hit both, with different timing and LGD behavior.

46. **[new_category][high] Group-customer master data is a governance capability, not just IT plumbing.** To enforce concentration limits the bank must identify parent/subsidiary links, common controllers, guarantees and local-government affiliations. Poor entity resolution can make formally compliant limits economically porous.

47. **[new_category] Basel/internal-rating tools can be overridden by mandate or model limitations.** PD/LGD, economic capital and stress tests improve information, but their influence depends on approval authority, data history and whether policy-priority lending receives exceptions or different hurdle rates.

48. **[new_category] Economic-capital allocation may be more informative ex post than ex ante during a policy surge.** A bank can later reprice branch performance or tighten sector capital charges after risks become visible. The chronology of tool adoption versus actual lending decisions is itself a research question.

## Contemporaneous judgment and closing counterfactuals

49. **[duplicate][high] The core contemporaneous debate likely remained “successful reform or latent credit cost?”** Strong profits, capital raising and low reported NPLs supported the success narrative; the opposite view emphasized stimulus vintages, refinancing, platform/property dependence and delayed recognition.

50. **[useful_refinement][high] State support can transform default risk into duration, pricing and capital drag.** A loan that is repeatedly refinanced may never realize a conventional default yet still deliver weak economic return and consume capital. Loss analysis should include opportunity cost and extension risk, not only write-offs.

51. **[new_category] Counterexample: policy-directed lending can strengthen franchise relationships.** Financing strategic SOEs, infrastructure and local projects can deepen deposits, settlement, payroll, custody and other business links. A low-spread loan may support broader customer economics, making loan-level profitability incomplete.

52. **[new_category] Counterexample: tighter post-boom controls can make the same institution safer before losses peak.** If banks strengthened centralized approval, monitoring and provisioning after recognizing emerging risks, later credit-cost realization does not imply controls were continuously weak throughout the slice.
