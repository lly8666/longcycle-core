## Orthogonal blind-memory leads

1. **[new_category | high] Late-1990s recapitalization and bad-asset transfer are two different balance-sheet operations.** New capital improves the liability/equity side and loss-absorbing capacity; moving problem assets changes asset composition. Treating them as one `cleanup` event hides which constraint each intervention addressed.

2. **[new_category | high] The remembered special-government-bond recapitalization likely involved a circular or coordinated balance-sheet chain among the sovereign/fiscal side, state banks and the monetary system.** Exact cash flows and accounting entries are uncertain; later Evidence must reconstruct who subscribed, what asset the bank received and how bank capital changed rather than simply saying `government injected cash`.

3. **[new_category | high] AMC transfers created a new creditor/claim-holder rather than making the underlying borrower debt disappear.** Corporate restructuring, collateral disposal, debt-equity swaps, recoveries or write-offs could occur after transfer; therefore bank derecognition and economy-wide loss resolution are separate states.

4. **[new_category | high] AMC funding itself is a material research object.** The asset manager needed liabilities/funding to acquire bank assets, potentially involving bonds to banks and/or official funding. Exact structures differ and must be verified; the key blind lead is that asset transfer can replace a bad loan with another claim rather than pure cash.

5. **[new_category | high] Face-value versus economic-value transfer is central to loss allocation.** If impaired assets moved at an administratively determined or book-related value, the eventual economic loss could remain embedded in AMC funding, fiscal support, bank claims or later recoveries. Do not equate transfer price with market fair value.

6. **[useful_refinement | high] One-to-one bank–AMC pairings are a useful starting index but should not be treated as perfectly exclusive portfolios.** Later Evidence should test whether all assets followed the headline pairing, which vintages qualified and whether later transactions crossed those simple boundaries.

7. **[new_category | high] Five-category classification changes the recognition state of a loan, not necessarily its contractual payment status at the same instant.** A loan may be classified as risky before default, while a rolled-over or technically current loan may still be economically weak. Classification, delinquency and impairment are distinct dimensions.

8. **[useful_refinement | high] `关注`-type classification is especially important for long-run comparability because it represents heightened risk without being identical to the later headline NPL numerator.** A migration from normal to watch/special-mention can signal deterioration before reported NPLs rise.

9. **[new_category | high] Interest-recognition policy can create a second accounting break beside NPL classification.** Whether interest on weak/overdue loans remains accrued, is suspended, reversed or recognized on cash basis affects both income and asset carrying values. Exact `停息/应收未收利息` vocabulary and rules require source verification.

10. **[new_category | high] Provisioning has at least three separate objects: regulatory/accounting requirement, expense recognized in current profit, and actual write-off/use of reserves.** Long-run bank profitability should not collapse these into one `credit cost` concept before verifying period-native accounting.

11. **[useful_refinement | high] Reported capital adequacy after recapitalization is not equivalent to a fully cleaned risk profile.** Risk weights, recognized NPLs, off-balance-sheet commitments and remaining untransferred weak loans can make the same capital ratio mean different things across reform stages.

12. **[new_category | high] `一级法人` centralization should be tested through specific control channels: loan authorization, branch balance-sheet/fund limits, personnel accountability, audit, large-exposure escalation and post-loan monitoring.** Legal-person unity alone does not prove these controls were operationally effective.

13. **[new_category | medium] Branch consolidation can have a risk-control rationale as well as a cost-cutting rationale.** Removing layers or weak outlets may reduce local capture and unauthorized lending while also shrinking staffing/overhead; later evidence should avoid interpreting all outlet reduction as retail strategy.

14. **[useful_refinement | high] SOE restructuring can change bank asset quality without normal borrower repayment.** Mergers, debt restructuring, policy closure, debt-equity swaps, fiscal support and AMC transfer can all alter the bank's exposure. `NPL resolution` therefore has multiple exit states.

15. **[new_category | high] Debt-equity swap mechanisms associated with AMCs may shift credit risk into equity/ownership and enterprise restructuring risk rather than extinguish economic exposure.** Exact programs, firms and timing are Evidence work; the important blind category is claim transformation.

16. **[new_category | medium] A bank's willingness to renew or extend an SOE loan may depend on whether it expected later official restructuring/AMC eligibility.** This creates a possible transition-era moral-hazard/forbearance mechanism, but memory is weak and it should be treated as a falsifiable hypothesis rather than assumed behavior.

17. **[useful_refinement | medium] `惜贷` after tighter accountability should be decomposed into branch officer incentives, borrower risk, capital/asset-quality pressure and aggregate liquidity.** A bank can have ample funding yet ration credit because marginal loans threaten future NPL accountability.

18. **[new_category | high] Bank heterogeneity affects cleanup not only through NPL origin but through recoverability.** CCB project assets may have physical/project collateral, BOC may have FX/trade or overseas claims, ABC may face dispersed rural/agricultural borrowers, while ICBC may have concentrated industrial/SOE exposures; recovery channels and restructuring tools can differ.

19. **[useful_refinement | medium] Policy-bank carve-out and AMC cleanup solve different legacy problems.** The first reallocates future/ongoing policy mandates; the second addresses already accumulated weak assets. A commercial bank could complete one transition while still carrying the other burden.

20. **[duplicate | medium] Generic `commercialization was incomplete` adds no independent category once governance controls, residual policy burdens, recognition rules and cleanup mechanics are represented explicitly.**

21. **[duplicate | medium] Generic `Asian crisis accelerated reform` is not an additional mechanism without specifying which capital, liquidity, governance or recognition response changed.** It remains contextual rather than a standalone lead.

## Counterexamples / falsification targets

- Asset transfer can improve the bank's reported NPL ratio without equivalent borrower repayment or economy-wide loss elimination.
- Capital injection can restore solvency buffers without fixing underwriting or branch incentives.
- Five-category classification can worsen reported asset-quality metrics simply because recognition becomes more forward-looking.
- A legally unified bank can still have material branch agency problems.
- A debt-equity swap changes the form of a claim; it is not automatically a recovery at par.
- Lower loan growth can reflect risk/accountability constraints even when liquidity policy is supportive.
