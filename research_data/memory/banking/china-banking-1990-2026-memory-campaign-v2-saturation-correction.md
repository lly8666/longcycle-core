## 2. Why the earlier stop rule failed

A large industry can stop producing new **headings** while still producing large amounts of new useful memory inside existing headings: forgotten actors, old regulatory vocabulary, failed institutions, product mechanics, balance-sheet semantics, specific credit-cycle transmission, management narratives, policy implementation details, regional variation, chronology, predecessor/successor links and counterexamples.

Therefore “top-level ontology stable” is only an orientation milestone, not saturation.

## 3. Correct Banking saturation standard

The Banking campaign now adopts a stronger, lithium-style exhaustion discipline adapted to banking:

1. independent atomic shards, each recalled without reading other shard raw outputs;
2. time-slice passes across the full 1990–2026 horizon;
3. bridge-theme passes for mechanisms that cross institutions/business lines;
4. per-shard passes for timeline, actors, products, metrics, mechanisms, contemporaneous expectations, failures, terminology and self-gap;
5. new leads classified as `new_category`, `useful_refinement` or `duplicate`;
6. long-tail/forgotten/failure/old-vocabulary/negative-space content must be explicitly tested;
7. at least **three consecutive low-novelty passes** are required before a shard may be considered saturation-eligible;
8. a campaign-level coverage matrix must show no material uncovered time × institution × business/risk/mechanism region;
9. discovery of a material missing causal antecedent can extend the historical horizon or reopen a shard;
10. no Internet evidence search for an unsealed shard.

## 4. What remains valid from Batch0

Batch0 remains useful as an ontology and hypothesis source. In particular, it already identifies major domains such as:

- bank types and institutional evolution;
- monetary/regulatory system;
- deposits, loans, NIM and ALM;
- capital, liquidity and asset quality;
- property, LGFV/local-government finance and shadow banking;
- wealth management, interbank/market business and retail finance;
- accounting/comparability issues, governance, technology, regional structure and demographics;
- approximate historical stages and candidate causal mechanisms.

But these are now inputs to shard planning, not proof of saturation.
