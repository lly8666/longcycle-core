## Blind Memory Leads — metrics and state variables

These are candidate metric families to verify, not asserted values.

### Funding and deposits

- Total deposits by quarter and major inflection points.
- Deposit growth during the 2020–2021 liquidity/venture-funding boom.
- Noninterest-bearing versus interest-bearing deposit mix.
- Deposit concentration by customer type and any disclosed concentration measures.
- Insured versus uninsured deposit share, with denominator and reporting date kept explicit.
- Deposit outflow / inflow rates by period; distinguish reported balances from later estimates of run speed.
- Deposit pricing, deposit beta, and migration from noninterest-bearing to interest-bearing products.
- Wholesale funding, brokered deposits, FHLB advances, discount-window or other secured borrowing where source-supported.

### Assets, duration and earnings

- Cash and cash-equivalent liquidity.
- Loan balances and loan mix.
- Available-for-sale (AFS) and held-to-maturity (HTM) securities balances.
- Carrying value versus fair value for securities, with accounting classification preserved.
- Unrealized gains/losses and where they do or do not flow through reported capital under the applicable accounting/regulatory treatment.
- Asset duration / sensitivity measures if actually disclosed; do not manufacture one number from a model narrative.
- Interest-rate hedges: type, notional, maturity, purpose, termination/reduction history, and contemporaneous rationale if source-supported.
- Net interest income, net interest margin, asset yield, funding cost and their stated sensitivity to rate/deposit assumptions.
- Tangible common equity and regulatory capital measures such as CET1, with exact definition and consolidation boundary.

### Liquidity and run capacity

- On-balance-sheet cash.
- Unpledged / pledgeable securities and collateral values at the relevant market date.
- Contractual or immediately available borrowing capacity, with collateral haircut and operational eligibility separated from headline capacity.
- Liquidity ratios actually applicable to the institution; do not assume large-bank LCR/NSFR requirements without checking tailoring thresholds and dates.
- Same-day or next-day depositor withdrawal requests only from dated primary or well-grounded source material.

## Blind Memory Leads — candidate mechanism chain

**Research-only mechanism hypothesis, not historical truth:**

1. A large deposit inflow during the 2020–2021 technology/venture-funding boom may have exceeded attractive near-term loan deployment opportunities.
2. The bank appears to have deployed a material portion of that funding into fixed-income securities, including longer-duration assets.
3. Rapid policy-rate increases in 2022 likely reduced the market value of those fixed-rate securities while also changing customer cash needs and the economics of deposit funding.
4. If deposits declined while securities carried large unrealized losses, liquidity needs could force a trade-off between realizing losses, raising wholesale funding, posting collateral, shrinking assets, or raising capital.
5. A visible loss realization / balance-sheet restructuring / capital-raising signal in early March 2023 may have changed depositor and investor expectations sharply.
6. A concentrated, digitally connected depositor base may have made confidence and withdrawal dynamics unusually fast once expectations changed.

Every arrow above needs independent claim-scoped verification. Later failure must not be used as proof that each prior link was already knowable or inevitable.

## Blind Memory Leads — contemporaneous Judgment / expectation candidates

These are the highest-value expectation questions for later source recovery. They should be preserved as dated claims by identified speakers, not paraphrased with hindsight.

1. **Deposit trajectory:** What did management expect about deposit normalization, client cash burn, venture funding conditions, and the timing of renewed deposit growth at each earnings date from roughly 2021 through early 2023?
2. **Rate path and NII:** What rate scenarios did management use or discuss, and how did those scenarios interact with asset yields, deposit beta, funding cost and net interest income expectations?
3. **Securities / duration positioning:** What was the contemporaneous rationale for securities duration and any hedge additions/removals? Was the trade-off framed as earnings protection, liquidity, capital, or another objective?
4. **Liquidity confidence:** What liquidity resources did management present as available, and under what assumptions? Did market participants or rating agencies challenge those assumptions before the final stress event?
5. **Capital adequacy versus economic value:** How did management/investors distinguish reported regulatory capital from unrealized economic losses? When did this become a decision-relevant concern in contemporaneous discourse?
6. **Customer concentration / stickiness:** What did management claim or imply about client relationships, operating deposits, uninsured concentration, or deposit stability before stress?
7. **Supervisory expectations:** What risks did supervisors identify contemporaneously, when were they known to the bank, and what remediation expectations existed? Post-event supervisory reports must be treated as later Outcome/reconstruction unless they explicitly preserve earlier known-time evidence.
8. **Capital raise / restructuring signal:** Immediately before the failure, what did management say it expected the balance-sheet actions to accomplish, and what did investors/depositors appear to infer at that time?

## Approximate timeline leads to verify after seal

No exact date below should be promoted from this packet alone.

- **2020–2021:** possible exceptional deposit growth alongside strong venture/technology funding conditions; possible expansion of securities holdings.
- **2021–2022:** possible changes in duration/hedging posture and increasing sensitivity to a higher-rate scenario.
- **2022:** rapid monetary tightening; falling fair values on fixed-rate securities; changing deposit mix/outflows and higher funding costs become increasingly relevant.
- **Late 2022 / early 2023:** profitability, deposit trajectory, liquidity and unrealized-loss questions may become more salient in disclosures and external analysis.
- **Early March 2023:** balance-sheet restructuring / securities sale and capital-raising actions appear to precede an acute confidence/liquidity event.
- **March 2023 and after:** failure/receivership and later official reviews become Outcome/reconstruction material and MUST NOT be backdated into earlier Judgment.

## Point-in-time / no-lookahead acceptance boundary

For each later assertion, preserve at minimum the distinction among:

1. **valid/effective time** — when the balance-sheet state, action, or condition applied;
2. **source/market-known time** — conservative earliest time a historical observer could have known the claim from the source;
3. **system/research adoption time** — when Longcycle ingested or reconciled it;
4. **expectation target time/range** — what future period a contemporaneous Judgment was predicting.

Replay rules for this subject:

- A 2022 cutoff cannot see the March 2023 failure or later official post-mortems.
- Later confirmation that securities lost value cannot rewrite what management or investors believed at an earlier cutoff.
- Post-event testimony may describe earlier events but is later-known reconstruction unless tied to an earlier source that was then knowable.
- If memory recalls only a quarter/month/rough sequence, keep that precision until Evidence proves more.
- Reported regulatory capital, accounting equity, fair-value economic loss, liquidity capacity and depositor behavior are distinct semantics; do not collapse them into a single "solvency" label.
